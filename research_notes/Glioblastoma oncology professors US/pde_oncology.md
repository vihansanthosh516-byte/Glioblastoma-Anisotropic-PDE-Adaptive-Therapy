# PDE-Based Tumor Modeling and Mathematical Oncology Researchers in the US

## Who publishes on PDE models of tumor growth or reaction-diffusion in cancer?

### Takeaway
Leading US researchers in PDE-based tumor modeling include Kristin Swanson (Mayo Clinic/ASU), Hermann Frieboes (University of Louisville), and Yang Kuang (Arizona State University). These researchers and their groups have published extensively on Fisher-Kolmogorov models, reaction-diffusion systems, and mechanistic coupling approaches for tumor growth prediction.

## Which labs study anisotropic diffusion, heterogeneous tumor environments, or spatial dynamics?

### Takeaway
Multiple research groups explicitly incorporate anisotropic diffusion (directional heterogeneity in tumor spread), including Kristin Swanson's Mathematical Neuro-Oncology Lab and groups at University of Louisville and ASU. Recent work shows that anisotropic modeling demonstrates higher proportions of subthreshold tumors than isotropic models and is sensitive to white-matter tract anisotropy in glioblastoma.

### Cited Findings

**Anisotropic Diffusion in Glioblastoma Research**
- Glioblastomas spread anisotropically, expanding primarily along white matter axon tracts and blood vessels rather than isotropically — [arXiv 2210.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01537)
- Swanson's reaction-diffusion model includes spatial dependence of diffusion coefficient to account for anisotropy based on rat experiments showing differential dispersion along white vs. gray matter — [arXiv 1707.09409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.09409)
- White matter tracts from diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) data are used to parameterize preferential cell migration directions in tumor models — [arXiv 2210.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01537)
- Anisotropic modeling demonstrates higher proportion and extent of subthreshold tumor (tumor below MRI detection limit) than predicted by isotropic modeling — [arXiv 2210.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01537)

**Heterogeneous Tumor Environments**
- Recent phenotype-structured reaction-diffusion models of avascular glioma growth incorporate heterogeneity of tumor cell populations — [arXiv 2509.22519](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22519)
- Structural anisotropy of extracellular environment impacts both tumor growth rate and phenotypic selection at invasive tumor front — [arXiv 2210.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01537)
- Treatment outcomes are highly sensitive to spatial heterogeneity, diffusion anisotropy, and timing of interventions — [arXiv 2210.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01537)

**Specific Anisotropic Modeling Methods**
- Researchers substitute scalar diffusion coefficient D with n-dimensional diffusion tensor to account for anisotropic tissue properties — [arXiv 1707.09409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.09409)
- Non-homogeneous reaction-diffusion models with spatially varying diffusion represent heterogeneous tissue environments and preferential migration along white matter — [arXiv Search Results](https://drepo.sdl.edu.sa/items/03a38d56-a8a4-48bb-81ac-5303949e4571)

### Inferences
- The field has made substantial progress from simple isotropic Fisher-Kolmogorov models toward patient-specific anisotropic formulations using DTI data from clinical MRI.
- Anisotropy is not a minor refinement but significantly affects predictions about actual tumor extent, with important implications for radiotherapy planning.
- Heterogeneity modeling is evolving from single-population PDE models to multi-population and phenotype-structured approaches.

### Gaps
- Specific group names and institution affiliations for authors of the 2022+ papers on anisotropic glioma models (e.g., arXiv 2210.01537) not identified in searches; full papers would be needed to determine research group locations.

## What mathematics and computational methods are they using?

### Takeaway
Researchers employ a spectrum of mathematical approaches: classical reaction-diffusion PDEs (Fisher-Kolmogorov), mechanistically-coupled PDE systems, anisotropic diffusion tensors, delay differential equations, and increasingly machine learning and AI for parameter estimation. Computational methods include finite element/difference schemes for PDE solving, multigrid techniques, and neural surrogate models (FNO mentioned in some work).

### Cited Findings

**Core PDE Formulations**
- Fisher-Kolmogorov equation for tumor growth: ∂u/∂t = ∇·(D∇u) + ρu(1−u/K), where u is tumor cell density, D is diffusion coefficient, ρ is growth rate, K is carrying capacity — [arXiv 1707.09409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.09409)
- Swanson's variant includes spatial dependence: ∂c/∂t = ∇·(D(x)∇c) + ρc, reflecting anisotropic diffusivity — [arXiv 1707.09409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.09409)
- Standard Fisher-Kolmogorov predicts tumor margin velocity: v_FK ≈ 2√(ρD) — [arXiv Search Results](https://scholar.barrowneuro.org/neurosurgery/2343)

**Mechanistically-Coupled Systems**
- PDE systems of Cahn-Hilliard type coupled with reaction-diffusion equations for nutrients and oxygenation — [AIJR](https://books.aijr.org/index.php/press/catalog/book/138/chapter/2069)
- Tissue growth models incorporating advective effects from nutrient/oxygen availability or cell self-propulsion (Perthame-Quirós-Vázquez framework) — [AIJR](https://books.aijr.org/index.php/press/catalog/book/138/chapter/2069)
- Multi-population models dividing tumors into actively proliferating and quiescent cells — [arXiv 1707.09409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.09409)

**Computational Solution Methods**
- Finite element and finite difference methods for solving coupled PDE systems in 2D/3D spatial domains — [AIJR](https://books.aijr.org/index.php/press/catalog/book/138/chapter/2069)
- Multigrid techniques for efficient numerical solutions — [arXiv Search Results](https://icqmb.ucr.edu/media/751/download)
- Parameter estimation using inverse problem approaches: L-BFGS-B bounded optimization, data assimilation from patient imaging — [Project Documentation](https://www.mitacs.ca/our-projects/incorporating-tumour-heterogeneity-into-spatial-optimization-of-radiation-therapy-of-brain-tumours/)

**Mathematical Oncology Approaches**
- Delay differential equations applied to within-host disease dynamics (Kuang's specialization) — [ASU Faculty](https://search.asu.edu/profile/4721247)
- Integration of machine learning and AI with mathematical models for patient-specific predictions — [Mayo Clinic](https://mayoclinic.org/biographies/swanson-kristin-r-ph-d/bio-20164843)
- Optimal control theory applied to treatment scheduling — [FSU Mathematics](https://math.fsu.edu/~wxm/applied_math/Seminar_Archives.htm)

**Anisotropic Tensor Methods**
- Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) data from clinical MRI used to derive patient-specific anisotropic diffusion tensors for tumor models — [arXiv 2210.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01537)
- Scalar diffusion coefficient D replaced with 3×3 symmetric positive-definite anisotropic diffusion tensor D(x,y,z) — [arXiv 1707.09409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.09409)

### Inferences
- The mathematical sophistication has evolved from 1-parameter Fisher-Kolmogorov to 5-10 parameter coupled systems with patient-specific tensor fields.
- Computational methods in the field are transitioning from classical PDE numerics toward hybrid physics-informed neural networks and data-driven surrogate models.
- Parameter estimation from clinical data is becoming as important as the PDE formulation itself in patient-specific applications.

### Gaps
- Limited detail on specific neural network approaches (FNO, physics-informed neural networks) in the published searches; only mentioned in passing.
- No comprehensive survey of software tools/packages used in the field (e.g., FEniCS, COMSOL, custom C++ codes).

## What are the key mathematical/computational challenges and open problems?

### Takeaway
Researchers identify several ongoing challenges: accurate parameter estimation from limited clinical imaging data, incorporating realistic tissue heterogeneity and anisotropy into parsimonious models, validation against longitudinal patient cohorts, and bridging scales from cellular mechanisms to organ-level predictions. Integration of machine learning with mechanistic PDEs and real-time treatment planning optimization remain open areas.

### Cited Findings

**Parameter Estimation and Inverse Problems**
- Patient-specific parameter estimation requires solving inverse problems to infer tumor growth rate ρ and diffusion parameters D from limited longitudinal MRI data — [arXiv 2210.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01537)
- MRI underestimates actual extent of glioma invasion; reaction-diffusion models attempt to predict "invisible" tumor burden — [Mayo Clinic Lab](https://mayo.edu:443/research/labs/mathematical-neuro-oncology)
- Balancing model complexity (anisotropic tensors, heterogeneous diffusion) with identifiability from clinical data — [arXiv 2210.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01537)

**Model Validation and Clinical Translation**
- Swanson's group has explored utility of patient-specific models for estimating patient survival and optimizing treatment strategies, but clinical adoption remains limited — [arXiv 1707.09409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.09409)
- Treatment outcomes are highly sensitive to spatial heterogeneity, diffusion anisotropy, and timing — requiring robust uncertainty quantification — [arXiv 2210.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01537)
- Validation against real patient cohorts needed to establish predictive accuracy and clinical utility — [arXiv 2210.01537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01537)

**Mechanistic Coupling and Complexity**
- Moving beyond reaction-diffusion toward coupled systems including nutrient/oxygen transport, immune cell recruitment, and genetic heterogeneity — [AIJR](https://books.aijr.org/index.php/press/catalog/book/138/chapter/2069)
- Two-population models (proliferating vs. quiescent) and phenotype-structured models emerging as next frontier — [arXiv 1707.09409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.09409)

### Inferences
- The field has moved from "can we build a model?" (1990s-2000s) to "can we personalize it to a patient?" (2010s-2020s) to now "can we integrate it with AI and clinical workflows?" (2020s+).
- Clinical integration and regulatory approval of model-guided treatment planning remains a major bottleneck.

### Gaps
- Limited recent literature on how these models integrate with modern AI/deep learning in clinical practice.
- No clear evidence of how FDA approval pathways treat mathematical oncology models.
- Specific failure modes and cases where models break down not detailed in public-facing materials.
