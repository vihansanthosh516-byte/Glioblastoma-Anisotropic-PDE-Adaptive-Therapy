# Track A prior art: patient-paired regional transcriptomic methods

Search date: 2026-11-16

## Scope and conclusion

This is a scoped prior-art inventory for classifiers or scores that distinguish pathology-defined glioblastoma regions or matched transcriptomic samples, with patients kept intact across evaluation folds. It covers relative-expression pair classifiers, matched-pair feature selection, stability/robust selection, pseudobulk and common classifiers, and spatial domain/gradient/graph methods. It is not possible to certify a literal list of every classifier that could be applied to expression data; the method families are open-ended. The inventory identifies direct precedents and a minimum defensible comparator set.

**Novelty conclusion:** Patient-level rank pairs are established. So are pair selection by class-specific ordering proportions, replication of pair effects across subjects or studies, stability selection on matched-pair models, and GBM cross-platform prediction with rank-pair features. A method described only as "patient-paired worst-case rank-pair selection" is therefore not demonstrably unique. The precise maximin objective (if it is the minimum donor-level agreement of each pair) may be a narrow new variant, but this search did not establish priority or absence. A novelty claim must state the formula and distinguish it from the methods below; it must not claim that rank-pair prediction, patient consistency, or robustness is new.

No expression matrix or test outcome from the project dataset was opened or analyzed.

## Directly overlapping rank-pair and relative-order methods

| Method | Existing method | Relevance / overlap | Source or implementation |
|---|---|---|---|
| TSP | Top-scoring pairs classify a sample using the within-sample ordering of two genes. The pair is selected for a large difference in ordering frequency between classes. | Exact overlap for using gene-order reversals as predictors. A maximin donor rule changes the selection objective, not the rank-pair representation. | [Geman et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1989150/); [tspair paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btp126); [Bioconductor source for tspair](https://git.bioconductor.org/packages/tspair) |
| k-TSP / switchBox | Combines multiple gene pairs, typically disjoint, with a voting rule. The switchBox implementation uses a greedy variance-optimization procedure and can filter features or pairs. | Required direct benchmark. It is the ordinary multi-pair predictor against which a worst-patient pair selector must be tested. | [Tan et al.](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/21/20/3896/203010); [switchBox paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4287945/); [Bioconductor package and source](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/switchBox.html) |
| Generalized TSP / Rgtsp | Extends pair rules to multiclass classification and provides a parallel implementation. | Relevant if there are more than two regions or a score produces multiple regional classes. | [Lin et al.](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/27/12/1729/256967); [Rgtsp repository](https://github.com/bioinfo-recetox/Rgtsp) |
| AUCTSP | Selects a pair using an AUC-based score to reduce some TSP overfitting and tie issues. | Required single-pair rank baseline. It still uses relative expression ordering and therefore overlaps with any rank-pair contribution. | [Kagaris et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6020231/); [authors' C code](https://github.com/SIU852343578/AUC-TSP) |
| MetaKTSP | Aggregates k-TSP evidence across independent studies for cross-study prediction. | Direct precedent for cross-study rank-pair replication. Its intended design requires multiple development studies; it cannot be reproduced as intended with only one cohort. | [Kim et al.](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/32/13/1966/1743805); [repository](https://github.com/metaOmic/MetaKTSP) |
| Top-scoring N / triplet ordering | Generalizes relative-expression classifiers from pairs to permutations of N features, including triplets. | Broader relative-order methods make novelty claims about ordering rules alone especially weak. It is a secondary comparator if computation and feature count permit. | [Magis and Price](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3663421/) |
| Rank-based trees and ensembles | Builds decision trees or ensembles from binary gene-pair order features. | Direct comparator for nonlinear combinations and interactions among gene-pair indicators; more flexible than selecting a small hand-weighted pair set. | [Yin et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10848444/); [ranktreeEnsemble code](https://github.com/TransBioInfoLab/ranktreeEnsemble) |
| Cross-study heterogeneity pGLMM | Converts all candidate gene pairs into binary within-sample order indicators, then fits a penalized GLMM with fixed and study-specific random effects. It selects effects that replicate across subjects/studies and uses nonzero fixed effects for new-subject prediction. | Closest methodological overlap found: pair-order predictors plus reproducibility across subjects/studies and external prediction. It differs from a per-pair worst-donor/minimum-concordance objective by modeling fixed and random effects across studies. | [Rashid et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7528965/); the article supplement provides R/Rcpp code; general implementation of penalized GLMMs: [glmmPen](https://cran.r-project.org/package=glmmPen) |
| GBM robust rank-pair ensemble | Published GBM work predicts sex-specific patient clusters across microarray and RNA-seq using gene-pair order features, a proportion-difference pair score, and a Robencla ensemble. The score combines class-versus-rest ordering proportions and expression-distance terms; later feature updating uses cross-dataset concordance. | Especially close disease/domain precedent. It demonstrates GBM rank-pair classifiers, patient consistency in ordering frequencies, cross-platform robustness, and a predictor built from paired expression comparisons. It does not target pathology-defined core versus infiltrative edge, and its score is not the same as a minimum over individual donors. | [Gibbs et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11815886/); [study code](https://github.com/Gibbsdavidl/GBM_Sex_Specific_Cluster_Prediction); [Robencla code](https://github.com/gibbsdavidl/robencla) |
| RankComp family | Uses stable within-sample relative expression orderings in a reference tissue and reversals to find differential genes in disease samples; variants support population- or individual-level DE. | Adjacent, not a region classifier: it establishes that relative orderings and reversal-based signals are established feature extraction tools. Do not present it as an identical classifier. | [RankComp paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btu522); [REOA package with RankComp/RankCompV2](https://github.com/pathint/reoa); [RankCompV3](https://github.com/pathint/RankCompV3.jl) |

### What would distinguish a worst-patient pair objective?

Ordinary TSP scores a difference in group ordering proportions. k-TSP optimizes the aggregate vote. The cross-study pGLMM models fixed and random predictor effects. The GBM robust ensemble paper scores class-versus-rest ordering proportions and expression-distance terms, then combines evidence across datasets. Stability selection counts selection frequency under resampling.

If the proposed selector instead computes a precisely defined donor-level pair correctness or signed margin for every training patient and selects pairs by the minimum over those patients, that exact operation was not found in the papers checked here. This is not proof of novelty: maximin/robust optimization is a general method family, and this search cannot establish that no analogous method exists in another application. The claim should be limited to the defined objective, subject to a further targeted search and source-code audit.

## Matched-pair feature selection and prediction

Matched case-control feature-selection methods were designed for paired expression profiles and are a close precedent for keeping patient-specific background separate. A review inventories these methods and reports examples in which selected features are passed to a classifier.

- **Paired t-test, modified paired t-test, and fold-change paired t-test:** rank genes from within-pair differences. The modified and fold-change versions adapt the statistic for matched data.
- **Conditional logistic regression (CLR):** conditions on matched strata. It directly uses the pair structure; unpenalized CLR can struggle when predictors greatly outnumber pairs.
- **RP-CLR:** random-penalized conditional logistic regression; uses bootstrap paired data and feature subsets to estimate variable importance. The RPCLR package is available.
- **PCU-CLR:** two-stage penalized conditional logistic regression for feature selection followed by unconditional logistic prediction; includes feature interactions in the selection stage.
- **BVS-CLR:** Bayesian variable selection within conditional logistic regression, returning posterior inclusion probabilities.
- **WL2Boost and one-step PQLBoost:** boosting approaches for correlated binary responses and paired designs.
- **Paired SVM, Gaussian naive Bayes, and logistic regression:** downstream classifier families used in the matched-feature literature.
- **Recent penalized CLR with stability selection:** fits L1/L2 conditional logistic regression for matched observations and includes a stable-CLR selection procedure. This is especially close to a robust feature-selection rationale, though it selects individual covariates rather than a worst-donor gene-pair statistic.

Sources: [Liang et al., review and method inventory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6158772/); [RPCLR software](https://rdrr.io/cran/RPCLR/); [penalizedclr paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11212437/); [penalizedclr code](https://github.com/veradjordjilovic/penalizedclr).

**Design note:** A core and margin specimen form one matched patient stratum. CLR or a model on within-patient differences is a valid comparator only if the target and prediction unit are made explicit. A model that uses patient ID as an ordinary trainable predictor cannot generalize to a held-out patient. For a classifier that emits one score per specimen, obtain a single patient-level score from the within-patient difference in predicted region score, and score it once per held-out patient.

## Robust and stability selection

- **Stability selection** repeatedly subsamples observations, fits a base selector, and retains features selected above a prespecified frequency threshold. Its error-control results have assumptions; selection frequency alone is not a guarantee of biological validity. Patients, not cells or spots, must be the resampling unit for this project. [Meinshausen and Buhlmann](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2010.00740.x).
- **Matched stable CLR** applies stability selection to penalized conditional logistic regression. This is a direct matched-patient comparator for the stability component. [penalizedclr methods paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11212437/); [implementation](https://github.com/veradjordjilovic/penalizedclr).
- **Stable recursive feature elimination and ensemble selection** have been proposed for gene-expression biomarker discovery. These establish broad precedent for resampling-based robust feature selection but do not, by themselves, match a patient-paired region endpoint. [StabML-RFE paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiolchem.2022.107747); [robust ensemble selector](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11786965/).
- **Cross-study random-effects models** explicitly estimate predictor heterogeneity between cohorts. Rashid et al. use rank-pair predictors; this is a closer precedent than generic feature stability. [Rashid et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7528965/).

A maximin selector may be different from a selection-frequency method, mean-consistency score, or random-effects model. The algorithm must define whether its unit is patient, patient pair, region, or cell; how ties/zero differences are treated; and whether minimum is computed before or after feature filtering. All such calculations must occur inside the training fold.

## GBM regional biology, signatures, and spatial analysis

These works establish region-specific signals and alternative feature sets. They are often discovery or score-projection studies, not patient-held-out classifiers.

| Precedent | What it establishes | Use in a fair comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Ivy Glioblastoma Atlas Project | Histology-linked expression signatures for anatomical GBM structures including leading edge, infiltrating tumor, and cellular tumor; regional profiles and public data. | Freeze independent region signatures and score them in held-out paired specimens. Check gene overlap and assay compatibility first. | [Anatomic transcriptional atlas](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6414061/); [Ivy GAP downloads](https://glioblastoma.alleninstitute.org/static/download) |
| Boehm et al., Spatial Trajectory Screening | Histology-annotated Visium GBM regions and a method for screening spatial expression gradients along a tissue trajectory, in addition to group differential expression. | Compare paired gene-signature scores or train-only gradient features where spatial coordinates and histology are released. Do not map a held-out cluster to the correct region using its held-out pathology label. | [Primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11343836/); [SPATA2 code](https://github.com/theMILOlab/SPATA2); [STS documentation](https://themilolab.github.io/SPATA2/articles/spatial-trajectory-screening.html) |
| Spatial GBM architecture with BayesSpace | Clusters Visium spots with a spatial prior and compares inferred regions with pathology. | Other spatial graph/domain approaches | STAGATE, GraphST, BASS, SpaceFlow, SpatialPCA, and conST identify spatial domains from expression and neighborhood/context features. | A broad software family, not a complete list. This is regional feature/signature discovery, not a proposed patient-held-out classifier. | [Primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12603948/); [study code](https://github.com/BaluPai/Human-GBM) |
| Earlier core/edge spatial profiling | GeoMx regions from pathology-selected GBM core and edge, linear mixed-effect modeling of region with patient as a random effect, and pathway/signature analysis. | Strong paired regional differential-expression and signature-score precedent; not held-out prediction. | [Primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11553870/) |
| Graph classification in CNS tumors | Uses spatial subgraphs to predict CNS tumor molecular subclasses. | Adjacent proof that graph-level predictions from spatial transcriptomics exist, but target is molecular subtype rather than GBM core/edge pathology. | [Primary study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43018-024-00904-z) |

For a single-cell or spatial count matrix, pseudobulk by patient x region (or by patient x region x cell type when cell-type-specific tumor biology is the target) avoids treating many cells from one patient as independent patients. Paired count models can include patient and region terms, then use training-only gene rankings or signed regional scores. The margin multi-omics paper explicitly reports both single-cell and pseudobulk core-margin DE; the earlier GeoMx paper uses patient-level random effects. Standard implementations include [DESeq2](https://github.com/thelovelab/DESeq2), [edgeR](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/edgeR.html), and [limma](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/limma.html). These are differential-expression tools, not standalone classifiers.

## Minimum fair comparator set for the same patient-level score

A reasonable benchmark should pre-register the common endpoint and use exactly one out-of-fold prediction per patient. For binary core versus infiltrative edge, a practical common score is the signed within-patient margin-minus-core model score; concordance is correct when its direction agrees with the prespecified edge-versus-core direction. Convert each arm to that same patient-level score before evaluating it. Do not compare cell-level accuracy from one arm with patient-level accuracy from another.

Minimum direct arms:

1. The proposed worst-patient rank-pair method.
2. Single-pair TSP and AUCTSP; multi-pair k-TSP/switchBox.
3. GBM rank-pair scoring/Robencla adapted from the published GBM cluster-prediction study.
4. Penalized CLR or regularized logistic regression on paired expression features, plus a paired-difference model.
5. Paired DE feature ranking (paired t for transformed values and, for counts, a patient-blocked DESeq2/edgeR/limma model) followed by the same fixed linear score.
6. A linear SVM and a nonlinear tree or boosting model on the same region/pair representation.
7. A patient-resampled stability-selection version of an established paired selector.
8. Frozen external GBM regional signatures, including IVY-GAP and the independent published infiltrative-margin signature, represented as signed within-patient score differences.
9. Spatial-trajectory or graph/domain features only if the supplied assay contains required spatial inputs; fit every trainable component in the training patients.

Keep every patient's paired specimens together in every outer and inner split. Fit normalization parameters, filtering, feature ranking, pair selection, tuning, direction selection, signature weighting, and spatial cluster-to-pathology mapping using training patients only. Use leave-one-patient-out or grouped outer folds. Same eligible patients, same genes or stated feature intersection, and same primary metric for every direct arm.

**Structural ineligibility is not a win:** MetaKTSP needs independent development cohorts; BayesSpace is designed for spot-grid data; image-assisted graph methods need histology images; methods requiring cell-level data cannot run on a region-level table. State those mismatches. Do not omit a close comparator without noting why it cannot be made executable on the released inputs.

## Search limits and follow-up

- The method inventory is not an exhaustive census of all generic classifiers, all spatial packages, or all robust optimization literature. It is an operationally scoped review of direct and adjacent methods.
- The exact worst-patient rank-pair objective remains unresolved. A further search should use the fully written mathematical definition and inspect references/citations and public code for "minimum subject consistency", "worst-case biomarker", "maximin feature selection", and rank-pair variants.
- The high-dimensional heterogeneity paper's supplementary material contains reproduction code; no separate GitHub repository was located in this search.
- No current package was installed or run. Repository presence is not a software-validation claim.

## Targeted follow-up: local-neighborhood residualization plus patient-paired scoring

This is a plausible analysis to benchmark, but its novelty is unresolved. The main components already have close precedents:

- **smiDE** directly adds spatially lagged expression from neighboring cells of other types as a nuisance covariate to reduce transcript-assignment artifacts, while estimating a covariate-of-interest effect with patient/sample effects. That closely overlaps neighborhood residualization plus a region effect, although smiDE is a differential-expression method rather than a held-out patient classifier. [Paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-025-03867-1); [analysis code](https://github.com/dan11mcguire/smiDE_Analysis).
- **SPLIT** was developed for Xenium and other spatial profiles to redistribute/correct cell-type contamination using reference profiles; its workflow also uses spatial neighborhoods to decide when contamination or label swaps are plausible. This overlaps the proposed correction step, though its target is count purification, not pathology-region prediction. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-026-03089-8); [source repository](https://github.com/bdsc-tds/SPLIT).
- **SpNeigh** explicitly supports Xenium and provides boundary/ring neighborhoods, inside-versus-outside-region differential expression, and spatial-gradient scoring. It covers much of the local region-context feature construction, but not patient-held-out paired classification. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/nargab/lqag039); [source repository](https://github.com/jinming-cheng/SpNeigh).
- **spiDE** uses patients as replicates and models how expression in an index cell type depends on local cell-type density, testing whether these within-patient niche slopes differ by condition. This is a close precedent for combining spatial neighborhood effects with patient-level inference, though the tested endpoint is a condition-dependent niche effect rather than a region classifier. [Method documentation](https://bhuvalab.github.io/spiDE/); [R source](https://github.com/BhuvaLab/spiDE).

**Exact candidate if the study proceeds:** within each outer patient-grouped training fold, fit gene-wise residualization of tumor-cell expression on local cross-type neighbor expression or neighborhood composition and technical covariates; use only those training-fitted residuals to train a regularized region classifier; aggregate cell predictions into one score per region and calculate one paired core-versus-margin score per held-out patient. Compare it with the already listed paired-expression baselines using the same patients and primary metric. Treat this as a benchmarkable combination of established components, not a novel method claim, unless a later mathematical-definition and source-code search establishes otherwise.

**Artzi release constraint:** the Zenodo record's file inventory lists `Final_meta.csv` and `large_sparse_matrices2.h5`. The paper describes histology-guided sampling, single-cell spatial transcriptomics, and a supplement figure on subcellular transcript neighborhoods. Those facts establish relevant spatial context in the study, but the record-level inventory and article abstract alone do not establish that per-cell pathology labels, coordinates, and cell-type annotations are all present and keyed to matrix rows. Confirm those fields from documentation/metadata before selecting a spatial algorithm; do not infer them from patient-level annotations. [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/22791462); [paper and supplement index](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerdiscovery/article/doi/10.1158/2159-8290.CD-25-2302/788658/Genomic-alterations-and-treatment-define).

**Novelty decision:** unresolved. Neighborhood residualization, spatial boundary/region analysis, and patient-level replication each have direct method precedents; their combination for pathology-region prediction could be a useful application but is not established as unique. No expression matrix or test outcome was accessed for this follow-up.
