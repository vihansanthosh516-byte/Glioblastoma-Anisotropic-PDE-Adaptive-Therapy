# Track B growth-zone study: data gate

Read-only feasibility and provenance audit, 2026-10-24. No project code or voxel arrays were run/read, no patients were scored, and no images were downloaded. I did run in-memory PowerShell joins over existing manifest and output metadata to count patient/pair overlap and diagnosis composition. Public sources were checked; the TCIA collection page surfaced in the official search result, while direct page opening returned an HTTP 500.

## 1. Does the proposed 82-patient cohort satisfy the proposed data criteria, and what can 50/32 support?

**Takeaway.** The count of 82 is supported for one narrow cohort: eligible patients with three nonempty first visit masks and a second-to-third visit gap of 14–365 days. It does not yet establish that 82 patients satisfy every modeling, clinical, registration, or evaluability criterion in the plan. The proposed 50/32 split is one small, same-source internal split; it cannot be presented as external validation.

**Cited Findings.** The local segmentation audit reports 88 eligible patients with three valid, nonempty first masks, six whose second-to-third gap falls outside the 14–365-day window, and 82 passing that visit-pair gate. It labels the cohort `provisional_not_ready_for_patient_scoring` and explicitly says it does not establish clinical suitability, source-release identity, registration quality, or forecast performance (`output/track_b_segmentation_value_audit_20261024.json`). The audit code confirms that the target gap is visit 3 minus visit 2, not visit 3 minus visit 1 (`src/fk_segmentation_value_audit.py`, lines 220–260). The manifest has 203 patients, 134 prior-eligible patients, 334 primary pairs, and a pre-existing five-fold patient split (`data/manifests/consort_mu.json`; `data/manifests/patient_manifest.csv`; `data/manifests/split_mu.csv`). The 82-person candidate set is mixed histology: 67 GBM and 15 non-GBM (metadata join of the candidate gate with `patient_manifest.csv`).

The dataset paper reports 203 glioma patients and 594 postoperative MRI timepoints; the TCIA collection page reports 203 patients and 596 timepoints. The local tree has 596 timepoint directories, of which 594 have all four MRI modalities plus a mask, while two have four modalities but no mask. This reconciles the count difference at the directory/mask level, but does not show the two image-only timepoints meet the planned label criteria. Sources: [Scientific Data full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12635165/), [official TCIA collection page](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/mu-glioma-post/).

The plan says both “82 patients that passed” the triplet audit and “all eligible visit pairs per patient.” The audit validates the second-to-third pair for the 82-person gate; it does not certify every later or earlier pair among those patients. Its all-pairs wording therefore exceeds the evidence currently in the gate. The plan also proposes 50 development and 32 held-out patients, but no such split file or hash is present; the repository currently has a different five-fold split (`Claude outputs/trackB_growth_zone_plan_claude.md`; `data/manifests/consort_mu.json`).

**Inferences.** A 50/32 division could support a patient-disjoint development run and a single internal evaluation, provided the cohort and split are frozen and every repeated visit stays with its patient. The independent unit for inference must remain the patient; multiple pairs from one person cannot be treated as independent. With 32 held-out patients, the plan has not shown that its minimum useful gain of 0.02 AUC is detectable; no sample-size or power analysis supports that threshold. If the outcome has few evaluable new-label cases, the effective test size will be smaller still. The 15 non-GBM patients also mean the 82-person result is a glioma result unless the cohort is restricted and re-counted as GBM-only.

**Gaps.** Before scoring, specify whether the target is exactly the second-to-third pair or all qualifying pairs, how repeated pairs are aggregated, how GBM versus other histologies are handled, and how patients with no new 1+3 voxels contribute to the primary estimand. Verify target AUC evaluability without inspecting held-out results or adjusting the endpoint. Freeze and hash the split; a random hash alone does not establish adequate balance or power. Reconcile the two missing-day scan records and two primary pairs assigned to ineligible patients reported by `output/track_b_local_release_audit_20261024.json`; confirm whether any touch the selected cohort.

## 2. Are the patients and visits fresh, and is the data release openly reusable?

**Takeaway.** The proposed cohort is already spent for a confirmatory held-out claim: all 82 patients and all 82 exact proposed second-to-third visit pairs occur in earlier Track B outputs. The dataset itself is openly downloadable under CC BY 4.0 with attribution, but the local files have only count-level provenance checks, not byte-level confirmation against a named TCIA release.

**Cited Findings.** The prior `forecastability` analysis includes 328 pairs from 133 patients and has a pair-level table with incoming/outgoing visits (`output/forecastability.json`, `output/forecastability_pairs.csv`; run manifest `output/forecastability.manifest.json`, dated 2026-10-06). A read-only join of the 82 audited candidate visit pairs against that table found all 82 exact pairs already present. This earlier analysis evaluated mask-volume change, erosion/dilation sensitivity, and persistence-related quantities; it was not the proposed growth-zone AUC, but it used the same patient visits and masks. Separately, `output/forecast_labels_core/results.json` reports 133 scored patients and 19 skipped; all 82 candidate patients occur in its per-patient cache, 80 have scored rows there, and two were skipped for an empty source mask on the different pair used in that run. None of the 82 proposed second-to-third pairs is the exact pair stored in that particular cache. This last result does not establish that the pairs were unseen by every other analysis.

TCIA lists dataset Version 1, updated 2025-03-21, with 203 subjects, 2,978 images, and CC BY 4.0 for images/segmentations, clinical data, and segmentation volumes; it requires the dataset citation. The dataset paper likewise says the data are public through TCIA and shared under CC-BY. The paper says bulk access is through free TCIA registration. Sources: [official TCIA collection page](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/mu-glioma-post/), [dataset DOI](https://doi.org/10.7937/7K9K-3C83), [dataset paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12635165/), [CC BY 4.0 terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

The local-release audit finds 596 timepoint directories and 2,978 NIfTI files, matching the official TCIA page’s image/timepoint metadata; 594 directories have masks and two are image-only. The audit is explicitly based on names and file counts, not downloaded-source checksums or contents (`output/track_b_local_release_audit_20261024.json`).

**Inferences.** Because the exact 82 visit pairs have already appeared in a prior analysis and the proposed endpoint/cohort were chosen after extensive Track B work, a new 50/32 split cannot create a genuinely untouched confirmatory test set. At best it is a post-hoc, patient-disjoint evaluation of a newly specified endpoint. The earlier analyses did not report growth-zone AUC, so that endpoint may still be new to this repository; that does not make the cases or scans unseen. Count consistency supports the local release matching the public collection at a coarse level, not a cryptographic release match.

**Gaps.** Do not describe the 32-person group as a fresh external or prospective test. If keeping this cohort, label the study exploratory/secondary and disclose prior use. A confirmatory claim needs a separate compatible cohort that was not previously inspected, or a genuinely prospective collection. Record TCIA access date, Version 1 identifier, collection DOI, download manifest/checksums, and required attribution. Determine whether the two image-only timepoints are formally in the public release and whether their omission is intentional. The TCIA page could not be opened directly in this audit; its current metadata was read from the official page’s search extract, so recheck it in a normal browser before submission.

## 3. What does “new tumor, labels 1+3” measure, and are registration and biology adequate?

**Takeaway.** The endpoint is “new voxels labeled NETC or enhancing tissue at the next scan,” not verified new viable tumor growth. Post-treatment effects, treatment timing, label ambiguity, and residual registration error can all create apparent new voxels. Published preprocessing is evidence of atlas registration, but neither the local header audit nor the plan’s 2 mm shift rule establishes accurate pairwise registration at the lesion boundary.

**Cited Findings.** The dataset paper defines label 1 as non-enhancing tumor core, including cystic, necrotic, or hemorrhagic parts; label 2 as FLAIR hyperintensity combining infiltrative tumor and vasogenic edema; label 3 as enhancing tissue; and label 4 as resection cavity and associated post-surgical changes. Thus the plan’s 1+3 union excludes label 2 and label 4, and its difference between visits detects change in an annotated imaging compartment, not histologic invasion (`[dataset paper, labels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12635165/)`). The same paper notes that pseudoprogression can appear as increased enhancement after chemoradiation, that pseudoprogression and radiation necrosis overlap with progression on conventional MRI, and that biopsy/resection is the gold standard for distinguishing them. It reports known label errors and inter-/intra-rater variability, despite expert manual refinement (same [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12635165/)).

The published pipeline resampled images to 1 mm isotropic resolution and rigidly registered them to the SRI24 atlas using CapTK/Greedy (`[methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12635165/)`). The local NIfTI audit reports identical 240×240×155 shape and 1×1×1 mm spacing across timepoints, with no header affine mismatches, but says it did not assess voxel content or registration quality (`output/track_b_nifti_header_audit_20261024.json`).

**Inferences.** The plan should call the primary outcome a *new 1+3 label region* or *new annotated core/enhancing voxels*. Calling it tumor growth overstates what these masks establish. It is particularly vulnerable to treatment-related enhancement/necrosis and to changes in human delineation. The dataset’s standard-space preprocessing reduces gross coordinate mismatch, but pairwise visual/quantitative quality control remains necessary; atlas-space headers do not prove that a small local displacement will not be scored as growth. A threshold on median global shift alone would not resolve local boundary alignment or distinguish genuine progression from treatment response.

**Gaps.** Freeze the exact candidate voxel mask: state whether current label-2 voxels are eligible, whether current label-4 cavity voxels are excluded, and how CSF/brain-mask boundaries are handled. This is necessary because the plan uses FLAIR label 2 as a predictor and calls the cavity a PDE barrier while defining targets only from labels 1+3. Predefine pairwise registration QA (reference image, rigid transform, metric, failure criteria, and blinded visual review) without using held-out labels to tune it. Define the result as predictive agreement with follow-up segmentations, not a clinical progression or viable-cell map. Report treatment interval and histology mix; if treatment timing cannot be aligned reliably, state that confounding as a central limitation.
