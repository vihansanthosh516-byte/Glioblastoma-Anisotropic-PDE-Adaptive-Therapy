# Track B prior art: longitudinal glioblastoma growth-zone forecasting

## 1. How much of spatial recurrence prediction already exists?

### Takeaway

Spatial prediction of future GBM recurrence is an established research area. The proposed target is narrower than most recurrence-map studies—predicting newly labeled core/enhancing voxels from one post-treatment visit to the next—but post-operative recurrence maps, cavity-aware proximity models, and voxel-wise MRI predictors already cover much of its feature and method space. “First recurrence-location prediction” or “first cavity-aware risk map” would be indefensible.

### Cited Findings

- **Akbari et al. (2016), “Imaging Surrogates of Infiltration Obtained Via Multiparametric Imaging Pattern Analysis Predict Subsequent Location of Recurrence of Glioblastoma,” DOI 10.1227/NEU.0000000000001202.** They trained maps from preoperative T1, T1-Gd, T2, FLAIR, DTI, and DSC MRI in a retrospective cohort of 31 patients and replicated in 34 new patients; the predicted peritumoral infiltration maps were compared with post-resection follow-up recurrence regions with pathology confirmation. The replication cohort had mean AUC 0.84 and odds ratio 9.29. This is **preoperative baseline-to-later-recurrence localization**, not a visit-k-to-k+1 forecast. **Access:** PubMed abstract verified; the linked PMC page returned an access challenge, so I did not verify the full text. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26813856/)
- **Kwak et al. (2024), ReSPOND, “Predicting peritumoral glioblastoma infiltration and subsequent recurrence using deep-learning-based analysis of multiparametric MRI,” DOI 10.1117/1.JMI.11.5.054001.** The study used preoperative mpMRI for 229 de novo GBM patients across six institutions, then compared its predicted infiltration maps with later recurrence scans confirmed by post-resection tissue analysis. It used leave-one-site-out evaluation; the learned maps were patch-based, and its manual Near/Far ROIs used tumor proximity and FLAIR characteristics. The endpoint is the later recurrence origin mapped back to the **preoperative** scan, not newly appearing voxels between two postoperative scans. **Access:** full text opened and checked in PMC. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11363410/)
- **Lao et al. (2022), “Voxelwise Prediction of Recurrent High-Grade Glioma via Proximity Estimation-Coupled Multidimensional Support Vector Machine,” DOI 10.1016/j.ijrobp.2021.12.153.** This is especially close to the proposed ingredients: in 50 patients, it used postoperative scans about two months before clinical recurrence, a risk field built from inverse distance to both stem-cell niches and the resection cavity, and a voxelwise SVM using T1, T1ce, FLAIR, T2, and ADC. It localized recurrence on prerecurrence scans (30-patient test set: recall 0.80, precision 0.69, F1 0.73, mean boundary distance 7.49 mm). It predicts subclinical recurrence on a scan before diagnosis, rather than forecasting the difference between two consecutive visit masks. **Access:** PubMed abstract verified; full text not independently opened. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34963559/)
- **Cepeda et al. (2023), “Predicting Regions of Local Recurrence in Glioblastomas Using Voxel-Based Radiomic Features of Multiparametric Postoperative MRI,” DOI 10.3390/cancers15061894.** This is a close postoperative baseline comparator. It extracted voxelwise radiomic features from the early postoperative peritumoral region (including T1ce, FLAIR, and ADC) and used later follow-up MRI to define recurrence labels; the authors report that their postoperative input scan was less than 72 hours after surgery and averaged 143 ± 42 days before recurrence. It estimated which regions would later become enhancing tumor, but did not forecast the next scan from the immediately preceding follow-up scan. The article reports 40 training and 15 test patients and an external test AUC of 0.81 ± 0.09 for CatBoost. **Access:** substantial full-text excerpts from the article were retrieved; a direct publisher fetch was rate-limited, so label this full-text-excerpt verified, not fully opened. [Article](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers15061894) · [Open-source implementation](https://github.com/smcch/Predicting_Glioblastoma_Recurrence_MRI)
- **Lao et al. is not the only cavity/proximity precedent.** Its risk-region construction explicitly combines the cavity and a second anatomical source through inverse-distance fields before MRI classification. Therefore, using “distance to tumor/cavity” plus MRI or calling the cavity a spatial boundary is not itself a novelty claim. [Lao et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34963559/)
- **A 2026 MambaDiff-Net study, “Predicting Recurrence Risk of Glioblastoma Based on Preoperative-Postoperative Longitudinal MRI: A Multicenter Study,”** used pre- and postoperative T2-weighted MRI in 139 patients to predict a patient-level recurrence-within-six-months outcome. Its model computes differences between scans and outputs recurrence-risk probabilities; it visualizes attention near cavity margins. It uses longitudinal images, but its reported target is a patient-level risk class, not a spatial map of next-visit new tumor voxels. **Access:** PubMed abstract verified; full text not checked. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42351913/) · [Article](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5354/13/6/668)
- **Kwak et al. (2024) and Akbari et al. (2016) also establish FLAIR/peritumoral infiltration mapping as prior art.** The former distinguishes near- and far-from-core edema ROIs using FLAIR appearance and tumor proximity; the latter uses multimodal peritumoral tissue signatures. Thus B1’s distance + FLAIR terms are a sound baseline, not a new method by themselves. [Kwak et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11363410/) · [Akbari et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26813856/)

### Inferences

- The most precise distinction from these recurrence-map studies is the **forecast origin and time step**: start with an already postoperative/post-treatment visit k, use only information available through k, and predict which eligible voxels are newly assigned core/enhancing labels at the next observed visit k+1. Prior maps commonly use preoperative MRI or an early postoperative baseline and target an eventual recurrence region; Lao et al. instead examines a scan roughly two months before clinical recurrence, but predicts a risk map for that recurrence rather than the mask increment at the next visit. This is a distinction of estimand, not proof that no prior work has done it.
- The proposed “physics reach” layer would need to show added value beyond both the cheap B1 and known cavity/proximity plus multiparametric-feature models. B1 alone is too weak to establish a meaningful advance given the Lao and Mariglia precedents.

### Gaps

- I did not verify the complete methods or all supplementary details for Akbari, Lao, Mariglia, or MambaDiff-Net; source-access labels above are explicit for that reason.
- I did not find a paper in this search that explicitly reports the exact target “new label-1/3 voxels at visit k+1 minus labels 1/3 at visit k” on MU-Glioma-Post. This is **not** a systematic review and does not support a “first” claim.

## 2. Has longitudinal forecasting or tissue/physics-guided growth already been done?

### Takeaway

Yes. Longitudinal future-mask generation and PDE-based future tumor modeling both exist, and very recent PREDICT-GBM work evaluates recurrence maps using both deep-learning and biophysical models. Existing papers do not all use the same endpoint as the proposed adjacent-visit change map, but they make a general “longitudinal tumor prediction” or “PDE maps recurrence” novelty claim untenable.

### Cited Findings

- **Liu et al. (TaDiff-Net; IEEE TMI 2025), “Treatment-aware Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Longitudinal MRI Generation and Diffuse Glioma Growth Prediction,” DOI 10.1109/TMI.2025.3533038.** Its conditional diffusion model takes sequential multiparametric MRI and treatment information and predicts future tumor masks and MRI at future time points; it was trained on real postoperative longitudinal glioma MRI and reports time-series segmentation and uncertainty estimates. A future mask can be differenced from the current mask to obtain new voxels, so the model already overlaps the broad “where will tumor appear next?” task. The article concerns diffuse glioma, and the abstract does not establish evaluation on MU-Glioma-Post or the exact label-1/3 increment endpoint. **Access:** arXiv abstract/page opened; journal DOI verified on arXiv; full methods and dataset details not verified. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05406) · [Official code](https://github.com/samleoqh/TaDiff-Net)
- **Zimmer et al. (PREDICT-GBM, npj Digital Medicine 2026), “A multicenter platform advancing personalized glioblastoma radiotherapy planning,” DOI 10.1038/s41746-026-03194-0.** It releases a longitudinal three-dataset cohort of 243 patients and benchmarks recurrence prediction using U-Net, GliODIL, LOTI, PINN-GBM, LMI, nnU-Net, and GlioMap against guideline-based radiation target volumes. Its primary metric is coverage of a later enhancing or enhancing-plus-necrotic recurrence under equal-volume constraints; it maps follow-up recurrence to preoperative images. Reported combined-cohort mean enhancing-recurrence coverage was 79.37% for U-Net and 78.91% for GliODIL, with modest paired gains over the guideline comparator. It is a major existing benchmark for spatial recurrence mapping and physics/data-driven methods, but is not the same as one-scan-ahead new-label prediction. **Access:** PubMed abstract verified and indexed full-text passages checked; direct PMC open was blocked. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42717239/) · [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03194-0) · [Official code](https://github.com/BrainLesion/PredictGBM) · [Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/LZimmer/PREDICT-GBM)
- **Balcerak et al. (Nature Communications 2025), “Individualizing glioma radiotherapy planning by optimization of a data and physics-informed discrete loss,” DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-60366-4.** GliODIL fits a reaction-diffusion tumor model and an imaging model to MRI/PET information, then evaluates where the modeled tumor predicts later recurrence for personalized target planning; the associated data include 152 GBM patients, with pretreatment FET-PET for 58. This is already a data-plus-PDE recurrence-map method. It is not a calibrated next-visit classifier for newly appearing binary label voxels. **Access:** Nature full-text/PDF excerpts inspected; the project and data links are in the primary paper. [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60366-4) · [Official code](https://github.com/m1balcerak/GliODIL)
- **Biller et al. (MICCAI 2026), “TumorFlow: Physics-Guided Longitudinal MRI Synthesis of Glioblastoma Growth.”** TumorFlow propagates continuous tumor-concentration fields with a biophysical growth model and conditions a generative framework to synthesize longitudinal MRI trajectories. The MICCAI paper page and author response position it as controllable physics-guided image synthesis, not fully validated individualized future prediction; it uses fixed diffusion/proliferation settings and notes limitations around postoperative changes. It therefore overlaps the proposed use of a physics propagation field, though its main output is synthesized MRI/trajectories. **Access:** MICCAI open-access paper page and its evaluation/reviewer-response text checked; DOI was not available on the proceedings page at time of search. [MICCAI paper page](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2026/1096-Paper4612.html) · [Official code](https://github.com/valentin-biller/lgm)
- **Giger et al. (2018), “The Direction of Tumour Growth in Glioblastoma Patients,” DOI 10.1038/s41598-018-19420-z.** The authors derived growth vectors from two pre-treatment time points in 56 patients and analyzed how observed directional growth related to white-matter fiber orientation. This supports white-matter anisotropy as a biologically motivated modeling feature, but is a retrospective description of pre-treatment growth direction, not a held-out forecast of post-treatment new tumor labels. **Access:** primary article and search-indexed full text inspected. [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-19420-z)
- **Liu et al. (2020), “Parametric Response Mapping of FLAIR MRI Provides an Early Indication of Progression Risk in Glioblastoma,”** analyzed serial normalized FLAIR in 52 subjects with voxelwise PRM; the 10–20-week changes were used to stratify patient progression-free and overall survival. This is longitudinal voxelwise FLAIR signal-change analysis, but its target is progression risk/survival rather than the spatial location of newly labeled core/enhancing tissue. **Access:** PubMed abstract verified; PMC full-text search excerpts available, direct page not opened. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32928633/) · [Full-text record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7947036/)
- **“Novel radiotherapy target definition using AI-driven predictions of glioblastoma recurrence from metabolic and diffusion MRI” (npj Digital Medicine 2025), DOI 10.1038/s41746-025-01861-2.** It uses pre-radiotherapy MRI, including diffusion and metabolic measures, to predict later voxel-level progression as contrast-enhancing or nonenhancing lesion and designs radiation target maps from those predictions. It reports voxelwise ROC-AUC 0.88 for enhancing progression and 0.81 for nonenhancing progression. This is a strong precedent for predicting future progression locations from prior images, with an endpoint of later lesion appearance rather than the immediate next-visit mask increment. **Access:** primary Nature article/full-text excerpts inspected. [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01861-2)
- **“Diffusion Anisotropy Predicts Directional Progression After Complete Resection of Frontal Glioblastoma” (2026).** In 29 completely resected frontal GBM cases, preoperative quantitative DTI directionality predicted medial/posterior/etc. progression direction with median accuracy 0.83, outperforming conventional FLAIR/isotropic diffusion measures (median 0.69). It is highly relevant to anatomical direction prediction but predicts a coarse direction category from preoperative DTI, not an adjacent-scan voxel map. **Access:** PubMed abstract only. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42775863/)
- **Gui et al. (2026 conference abstract), “Glioblastoma Distant Recurrences Preferentially Occur Along White Matter Tracts Mapped Using Probabilistic Diffusion Tractography,” DOI 10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.06.216.** In 35 distant recurrences, 29 occurred within 0.5 cm of a probabilistic tractography map initialized from the primary tumor with a 1 cm expansion. This directly establishes that a primary-tumor-seeded white-matter reach/path map for recurrence localization is already being explored. It is a conference abstract, tests distant recurrence proximity, and does not forecast next-visit voxel-level new core. **Access:** conference abstract page checked. [Abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360301626010576)
- **Harris et al. (2022), “Tracking glioblastoma progression after initial resection with minimal reaction-diffusion models,” DOI 10.3934/mbe.2022256.** It applies Fisher–Kolmogorov and two-population reaction-diffusion models to MRI-derived 3-D patient brain geometries for GBM undergoing recurrent-tumor treatment. It establishes reaction-diffusion modeling in postoperative/recurrent settings, but the abstract describes a preliminary modeling effort rather than a locked held-out next-scan spatial forecast. **Access:** PubMed abstract verified. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35603364/)

### Inferences

- A steady-state “reach” map is not automatically a new tumor-growth model. The plan must define the equation, domain, source/target conditions, outer boundary, and cavity/CSF conditions. For example, an anisotropic harmonic potential with the tumor boundary fixed to 1, an outer boundary fixed to 0, and zero flux at cavity/CSF barriers is mathematically a potential/harmonic-measure feature; an anisotropic Eikonal equation instead gives weighted travel time. These are different objects and neither should be described as a time-evolving tumor forecast without validation.
- “Faster diffusion in FLAIR label 2 and white matter” is not patient-specific tractography if the available data do not include DTI. A registered atlas prior can encode generic white-matter location, but cannot show that the patient's tumor follows their own tract directions. Existing DTI papers make this distinction important. [Giger et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-19420-z) · [Gui et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360301626010576)
- The novelty space, if any, is a **specific comparison of an interpretable tissue/cavity-aware reach feature for one-visit-ahead segmentation increments**, under strict patient-level temporal evaluation—not “PDE tumor growth prediction,” “white-matter-guided recurrence,” or “AI predicts recurrence location.” PREDICT-GBM and GliODIL already occupy those broader categories. [PREDICT-GBM](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03194-0) · [GliODIL](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60366-4)

### Gaps

- The TaDiff abstract says its model predicts tumor masks at future time points, but I did not inspect full paper tables, cohort composition, visit spacing, or whether it reports strictly one-scan-ahead held-out-patient testing. The actual overlap could be closer than the abstract makes clear.
- I did not verify whether PREDICT-GBM, TaDiff, or published radiomic implementations can be run on MU-Glioma-Post without substantial conversion; “open code” does not mean directly comparable or runnable on this project's hardware.
- I did not find a public study that explicitly compares an anisotropic harmonic/Eikonal reach field against a matched distance-plus-FLAIR baseline for consecutive post-treatment mask increments. This is a bounded search result, not proof of absence.

## 3. What is a defensible research gap and how should this candidate be made reliable?

### Takeaway

A defensible gap may remain in a tightly specified **next-observed-visit voxel-increment** task on public postoperative data, but the method is not yet demonstrably novel: TaDiff forecasts future masks, Lao already combines cavity distance and multimodal features, and PREDICT-GBM/GliODIL already benchmark physics- and learning-based recurrence maps. Frame the work as a test of incremental value, not a promise to beat the field.

### Cited Findings

- The candidate plan defines the target as label 1+3 at visit k+1 minus label 1+3 at visit k, evaluates candidate voxels within 20 mm of current tumor/cavity, and compares distance-only, distance+FLAIR, radiomic-style ML, and a shared-parameter PDE reach feature. The plan itself says registration between visits has not yet been checked. [Local Track B plan](../../Claude%20outputs/trackB_growth_zone_plan_claude.md)
- MU-Glioma-Post is a public longitudinal postoperative dataset with up to six time points per patient and segmentations for nonenhancing tumor core, FLAIR hyperintensity, enhancing tissue, and resection cavity. It is therefore a plausible source for the proposed label-increment endpoint, subject to verifying the exact patient/pair subset and label reliability. [Dataset article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12635165/) · [TCIA collection](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/mu-glioma-post/)
- PREDICT-GBM itself identifies longitudinal alignment challenges caused by mass effect, surgery, and therapy-related deformation, and reports that recurrence-annotation quality can materially alter absolute coverage and the apparent advantage of a model. These are central threats to any “new voxel” label made by subtracting serial masks. [PREDICT-GBM](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03194-0)
- Post-treatment MRI abnormalities can mix tumor progression and treatment effects; the proposed mask label is an imaging label, not histologic confirmation of each newly labeled voxel. The FLAIR PRM paper likewise notes that FLAIR hyperintensity can represent edema and cannot by itself distinguish edema from nonenhancing tumor. [PRM FLAIR paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7947036/) · [MU-Glioma-Post dataset article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12635165/)

### Inferences

- Recommended research question: **“Among eligible brain voxels near the current tumor/cavity, does a pre-specified, tissue- and cavity-aware anisotropic reach field improve patient-level prediction of newly segmented core/enhancing voxels at the next observed MRI over a strong distance-plus-multiparametric baseline?”** Keep “newly segmented” in the wording; the labels cannot establish that every new voxel is biological tumor growth.
- Strengthen the comparison before coding: retain B0/B1, but add a Lao-style cavity-plus-tumor proximity model, a postoperative radiomic/CatBoost baseline, and (if feasible) the public PREDICT-GBM/GlioMap or TaDiff benchmark. At minimum, B2 should use the same inputs and same patient split as the hybrid; a hybrid win only over B1 does not establish superiority over existing methods. [Lao et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34963559/) · [Cepeda et al.](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers15061894) · [PREDICT-GBM](https://github.com/BrainLesion/PredictGBM) · [TaDiff-Net](https://github.com/samleoqh/TaDiff-Net)
- Freeze an exact mathematical field and a priori ablations. If the field is an anisotropic harmonic potential, call it a risk/reach feature; if it is an Eikonal travel-time, state the metric tensor and boundary conditions. Compare with Euclidean distance and weighted geodesic distance so any gain cannot be credited merely to a second distance transform. This is a methodological recommendation, not a finding that an existing paper has already used the exact proposed PDE.
- Define pair eligibility and time carefully: warp the k mask into the k+1 space (or vice versa) with a validated registration, define the new-label set after registration, record interval length, and either model time explicitly or stratify by interval length. Use only visit-k information for features. If patients contribute multiple pairs, aggregate/cluster at the patient level; confidence intervals must resample patients, not voxels. Do not use the true new volume to pick top-N for a claim of prospective prediction; it can be reported only as an oracle location-only diagnostic alongside a non-oracle threshold/volume model.
- Keep all pairs with no positive new-label voxel as a separate false-alarm analysis; per-pair AUC is undefined when either class is absent. Report how many pairs are excluded from AUC and how many generate false alerts. If a pre-set minimum AUC gain of 0.02 is used, describe it as a project-defined practical threshold, not a clinically established effect size.
- The strongest positive outcome this design could support is “the pre-specified reach-field feature added measurable predictive information over selected baselines on this held-out subset.” It cannot support “better than all methods,” clinical benefit, accurate tumor-cell migration, or a first-in-field claim without external prospective validation and a broader literature search.

### Gaps

- Before the method is frozen, verify the MU scan ordering, interval distribution, exact segmentation provenance/version, and whether the 82 eligible cases/pairs are distinct from any previously scored split. The local plan says the registration audit and prior-use audit remain open. [Local Track B plan](../../Claude%20outputs/trackB_growth_zone_plan_claude.md)
- A systematic search remains necessary across Scopus/PubMed/IEEE/SPIE proceedings and citation chains using “one-step-ahead,” “longitudinal segmentation,” “future tumor mask,” “voxelwise recurrence,” “postoperative MRI,” “GliODIL,” and “TaDiff.” I used targeted web searches and primary article pages, not a registered systematic-review protocol.
- Several 2026 sources are very recent conference/preprint/published-online work. A further check is needed for subsequent journal versions, supplementary materials, code releases, and whether methods or results have changed since the sources checked here.
