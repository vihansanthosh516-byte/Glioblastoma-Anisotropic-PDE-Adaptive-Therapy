# Research Plan

## Project Title

**How Will Multimodal Information Change Simulated Glioblastoma Treatment Scheduling? A Mathematical Study of Molecular Signals, Longitudinal MRI, and Drug Suppression**

## Rationale

Glioblastoma can contain distinct cell states, extend beyond the enhancing tumor, and change during treatment. Standard care includes temozolomide given in repeated courses (Stupp et al., 2005). Single-cell RNA sequencing can measure molecular patterns (Yu et al., 2020). Serial MRI, T2-FLAIR, and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps can describe radiographic changes over time (Akbari et al., 2016; Hoff et al., 2021; Moore-Palhares et al., 2025). Recent work has mapped recurrence-prone tissue in the peri-enhancing zone and used MRI to model treatment response, while other groups have combined mathematical tumor models with reinforcement learning (Bertalan et al., 2026; Q. Liu et al., 2025; Z. Liu et al., 2025). The project will not claim that multimodal therapy modeling, MRI forecasting, or adaptive scheduling is new by itself.

The project will make the Duke patient MRI analysis its main empirical study. It will test whether newly appearing peritumoral FLAIR-labeled regions receive a necrotic- or enhancing-core label at the next visit more often than persistent FLAIR-labeled regions at the same distance from the current core. It will also test whether local ADC measurements improve prediction of that transition. Related studies already examine peritumoral imaging and later recurrence; the narrower question here is the three-visit, distance-matched comparison of newly appearing and persistent FLAIR-labeled tissue. These outcomes describe changes in automated MRI labels. They do not prove that a region contains invading tumor cells.

The three tracks will then form one connected computational framework. Track A will provide previously generated donor-held-out molecular prediction summaries as simulation inputs. Track B will provide patient-level MRI transition and ADC observations from the Duke cohort. Track C will represent tumor growth, treatment suppression, and resistant cells. The source cohorts do not contain a shared patient identifier, so I will not merge people across datasets or assign Track A measurements to Duke patients. Instead, I will build virtual patients and explicitly vary the assumed relationships among the molecular signal, MRI signal, and drug resistance. This design will show when combining these signals helps a simulated treatment policy and when uncertain or misleading links remove that benefit.

The mathematical contribution will be a simulation-based value-of-information analysis: on shared held-out virtual worlds, I will measure how adding molecular and MRI/ADC signals changes simulated progression time and treatment-course use relative to a development-selected fixed schedule and a development-selected one-signal policy. I will report these paired changes under four predeclared assumptions about cross-track dependence. This is a finite scenario analysis, not a general theorem or evidence that treatment would work in patients. The Duke MRI transition analysis will provide a separate patient-derived description of one signal; it will not be merged with Track A donors or presented as a treatment effect.

## Research Question, Hypotheses, Engineering Goals, and Expected Outcomes

### Research Questions

**Central mathematical question:** Across four predeclared assumptions about how molecular and MRI/ADC signals relate to latent drug resistance, how will the fused policy's 730-day restricted mean time to simulated progression and treatment-course use compare with the best development-selected fixed schedule and the best development-selected single-modality policy on held-out virtual worlds?

1. **Primary patient question:** At the same physical distance from the existing core, will newly appearing nonenhancing FLAIR tissue have a higher next-scan necrotic/enhancing core-label conversion rate than persistent FLAIR tissue?
2. **ADC question:** Will current ADC and its change over time improve patient-held-out prediction of that transition beyond the prespecified MRI and clinical baseline?
3. **Integrated simulation question:** Under the moderate-concordance assumption, will the fused policy improve 730-day restricted time to simulated progression, without greater mean course use, versus both the best development-selected fixed schedule and the best development-selected single-modality policy, under both progression definitions and in both calibration families?
4. **Robustness question:** How will the paired changes in progression time and course use vary across the independent, weak-concordance, moderate-concordance, and reversed molecular-to-resistance scenarios?

### Hypotheses

1. **Primary Duke hypothesis:** At matched physical distances from the middle-visit core, newly appearing FLAIR-labeled regions will have a higher next-visit core-label conversion rate than persistent FLAIR-labeled regions.
2. **Secondary ADC hypothesis:** Adding current normalized ADC and its change from the prior visit will lower patient-held-out Brier score for predicting next-visit core-label conversion.
3. **Virtual-patient hypothesis:** Under the prespecified moderate-concordance scenario, the fused treatment policy will increase 730-day restricted mean time to progression relative to both the best fixed schedule and the best single-modality policy selected on development simulations, under both progression definitions and in both calibration families, without increasing mean course use for either endpoint. The simultaneous test will include both comparators.
4. **Robustness hypothesis:** The paired advantage of fusion will be smaller under independent or weak dependence than under moderate concordance, and may be absent or reversed when the molecular-to-resistance relationship is reversed. This pattern will be evaluated on held-out virtual worlds and may not occur.

These are directional hypotheses. I will report the measured results even if they do not support them.

### Engineering Goals

1. Build an auditable Duke pipeline that links consecutive patient visits, checks MRI registration, measures tissue-label transitions, and keeps all visits from each patient in one validation fold.
2. Use Track A donor-level results as a transparent uncertainty range for a simulated molecular signal, without reinterpreting that signal as drug sensitivity.
3. Calibrate a two-population tumor model against published aggregate treatment outcomes before comparing treatment policies.
4. Build virtual patients that combine separate Track A and Track B signal distributions with Track C tumor and treatment states without falsely linking source-cohort participants.
5. Quantify the incremental value of the fused belief-state controller against development-selected fixed and single-modality policies, alongside no-information controls, using shared virtual patients, scan schedules, noise, and treatment limits.
6. Save the data flow, exclusions, settings, seeds, software versions, code fingerprints, model failures, and all primary and secondary results.

### Expected Outcomes

The Duke analysis will estimate whether newly appearing FLAIR-labeled regions will show higher, similar, or lower next-visit core-label conversion than persistent regions. The ADC analysis will test whether adding ADC features will improve held-out prediction. The simulation will compare fusion under different assumptions and may show benefit, no advantage, or harm. I will report each result by its evidence source: patient MRI analysis or virtual-patient simulation.

3. **Register scans and freeze quality checks before outcome scoring.** Dataset modalities within a visit share a registered grid, but visits are not registered to one another. I will rigidly align the prior- and next-visit T1-c images to the middle-visit T1-c image using image intensities only, then apply each transform to that visit’s other modalities and segmentation mask. The moving-to-fixed transform will use millimeter physical coordinates from the NIfTI affines, be centered at the physical center of the middle-visit image, and use rotation matrix Rz(γ)Ry(β)Rx(α), with translations in millimeters. I will maximize mean-centered normalized cross-correlation over finite, nonzero fixed-image voxels using Powell optimization from zero transform: first on every second voxel (xtol 0.01, ftol 0.0001, maximum 2,000 iterations), then on the full 1 mm grid from the coarse result (xtol 0.001, ftol 0.00001, maximum 2,000 iterations). I will resample with the inverse transform; intensity images will use linear interpolation, and labels will use nearest-neighbor interpolation. Outside-field voxels will be zero. No retry or manual transform adjustment will be allowed. A registered visit will pass only when NCC is at least 0.80, the largest absolute Euler angle is at most 10 degrees, and translation-vector length is at most 10 mm. These are operational thresholds, not validated clinical accuracy limits. Before patient scoring, I will use synthetic known-transform tests and report released vessel-landmark errors when corresponding landmarks exist; landmarks will not tune transforms or remove points. Failed pairs will be excluded before target scoring with reasons recorded. I will freeze and hash the passing patient/window list, protocol, code, and patient-grouped folds before opening any held-out next-visit label voxels.

   I will resample registered data to a 2 mm isotropic grid aligned to the middle visit; intensities will be averaged and labels will use nearest-neighbor sampling. Labels 1 and 3 will define the operational core, label 2 will define nonenhancing FLAIR signal abnormality, and label 4 will define the resection cavity. Label 0 and other values will not be called normal because their meaning is not established. The middle-visit foreground B2 will be the union of finite, nonzero T1-c and FLAIR voxels; it is not an anatomical brain mask. Origin-only support S will be B2 union nonzero labels from the prior and middle visits. No next-visit image or label will define S, registration, exclusions, or prediction features. Target-core voxels outside S will count as misses in every forecast comparison.

4. **Measure the primary Duke transition.** For each window, I will call the prior, middle, and next operational core masks C1, C2, and C3, and the prior and middle label-2 masks F1 and F2. The middle-visit risk region R2 will be F2 outside a 2 mm dilation of the union of C2 and the cavity. Newly appearing tissue A2 will be R2 outside a 2 mm dilation of F1. Persistent tissue P2 will be R2 inside that dilation. The 2 mm margins will reduce sensitivity to one-voxel registration and labeling changes.

   I will calculate each A2 and P2 voxel’s distance to the nearest C2 voxel, using 2 mm physical spacing. Distance bands will be 2–10 mm, more than 10–20 mm, more than 20–30 mm, and more than 30 mm. A history-defined window will be eligible when C2 is nonempty and at least one band contains at least 10 A2 voxels and 10 P2 voxels. I will apply this eligibility rule without viewing C3. At the next visit, labels 1 or 3 will count as a recorded core label. Labels 0, 2, and 4 will count as no recorded core label for this operational endpoint, without treating label 0 as biological absence.

   Within each eligible distance band, I will calculate the fraction of A2 and P2 voxels receiving a next-visit core label. I will subtract the P2 fraction from the A2 fraction. Each band will receive weight proportional to the smaller of its A2 and P2 voxel counts. I will average bands within windows, windows within patients, and then patients with equal weight. I will interpret the primary estimate as an association between automated MRI labels, not as a causal effect or histologic measurement. I will report the mean patient-level difference and a 95% percentile-bootstrap interval from 10,000 patient-cluster resamples using NumPy PCG64 seed 20261104 and linear percentile quantiles. If fewer than 20 patients qualify, I will stop the primary inferential analysis. With 20–34 patients, or an interval half-width greater than 0.10, I will report a feasibility estimate. I will interpret the primary hypothesis as supported only if at least 35 patients qualify, the interval half-width is at most 0.10, and the lower confidence bound is above zero. I will also report the next-visit label distribution and a descriptive sensitivity analysis excluding label-0 voxels.

5. **Test whether ADC adds predictive information.** I will normalize each ADC map by its median positive value inside that visit’s finite, nonzero T1-c-or-FLAIR foreground and use the logarithm of normalized ADC. Within R2, the predictors will be current log-normalized ADC and its change from the prior visit. The baseline logistic model will include distance band, new-versus-persistent status, robust-standardized FLAIR intensity, the observed middle-to-next scan interval, time since known surgery, radiation phase, systemic-therapy status, and treatment events between the prior and middle visits. The observed forecast interval is available only in this retrospective dataset; this model will not be described as a prospective tool unless a future scan interval is known at prediction time. FLAIR intensity will be standardized within B2 using its median and 1.4826 times its median absolute deviation; a zero or nonfinite scale will make that window unavailable for this model. Unknown treatment history will remain an explicit category. No event after the prediction origin will be used as a predictor.

   I will compare the baseline model with the same model plus ADC features using five patient-grouped outer folds. I will sort anonymized patient IDs, permute them with NumPy PCG64 seed 20261103, split them into five sequential folds, and keep every window from one patient in that patient’s fold. Inner folds will use the same procedure on outer-training patients with seed 20261104 plus the outer-fold index; inner training must contain at least 10 patients. The models will use L2 logistic regression with the lbfgs solver, tolerance 0.0001, maximum 1,000 iterations, and no class weights. Training weights will be equal by patient, then by that patient’s windows and at-risk voxels. Inner patient-grouped folds will select C from {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} by patient-macro Brier score; ties will select the smaller C. Scaling and feature selection will use training patients only. Both models will use the same ADC-complete patients and folds. I will report held-out patient-macro Brier scores and the paired difference, with 10,000 patient-cluster bootstrap resamples using NumPy PCG64 seed 20261104 and linear percentile quantiles. Positive improvement means the baseline Brier score minus the ADC-model Brier score is greater than zero.

6. **Run the secondary Track B forecast benchmark.** I will forecast C3 from C1 and C2 with the isotropic Fisher–Kolmogorov model ∂u/∂t = D∇²u + ρu(1−u), on the common support and actual visit intervals. The solver will use a no-flux boundary, a maximum time step of 0.5 day, diffusion stability fraction 0.9, and reaction stability limit 0.05. It will not include a treatment-kill term, so it will forecast recorded label evolution under observed care.

   I will compare the PDE forecast with seven prespecified mask comparators: copy-last, fixed growth, volume-matched persistence, FLAIR-front, two-history shape, PDE-only, and a time-conditioned FiLM U-Net. I will also report a matched-volume no-PDE ranker as a separate ablation and a count-only model on count metrics; neither will be turned into an extra mask or mixed into the seven-comparator family. The PDE will use log-uniform priors ρ ∈ [10⁻⁵, 10⁻¹] day⁻¹ and D ∈ [10⁻⁵, 10] mm²/day, a 21 × 21 coarse log grid, an 11 × 11 refinement over the region containing 90% of posterior mass, 200 posterior draws, and 256 deterministic surface anchors. For each ρ–D pair, I will rank the field within S to match the observed C2 volume, then calculate signed-distance residuals at the 256 C2 surface anchors. The boundary likelihood will be a normalized zero-mean multivariate Gaussian with covariance Σᵢⱼ = σᵦ² exp(−dᵢⱼ/ℓ) + τᵦ²I. I will multiply this likelihood by the log-uniform prior and log-grid quadrature weights, normalize over the grid, and sample 200 draws with replacement. Because each fit will use only two history masks, I expect ρ and D may remain correlated; I will treat the forecast, not separate parameter estimates, as the target. The boundary-residual covariance will use σᵦ = 0.8 mm, τᵦ = 0.3 mm, initial ℓ = 6 mm, and candidate length scales ℓ ∈ {5, 10, 20} mm. Five-fold patient-held-out calibration will use outer-training patients only, at least eight calibration patients, and at least two valid residual patients. For each ℓ, patient-balanced semivariogram method-of-moments estimates of σᵦ² and τᵦ² will use nonnegative least squares; I will select ℓ by lowest weighted RMSE. I will interpret the covariance as representing combined model, registration, treatment, and label residuals, not scanner noise or mean boundary bias. The one-decade narrower prior will use ρ ∈ [10⁻⁴, 10⁻²] day⁻¹ and D ∈ [10⁻⁴, 1] mm²/day; the wider prior will use ρ ∈ [10⁻⁶, 1] day⁻¹ and D ∈ [10⁻⁶, 100] mm²/day. Sensitivity results will be descriptive and cannot select the primary prior. I will save each outer-test forecast and its selected settings before opening that window’s next-visit mask for scoring.

   The candidate will rank each raw PDE field within S to match its corresponding outer-training-only volume draw, perturb the matched boundary using the fitted covariance and pre-generated seeds, average 200 binary masks, and threshold inclusion probability at 0.5. The shared history-only volume model will estimate each training patient’s log-volume slope from C1 and C2, shrink it toward the equal-patient outer-training median using an inner-selected time constant from {30, 90, 180, 365} days, fit log-volume predictive variance a + bT by patient-weighted nonnegative least squares on squared held-out training residuals, and generate 200 positive lognormal volume draws with seed 20261028. Boundary and forecast seeds will be 20261029 and 20261028.

   Copy-last will return C2 unchanged. Fixed growth will dilate C2 by r = k × forecast days, with k ∈ {0, 0.01, …, 0.19} mm/day and a 20 mm cap; inner patient-grouped validation will select k and ties will go to the smaller value. Volume-matched persistence will rank S by signed distance from C2, then flat voxel index, and assign the shared volume draws. FLAIR-front will rank C2 first, A2 second, P2 third, then remaining S by distance from C2 and flat voxel index, and assign the same draws. The two-history arm will extrapolate the signed-distance change from C1 to C2 by f = min(T/(g + τ), m), where T is forecast days and g is history-gap days; its grid will be τ ∈ {0, 7, 28} days, m ∈ {1, 4, 8}, maximum distance change {10, 30} mm, plus f = 0. If g < 1 day or C1 is empty or fills S, f will be zero. Inner validation will select settings; scores within 0.005 mean patient-macro Dice will tie, resolved toward f = 0, smaller m, larger τ, then smaller distance cap. The two-history arm will use the same training-only volume draws and boundary seeds as the candidate. The matched-volume no-PDE ablation will rank voxels by signed distance from C2 to match the same training-only volume draws and use the same boundary covariance and perturbation seeds, but will not run or use the PDE. The PDE-only arm will omit volume matching and boundary perturbation, select c ∈ {0.05, 0.10, …, 0.50} by outer-training-only inner-validation Dice (ties to larger c), threshold raw PDE draws, average the binary masks, and call voxels included at probability ≥0.5. A valid empty forecast will remain empty.

It will train for 300 epochs with Dice plus binary cross-entropy, Adam at 0.001 with betas (0.9, 0.999), batch size 1, no weight decay, random axis flips, axial 90-degree rotations, and the final epoch retained. Three fold-specific seeds will be 20261102 + fold, +1000, and +2000. I will average the three probability maps, threshold at 0.5, and clip to S. The count-only model will define a new core piece as a 26-connected component of C3 outside a 2 mm dilation of C2 with at least two voxels. A training-only Poisson GLM will use log(|C2|) as predictor and log(forecast days) as offset; if training has zero events, its rate will be 0.5 divided by total training days. It will be scored only by Poisson deviance, count MAE, and Brier score for any new piece, with event probability 1 − exp(−predicted count). Fixed growth, PDE cutoffs, volume draws, and neural models will be selected or fitted using outer-training patients only. All mask methods will use the same windows, support, target, and scoring rule. Five outer patient-grouped folds will use NumPy PCG64 seed 20261103; inner folds will use seed 20261104 plus the outer-fold index, with at least 10 inner-training patients. The endpoint will be patient-macro Dice; both-empty Dice will be 1, and one-empty Dice will be 0. I will report each paired patient-level contrast using 10,000 shared patient-bootstrap resamples, seed 20261110, and one-step max-T simultaneous 95% intervals. The candidate will count as better than the required comparator family only if every adjusted lower confidence bound is above zero. A missing required arm will make the family incomplete. This forecast result will remain separate from the primary transition result.

8. **Calibrate the Track C drug-suppression model.** I will use a normalized two-population model with drug-sensitive burden S and drug-resistant burden R, total burden B = S + R, and carrying capacity K = 1:

   - dS/dt = rS[1 − (S + aᵣₛR)] − mruS − μuS + ν(1−u)R
   - dR/dt = r(1 − c)R[1 − (aₛᵣS + R)] − εmruR + μuS − ν(1−u)R

   Here r is untreated growth, m is the sensitive-cell drug-kill multiplier, c is the resistant-cell growth cost, ε is residual drug sensitivity in resistant cells, μ is drug-induced sensitive-to-resistant switching, ν is off-drug reversion, and u equals 1 on treatment days and 0 otherwise. Initial values will be S0 = (1−f0)B0 and R0 = f0B0. I will set K = 1 and aₛᵣ = aᵣₛ = 1. The solver will use fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration with 0.25-day steps. All parameter values, including μ and ν, are assumptions; none will be estimated from Duke MRI.

   One course will represent five modeled treatment days in a 28-day cycle. This is a normalized on/off drug-suppression input, not a prescribed drug dose or a claim of pharmacologic equivalence. The standard schedule will use six courses; the dose-dense calibration arm will multiply modeled exposure by 2.0, and the 12-versus-6 comparison will be a calibration check only. Adaptive policies may use at most 12 courses over 730 days. As a course-limit sensitivity analysis, I will repeat policy comparisons with a six-course cap and with a mixed cap of three courses for 63% and six courses for 37% of virtual patients; that split is an assumption based on aggregate trial reporting, not individual treatment records. I will use the registered Track C world generator. In the single-response family, it will shift r from its base log range 0.001–0.05 per day by exp(−0.7 to 0.9), scale m from its base log range 6–20 by exp(log(0.05) to 0), shift f0 from its base log range 0.001–0.1 by exp(−0.7 to 2.3) with a 0.9 cap, draw B0 centers from 0.12–0.55 with a 0.10 half-width, draw c from 0–0.25, residual sensitivity ε from 0–0.2, and set μ and ν from {0, 0.01, 0.04} per day. In the mixture family, the nonresponder m scale will range from 0.02 to 0.30 and responder fraction q from 0.10 to 0.60; responders will retain the base m range 6–20. I will report the two families separately and will not select a family by its policy result.

   I will draw 500 Latin-hypercube worlds per family and 400 virtual patients per world for calibration, using development seeds only. The standard schedule will use six courses, with treatment on days 0–4 of each 28-day cycle, and scans every 84 days. I will retain a world only if its standard-arm Kaplan–Meier median progression-free survival on the scan-based RANO-like endpoint, linearly interpolated between scan days, is 143–186 days; its dose-dense-versus-standard Cox hazard ratio with Efron ties is within the predeclared acceptance range 0.75–1.00 around the reported RTOG 0525 estimate of 0.87; and its day-168 landmark 12-versus-6-course hazard ratio is within 0.71–1.38 (Gilbert et al., 2013; Balaña et al., 2020). The landmark comparison will include only patients without a progression call at day 168. The dose-dense arm will multiply modeled drug exposure by 2.0; 1.5 and 3.0 will be sensitivity values. The observation model will be log burden plus Gaussian noise with standard deviation 0.05, 0.10, or 0.20, balanced across patients. Published aggregate trial outcomes constrain the worlds; they are not individual patient response data and do not validate individual predictions. I will require at least 30 worlds per family to pass all calibration checks. If fewer pass, I will report that result and will not claim that a treatment policy is validated.

9. **Build the virtual patients without false patient matching.** For each Track B outer-held-out patient, I will calculate the mean cross-fitted predicted conversion probability and observed conversion fraction across that patient’s eligible windows. I will define the patient-level MRI/ADC residual as logit(observed fraction) minus logit(predicted probability), after clipping both to [10⁻⁶, 1−10⁻⁶]. The MRI/ADC marginal distribution will be the empirical distribution of those patient-level predicted probabilities. I will sample Track C parameter rows from accepted calibration worlds, preserving their joint parameter combinations. I will convert each row’s empirical rank for initial resistant fraction f0 to a standard-normal score Z_R. A Gaussian copula will generate correlated latent normal scores Z_M and Z_I conditional on Z_R. I will threshold Z_M at zero to make a binary Core-like/Periphery-like state, map the normal cumulative probability of Z_I through the empirical held-out MRI/ADC risk distribution, and add a patient-level held-out residual to repeated MRI/ADC observations. This deliberately treats MRI/ADC risk as a persistent patient-level signal with repeated measurement error. I will generate one baseline molecular observation by drawing an A5 reliability and flipping the latent molecular state with probability 1 − reliability. No A5 donor, Duke patient, or trial participant will be treated as the same person as a virtual patient.

   I will generate four fixed correlation matrices for latent normal scores Z_M, Z_I, and Z_R: independent (all pairwise correlations 0); weak concordance (all 0.25); moderate concordance (all 0.50); and reversed molecular-to-resistance association (Z_M–Z_I = 0.25, Z_I–Z_R = 0.25, Z_M–Z_R = −0.25). I will verify positive definiteness before use. I will keep the same Track C parameter rows, marginal MRI/ADC distribution, A5 reliability values, and random-number streams across scenarios; only the copula dependence matrix will change. The moderate-concordance matrix will be the policy’s fixed assumed prior in every scenario. The policy will not receive the generating scenario label or hidden patient parameters. The moderate scenario is the primary conditional scenario; all others test robustness. These correlations are analyst-selected assumptions, not estimates from the patient cohorts.

10. **Train and compare treatment policies.** The fused policy will use an uncertainty-aware belief-state model-predictive controller. At baseline, it will receive one binary molecular observation. Its likelihood will average over the six A5 donor reliability values and the moderate-concordance copula prior; the controller will not know which A5 reliability or generating dependence scenario produced that observation. At each 84-day scan, it will receive noisy log-burden, with standard deviation selected equally from {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}, and a simulated MRI/ADC risk score. The latter will be generated as logit(I observed) = logit(I latent) + e, where e is sampled from patient-level outer-held-out residuals of the Track B ADC-extended model; probabilities will be clipped to [10⁻⁶, 1−10⁻⁶]. The filter will use the three likelihoods conditional on each particle’s latent state and the same moderate-concordance prior in all generating scenarios. It will never receive hidden tumor parameters or the virtual patient’s selected cross-track correlation.

   The filter will use 500 particles initialized from the registered calibration-family prior over growth, drug kill, resistant fraction, resistance cost, residual drug sensitivity, starting burden, switching, reversion, and responder class where applicable. Switching and reversion will use the discrete assumed levels {0, 0.01, 0.04}/day; they are not estimated from Duke data. The filter will use systematic resampling when effective sample size falls below 250 and a Liu–West move with h = 0.90 on continuous parameters; resampled discrete levels will remain discrete. At each scan, it will compare all eight on/off treatment patterns for the next three 28-day cycles, repeat each pattern over a six-cycle (168-day) prediction window, and apply only the first three-cycle block before replanning. It will evaluate 16 posterior prediction draws and select the pattern maximizing min(RMST₁₆₈,baseline − 5 days × expected courses_baseline, RMST₁₆₈,RANO-like − 5 days × expected courses_RANO-like). Each endpoint will use its own progression-stop time and course count. I will use this as a receding-horizon approximation and will evaluate held-out policy outcomes over the full 730-day period. I will treat the five-day course cost as an analyst-selected utility penalty, not as a measured toxicity or time cost. The integrated policy will have a 12-course maximum and will obey the same action and scan limits as its comparators.

   I will compare the fused policy with the standard six-course schedule; the best of 56 fixed schedules selected only on development worlds; the Track-C-only belief controller; an MRI/ADC-only controller; a molecular-only controller; a late-fusion controller that equally averages development-standardized molecular and MRI/ADC risk scores; and a tabular Q-learning baseline trained only on development worlds. The 56-schedule catalog will cover 52 cycles and allow at most 12 courses: front-loaded schedules with 0–12 courses; schedules treating every 2, 3, 4, or 6 cycles from start cycles 0–3; and two-block schedules with 2, 4, or 6 courses per block separated by 2, 4, or 8 untreated cycles. Duplicate schedules will be removed. On development worlds, I will select the fixed comparator by maximizing the mean across virtual patients of min(RMST₇₃₀,baseline − 5 × courses_baseline, RMST₇₃₀,RANO-like − 5 × courses_RANO-like); ties will prefer fewer mean courses averaged across endpoints, then catalog order. I will select one single-modality comparator from the MRI/ADC-only and molecular-only controllers using the same development-only objective; ties will favor MRI/ADC-only. The fixed and single-modality comparators will be selected once using development worlds pooled equally across both calibration families and all four dependence scenarios, then used unchanged on every held-out scenario. I will report the held-out 56-schedule frontier only as a descriptive upper-envelope benchmark, not as a deployable comparator or confirmatory claim. The Q-learning state will use cycle, observed relative-burden bin, recent-burden trend bin, and courses used. Burden-ratio bin edges will be {0.5, 0.8, 1.0, 1.25, 1.5}; trend boundaries will be −0.10 and +0.10 log units. Actions will be treat or withhold. Q-learning will train for 50 episodes with learning rate 0.1, discount 1, initial exploration 0.25, decay 0.98, minimum exploration 0.02, and a five-day course penalty. I will also run no-signal controls and signal-shuffle controls that permute each signal across virtual patients within a world. A complete-information oracle may be reported as a diagnostic ceiling but will not count as a practical competitor. Every policy will receive identical virtual patients, latent parameter draws, course limits, observation-noise streams, and scan schedules.


11. **Separate development and final evaluation.** Before policy tuning, I will reserve non-overlapping seed blocks in the project seed registry: 20263000 for calibration and development, 20264000 for held-out evaluation, and 20264001 for bootstrap resampling. I will derive every child seed deterministically with NumPy SeedSequence from the root plus coded family, scenario, world, replicate, and arm identifiers; the integer code table will be saved before evaluation. I will use 500 calibration worlds per Track C family with 400 simulated patients per world, 500 development worlds with 100 virtual patients per world, and 1,000 held-out worlds per dependence scenario with 100 virtual patients and 20 shared observation-noise replicates per world. I will select all model settings, the best fixed schedule, and the best single-modality policy using development worlds only. I will freeze the code, parameters, folds, seed registry, package lock, and scoring rules before held-out evaluation. I will evaluate the held-out set once and report it unchanged.

12. **Apply failure rules, time, seal, and report.** A typed expected PDE failure, empty required history core, unavailable training-only nuisance fit, insufficient boundary-calibration data, nonfinite field, or invalid forecast volume will use the prespecified C2 persistence fallback for PDE-dependent arms; the patient will remain, and the failure reason will be recorded. A valid empty PDE-only mask will remain empty. Missing required arms, nonfinite outputs, registration failure, hash changes, or unexpected exceptions will make the affected comparison incomplete or stop it; there will be no silent patient removal, retry, or post-result setting change. Before outcome scoring, I will run a full-size synthetic timing pilot that includes registration, inner and outer fitting, all forecast arms, neural training, calibration, output writing, and policy evaluation. The projected wall time must be at most 1,000 hours on the project computer; otherwise, I will amend the plan or stop before scoring. Before opening held-out target labels, I will hash the protocol, source code, exact package locks, hardware/thread settings, metadata cohort and window list, fold map, QC list, support rule, seeds, and settings. Each held-out prediction and its settings will be saved before its target label is loaded. I will create a run manifest with permitted input hashes, de-identified cohort and window counts, exclusions, scan intervals, registration and landmark metrics, treatment summaries, folds, software versions, model failures, fallbacks, and runtime. Restricted images, masks, landmarks, and patient-level outputs will remain local. I will report the Duke patient analysis and virtual-patient policy study in separate result tables, then explain how the patient-derived distributions informed the simulation without presenting the simulation as patient treatment evidence.

The project will use de-identified patient MRI and metadata under restricted access, plus public molecular data and computer simulations. Potential risks will include disclosure of health information, use outside the dataset agreement, and confusion between simulated outputs and clinical evidence. I will keep the Duke archive, images, masks, landmarks, patient-level tables, and predictions in access-controlled local storage. I will not try to identify patients or contact them. I will share only outputs allowed by the Duke agreement and school or competition approval.

No person will receive a diagnosis or treatment recommendation. No drug will be administered. Simulated treatment courses are mathematical inputs, not medical instructions. I will report automated MRI labels as radiographic labels, not verified tumor boundaries. Long computation will run on a ventilated surface with the computer connected to power.

## Data Analysis

### Primary patient-data analysis

The primary estimate will be the equal-patient mean difference in distance-standardized next-visit core-label conversion between newly appearing and persistent middle-visit FLAIR regions. The 95% interval will use 10,000 patient-cluster percentile-bootstrap resamples. Patients, not voxels, visits, or regions, will be the units of inference. The primary rule requires at least 35 evaluable patients, interval half-width no greater than 0.10, and a lower confidence bound above zero. If fewer patients qualify or precision is wider, I will report a feasibility estimate or stop according to the thresholds in the Procedure.

The ADC analysis will report paired held-out patient-macro Brier scores for the baseline and ADC-extended models. The Track B forecast comparison will report patient-macro Dice and simultaneous paired confidence intervals for every required comparator. Neither secondary analysis will change the primary transition rule.

### Integrated virtual-patient analysis

For each virtual patient, I will calculate restricted mean time to progression at 365 and 730 days under two predeclared outcomes: (1) true simulated burden first reaching 1.2 times starting burden, and (2) a scan-based RANO-like call at 84-day scans, when burden reaches 1.4 times the greater of the prior scan nadir or 0.10 times starting burden (Ellingson et al., 2017). Restricted mean time will be the average of min(time to progression, horizon). The second endpoint will be a model approximation, not a formal RANO assessment.

I will define paired policy differences as ΔRMST = RMST_fused − RMST_comparator and Δcourses = courses_fused − courses_comparator. Positive ΔRMST will favor fusion; Δcourses at or below zero will indicate that fusion did not use more courses. In the moderate-concordance scenario, the primary comparator set will include the best development-selected fixed schedule and the best development-selected single-modality policy. I will construct simultaneous 95% max-|t| bootstrap intervals across 16 primary contrasts: eight 730-day ΔRMST contrasts (two comparators × two progression endpoints × two calibration families) and eight endpoint-specific Δcourse contrasts over the same combinations. Bootstrap resamples will sample held-out worlds and keep all patients and noise replicates from each sampled world together. A robust primary simulated claim will require all eight ΔRMST lower bounds above zero, all eight Δcourse upper bounds at or below zero, and successful calibration in both families. I will also compare the fused policy with the held-out fixed-schedule frontier at no greater endpoint-specific mean course use; this frontier will remain a descriptive upper-envelope benchmark, not a deployable comparator or confirmatory claim. Track-C-only, separate molecular-only and MRI/ADC-only results, late fusion, Q-learning, no-information, and signal-shuffle controls will be reported as secondary contrasts with simultaneous max-|t| intervals across the full prespecified secondary family of policies, endpoints, horizons, scenarios, and calibration families. I will use 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples over held-out simulation worlds. I will report the same outcomes separately for all four dependence scenarios. No confidence interval will treat repeated scans, image voxels, or noise replicates as independent worlds.
