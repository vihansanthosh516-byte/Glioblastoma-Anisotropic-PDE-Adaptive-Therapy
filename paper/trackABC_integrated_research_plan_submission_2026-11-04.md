# Research Plan

## Project Title

**Integrating Tumor Biology and Longitudinal MRI for Glioblastoma Progression Modeling and In-Silico Therapy Optimization**

## Rationale

Glioblastoma can contain distinct cell states, extend beyond the enhancing tumor, and change during treatment. Standard care includes temozolomide given in repeated courses (Stupp et al., 2005). Single-cell RNA sequencing can measure molecular patterns (Yu et al., 2020). Serial MRI, T2-FLAIR, and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps can describe radiographic changes over time (Akbari et al., 2016; Hoff et al., 2021). Recent work has mapped recurrence-prone tissue in the peri-enhancing zone, tested mid-treatment ADC-based maps of regions that later recur, and combined mathematical tumor models with reinforcement learning (Bertalan et al., 2026; Moore-Palhares et al., 2025; Rezvanjou et al., 2026; Q. Liu et al., 2025; Z. Liu et al., 2025). Same-patient molecular-imaging work is also established: Hu et al. (2021) linked 95 image-localized biopsies from 25 untreated glioblastoma patients to spatially matched multiparametric MRI to model regional EGFR amplification and prediction uncertainty, then evaluated a separate set of 24 biopsies from 7 high-grade-glioma patients. A 2026 prospective pilot used machine-learning infiltration maps to guide radiation dose escalation in 20 patients; the comparative analysis used historical matched controls, and the report also described radiation necrosis in 47% of treated patients versus 12% of controls, so its survival findings remain preliminary and are not randomized evidence (Akbari et al., 2026). A 2026 systematic review identified 21 neuro-oncology digital-twin studies, including 12 on glioblastoma or high-grade glioma; it found that multimodal data fusion and external validation remained limited. A separate 2026 review specifically describes MRI-centered integration of molecular, pathology, and treatment-history data as a current glioblastoma research direction. These reviews identify field needs; they do not establish this project’s uniqueness (Singh et al., 2026; Zadeh-Shirazi et al., 2026). Other direct precedents include Bayesian MRI-informed, risk-aware radiotherapy planning demonstrated in a 100-patient in-silico high-grade-glioma cohort; weekly MRI data assimilation with a two-species reaction-diffusion model in 21 patients; a patient-specific MRI/ADC treatment-response and radiotherapy-target framework in 21 patients; PDE-constrained spatiotemporal radiotherapy optimization; Q-learning for temozolomide scheduling in virtual glioblastoma; and a recent preprint combining reaction-diffusion, learned residual correction, recursive updating, and treatment scheduling on patient-data-informed synthetic trajectories (Chaudhuri et al., 2023; Miniere et al., 2025; Ty et al., 2026; Kao et al., 2026; Ebrahimi Zade et al., 2020; Liu, Trimboli, & Li, 2026, preprint; Corwin et al., 2013; Z. Liu et al., 2025). Uncertainty and robustness analysis for patient-specific glioblastoma response metrics also have prior precedent (Hawkins-Daarud et al., 2019). TumorTwin is a published modular Python framework for patient-specific imaging-based tumor models, longitudinal MRI calibration, and high-grade-glioma treatment-response simulation, so those software components are not novel by themselves (Kapteyn et al., 2026). The project will therefore not claim that multimodal therapy modeling, MRI forecasting, radiogenomics, digital twins, mechanistic-learning hybrids, uncertainty analysis, or adaptive scheduling is new by itself. Its candidate methodological question will be whether explicitly uncertain, unlinked molecular and MRI-derived signals change treatment-policy value and worst-case regret across shared held-out virtual worlds. This targeted review has not established that this narrower design is unique; the project will treat it as an open question, not a novelty claim.

The project will make the Duke patient MRI analysis its main empirical study. It will test whether newly FLAIR-labeled peritumoral regions receive an automated necrotic- or enhancing-core label at the next visit more often than persistent FLAIR-labeled regions at the same distance from the current core. It will also test whether local ADC measurements improve prediction of that transition. Related studies already examine peritumoral imaging and later recurrence; the narrower question here is the three-visit, distance-matched comparison of newly and persistently FLAIR-labeled regions under observed care. These outcomes will describe transitions between automated MRI labels. They will not prove that a region contains invading tumor cells.

The three tracks will then form one connected computational framework. Track A will provide previously generated donor-held-out molecular prediction summaries as simulation inputs. Track B will provide patient-level MRI transition and ADC observations from the Duke cohort. Track C will represent tumor growth, treatment suppression, and resistant cells. Because the source cohorts have no shared patient identifiers, I will not merge people or assign Track A measurements to Duke patients; the title’s “tumor biology” component will enter only as a prespecified scenario signal derived from the exploratory A5 summary, not as a measured Duke-patient biomarker or resistance assay. Instead, I will build virtual patients and explicitly vary the assumed relationships among the molecular signal, MRI signal, and drug resistance. This design will show when combining these signals helps a simulated treatment policy and when uncertain or misleading links remove that benefit.

The mathematical contribution will be a paired, out-of-grid stress test of whether dependence-aware belief updating changes simulated treatment utility relative to the same controller with conditional independence. I will vary signal reliability, factor loadings, residual dependence, and likelihood mismatch, then compare dependence-aware and robustness-aware policies with single-channel, no-signal, and fixed-schedule controls. The primary contrast will use unique virtual-world IDs; patient and measurement-noise replicates will remain nested within each world. Track A and Duke data will not share participants. Their summaries will inform permitted marginal signal ranges only; every molecular-to-resistance relationship will remain an explicit simulation assumption. I will report all development regimes, every held-out anchor, and any harmful regime. This finite scenario study will not prove a general theorem, patient treatment benefit, or novelty by itself. The Duke MRI transition analysis will remain a separate patient-derived description of automated label transitions.

## Research Question, Hypotheses, Engineering Goals, and Expected Outcomes

### Research Questions

**Central mathematical question:** Across two aggregate-constrained simulator families and genuinely out-of-grid virtual worlds, when will a controller that models dependence between a baseline biology-like report and MRI-like evidence improve or harm simulated treatment utility compared with the same controller assuming conditional independence? How will the result change under single-channel, no-signal, and signal-model-mismatch controls?

1. **Primary patient question:** At the same physical distance from the existing core, will newly FLAIR-labeled nonenhancing regions have a higher next-visit automated necrotic/enhancing core-label rate than persistent FLAIR-labeled regions?
2. **ADC question:** Will current ADC and its change over time improve patient-held-out prediction of that transition beyond the prespecified MRI and clinical baseline?
3. **Integrated simulation question:** In held-out worlds with nonzero residual dependence, will a dependence-aware controller improve paired worst-endpoint utility over an otherwise identical conditional-independence controller when its assumed signal model is correctly specified? How will the difference change under wrong-dependence and wrong-sign stress tests? The world-level q value will remain an illustrative setting for a synthetic report; it will not be treated as a patient assay accuracy, resistance measure, or biological link.
4. **Robustness question:** Across the 27 development combinations and five out-of-grid evaluation anchors, how will the fused policies help, tie, or harm as signal reliability, factor loadings, and residual dependence change?

### Hypotheses

1. **Primary Duke hypothesis:** Under observed care, at matched physical distances from the middle-visit core, newly FLAIR-labeled regions will have a higher next-visit automated core-label rate than persistent regions; I will report prior-label composition and the prespecified prior-core/cavity sensitivity.
2. **Secondary ADC hypothesis:** Adding current normalized ADC and its change from the prior visit will lower patient-held-out Brier score for predicting next-visit core-label conversion.
3. **Virtual-patient hypothesis:** In correctly specified held-out worlds with nonzero residual dependence, dependence-aware fusion will have higher mean worst-endpoint utility than the otherwise identical conditional-independence controller. This directional hypothesis may fail; wrong-dependence and wrong-sign worlds may favor the simpler controller. The A5-derived q value will remain an illustrative synthetic-report setting, not an assay estimate or resistance measure.
4. **Robustness hypothesis:** The robustness-aware controller will have lower worst-case regret than fixed-prior fusion across all five out-of-grid evaluation anchors, measured using the frozen utility. This directional hypothesis may fail.

These are directional hypotheses. I will report the measured results even if they do not support them.

### Engineering Goals

1. Build an auditable Duke pipeline that links consecutive patient visits, checks MRI registration, measures tissue-label transitions, and keeps all visits from each patient in one validation fold.
2. Use the frozen Track A A5 donor-level values only as analyst-selected, world-level reliability scenarios for a synthetic binary report of a Core-like/Periphery-like state. I will vary the assumed association between that state and simulated resistance separately; A5 will not be interpreted as a resistance assay.
3. Evaluate a two-population tumor and drug-suppression model across prespecified parameter ranges before comparing treatment policies.
4. Build virtual patients from separate Track A and Track B signal distributions and Track C tumor and treatment states without matching people across cohorts. I will make each cross-source association an explicit simulation assumption rather than an estimate from linked patient data.
5. Quantify the incremental value of the fused belief-state controller against development-selected fixed and single-modality policies, alongside no-information controls, using shared virtual patients, scan schedules, noise, and treatment limits.
6. Save the data flow, exclusions, settings, seeds, software versions, code fingerprints, model failures, and all primary and secondary results.

### Expected Outcomes

The Duke analysis will estimate whether newly appearing FLAIR-labeled regions will show higher, similar, or lower next-visit core-label conversion than persistent regions. The ADC analysis will test whether adding ADC features will improve held-out prediction. The simulation will compare fusion under different assumptions and may show benefit, no advantage, or harm. I will report each result by its evidence source: patient MRI analysis or virtual-patient simulation.

2. **Verify the archive and create the Duke cohort from metadata.** I will verify the downloaded archive against any checksum supplied with the authorized download, record the checksum source, calculate a local SHA-256, and record the archive name and size. Before opening voxel arrays, a header-only check will verify that required images and masks are 3D NIfTI files with finite nonsingular affines, millimeter spatial units, and positive voxel spacing. Any hash or header failure will stop that file; I will not repair it manually. Before reading image or mask voxels, I will inspect only permitted metadata: anonymized patient and visit identifiers, session/path identifiers, relative scan dates, available modalities, diagnosis, recorded IDH field, surgery dates, radiation and drug-treatment dates, and recurrence flags. The primary cohort will include records whose diagnosis field identifies glioblastoma and whose released IDH field is exactly “Negative.” I will report this as the dataset-coded IDH field, not as proof of comprehensive IDH1/2 wildtype status. I will exclude missing, indeterminate, or conflicting diagnosis or IDH records from the primary cohort and report their counts. I will check prior-use overlap where permitted; if person-level overlap cannot be established, I will not call the analysis independent external confirmation.

   I will sort sessions by numeric relative ScanDate; ScanNumber will be used only to join metadata to file paths, not to set chronology. I will require dates to be finite, unique within a patient, and strictly increasing after sorting. I will exclude windows with unresolved dates before voxel reads. I will use every eligible set of three adjacent sessions as a prior, middle, and next-visit window and will not skip a missing or unusable visit to create a nonconsecutive window. A primary window will require T1-c, FLAIR, and segmentation labels at all three visits. ADC at the prior and middle visits will be required only for the ADC analysis. I will report approximate scan intervals and all recorded treatment events between visits. Because the authors report that extracted dates may be off by several days, I will repeat interval and treatment-phase classifications under ±3- and ±7-day date-jitter stress scenarios; these will be analyst-selected stress bounds, not verified error limits. The comparison will estimate transitions under observed care, not untreated natural history. Windows with surgery between the middle and next visits will remain in the primary analysis and will also be excluded in a prespecified descriptive sensitivity analysis. The next-visit T1-c will be used only for the predeclared evaluation alignment and QC; it may determine technical eligibility, but it will not enter support, features, tuning, or forecast generation. QC failures will exclude the whole window from all arms before target labels are read. I will treat this analysis as retrospective; it will not represent a live prediction available at the middle visit.

3. **Register scans and freeze quality checks before outcome scoring.** I will treat released modalities as sharing a registered grid within each visit, and I will register consecutive visits to one another. I will rigidly align the prior- and next-visit T1-c images to the middle-visit T1-c image using image intensities only, then apply each transform to that visit’s other modalities and segmentation mask. The moving-to-fixed transform will use millimeter physical coordinates from the NIfTI affines, be centered at the physical center of the middle-visit image, and use rotation matrix Rz(γ)Ry(β)Rx(α), with translations in millimeters. I will maximize mean-centered normalized cross-correlation over finite, nonzero fixed-image voxels using Powell optimization from zero transform: first on every second voxel (xtol 0.01, ftol 0.0001, maximum 2,000 iterations), then on the full 1 mm grid from the coarse result (xtol 0.001, ftol 0.00001, maximum 2,000 iterations). I will resample with the inverse transform; intensity images will use linear interpolation, and labels will use nearest-neighbor interpolation. Outside-field voxels will be zero. No retry or manual transform adjustment will be allowed. A registered visit will pass only when NCC is at least 0.80, the largest absolute Euler angle is at most 10 degrees, and translation-vector length is at most 10 mm. I will treat these as operational screens, not validated accuracy limits. Before patient scoring, I will use synthetic known-transform tests and report released vessel-landmark errors only for matching visits that contain them; these landmarks will not tune transforms or remove points and will not validate every adjacent-visit registration. Failed pairs will be excluded before target scoring with reasons recorded. I will freeze and hash the passing patient/window list, protocol, code, and patient-grouped folds before opening any held-out next-visit label voxels.

   I will use the released/resampled 1 mm grid for the primary MRI transition analysis, with nearest-neighbor interpolation for labels. I will run a prespecified 2 mm sensitivity analysis for the transition outcome. Secondary PDE forecasts will use a 2 mm isotropic grid aligned to the middle visit; intensities will be averaged, and labels will use nearest-neighbor sampling. Labels 1 and 3 will define the operational core, label 2 will define nonenhancing FLAIR signal abnormality, and label 4 will define the resection cavity. Label 0 and other values will not be called normal because their meaning is not established. The middle-visit foreground B2 will be the union of finite, nonzero T1-c and FLAIR voxels; it is not an anatomical brain mask. Origin-only support S will be B2 union nonzero labels from the prior and middle visits. The next-visit image may define only the frozen evaluation alignment and technical QC; it will not define support or prediction features, and its QC may determine a technical exclusion that applies to all arms before target labels are read. Target-core voxels outside S will count as misses in every forecast comparison.

4. **Measure the primary Duke transition.** For each window, I will call the prior, middle, and next operational core masks C1, C2, and C3, and the prior and middle label-2 masks F1 and F2. The middle-visit risk region R2 will be F2 outside a 2 mm dilation of the union of C2 and the cavity. I will call A2 “newly FLAIR-labeled” because it will be R2 outside a 2 mm dilation of F1; I will call P2 persistent FLAIR because it will be R2 inside that dilation. “Newly FLAIR-labeled” will not mean newly abnormal tissue. I will report the mapped prior-label composition of A2 and run a prespecified sensitivity excluding voxels previously labeled core (1 or 3) or cavity (4). I will report necrotic-label and enhancing-label transitions separately as descriptive components. The 2 mm margins will reduce sensitivity to one-voxel registration and labeling changes.

   I will calculate each A2 and P2 region’s distance to the nearest C2 voxel using 1 mm physical spacing. I will compare the two region types in 1 mm distance strata from 2 to 30 mm and retain only strata with at least 80 mm³ of each region type. Regions beyond 30 mm will be descriptive. A history-defined window will be eligible when C2 is nonempty and at least one stratum meets this rule. I will apply this rule without viewing C3. At the next visit, labels 1 or 3 will count as a recorded automated core label. Labels 0, 2, and 4 will count as no recorded core label for this operational endpoint, without treating label 0 as biological absence.

   Within each retained 1 mm stratum, I will calculate the fraction of A2 and P2 volume receiving a next-visit automated core label and subtract the P2 fraction from the A2 fraction. Each stratum will receive weight proportional to the smaller of its A2 and P2 volumes. I will average strata within windows, windows within patients, and patients with equal weight. I will interpret the primary estimate as an association between automated MRI labels under observed care, not as a causal effect or histologic measurement. I will report scan-interval and treatment-event summaries and prespecified sensitivity analyses by interval and recorded treatment status, and excluding surgery between the middle and next visits; if few strata qualify, I will label them descriptive. I will report the mean patient-level difference and a 95% percentile-bootstrap interval from 10,000 patient-cluster resamples using NumPy PCG64 seed 20261104 and linear percentile quantiles. Before opening next-visit labels, I will run label-free precision checks across plausible conversion rates and keep the decision thresholds fixed. These thresholds will be practical feasibility rules, not a power calculation. If fewer than 20 patients qualify, I will stop the primary inferential analysis. With 20–34 patients, or an interval half-width greater than 0.10, I will report a feasibility estimate. I will interpret the primary hypothesis as supported only if at least 35 patients qualify, the interval half-width is at most 0.10, and the lower confidence bound is above zero; these will be predeclared practical thresholds, not power-derived guarantees. I will also report the next-visit label distribution and a descriptive sensitivity analysis excluding label-0 voxels.

5. **Test whether ADC adds predictive information.** I will normalize each ADC map by its median positive value inside that visit’s finite, nonzero T1-c-or-FLAIR foreground and use the logarithm of normalized ADC. Within R2, the predictors will be current log-normalized ADC and its change from the prior visit. The baseline logistic model will include distance band, new-versus-persistent status, robust-standardized FLAIR intensity, the observed middle-to-next scan interval, time since known surgery, radiation phase, systemic-therapy status, and treatment events between the prior and middle visits. The observed forecast interval is available only in this retrospective dataset; this model will not be described as a prospective tool unless a future scan interval is known at prediction time. FLAIR intensity will be standardized within B2 using its median and 1.4826 times its median absolute deviation; a zero or nonfinite scale will make that window unavailable for this model. Unknown treatment history will remain an explicit category. No event after the prediction origin will be used as a predictor.

   I will compare the baseline model with the same model plus ADC features using five patient-grouped outer folds. I will sort anonymized patient IDs, permute them with NumPy PCG64 seed 20261103, split them into five sequential folds, and keep every window from one patient in that patient’s fold. Inner folds will use the same procedure on outer-training patients with seed 20261104 plus the outer-fold index; inner training must contain at least 10 patients. The models will use L2 logistic regression with the lbfgs solver, tolerance 0.0001, maximum 1,000 iterations, and no class weights. Training weights will be equal by patient, then by that patient’s windows and at-risk voxels. Inner patient-grouped folds will select C from {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} by patient-macro Brier score; ties will select the smaller C. Scaling and feature selection will use training patients only. Both models will use the same ADC-complete patients and folds. I will report held-out patient-macro Brier scores, calibration summaries, and the paired difference, with 10,000 patient-cluster bootstrap resamples using NumPy PCG64 seed 20261104 and linear percentile quantiles. I will define positive improvement as a baseline Brier score minus the ADC-model Brier score greater than zero.

6. **Run the secondary Track B forecast benchmark.** I will forecast C3 from C1 and C2 with the isotropic Fisher–Kolmogorov model ∂u/∂t = D∇²u + ρu(1−u), on the common support and actual visit intervals. The solver will use a no-flux boundary, a maximum time step of 0.5 day, diffusion stability fraction 0.9, and reaction stability limit 0.05. It will not include a treatment-kill term, so it will forecast recorded label evolution under observed care.

   I will compare the PDE forecast with seven prespecified mask comparators: copy-last, fixed growth, volume-matched persistence, FLAIR-front, two-history shape, PDE-only, and a time-conditioned FiLM U-Net. I will also report a matched-volume no-PDE ranker as a separate ablation and a count-only model on count metrics; neither will be turned into an extra mask or mixed into the seven-comparator family. The PDE will use log-uniform priors ρ ∈ [10⁻⁵, 10⁻¹] day⁻¹ and D ∈ [10⁻⁵, 10] mm²/day, a 21 × 21 coarse log grid, an 11 × 11 refinement over the region containing 90% of posterior mass, 200 posterior draws, and 256 deterministic surface anchors. For each ρ–D pair, I will rank the field within S to match the observed C2 volume, then calculate signed-distance residuals at the 256 C2 surface anchors. The boundary likelihood will be a normalized zero-mean multivariate Gaussian with covariance Σᵢⱼ = σᵦ² exp(−dᵢⱼ/ℓ) + τᵦ²I. I will multiply this likelihood by the log-uniform prior and log-grid quadrature weights, normalize over the grid, and sample 200 draws with replacement. Because each fit will use only two history masks, I expect ρ and D may remain correlated; I will treat the forecast, not separate parameter estimates, as the target. The boundary-residual covariance will use σᵦ = 0.8 mm, τᵦ = 0.3 mm, initial ℓ = 6 mm, and candidate length scales ℓ ∈ {5, 10, 20} mm. Five-fold patient-held-out calibration will use outer-training patients only, at least eight calibration patients, and at least two valid residual patients. For each ℓ, patient-balanced semivariogram method-of-moments estimates of σᵦ² and τᵦ² will use nonnegative least squares; I will select ℓ by lowest weighted RMSE. I will interpret the covariance as representing combined model, registration, treatment, and label residuals, not scanner noise or mean boundary bias. The one-decade narrower prior will use ρ ∈ [10⁻⁴, 10⁻²] day⁻¹ and D ∈ [10⁻⁴, 1] mm²/day; the wider prior will use ρ ∈ [10⁻⁶, 1] day⁻¹ and D ∈ [10⁻⁶, 100] mm²/day. Sensitivity results will be descriptive and cannot select the primary prior. I will save each outer-test forecast and its selected settings before opening that window’s next-visit mask for scoring.

   The candidate will rank each raw PDE field within S to match its corresponding outer-training-only volume draw, perturb the matched boundary using the fitted covariance and pre-generated seeds, average 200 binary masks, and threshold inclusion probability at 0.5. The shared history-only volume model will estimate each training patient’s log-volume slope from C1 and C2, shrink it toward the equal-patient outer-training median using an inner-selected time constant from {30, 90, 180, 365} days, fit log-volume predictive variance a + bT by patient-weighted nonnegative least squares on squared held-out training residuals, and generate 200 positive lognormal volume draws with seed 20261028. Boundary and forecast seeds will be 20261029 and 20261028.

   Copy-last will return C2 unchanged. Fixed growth will dilate C2 by r = k × forecast days, with k ∈ {0, 0.01, …, 0.19} mm/day and a 20 mm cap; inner patient-grouped validation will select k and ties will go to the smaller value. Volume-matched persistence will rank S by signed distance from C2, then flat voxel index, and assign the shared volume draws. FLAIR-front will rank C2 first, A2 second, P2 third, then remaining S by distance from C2 and flat voxel index, and assign the same draws. The two-history arm will extrapolate the signed-distance change from C1 to C2 by f = min(T/(g + τ), m), where T is forecast days and g is history-gap days; its grid will be τ ∈ {0, 7, 28} days, m ∈ {1, 4, 8}, maximum distance change {10, 30} mm, plus f = 0. If g < 1 day or C1 is empty or fills S, f will be zero. Inner validation will select settings; scores within 0.005 mean patient-macro Dice will tie, resolved toward f = 0, smaller m, larger τ, then smaller distance cap. The two-history arm will use the same training-only volume draws and boundary seeds as the candidate. The matched-volume no-PDE ablation will rank voxels by signed distance from C2 to match the same training-only volume draws and use the same boundary covariance and perturbation seeds, but will not run or use the PDE. The PDE-only arm will omit volume matching and boundary perturbation, select c ∈ {0.05, 0.10, …, 0.50} by outer-training-only inner-validation Dice (ties to larger c), threshold raw PDE draws, average the binary masks, and call voxels included at probability ≥0.5. A valid empty forecast will remain empty.

   The FiLM U-Net will use binary C1, C2, and support channels plus log(forecast days/91) and log(history days/91); four levels with widths 16/32/64/128; two 3 × 3 × 3 convolutions per level; instance normalization; and zero-initialized 16-unit FiLM networks. It will train for 300 epochs with Dice plus binary cross-entropy, Adam at 0.001 with betas (0.9, 0.999), batch size 1, no weight decay, random axis flips, axial 90-degree rotations, and the final epoch retained. Three fold-specific seeds will be 20261102 + fold, +1000, and +2000. I will average the three probability maps, threshold at 0.5, and clip to S. The count-only model will define a new core piece as a 26-connected component of C3 outside a 2 mm dilation of C2 with at least two voxels. A training-only Poisson GLM will use log(|C2|) as predictor and log(forecast days) as offset; if training has zero events, its rate will be 0.5 divided by total training days. It will be scored only by Poisson deviance, count MAE, and Brier score for any new piece, with event probability 1 − exp(−predicted count). Fixed growth, PDE cutoffs, volume draws, and neural models will be selected or fitted using outer-training patients only. All mask methods will use the same windows, support, target, and scoring rule. Five outer patient-grouped folds will use NumPy PCG64 seed 20261103; inner folds will use seed 20261104 plus the outer-fold index, with at least 10 inner-training patients. The endpoint will be patient-macro Dice; both-empty Dice will be 1, and one-empty Dice will be 0. I will report each paired patient-level contrast using 10,000 shared patient-bootstrap resamples, seed 20261110, and one-step max-T simultaneous 95% intervals. The candidate will count as better than the required comparator family only if every adjusted lower confidence bound is above zero. A missing required arm will make the family incomplete. This forecast result will remain separate from the primary transition result.

8. **Define and simulate the Track C drug-suppression worlds.** I will use a normalized two-population model with drug-sensitive burden S and drug-resistant burden R, total burden B = S + R, and carrying capacity K = 1:

   - dS/dt = rS[1 − (S + aᵣₛR)] − mruS − μuS + ν(1−u)R
   - dR/dt = r(1 − c)R[1 − (aₛᵣS + R)] − εmruR + μuS − ν(1−u)R

   Here r is untreated growth, m is the sensitive-cell drug-kill multiplier, c is the resistant-cell growth cost, ε is residual drug sensitivity in resistant cells, μ is drug-induced sensitive-to-resistant switching, ν is off-drug reversion, and u equals 1 on treatment days and 0 otherwise. Initial values will be S0 = (1−f0)B0 and R0 = f0B0. I will set K = 1 and aₛᵣ = aᵣₛ = 1. The solver will use fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration with 0.25-day steps. All parameter values, including μ and ν, are assumptions; none will be estimated from Duke MRI.

   One course will represent five modeled treatment days in a 28-day cycle. I will use this as a normalized on/off drug-suppression input, not as a prescribed drug dose or a claim of pharmacologic equivalence. The standard schedule will use six courses; a simulated dose-dense schedule will multiply modeled exposure by 2.0, with 1.5 and 3.0 as sensitivity values; any 12-versus-6 schedule will be a model sensitivity, not a reconstruction of a clinical PFS comparison. I will limit adaptive policies to at most 12 courses over 730 days. As a course-limit sensitivity analysis, I will repeat policy comparisons with a six-course cap and with a mixed cap of three courses for 63% and six courses for 37% of virtual patients; that split is an assumption based on aggregate trial reporting, not individual treatment records. I will use the registered Track C world generator. In the single-response family, it will shift r from its base log range 0.001–0.05 per day by exp(−0.7 to 0.9), scale m from its base log range 6–20 by exp(log(0.05) to 0), shift f0 from its base log range 0.001–0.1 by exp(−0.7 to 2.3) with a 0.9 cap, draw B0 centers from 0.12–0.55 with a 0.10 half-width, draw c from 0–0.25, residual sensitivity ε from 0–0.2, and set μ and ν from {0, 0.01, 0.04} per day. In the mixture family, the nonresponder m scale will range from 0.02 to 0.30 and responder fraction q from 0.10 to 0.60; responders will retain the base m range 6–20. I will report the two families separately and will not select a family by its policy result.

   I will draw 500 Latin-hypercube worlds per family over the full prespecified parameter ranges, using development seeds only, and simulate 400 virtual patients per world. I will not discard worlds based on their outcome or on a match to trial summary statistics. The standard schedule will use six courses, treatment on days 0–4 of each 28-day cycle, and scans every 84 days. The model will report time to modeled progression under the baseline and RANO-like rules, not clinical PFS, because it does not simulate death. I will display the RTOG 0525 and GEINO 14-01 aggregate results as context, not as direct calibration gates or validation targets. I will report all sampled worlds, both response families, treatment use, and uncertainty without selecting a favorable subset. A later trial-aligned PFS model would require a separate dated amendment specifying the trial time origin, adjuvant delay, assessment schedule, progression definition, death/censoring process, and risk set before any new policy-outcome evaluation.

9. **Build the virtual patients without false patient matching.** For each Track B outer-held-out patient, I will calculate the cross-fitted predicted conversion-risk distribution across that patient’s eligible windows. I will use this only as an empirical marginal template for a hypothetical imaging channel. I will not treat logit(observed fraction) minus logit(predicted probability) as measurement error around latent resistance; it combines sampling variation, patient heterogeneity, and model miscalibration. I will specify separate assumed observation likelihoods linking molecular and imaging signals to either initial or current resistant fraction, and I will vary their reliability and temporal structure. A static-trait channel tied to initial resistance and a dynamic-state channel tied to current resistance will be distinct scenarios. I will model persistent patient-level bias separately from visit-level measurement noise. I will use the full prespecified parameter-world distribution, without outcome-based filtering, to define the frozen joint distribution for each family. Held-out evaluation will draw fresh parameter vectors with the reserved evaluation seed block; it will not reuse parameter-characterization or development world IDs. No A5 donor, Duke patient, or trial participant will be treated as the same person as a virtual patient.

   I will map each simulator resistance fraction f0 through the fixed log-uniform reference CDF over the declared f0 envelope to obtain Z_R = Φ⁻¹(F_ref(f0)); I will draw only E_B and E_I as independent standard-normal variables. I will form Z_B = a Z_R + sqrt(1−a²) E_B and Z_I = b Z_R + sqrt(1−b²)[d E_B + sqrt(1−d²) E_I]. I will define C = 1[Z_B ≥ 0] and generate molecular report M with world-level q. I will treat a and b as factor loadings and d as the residual-factor coefficient, not as empirical cohort correlations. I will report the induced state prevalence and realized associations for each world. I will calibrate MRI-score location and scale using only the permitted training/history template; I will not map ranks to a target-derived distribution. I will use a,b,d ∈ {−0.5, 0, 0.5} for the 27 development combinations. The independent setting (0,0,0) will remain a development negative control, not a held-out anchor. I will evaluate these five out-of-grid anchors: strong aligned dependence (0.75,0.75,0.75); complementary residual dependence (0.75,0.75,−0.75); reversed molecular loading (−0.75,0.75,0.50); reversed MRI loading (0.50,−0.75,−0.50); and both loadings reversed (−0.75,−0.75,0.75). The first two will define the correctly specified nonzero-dependence primary set. I will keep unique Track C parameter-world IDs and paired random streams across policies; patient and noise replicates will remain nested within world IDs. These coefficients will be analyst-selected assumptions, not estimates from patient cohorts.

10. **Train and compare treatment policies.** The fixed-prior fused policy will use an uncertainty-aware belief-state model-predictive controller. At baseline, it will receive one binary molecular report M and the simulated MRI/ADC signal, never the latent state C, resistance Z_R, or the generating q. For the primary held-out evaluation, the frozen likelihood will use symmetric sensitivity and specificity equal to the equal-donor mean A5 value as the world-level q scenario; development will map all six q values and the prespecified asymmetric channels. For the correctly specified primary contrast, I will give the dependence-aware likelihood the matching, predeclared population-level q, a, b, and d values; it will not receive a virtual patient's latent state or tumor parameters. This will be an idealized observation-model test. In mismatch analyses, I will freeze the policy likelihood before evaluation and will not reveal the generating scenario. The robustness-aware policy will use only its predeclared uncertainty set. At each 84-day scan, it will receive noisy log-burden, with standard deviation selected equally from {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}, and a simulated MRI/ADC signal generated from the prespecified training-only marginal template and chosen static-trait or dynamic-state observation model. I will not use patient-level prediction residuals as resistance measurement error. A second robustness-aware controller will select the action maximizing minimum expected utility over the frozen dependence and reliability set. Neither controller will receive hidden tumor parameters or the virtual patient’s generating scenario.

   The filter will use 500 particles initialized from the registered parameter prior for its response family over growth, drug kill, resistant fraction, resistance cost, residual drug sensitivity, starting burden, switching, reversion, and responder class where applicable. Switching and reversion will use the discrete assumed levels {0, 0.01, 0.04}/day; they are not estimated from Duke data. The filter will use systematic resampling when effective sample size falls below 250 and a Liu–West move with h = 0.90 on continuous parameters; resampled discrete levels will remain discrete. At each scan, it will compare all eight on/off treatment patterns for the next three 28-day cycles, repeat each pattern over a six-cycle (168-day) prediction window, and apply only the first three-cycle block before replanning. It will evaluate 16 posterior prediction draws and select the pattern maximizing min(RMST₁₆₈,baseline, RMST₁₆₈,RANO-like) − 5 days × expected courses_shared. Treatment will stop after the first latent simulator progression event under either endpoint; an ongoing 28-day cycle will finish, and no later cycle will receive treatment. The policy will not receive the hidden event time, so this will be an idealized simulator rule, not a clinical stopping rule. Both endpoint times will be scored on the same trajectory and course count. I will use this as a receding-horizon approximation and will evaluate held-out policy outcomes over the full 730-day period. I will treat the five-day course cost as an analyst-selected utility penalty, not as a measured toxicity or time cost. As a prespecified preference sensitivity, I will also retrain adaptive policies and reselect fixed and single-modality comparators on development worlds using penalties of 0 and 10 days per course; I will evaluate each unchanged policy on the same held-out worlds. These values will test dependence on the chosen utility weight and will not be interpreted as clinical toxicity estimates. Each integrated policy will have a 12-course maximum and will obey the same action and scan limits as its comparators.

   I will compare dependence-aware fusion with an otherwise identical conditional-independence ablation that sets d=0 while keeping the controller, priors, inputs, particles, actions, utility, and random streams fixed. This paired comparison will isolate the modeled residual-dependence contribution. I will also compare with the standard six-course schedule; a development-selected fixed schedule; a Track-C-only controller; MRI-only and molecular-only controllers; and no-signal controls. I will implement repeated MRI-like observations at each scheduled scan and update the policy likelihood once per observation, while separating persistent patient-level bias from visit-level noise. Synthetic tests will verify the serial likelihood, missing-scan behavior, no double-counting, and future-observation isolation before outcome evaluation. I will include late fusion and signal-shuffle controls as secondary methods. The current tabular Q-learning implementation will be labeled a burden-only reference because it does not receive the molecular or MRI signal. I will either add and test a same-input Q-learning arm before protocol freeze or exclude Q-learning from same-information superiority claims. The 56-schedule catalog will cover 52 cycles and allow at most 12 courses; I will select its comparator using development worlds only, with ties favoring fewer courses and then catalog order. I will select single-modality comparators using the same development-only objective. I will report every held-out anchor, both progression endpoints, course use, and the complete regime map. The five-day course penalty will remain an analyst-selected utility weight; I will repeat selection at 0 and 10 days as secondary preference sensitivities. A complete-information oracle may appear only as a diagnostic ceiling. Every policy will receive the same virtual patients, parameter-world IDs, scan schedules, and noise streams. The simulated courses will represent generic drug suppression only; they will not represent a clinical regimen or estimate treatment benefit.

## Data Analysis

### Primary patient-data analysis

I will treat the patient MRI transition and virtual-patient policy comparison as separate primary components with separate conclusions; I will not combine them into one pass/fail claim. The primary patient estimate will be the equal-patient mean of within-window, 1 mm distance-stratified differences in next-visit automated core-label rate between newly FLAIR-labeled and persistent regions. Only strata with at least 80 mm³ of both region types will contribute; each stratum will receive the smaller-region-volume weight. The 95% interval will use 10,000 patient-cluster percentile-bootstrap resamples. Patients, not voxels, visits, or regions, will be units of inference. I will treat the patient rule as met only if at least 35 patients are evaluable, the interval half-width is no greater than 0.10, and the lower confidence bound is above zero. If fewer patients qualify or precision is wider, I will report feasibility only.

The ADC analysis will report paired held-out patient-macro Brier scores for the baseline and ADC-extended models. The Track B forecast comparison will report patient-macro Dice and simultaneous paired confidence intervals for every required comparator. Neither secondary analysis will change the primary transition rule.

### Integrated virtual-patient analysis

For each virtual patient, I will calculate restricted mean time to progression at 365 and 730 days under two predeclared outcomes: (1) true simulated burden first reaching 1.2 times starting burden, and (2) a scan-based RANO-like call at 84-day scans, when burden reaches 1.4 times the greater of the prior scan nadir or 0.10 times starting burden (Ellingson et al., 2017). Restricted mean time will be the average of min(time to progression, horizon). The second endpoint will be a model approximation, not a formal RANO assessment.

I will define each virtual patient's restricted endpoint times as Tᵢ,base = min(time to simulated burden progression, 730 days) and Tᵢ,RANO = min(time to the scan-based RANO-like event, 730 days). The patient-level robust utility will be Uᵢ = min(Tᵢ,base, Tᵢ,RANO) − 5 × Cᵢ, where Cᵢ is the number of courses delivered by day 730 on that same policy-specific trajectory. I will average Uᵢ within each virtual world, then calculate paired policy differences between the world means. This is the mean of patient-level worst-endpoint utility; it is not the lower of the two cohort-average RMST values. The primary contrast will be paired ΔU = U_dependence-aware − U_conditional-independence over unique world IDs in the two correctly specified nonzero-dependence anchors and both response families. I will use simultaneous 95% max-|t| intervals across the two response families. A positive primary result will require both lower bounds above zero; otherwise I will report the estimate and uncertainty without claiming a dependence-model advantage. I will report endpoint-specific RMST differences and policy-specific course counts. I will evaluate wrong-dependence, wrong-sign, single-channel, no-signal, and shuffled-signal controls across all five anchors as prespecified robustness analyses. I will bootstrap unique world IDs, keeping all virtual patients and noise replicates within each sampled world together; repeated patients or trajectories will not count as independent worlds. I will define regret against the best mean utility among the predeclared deployable policies and report worst-case regret across the five anchors. The held-out fixed-schedule frontier will remain descriptive only. I will repeat policy selection at 0- and 10-day course penalties as preference sensitivities and report any ranking changes. No result will be selected by choosing a favorable anchor after evaluation.
