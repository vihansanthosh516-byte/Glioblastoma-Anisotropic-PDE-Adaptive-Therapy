## Findings

### 1. Existing interval candidates remain rejected

- The observed-SE max-T screen passed **0/24** predeclared cells.
- Student-t with Bonferroni passed **14/24** cells in its first screen and **35/44** in its larger-world extension. Coverage was not monotonic in world count; the worst larger-screen cell had 88.48% joint coverage.
- The distribution-free Hoeffding interval is valid for independent bounded world differences, but its half-width is far above 14 days at current seed capacity.

These are prior saved synthetic screens, not policy results: [max-T screen](trackc_max_t_coverage_audit_result_2026-11-08.md), [Student-t screen](trackc_t_bonferroni_coverage_audit_result_2026-11-08.md), [larger-world extension](trackc_t_bonferroni_worldcount_extension_result_2026-11-08.md), and [distribution-free precision audit](trackc_distribution_free_precision_audit_2026-11-08.md).

### 2. A distribution-free lower bound rules out guaranteed 14-day precision at current capacity

The configured utility is the minimum of two progression times capped at 730 days, minus five days for each course through the cutoff. With at most 12 courses, patient utility lies in `[-60, 730]` days, so a paired candidate-minus-control world difference lies in `[-790, 790]` days.

For two simultaneous two-sided intervals with familywise 95% coverage, each marginal interval must have at least 95% coverage. Under equal Bonferroni allocation, each interval needs 97.5% marginal coverage. Consider independent world differences supported on `[-790, 790]`:

- `P0`: every difference is zero.
- `P+`: a difference is `+790` with probability `p` and zero otherwise.
- `P−`: a difference is `−790` with probability `p` and zero otherwise.

All three distributions can produce the same all-zero sample. Under `P+` and `P−`, that sample occurs with probability `(1−p)^n`. If this probability exceeds 0.025, a deterministic distribution-free interval with the required coverage must include both alternative means, `−790p` and `+790p`, on that sample. Its half-width must therefore be at least `790p`.

Under equal Bonferroni allocation, the all-zero-data lower bound is `790 × [1−0.025^(1/n)]` days:

| Independent worlds per family | Necessary half-width on all-zero data |
| ---: | ---: |
| 7 | 323.60 days |
| 14 | 182.99 days |
| 200 | 14.44 days |

At least **207 independent worlds per family** are needed merely to clear this bound under equal Bonferroni allocation. This is not sufficient to guarantee 14-day precision for the actual design. More generally, the familywise 95% requirement implies at least 95% marginal coverage per family; even using that less stringent marginal level, seven worlds require a half-width of at least 275.05 days on all-zero data. These bounds apply to deterministic, uniformly valid intervals built from independent bounded world differences. A narrower interval would need defensible additional assumptions or a tighter proven support; a method name alone cannot supply either.

This is a worst-case guarantee result. It does not say every interval computed from every possible nonzero sample must exceed 14 days. It says the current design cannot guarantee that precision for every distribution in the stated bounded class without extra assumptions or a tighter proven range.

The current development seed block has 89 free scenario seeds. The proposed schedule uses six scenario seeds per world, giving an upper capacity of 14 total worlds, or seven per family with an equal split. The 207-world threshold projects to about 164.22 serial hours for both families even at the existing two-patient-per-cohort timing reference; larger cohorts and method overhead add cost. The timing source is a small synthetic pilot, not a bound.

### 3. Initial integrated-v2 sampler mismatch audit and status

The integrated configuration is not an execution seal. The initial mismatch findings and current follow-up status are:

- **World law — resolved in the development sampler.** The proposed law now draws independent worlds, with independent per-world uniform `mu` and `nu`; global batch balancing is removed to match the population-mean estimand.
- **Patient-level `c` — resolved in the development sampler.** `c` is drawn per patient; the historical calibration generator is unchanged.
- **Per-world `q_signal` — resolved in the development sampler.** It is sampled from `{0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9}`; mixture responder probability is separately named `pi_response`.

Further source inspection found these additional execution mismatches:

- **Patient-axis order — resolved in the development sampler.** It now uses `[r, m, f0, B0, c, epsilon]` and maps `epsilon` to the simulator field `eps`.
- **Sigma assignment — resolved in the development sampler.** Balanced noise levels are assigned once over sorted patient IDs across all three cohorts, so each level can appear even when a cohort has only two patients.
- **Bit generator and seeds — partially resolved.** The proposal now uses explicit PCG64DXSM for world, cohort, sigma-assignment, and joint-signal draws, with explicit numeric schedule roles checked by the registry. The seed template remains unmaterialized and unreserved; policy trajectory generation remains PCG64 in the recorded environment.
- **Signal streams — resolved for signal generation.** The config now names joint calibration and evaluation streams, matching the dependence-preserving signal generator.
- **Policy and observation streams — mechanism reviewed; variance effect remains unmeasured.** Every arm receives the same evaluation seed and the same scenario. The simulator pre-draws scan noise before `policy.reset`, and generates missing-scan draws from a deterministic child seed. A scratch-copy synthetic test confirmed that two no-treatment policies see identical scan sequences even when their `reset` methods consume 0 versus 1,000 draws. The policy's internal particle-filter random numbers then use the continuing generator; if arms take different resampling branches, later internal draws can desynchronize. This is a defined common-random-number coupling, not evidence of marginal score bias, but it can change the variance of paired differences. Preserve and validate this exact coupling when validating the world-level interval; a separate-stream design would be a protocol change and is not adopted here.
- **Treatment-stop rule:** the machine-readable configuration now names `stop_endpoint_code="observed_two_scan"`. `PolicyBenchmarkSettings` defaults to that rule and rejects every other stop endpoint, including direct calls through the low-level benchmark. A test checks parity between the config and settings. The benchmark and world-runner suites passed 32 tests in a Git-archive scratch copy (Python 3.14; 2026-11-09). This closes the stop-rule bypass only; there is still no general config loader, and other settings can still drift until the complete driver consumes and validates the config.
- **Penalty sensitivities and bootstrap:** the configuration lists course-penalty sensitivities of 0 and 10 days and a `world_bootstrap` stream. One `run_shared_policy_world` call uses one penalty value (default 5); the development schedule has no bootstrap role. The research driver and sensitivity orchestration remain unimplemented.

The target population, categorical weights, randomization unit, and generator must agree before a confidence method can be validated for the intended estimand. If independent world-level inference is retained, a defensible option is to draw `(mu, nu)` and `q_signal` independently from their declared uniform distributions per world and draw `c` per patient. A balanced design can use complete independently randomized blocks, or another interval that accounts for the actual stratified design. Do not treat patients within one world, or points within one randomized QMC scramble, as independent worlds.

### 4. Literature does not remove these project-specific gates

Independent randomized QMC replications can support interval estimation, but ordinary Student-t intervals are approximate and depend on the integrand and replication regime. Published CLT results state regularity and relative-growth conditions; they are not a guarantee for this simulator's thresholded decisions, censoring, and minimum-of-capped-endpoints utility. Betting intervals for bounded means offer finite-sample guarantees under their sampling assumptions, but boundedness does not remove the rare-event precision limit above. No cited result validates an interval for this project's current generator without a matching outcome-blind check.

#### Paired random-stream behavior check (2026-11-09)

In `simulate_two_endpoints`, all per-cycle scan noise is drawn before `policy.reset` receives the same generator. Missing-scan uniforms use a deterministic child seed derived from the evaluation seed. Thus paired arms on the same virtual patients and evaluation seed receive common exogenous scan and missingness realizations, even if their policy reset consumes different numbers of random values. The focused regression test passed, then the complete `tests/test_trackc_policy_benchmark.py` module passed in a scratch copy (21 passed; Python 3.14).

After reset, stochastic policy internals continue consuming the generator. Different particle-filter resampling paths can therefore desynchronize later internal draws between arms. This defines the policy-level pairing and does not change each arm's marginal simulation law, but it can affect the variance of the paired world contrast. Keep the coupling fixed and include it in outcome-blind inference validation; do not treat within-world policy randomness as extra independent worlds. No policy outcomes or patient data were used.

#### Betting-interval feasibility check (2026-11-09)

The hedged betting confidence interval (HBCI) is a legitimate candidate for a fixed-time mean interval when each family's world-level differences are independent, identically distributed, and bounded. The published method adapts to observed variance and asymmetry and can be substantially narrower than Hoeffding on favorable data. That does not make it a route around the project's all-zero-sample lower bound.

With seven worlds per family and Bonferroni allocation `alpha = 0.025` per contrast, choose distributions supported on `{-790, 0, +790}` with a rare `+790` or `-790` event. As its probability approaches `1 - 0.025^(1/7)`, the all-zero sample remains just more likely than 2.5%, while the two alternative means approach `+323.6` and `-323.6` days. Any deterministic interval with the required uniform per-family coverage must include both means on that identical all-zero sample; its half-width is therefore at least about 323.6 days there. Even at 95% marginal coverage without the Bonferroni penalty, the corresponding lower bound is 275.1 days. Thus an HBCI cannot guarantee a 14-day half-width over the declared bounded distribution class at current seed capacity. It might produce a narrower interval for some realized data, but that would not establish the predeclared uniform precision guarantee.

The HBCI literature assumes a bounded-mean sampling model; it does not validate the interval for this project's simulator, population law, or simultaneous two-family rule. The 2026 RQMC study uses independent randomized-QMC replicates and reports method performance for its integration settings; its RQMC gains do not transfer automatically to the current iid-world design or to this thresholded, capped simulator utility. No HBCI or RQMC interval was implemented or screened, and no policy outcomes were generated. Reconsider only after a defensible change in available independent worlds or a predeclared, scientifically justified tighter support/structural assumption is independently reviewed; do not add assumptions because they make the interval pass.
