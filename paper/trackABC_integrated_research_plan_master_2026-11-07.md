# Research Plan

## Project Title

**Integrating Tumor Biology and Longitudinal MRI for Glioblastoma Progression Modeling and In-Silico Therapy Optimization**

## Rationale

Glioblastoma varies across tumor regions and over time. Single-cell RNA sequencing can measure molecular patterns, while serial MRI and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps can describe radiographic change. Mathematical models can represent tumor growth, treatment suppression, and resistant populations. Standard care includes radiotherapy and temozolomide (Stupp et al., 2005). Prior studies have already combined tumor-growth models, imaging, molecular features, digital twins, and treatment scheduling (Akbari et al., 2016; Bhattacharya et al., 2025; Chaudhuri et al., 2023; Ebrahimi Zade et al., 2020; Q. Liu et al., 2025; Zimmer et al., 2025; Z. Liu et al., 2025). This project will not claim that broad multimodal treatment modeling is new or that its method is best.

The three tracks will connect through one virtual-patient simulation, not through patient matching. Track A will contribute a fixed summary of an exploratory mixed-cell expression classifier. Track B will contribute only aggregate MRI summaries that the accepted data-use terms permit. Track C will represent tumor growth, treatment suppression, and resistance mathematically. The available cohorts do not share patient identifiers, and neither Track A labels nor Track B MRI labels measure drug resistance directly. Every connection among molecular-like signals, MRI-like signals, and simulated resistance will therefore be an explicit assumption.

The study will test a narrow methodological question: whether a controller that allows residual dependence between imperfect molecular-like and MRI-like signals can improve simulated treatment utility over a matched controller that assumes conditional independence. Fresh synthetic worlds will include informative, uninformative, weak, reversed, and misspecified signal relationships. Scientifically, the comparison will test how uncertainty about signal dependence affects a mathematical treatment policy. Its possible future societal value will be to guide what should be validated next; this simulation will not recommend or select treatment for any person. A positive result will apply only to the stated simulator and assumptions. A null or harmful result will also be reported.

## Research Question, Hypotheses, Engineering Goals, and Expected Outcomes

### Research Questions

**Central question:** Across fresh, independently generated synthetic glioblastoma-like worlds, will a finite-set dependence-aware treatment controller achieve higher prespecified robust utility than a matched finite-set conditional-independence controller, when both receive the same molecular-like and MRI-like observations and have the same treatment options?

1. How will the paired utility difference change when the simulated signals are informative, uninformative, weakly related, reversed, or misspecified?
2. How will the utility and course-use tradeoff change across the prespecified signal and tumor-response scenarios?
3. How sensitive will the policy ranking be to assumed drug suppression, resistance, scan loss, observation noise, and the cost assigned to modeled treatment courses?

### Hypotheses

1. **Primary simulation hypothesis:** In the predeclared correctly specified nonzero-dependence scenarios, the dependence-aware controller will have higher equal-world mean robust utility than its matched conditional-independence controller. This hypothesis may fail.
2. **Negative-control hypothesis:** When both channels are uninformative (`q_signal = 0.5` and `a = b = d = 0`) or their assumed relationship is reversed, multimodal fusion will not consistently outperform the no-signal or single-signal controls. The project will report cases where fusion harms performance.

### Engineering Goals

1. Verify the provenance, hashes, definitions, and permitted use of fixed Track A and Track B summaries without reopening expression or patient image and mask values.
2. Build one auditable synthetic pipeline with separate molecular-like and MRI-like observation channels, sensitive and resistant tumor compartments, treatment suppression, and constrained policies.
3. Compare policies on identical virtual worlds, signal draws, observation-noise streams, scan schedules, and course budgets.
4. Separate development, calibration, and evaluation data and reserve fresh evaluation-world seeds before outcome scoring.
5. Report prior patient analyses separately from the new simulation and make no patient-specific treatment, causal, or clinical-benefit claim.

### Expected Outcomes

The study will estimate where dependence-aware fusion helps, ties, or harms under the declared simulator. It may show a conditional advantage, no measurable advantage, or worse performance. The result will not establish a validated resistance biomarker, clinical treatment benefit, or superiority over methods that were not compared fairly.

## Procedures

1. **Verify permissions and protect the data boundary.** I will retain the written Duke access terms and the school and competition approval conditions. I will verify that each planned use of an aggregate or derivative is allowed by the accepted agreement. I will not upload restricted files or share restricted outputs. If the agreement does not permit a Duke-derived summary, I will omit it and use only prespecified synthetic ranges. I will not reopen patient images, masks, ADC maps, or patient-level files for this study.

2. **Verify the fixed Track A and Track B inputs.** I will check each permitted result record’s provenance, hashes, cohort definition, and metric meaning. Track A labels will remain author-supplied MRI-marked sampling-point labels; they will not be called malignant-cell truth, invasion, or a resistance assay. Track B outcomes have already been examined and will be treated as exploratory context, not unseen validation. If a record fails its provenance or rights check, I will omit it and record the reason.

3. **Generate separate synthetic signal channels.** I will represent Track A with a hypothetical binary molecular-like report. I will name its assumed report accuracy `q_signal` so it cannot be confused with the responder fraction in the tumor-response mixture. The six archived A5 donor-level balanced-accuracy values, in donor order GS1, GS2, GS3, GS5, GS6, and GS13, will be 0.7485868503, 0.6894338381, 0.4587969245, 0.8339715268, 0.4996343158, and 0.8413786704. The candidate model set will use these values plus `q_signal = 0.5`, with duplicate values removed. These values will define stress-test settings only; they will not be treated as measured accuracy for a resistance assay. For the primary evaluation, I will draw one `q_signal` value uniformly from the six A5 values for each world and hold it fixed across that world's virtual patients. I will evaluate a full no-information control at `q_signal = 0.5` and `a = b = d = 0`; I will separately evaluate `q_signal = 0.5` at the primary anchor to test an uninformative molecular channel while the MRI-like channel remains informative. A5 did not measure resistance or validate a clinical assay. The latent molecular-like state will be simulation-only, not a tumor-core label or a malignant-cell diagnosis.

   For each virtual patient, I will map the simulated resistant fraction `f0` to a standardized score using fixed log-uniform reference bounds `L = 0.001 × exp(−0.7)` and `U = 0.9`:

   - `ZR = normal_quantile(clip((ln(f0) − ln(L)) / (ln(U) − ln(L)), 1e−8, 1−1e−8))`.
   - `ZB = a × ZR + sqrt(1 − a²) × EB`.
   - `ZI = b × ZR + sqrt(1 − b²) × (d × EB + sqrt(1 − d²) × EI)`.

   Here, `EB` and `EI` will be independent standard-normal draws. The synthetic latent molecular-like state will be `C = 1[ZB ≥ 0]`; the observed report will flip that state with probability `1 − q_signal`. The MRI-like baseline observation will be the continuous score `ZI`, optionally shifted and scaled using only an authorized history-only template. If no permitted template exists, I will use the standard-normal score. Later MRI-like volume readings will be generated from the simulated burden and scan noise; they will not be Duke patient scans. ADC will not enter the policy unless a separate verified aggregate input is allowed and adopted by amendment. Coefficients `a`, `b`, and `d` will be simulation factors, not measured correlations. Setting `d = 0` will represent conditional independence of the signal errors given `ZR`; it will not imply marginal independence. Track A and Track B people will never be joined.
4. **Represent tumor growth and treatment suppression.** I will use two normalized tumor compartments: sensitive burden `S` and resistant burden `R`, with total burden `B = S + R` and carrying capacity normalized to 1. The model will use the project's two-population equations:

   - `dS/dt = r × S × [1 − (S + a_rs × R)] − m × r × u × S − μ × u × S + ν × (1 − u) × R`.
   - `dR/dt = r × (1 − c) × R × [1 − (a_sr × S + R)] − ε × m × r × u × R + μ × u × S − ν × (1 − u) × R`.

   Initial conditions will be `S(0) = (1 − f0) × B0` and `R(0) = f0 × B0`. The primary competition settings will use `a_rs = a_sr = 1`. I will generate each world by Latin-hypercube sampling over these fixed world axes: `log_r_shift` from −0.7 to 0.9; `log_f0_shift` from −0.7 to 2.3; `B0_center` from 0.12 to 0.55; and `c` from 0 to 0.25. In the single-response family, `log_m_scale` will range from `ln(0.05)` to 0. In the mixture-response family, `log_m_scale_non` will range from `ln(0.02)` to `ln(0.30)`, and the assumed responder fraction `π_response` will range from 0.1 to 0.6. Each world will also use `μ` and `ν` levels of 0, 0.01, or 0.04 day⁻¹, assigned with equal weight across the world design. These levels will be scenario assumptions, not estimates from patient data. Within a world, I will draw virtual-patient parameter vectors by Latin hypercube from `r = 0.001–0.05` day⁻¹ and `m = 6–20` on log scales, multiply the relevant range by its world scale, draw `f0 = 0.001–0.1` on a log scale and multiply by `exp(log_f0_shift)`, with an upper cap of 0.9, and draw `B0` uniformly from `B0_center ± 0.10`. In mixture worlds, a separate random stream will assign each simulated patient to the responder class with probability `π_response`; responders will use the unscaled `m = 6–20` range, while nonresponders will use the world-scaled weak range. The families will share the other parameter ranges and will be reported separately. I will draw `ε` from 0–0.2 and assign observation noise `σ` from 0.05, 0.10, and 0.20 in balanced proportions. These are simulation assumptions, not patient or drug measurements.

   I will integrate the equations with the project's fourth-order Runge–Kutta method at a 0.25-day step and compare against a 0.05-day step before policy evaluation. Each cycle will span 28 days. At each cycle, a policy will choose either no course or one modeled course. A course will apply `u = 1` for the first 5 days and `u = 0` otherwise. The simulator horizon will be 1,456 days, with a maximum of 12 modeled courses. These are simulation actions, not dosing instructions. Scheduled noisy log-volume readings will follow `Y_j = ln(B(t_j)) + Normal(0, σ²)`; the policy will receive only readings scheduled to be visible.
5. **Construct matched treatment policies.** I will use the fixed finite-set dependence-aware minimax controller as the primary candidate and a matched finite-set `d = 0` minimax controller as the primary comparator. The model set will contain every unique combination of `q_signal` values (the six A5 values plus 0.5) and `a`, `b`, and `d` values in `{−0.5, 0, 0.5}`; before duplicate removal it will contain at most 189 models. The comparator will use the same `q_signal`, `a`, and `b` support with `d` fixed to zero. Each model will maintain its own particle filter, initialized from a shared prior particle cloud and updated with the same molecular-like report, MRI-like score, and visible log-volume readings. The filter prior will use independent parameter draws: log-uniform `r` from 0.001 to 0.05 day⁻¹, `m` from 0.1 to 20, and `f0` from `0.001 × exp(−0.7)` to 0.9; uniform `c` from 0 to 0.25, `ε` from 0 to 0.2, and `B0` from 0.05 to 0.5. At each scheduled scan, each controller will score all eight treatment/no-treatment patterns over the next three 28-day cycles, repeat each pattern over a six-cycle (168-day) prediction window with no terminal value after that window, and choose the pattern with the highest worst-case mean utility across its model set. It will apply the first three-cycle pattern and replan at the next scan. The controller will use 500 particles per model and 16 common stratified posterior prediction draws per model; ties will favor the first pattern in a fixed ordering that prioritizes fewer treatment courses. The intended difference between the primary controllers will be the inclusion of nonzero residual-dependence models in the candidate set.

   I will report the no-signal controller, development-selected fixed schedules, simple scan-based controls, and burden-only Q-learning as secondary references. The Q-learning policy will not be described as a same-information comparator because it will not receive the same molecular-like and MRI-like inputs. Leave-one-out model-averaged signal policies will remain descriptive because their weighting uses other evaluation patients' baseline signals. I will report the single-response and mixture-response simulator families separately.

   The primary treatment-stop rule will use noisy scan readings every 84 days. Treatment will stop after two consecutive observed readings reach at least 1.4 times the lowest earlier observed volume, with a floor of 0.10 times the observed starting volume. A missed scheduled scan will reset confirmation. This will be a synthetic observation rule, not formal RANO. Policies will receive observed signals and the declared observation-noise level, not hidden tumor states or true parameters. Scan intervals of 28, 56, and 112 days and a 10% independent scheduled-scan-missingness scenario will be reported as secondary sensitivities; the missingness scenario will affect only visible scheduled scans and will be specified identically across policies. In the missingness sensitivity, the MPC will use the same fixed 10% future-scan-loss assumption and common missingness draws across candidate action patterns; the primary complete-follow-up analysis will use a 0% assumption.

6. **Separate development, calibration, and evaluation.** Within each world, I will create disjoint synthetic patient cohorts for model selection, course-use calibration, and evaluation. The evaluation-world seeds, patient-cohort seeds, signal seeds, policy seeds, and schedule-mixture seeds will be distinct and recorded in a sealed manifest. I will use the 27 combinations of `a`, `b`, and `d` in `{−0.5, 0, 0.5}` for development. The full no-information negative control will use `q_signal = 0.5` and `a = b = d = 0`. The primary evaluation anchor will be `(a, b, d) = (0.5, 0.5, 0.5)` in both response families, with `q_signal` drawn from the six A5 values as specified above. I will separately evaluate `q_signal = 0.5` at that primary anchor as an uninformative molecular-channel control; the MRI-like channel will remain informative there. Secondary out-of-grid anchors will be `(0.75, 0.75, 0.75)`, `(0.75, 0.75, −0.75)`, `(−0.75, 0.75, 0.50)`, `(0.50, −0.75, −0.50)`, and `(−0.75, −0.75, 0.75)`. I will report each anchor separately rather than pool assumptions for a favorable mean.

   Before any evaluation outcome is generated, I will implement and test a driver that atomically reserves distinct seeds, enforces patient-cohort separation, runs every predeclared primary world and comparator, and records all outputs and failures. An outcome-blind timing and precision pilot will determine whether the fresh seed and runtime capacity can support the planned inference. The current registry audit supports at most 14 six-scenario-seed world blocks before checking the five policy seeds per world and before allocating multiple response families and secondary settings; this is not a justified sample size. The primary evaluation will proceed only if a fresh audit can reserve all required seeds and the precision target is met using development worlds. If not, I will report feasibility only and make no superiority claim. Previously exposed development seeds or outcomes will not be reused as confirmation.
7. **Check model calibration and numerical behavior.** Before interpreting policy differences, I will run the frozen numerical convergence checks and compare simulated summaries only with published aggregate targets that have a compatible population, time origin, endpoint, and treatment definition. RTOG 0525 progression-free survival begins at randomization and includes progression or death (Gilbert et al., 2013), while this simulator begins at a modeled treatment course and has no death process; its median will not be treated as a direct calibration target. GEINO 14-01 is a post-cycle-six landmark comparison (Balaña et al., 2020) and will be used only in a separately defined landmark sensitivity. I will preserve known calibration failures. If no compatible calibration gate can be passed, I may still report a conditional mathematical comparison, but I will not claim clinical calibration or therapy benefit.

8. **Freeze analysis before evaluation outcomes.** Before generating evaluation outcomes, I will record the exact anchors, response families, cohort sizes, world count, precision target, runtime cap, treatment-stop rule, scan-loss settings, inference method, resampling seed, multiplicity family, code and protocol hashes, and reserved seed list. I will validate the multi-world driver and interval procedure on synthetic records with known contrasts before applying them to evaluation worlds. I will not start evaluation if any required choice, code path, or seed reservation remains unresolved. I will report failed fits, fallbacks, missing arms, and all negative results without changing settings after evaluation.

## Risk Assessment

The new phase will use computer simulation and fixed, permitted aggregate records. It will not recruit or contact people, administer treatment, or provide clinical recommendations. Main risks will be misuse of restricted data, disclosure of sensitive patient information, and readers mistaking a simulation for medical evidence. I will keep Duke materials in access-controlled local storage, follow the exact accepted agreement, and share only outputs allowed by that agreement. No new patient image, mask, or ADC value will be read under this plan. Simulated treatment suppression will represent mathematical assumptions, not medical instructions or evidence of patient benefit. Long computations will run on a ventilated surface with the computer connected to power.

## Data Analysis

### Fixed Track A and Track B inputs

I will report the archived Track A and Track B results only as prior exploratory evidence, with their original denominators and limitations. The A5 result will retain its failed five-of-six donor rule. Its mixed-cell expression and MRI-marked labels will not be interpreted as malignant-cell truth or as evidence of resistance. The Duke cohort and target outcomes have already been examined; I will not calculate a new patient-level estimate or claim confirmation from them. I will use permitted aggregate summaries only to define clearly labeled synthetic scenario ranges.

### Primary in-silico comparison

For each policy's simulated treatment path, I will calculate two latent-burden progression times through 730 days: (1) the interpolated time when true burden first reaches 1.2 times its starting burden, and (2) the first scheduled scan when true burden reaches 1.4 times the larger of the prior true-burden scan nadir or 0.10 times starting burden. The second is a latent-burden, scan-sampled RANO-like mathematical proxy, not a clinical RANO assessment (Ellingson et al., 2017). The two noisy observed readings will determine the treatment-stop trigger; they will not replace these latent progression outcomes. For each policy, I will define utility as the smaller of those two progression times, each restricted at 730 days, minus five simulated days per course delivered through day 730. The five-day penalty will be a declared model preference and will be tested at 0 and 10 days per course.

The primary contrast will be the paired utility difference between the finite-set dependence-aware controller and the matched `d = 0` controller. Within each world, I will average paired patient differences; I will then weight independent worlds equally. The primary family will contain the primary signal anchor in both response families. Before evaluation, I will resample the independent worlds with replacement 10,000 times. For each resample and each primary contrast, I will calculate the studentized deviation from the observed world-mean contrast using that contrast's bootstrap standard deviation. I will use the 95th percentile of the maximum absolute studentized deviation across the two response-family contrasts as the simultaneous critical value and form max-T intervals. I will validate this procedure on synthetic records with known effects before the evaluation run and freeze the bootstrap seed in the protocol. The target will be a 95% interval half-width of at most 14 simulated days for each primary contrast; this is a Monte Carlo precision target, not a clinically meaningful difference. World count and precision will be set using separate development worlds, without using evaluation outcomes. If the seed capacity or precision target cannot be met, I will report feasibility only. A positive result will require both adjusted lower confidence bounds to exceed zero. If precision fails or any required arm is missing, I will report the comparison as inconclusive or feasibility-only. If model calibration is insufficient, I may still report a within-simulator comparison but will not claim clinical calibration or therapy benefit.

I will report both progression times, treatment-course counts, censoring, per-world differences, anchor-specific results, and all sensitivity results. I will show independent, weak, reversed, and misspecified signal relationships separately. The independent uncertainty unit will be the generated world, not the nested virtual patient, scan, or noise draw. I will make no patient-benefit, causal, or generalization claim.

## Additional Project Information

This will be a continuation project. The new procedures above will be prospective. The following completed work will be disclosed as prior work and will not be presented as newly performed.

### Track A: molecular and spatial-expression analyses

The project previously analyzed Yu et al. 2020 (GSE117891) using six frozen donors: GS1, GS2, GS3, GS5, GS6, and GS13. The A5 pipeline used exact metadata joins, a minimum of 1,000 UMIs and 500 detected genes per cell, training-fold-only feature processing, and nested leave-one-donor-out validation. The candidate had lower mean class-balanced AURC than L2 logistic regression (0.1479 versus 0.1764) but won in only four of six donors, so it failed the frozen five-of-six rule. Its mean Brier score was worse (0.2335 versus 0.1944); removing the gene gate produced nearly the same mean AURC (0.1473). The analysis involved mixed tumor and peritumoral cells with author-supplied MRI-marked point labels, not malignant-cell-only ground truth. The eight-donor sensitivity run was not performed.

Earlier random-cell validation showed donor leakage: patient-level balanced accuracy was 0.638, close to the cells-per-patient control at 0.646. A seven-donor composition audit found that region was confounded with library and cell composition. A sealed same-cell-type analysis and candidate-model controls did not establish malignant-cell-specific prediction. Three sealed secondary analyses of SenMayo senescence, OXPHOS, and TAM activation failed their adjusted success rules. Calibration curves, within-library analysis, scANVI, and attention analysis remain deferred and were not run in A5. Spatial and public-cohort screens did not identify a cohort passing all donor-count, region-map, label, reuse-term, and independence gates. Track A will make no external-confirmation claim. The local Table S1 marker records no reported IDH1 R132 mutation for four additional donors but does not establish comprehensive IDH1/2-wildtype status.

### Track B: longitudinal MRI analyses

The project previously built synthetic-only MU and LUMIERE pipelines, timing pilots, fixed-growth and learned-baseline comparators, and a guarded LUMIERE runner. No MU or LUMIERE patient masks were scored. LUMIERE source-label, support, and derived-output questions remained unresolved, so LUMIERE will not contribute patient outcomes to the present simulation. Prior-art screening identified related PDE-based digital-twin and learned tumor-forecasting work, including SoC-DT and PREDICT-GBM (Bhattacharya et al., 2025; Zimmer et al., 2025); the project will not claim broad PDE-plus-learned-model integration as new.

A Duke patient-mask analysis was completed on 2026-11-04. It used a 10-mm prior-FLAIR dilation, excluded regions within 10 mm of the current core, and summarized three distance bands. The recorded primary estimate was −2.2 × 10⁻⁶, with a 95% interval from −0.003675 to 0.004071. Destination-label, label-0, distance, interval, and registration summaries were also examined after target access. These outcomes are exposed exploratory evidence, not untouched confirmation. A preliminary audit found that target voxels outside the resampled field of view were set to zero; the effect of this coverage issue is unknown. A separate ADC preflight is marked incomplete and does not establish the status of every possible ADC analysis. The current Duke cohort and outcomes will not be rescored or retuned in this plan. ### Track C: mathematical simulation and treatment-policy work

The project previously developed a two-population growth and treatment-suppression simulator, particle-filter methods, fixed and adaptive treatment policies, signal-generation prototypes, and synthetic benchmark code. Earlier simulations produced mixed or null policy comparisons. Scan-responsive policies often did not exceed the best fixed schedule at equal course use; oracle policies sometimes performed better but relied on hidden true parameters and were not feasible controllers. Results depended on the progression endpoint, response family, and assumed parameter ranges. These were synthetic development findings, not policy-benefit results; they motivate testing a finite-set minimax controller instead of relying on a single fitted dependence value. Signal-action and policy-mechanics tests did not establish treatment benefit. No sealed Stage 3 evaluation was authorized.
