# Companion Protocol: Three GBM Computational Studies

This protocol will define the technical boundaries for the research plan. Track A will evaluate donor-held-out molecular classification, Track B will evaluate patient-level PDE tumor-mask forecasts, and Track C will compare policies in virtual scenarios. The tracks will use separate datasets and evaluation units. No procedure will join them into a patient-level model or support a clinical-benefit claim.

## 1. Prior exposure, data terms, and shared controls

Before each analysis, I will create or update a source manifest and a prior-exposure manifest. They will record dataset release, source files and checksums, labels, donor or patient identifiers, prior code and evaluation-set use, access terms, and permitted processing locations. If prior identifiers or split membership cannot be reconstructed, I will treat the affected cohort as previously examined. Any reused donors, patients, scenarios, or evaluation targets will remain exploratory, even if I calculate a new metric or create a new split.

I will verify file-specific reuse terms, label meanings, and source lineage before using the UCSC Cell Browser export. I will follow the TCIA attribution and data-use terms for MU-Glioma-Post. Public availability or matching image headers will not count as proof of valid labels or longitudinal registration. I will use the restricted Duke collection only after its access permission, school-use approval, and all data and protocol gates are documented. CFB-GBM and UCSD-PTGBM will remain separate exploratory annotation-change work and will not count as Track B PDE evidence.

Before opening an evaluation target, I will freeze that track's inclusion rules, primary outcome, comparator, data split, code version, random seeds, and failure rules. I will use the donor, patient, or simulated parameter scenario as the independent unit. I will keep repeated cells, scans, noise streams, and particles grouped within that unit. I will calculate paired, two-sided 95% bootstrap intervals using 10,000 resamples at the appropriate independent-unit level. I will label secondary comparisons exploratory and adjust their p-values with Holm's method. I will not describe any project threshold as a clinical minimum important difference.

I will plan computation for an 8-core, 31.6-GiB computer without a GPU. I will profile development-only workloads before the final runs and will use sparse or cropped arrays where the model permits. I will keep medical data local and will not transfer them to Kaggle or another cloud service unless the source terms and school review explicitly allow it.

## 2. Track A protocol: donor-held-out molecular classification

### 2.1 Source and eligibility

I will consider the UCSC Cell Browser multiomic-gbm release only after I verify the exact files, file-specific reuse terms, label meanings, assay and library records, and source-backed sample-to-donor identity. I will use source-labeled GBM Core and GBM Periphery cells as the primary target. I will exclude cells with unresolved target labels or donor identity, and I will not infer donor aliases from filenames unless the source documentation supports that mapping.

Before fitting, I will count eligible donors with both classes. Fewer than six two-class donors will trigger a feasibility stop. Six to nine will permit descriptive nested evaluation only; I will make no inferential superiority claim with fewer than ten. If class is perfectly confounded with donor, library, or batch, I will stop the biological prediction claim and report the confounding as a feasibility limit. I will not score spatial transfer unless I first establish independent spatial labels, specimen-to-donor lineage, and permitted use.

### 2.2 Model and evaluation

I will create outer leave-one-donor-out folds and inner donor-grouped folds. Every cell from a donor will remain in one fold. I will fit normalization, gene filtering, feature selection, and regularization choices using training donors only. The candidate will be one sparse logistic classifier with equal total training weight per donor. The comparator will be ordinary logistic regression using the same folds and a fixed, small regularization-selection budget.

I will calculate balanced accuracy separately for each outer held-out donor and will use the equal-weight donor mean as the primary outcome. I will estimate the paired difference from ordinary logistic regression by resampling donors, not cells. I will report per-donor values, fold sizes, exclusions, and the complete inclusion flow. Cell-type-only and library/batch-only models will serve as leakage diagnostics. If the atlas's earlier project use is confirmed by the exposure manifest, every result on that atlas will remain exploratory.

I will call the comparison inconclusive if the paired interval includes no difference. A failed license, identity, label, class-confounding, or donor-count gate will stop the model comparison; I will report the audit and feasibility limit instead of adding model variants.

## 3. Track B protocol: isotropic reaction-diffusion forecast

### 3.1 Cohort, target, and access gates

I will treat each patient as one evaluation unit and keep all of that patient's visits within the same outer fold. I will use the first two eligible masks as history and the immediate third mask as the target. I will issue a forecast at day 90 after the second history scan and include only patients whose third scan falls 76–104 days after that origin. I will report the actual scan interval and call this a retrospective nominal 90-day target-window forecast; I will not describe it as an exact-day forecast or as a schedule-known-at-origin prediction.

After source review, I will define the target as the union of labels 1 and 3 and call it the source-defined tumor core, not whole-tumor extent or biological growth. Before I access target-mask voxel arrays for scoring, I will require source-backed evidence that (a) longitudinal registration quality passes for every selected patient, (b) zero is a valid negative label, (c) the target-support definition covers the entire target image grid, and (d) each image and mask has matching geometry. I will define the origin-only model domain separately from target scoring support; I will not infer valid target negatives from MRI intensity, image headers, or a target-derived crop. Missing or contradictory source evidence will stop scoring before I open target masks.

I will use the 18-patient MU metadata-selected cohort defined by the current inclusion rules only if the source, date, mask, and support checks pass again. I will classify MU development and evaluation as exploratory because prior cohort exposure will be documented in the exposure manifest. The Duke longitudinal collection will be the only candidate external patient test. Before any Duke target scoring, I will require documented access and school approval; source-backed tumor-mask meaning, registration, and full-grid support; patient-overlap review; matching image/mask geometry; and a verified visit sequence with at least three chronologically ordered mask visits per patient. For each patient, I will use the first two visits as history and the immediate third visit as target, and I will include the patient in the external sample-size estimate only when the second-to-third interval falls within 76–104 days and the metadata gates pass. I will estimate the expected eligible Duke sample size using metadata only. I will resample paired MU development Dice differences 10,000 times at that expected sample size. A projected 95% interval half-width above 0.05 Dice will make the Duke analysis descriptive only and will rule out a confirmatory external claim. I will treat this as a rough feasibility proxy based on MU paired-difference variability, not proof that the external Duke variability will match MU. The 0.05 value will be a project precision target, not a clinical threshold.

### 3.2 PDE posterior and hybrid forecast

I will use the isotropic Fisher–Kolmogorov equation ∂c/∂t = D∇²c + ρc(1−c), with diffusion tensor D times the identity matrix. The first history mask will initialize the concentration field; both history masks, and no target mask, will enter the patient-specific fit. On a 2-mm grid, I will fit ρ from 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻¹ per day and D from 10⁻⁵ to 10 mm²/day under fixed log-uniform marginal priors. I will evaluate a 21-by-21 coarse log grid, refine the region containing 90% of the coarse posterior mass on an 11-by-11 grid, and draw 200 joint posterior pairs. I will fit signed-distance residuals at 256 deterministic surface anchors with a normalized multivariate Gaussian likelihood. Its covariance will be Σₖₗ = σᵦ² exp(−dₖₗ/ℓ) + τᵦ²I, where dₖₗ will be the distance between anchors. I will report posterior ridges and parameter-bound mass. If ρ and D are not separately identifiable, I will avoid biological interpretation of the separate parameters while still reporting forecast performance if all forecast gates pass.

I will estimate the population log-volume slope, volume-trend shrinkage time constant, and horizon-dependent log-volume variance from training patients only. I will select the shrinkage time constant from 30, 90, 180, and 365 days using training-only validation. I will estimate variance as a + b·h from training-only residuals and will draw positive future volumes from the resulting lognormal distribution. For each posterior draw, I will forecast PDE shape from the second history mask through day 90, match the shape to the corresponding volume draw, and apply correlated boundary uncertainty estimated from training-only residuals. I will average the 200 binary forecast draws voxel by voxel to form an inclusion-probability map. Inside validated support, I will include voxels with probability at least 0.5 in the hybrid point mask. I will form the no-PDE point mask by ranking its inclusion probabilities within the same support to match the hybrid point-mask voxel count; I will break ties by ascending flat voxel index. I will use these locked point masks for Dice scoring.

I will repeat the primary MU comparison with one-decade-narrower bounds (ρ: 10⁻⁴–10⁻² per day; D: 10⁻⁴–1 mm²/day) and one-decade-wider bounds (ρ: 10⁻⁶–1 per day; D: 10⁻⁶–100 mm²/day). I will preserve the same eligible patients, patient folds, and random seeds across the primary and bound-sensitivity runs. I will also refit the primary analysis on only patients whose second-to-third interval is strictly greater than 78 and strictly less than 102 days, excluding edge intervals of 76–78 and 102–104 days from all outer and inner fitting. I will report these as sensitivities, not as alternative primary analyses. If exclusions leave fewer than five patients or remove an outer fold, I will report the central-window refit as infeasible rather than alter its window or folds.

Within each of five outer patient folds, I will calibrate boundary uncertainty using five inner patient-grouped folds and held-out forecast residuals. I will begin from the prespecified numerical initialization σᵦ=0.8 mm, τᵦ=0.3 mm, and ℓ=6 mm, then select ℓ from 5, 10, or 20 mm using training-fold residuals only. The starting values will be computational initial values, not measured truth. Boundary calibration will require at least eight training patients and a non-boundary length-scale selection. If any fold's calibration is unavailable, requires a persistence fallback, or selects a search-boundary length scale, I will retain the affected patients in the cohort accounting and will report the fallback and calibration failure. I will not make a positive PDE superiority claim from that run.

### 3.3 Comparators, scores, and failure rules

The primary comparator will be a no-PDE signed-distance volume ranker using the same forecast volume draws and boundary covariance as the PDE hybrid. Persistence and a deterministic geometric-train-rate forecast will be secondary controls. I will select any comparator settings using MU development patients only and will freeze them before any Duke targets will be opened. Every method will use the same patients, target masks, forecast horizon, and target-support mask.

Patient-macro Dice will be the primary geometry outcome. I will score both-empty masks as 1 and exactly one empty mask as 0. I will report paired patient-level differences and 10,000 patient-bootstrap resamples. Surface distance and absolute volume error will be secondary outcomes, with Holm-adjusted secondary comparisons. I will include every eligible patient in the cohort flow and will report empty masks, failed fits, forecasts reaching the computational-domain edge, boundary-draw collapses, and persistence fallbacks. Forecasts that reach the computational-domain edge will not be clipped into an apparently valid mask. An inconclusive interval, calibration failure, or unmet source/access gate will not support a positive claim. Even a favorable approved Duke comparison will support only the locked cohort comparison, not clinical validity.

## 4. Track C protocol: simulation-only treatment-policy comparison

### 4.1 Simulator and assumptions

I will simulate sensitive and resistant burdens S and R, total burden B=S+R, and treatment indicator u in {0,1} using these equations:

dS/dt = rS[1−(S+a_rsR)]−mruS

dR/dt = r(1−c)R[1−(a_srS+R)]−εmruR

The base competition coefficients will be a_rs=a_sr=1, and the carrying capacity will be normalized to 1. I will draw r=0.001–0.05 per day, m=6–20, initial resistant fraction f₀=0.001–0.1, resistance cost c=0–0.25, resistant-treatment multiplier ε=0–0.2, and initial burden B₀=0.05–0.5, using the existing log-uniform or uniform parameter rules. I will use observation-noise standard deviations of 0.05, 0.10, and 0.20 on log burden. I will label every parameter and drug-effect value as an assumption, not an empirical measurement.

I will use 28-day decision cycles, treatment on days 0–4 of any assigned cycle, a shared maximum of 12 courses, and a 52-cycle (1,456-day) horizon. I will integrate with a 0.25-day fourth-order Runge–Kutta step and refine threshold crossings within a step. I will define simulated time to progression as the first time true burden B reaches 1.2B₀, capped at 1,456 days. I will identify this threshold as a model endpoint, not RANO, a clinical progression rule, or a measured patient outcome.

### 4.2 Scenario sets, policies, and calibration

Before analysis, I will inventory scenario seeds already used in the repository and will treat those scenario sets as development material. With fresh seeds, I will generate 300 development parameter scenarios, 150 filter-calibration scenarios, and 600 held-out parameter scenarios. I will balance the development, calibration, and held-out sets across the three noise levels, giving 100, 50, and 200 scenarios per level, respectively. I will generate two independent observation-noise streams per held-out and calibration scenario and will keep both streams grouped with their parameter scenario. I will use common parameter scenarios and matched noise streams for every policy. I will tune policies only on the development set and reserve the calibration set for interval assessment.

The simple-policy library will include no treatment; periodic treatment with cycle spacing q=1, 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6 and start cycle 0 or 2; the following raw-reading on/off thresholds (a_on, a_off): (0.6,0.3), (0.6,0.4), (0.6,0.5), (0.8,0.3), (0.8,0.5), (0.8,0.7), (0.9,0.5), (1.0,0.3), (1.0,0.5), (1.0,0.7), (1.1,0.7), and (1.2,0.8); and single raw-reading thresholds 0.6, 0.7, 0.75, 0.8, 0.85, 0.9, 0.95, 1.0, 1.05, 1.1, 1.15, and 1.18. For each observation y_j=log B_j+noise, I will define its raw relative-burden reading as m_j=exp(y_j−y₀), with the cycle-zero reading y₀ as the baseline. An on/off policy will start treatment when m_j is at least a_on and will remain on while m_j is above a_off. A single-threshold policy will use the same threshold a for starting and stopping, with no hysteresis. I will select the best simple policy on development scenarios using the same lower-tail objective as the primary comparison.

The resistance-aware candidate will use a 500-particle filter with systematic resampling below half the effective particle count and one Liu–West move with h=0.90. The filter will receive the observation-noise standard deviation as known and will use a Gaussian observation likelihood without process noise. I will evaluate risk-containment thresholds p*=0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, and 0.35 over one- or two-cycle untreated-risk horizons, with a filter prior allowing m=3–20. At each cycle, I will treat when the filter's probability of reaching the model progression threshold within the selected number of untreated cycles is at least p*. I will select its settings using development scenarios only. I will report empirical 90% interval coverage and width for burden and resistant fraction on the separate calibration set. I will treat calibration as descriptive; I will not use it to claim that the filter estimates patient resistance reliably.

### 4.3 Primary outcome, uncertainty, and sensitivities

For each held-out parameter scenario, I will average its two noise-stream time-to-progression outcomes and calculate the lower-tail mean (CVaR at 10%) separately at each noise level. I will calculate the primary score as the minimum of those three noise-specific CVaR values. I will estimate the primary contrast as the resistance-aware policy score minus the score of the development-selected best simple policy. The lower-tail mean will include fractional weight on the cutoff scenario when needed to cover exactly 10% of the scenarios. I will use 10,000 paired bootstrap replicates, resampling scenarios within noise level and preserving both noise streams and policy pairing; each replicate will recalculate all three CVaRs and their minimum. A failed or non-finite simulator run will count as zero days for the primary outcome and will also be reported as a technical failure; I will not drop failed scenarios.

I will report each noise-specific CVaR, median time to progression, fraction capped at the horizon, burden integral, resistant fraction, courses used, and technical failures. I will adjust secondary policy comparisons with Holm's method and will label them exploratory.

I will run three fresh sensitivity sets of 100 scenarios each without policy retuning: 40% missing post-baseline observations; a 5% decline in the true treatment-strength ratio m after each completed course while the policy filter will retain its base model; and changed competition coefficients a_sr=2.0 and a_rs=0.5. In the missing-observation set, I will never remove the cycle-zero baseline; raw-reading policies will carry forward their last reading when a later reading is missing, and the particle filter will skip that measurement update when it is told that the reading is missing. I will describe robustness only over these specified simulated departures. Before opening held-out outcomes, I will time a 100-scenario development pilot. If the projected full run exceeds 12 hours on the planned computer, I will reduce the held-out set once to 300 scenarios (100 at each noise level) based only on runtime and will freeze that choice before evaluation.

## 5. Reporting and interpretation rules

I will describe a prespecified comparison as positive only if its data and protocol gates pass and its paired 95% interval favors the candidate. I will call an interval that includes no difference inconclusive and will report negative findings, failed gates, technical failures, and missing data. Track A and MU Track B analyses will remain exploratory when prior exposure is confirmed. Track C will remain conditional on its mathematical assumptions. No result will support a treatment recommendation, patient-specific benefit, or clinical efficacy claim.

I will not claim that a method is unique because it receives a favorable score. I will make any originality statement only after comparing the exact method components, datasets, and evaluation design with relevant literature. If the literature comparison does not establish novelty, I will describe the work as an application or evaluation study.
