# Track C source-checked prior-art review (2026-11-04)

## Scope and conclusion

This review follows the Track C restart request. It covers named adaptive-therapy work, GBM and other-cancer dosing control, clinical TMZ schedules, response criteria, MGMT-stratified outcomes, GBM imaging models, reinforcement learning, and value-of-information methods. The table gives the source URL opened and separates full text from abstract-only or search-rendered access.

This is a source-checked review, not a systematic review of every dosing method in oncology. Searches focused on the named papers and primary-source papers surfaced by targeted searches and citation trails. No finite search can establish that a method is unique against every existing method. The evidence below does not support a broad novelty claim.

The review does preempt generic novelty claims for adaptive therapy, threshold dosing, particle-filter or belief-state control, MPC, reinforcement learning, and drug-use penalties. A narrower candidate gap remains an inference: a buildable controller for GBM/TMZ evaluated on the same trial-anchored simulated patients under both progression definitions, with scan noise, course use, and paired lower-tail outcomes. No opened source below reports that exact combination. This does not prove novelty.

## Implications for Track C

1. Existing work preempts generic claims for adaptive treatment, dose skipping, threshold feedback, MPC, state estimation, reinforcement learning, and resistance-aware scheduling. Yauney and Shah provide an LGG reinforcement-learning baseline; Zade et al. provide an abstract-level GBM/TMZ Q-learning precedent whose details remain unverified.
2. The closest disease-and-drug match is Segura-Collar et al.; it is an open-loop TMZ schedule study, not scan-based feedback. Delobel et al. add MRI-threshold dose skipping in low-grade glioma. Both belong in the baseline discussion.
3. Browning and Sargun overlap with measurement uncertainty and belief-space control. Adding a particle filter or MPC to Track C is not unique by itself.
4. RANO 2010 and Macdonald criteria use clinical status, steroids, new lesions, and imaging context. Track C's simulated 1.4-times-nadir true-burden call omits these features and is only RANO-like. It must not be called RANO.
5. Test H is simulated perfect-information headroom over 56 schedules. It is not clinical MGMT-assay value. Test K's responder-equals-methylated mapping is an assumption, and no controlled world passes the three MGMT anchors together.
6. Any marker result must report assumed sensitivity and specificity, prevalence, course use, both progression definitions, and paired tail outcomes. It must not imply that MGMT measures the simulator's responder class.
7. A search of this scope cannot show that a method beats all methods in the field. A narrow novelty claim remains unverified until the literature coverage is expanded and every relevant baseline is run on shared simulated patients under the same score.

## Access gaps
