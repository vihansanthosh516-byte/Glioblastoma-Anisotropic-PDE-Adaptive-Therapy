# Track B Research Plan Draft

## Project Title

**Which newly appearing peritumoral FLAIR regions acquire a core label at the next visit? A distance-matched Duke MRI study with serial ADC**

## Rationale

Glioblastoma can extend beyond the contrast-enhancing mass (Akbari et al., 2016; Kwak et al., 2024). T2-FLAIR signal outside the enhancing region can reflect infiltrating tumor, edema, treatment effects, or a mixture. Apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps measure water diffusion and can add a different imaging signal, but neither FLAIR nor ADC identifies tumor cells directly (Hoff et al., 2021; Moore-Palhares et al., 2025). The project will therefore study transitions between MRI labels, not claim to measure cellular invasion.

Prior work already uses peritumoral MRI to estimate infiltration or recurrence location, serial voxelwise FLAIR response mapping, longitudinal radiomics, and serial ADC to study recurrence (Akbari et al., 2016; Hoff et al., 2021; Chougule et al., 2022; Kwak et al., 2024; Moore-Palhares et al., 2025). Jamora et al. (2026) measured serial FLAIR volume and spatial migration during chemoradiation. A particularly close 2026 study mapped follow-up recurrence-prone versus stable subregions back into the baseline peri-enhancing zone, compared FLAIR-positive and FLAIR-negative tissue, and found lower ADC in recurrence-prone FLAIR-positive regions (Bertalan et al., 2026). Therefore, FLAIR change, ADC, and future-region mapping are not novel by themselves. The narrower candidate contribution is a three-visit transition test: classify middle-visit nonenhancing FLAIR as newly appearing or persistent relative to the previous visit, compare those regions within the same patient and physical-distance band from the current core, and measure which receive a core label at the next visit. The ADC trajectory will be a secondary predictor. This is a candidate extension of prior imaging work, not a verified novelty claim; a focused literature and citation review will be completed before public claims. Persistence, resolution, and no-difference outcomes will be reported alongside conversion.

Track B began as a patient-specific tumor-growth forecasting project. Prior work already uses reaction-diffusion forecasts, peritumoral FLAIR, serial ADC, and recurrence-location prediction, so a forecast-only novelty claim would be weak. The student chose this question to make the Duke study ask a more biology-relevant question about the next-visit fate of tissue outside the current core. The candidate contribution is a patient-held-out, distance-matched analysis of regional label transitions across repeated clinical MRI visits, with serial ADC as a second imaging signal and a patient-specific reaction-diffusion forecast as a secondary benchmark. This is potentially informative biology, but the measurements are automated MRI labels rather than tissue samples. A systematic literature and citation review must be completed before any public novelty claim. The project will not claim to be the first. If replicated and later compared with pathology, this type of analysis could help researchers prioritize regions for biological validation; this study alone will not establish tumor-cell infiltration or guide care.

The Duke record describes 61 patients and 503 sessions, with 3–18 sessions per patient, six MRI sequences, automated segmentation labels, relative clinical dates, treatment information, and vessel landmarks for preoperative and recurrence scans. It also states that the released sessions share an atlas field of view but still need longitudinal registration. The record names an nnU-Net segmentation workflow but does not state the exact trained checkpoint version; the masks will therefore be treated as source-provided automated labels, not expert ground truth. These totals do not establish the eligible sample size, and landmark pairs are not available for every consecutive scan pair (Criscuolo et al., 2026; Yang, n.d.; Zhang et al., 2025).

## Research Question, Hypotheses, Engineering Goals, and Expected Outcomes

### Research Question

Among Duke patients whose released records identify glioblastoma and code IDH mutation status as Negative, are newly appearing nonenhancing T2-FLAIR-labeled regions more likely than persistent FLAIR-labeled regions at the same distance from the current core to receive a necrotic- or enhancing-core label at the next visit, and does local ADC trajectory add information about that transition?

### Hypotheses

1. **Primary radiographic tissue-state hypothesis:** within patients and the same physical-distance band from the current core, newly appearing nonenhancing FLAIR tissue will have a higher next-visit core-label conversion rate than persistent FLAIR tissue.
2. **Key secondary imaging hypothesis:** adding current normalized ADC and its change from the previous visit will improve held-out prediction of next-visit core-label conversion over the prespecified clinical-imaging baseline. The expected direction is less ADC increase or a relative ADC decrease in regions that later receive a core label.
3. **Secondary forecasting objective:** a patient-specific reaction-diffusion forecast will be compared with history-only, FLAIR-front, volume-matched, and learned forecasts. This comparison will not determine whether the primary biological hypothesis is supported.

The primary hypothesis will be supported only if the patient-level 95% percentile-bootstrap interval for the distance-standardized conversion-rate difference has a lower bound above zero, at least 35 patients are primary-evaluable, and the interval half-width is no greater than 0.10. With 20–34 evaluable patients, the result will be labeled feasibility even if the interval is positive; fewer than 20 will stop the primary inferential analysis. The ADC hypothesis is secondary and will count as supported only if the patient-bootstrap 95% interval for held-out Brier-score improvement has a lower bound above zero on the same ADC-complete patients for both models. The forecast benchmark is secondary: an overall claim that the candidate outperformed its required comparators will require every simultaneous max-T-adjusted lower confidence bound to exceed zero. Copy-last and the two-history model are included in that seven-comparator family. Neither secondary result can change the primary decision. Patients, not scans or voxels, will be the units of inference. A positive finding will describe a temporal association among automated MRI labels under recorded care; it will not establish histology, causation, treatment response, or clinical benefit.

### Engineering Goals

The project will build and lock a pipeline that will:

1. Identify the same patient across visits and select eligible longitudinal windows using metadata and fixed image-quality rules.
2. Register scans using MRI intensities only, then check alignment with fixed QC rules and released vessel landmarks where matching landmarks exist.
3. Define tissue-state transitions using the released segmentation labels and measure them at the patient level.
4. Keep every window from a patient in one validation fold and fit all population settings on training patients only.
5. Compare the reaction-diffusion forecast with prespecified baselines on the same patients, support, and held-out visits.
6. Record exclusions, image QC, software versions, code and input hashes, model failures, fallbacks, and runtime.

### Expected Outcomes

Newly appearing FLAIR-labeled tissue may convert to a core label more often than persistent FLAIR tissue, may show no difference, or may convert less often. ADC may add predictive information, add none, or perform inconsistently across patients. The reaction-diffusion forecast may outperform all, some, or none of its secondary comparators.

If the primary hypothesis passes, the project will report that newly appearing FLAIR-labeled tissue was associated with a higher rate of subsequent core-label conversion in this Duke cohort after matching by distance from the current core. It will not describe that tissue as proven infiltrating tumor. If the precision condition fails, the result will be described as a feasibility estimate even if its point estimate is positive.

## Procedure

1. **Confirm data use and school review.** Before reading patient images, masks, or landmark coordinates, the student will save the Duke access grant and the exact accepted license or custom agreement. The student will verify whether analysis, patient-level derived values, aggregate results, figures, and model weights may be retained or shared. The student will obtain written school or competition review for this secondary analysis of restricted human MRI. The Zenodo record offers CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 or a custom Duke agreement; the student will follow the exact accepted terms and will not share derived masks, predictions, or weights unless those terms and school review allow them. If either permission does not cover the planned work, analysis will stop before those data are opened.

2. **Verify and protect the archive.** The student will verify the downloaded archive against its expected size and the checksum supplied with the authorized download, record the checksum source, calculate a local SHA-256, and record the archive size and filename. Because the restricted Zenodo API may not expose a file checksum, the student will document whether the checksum can be independently verified from the record. Any mismatch will stop the run. Before reading image voxels, a header-only check will verify that required files are 3D NIfTI, have finite nonsingular affines, report millimeter spatial units, and have positive voxel spacing. Files that fail these checks will be recorded as input failures; no manual repair will be made. The restricted archive, extracted scans, labels, landmark coordinates, patient-level tables, and predictions will remain in access-controlled local storage. None will be committed to a public repository or uploaded to an external service.

3. **Make a metadata-only cohort list.** Before reading any image or label voxels, the student will inspect only permitted metadata: anonymized patient and visit identifiers, session order, relative scan dates, file presence, MRI modalities, diagnosis and IDH fields, surgery dates, radiation and chemotherapy dates, and recurrence-session flags. The primary cohort will include patients whose released Diagnosis field identifies glioblastoma and whose released IDH field is exactly "Negative". Entries marked "Not Provided" or "indeterminate" and records with a conflicting or mixed primary-tumor diagnosis will be excluded from the primary cohort and counted in the metadata flow. "Negative" will mean only the dataset-coded IDH field; without the test scope, it will not be treated as comprehensive IDH1/2 sequencing or independent molecular confirmation. The student will check prior-use overlap where permitted; if person-level overlap cannot be assessed, the study will not be called an independent external confirmation.

   Primary windows will require T1-c, FLAIR, and segmentation labels at all three visits, plus dates and treatment/surgery metadata. ADC at the prior and middle visits will be required only for the secondary ADC analysis; its absence will not alter primary eligibility. T1 and T2, and DWI, will not be read unless a dated amendment gives them a defined role. Sessions will be sorted by numeric relative ScanDate; ScanNumber will be used only to join metadata to archive paths, not as the time order. Every set of three adjacent sessions in date order meeting these criteria will be included; a missing or unusable visit will not be skipped to create a nonconsecutive window. If a date is missing, duplicated, or nonnumeric, or if source metadata leaves chronology unresolved, affected windows will be excluded before image or label reads and reported. Windows will not be chosen based on target-mask appearance or outcome. Relative date values must be finite and unique within a patient; intervals will be calculated after date sorting and must be strictly positive. The source warns that dates may be off by a few days, so actual intervals will be reported as approximate metadata intervals. Radiation and drug-treatment events will be recorded for each interval. They will not be treated as absent, and the study will estimate transitions under observed care rather than untreated natural history. Surgery between the middle and next visits may directly change the core label; it will be reported, and a predeclared descriptive sensitivity will repeat the transition comparison after excluding windows with an intervening resection. The primary comparison matches physical distance but not FLAIR intensity or treatment, so those factors will be described and the association will not be interpreted causally. At least 20 patients must remain after the history-only primary tissue criteria for a feasibility analysis: a nonempty middle-visit core and at least one distance band with at least 10 newly appearing and 10 persistent FLAIR voxels. Fewer than 20 will stop the primary inferential analysis. A confirmatory interpretation of the primary result will additionally require at least 35 primary-evaluable patients and an observed 95% confidence-interval half-width, defined as half the interval width, no greater than 0.10; the planning threshold of 35 targets that precision if the patient-level risk-difference standard deviation is 0.30. This is a planning assumption, not a power guarantee. Fewer than 35 evaluable patients or a wider observed interval will limit the result to feasibility.

4. **Register images and freeze QC.** The dataset guide states that modalities within a session share a registered grid but separate sessions are not longitudinally registered. After separate approval for image reads, the student will rigidly align each previous- and next-visit T1-c image to the middle-visit T1-c image, which is the prediction origin. The resulting visit transform will also be applied to that visit’s other modalities and segmentation label. The student will estimate one rigid moving-to-fixed transform in millimeter physical coordinates defined by the NIfTI affines and centered at the physical center of the fixed middle-visit image. The rotation matrix will be Rz(gamma) x Ry(beta) x Rx(alpha), with alpha, beta, and gamma the x-, y-, and z-axis rotations in degrees; translations will be in x, y, and z millimeters. Resampling will use the inverse transform from each fixed-grid voxel center to the moving image. The objective will be mean-centered normalized cross-correlation over voxels where the fixed middle-visit T1-c image is finite and nonzero. Powell optimization will start at zero transform, first on every-second-voxel downsampling with options xtol = 0.01, ftol = 0.0001, and maxiter = 2,000, then on the full 1 mm grid from the coarse result with xtol = 0.001, ftol = 0.00001, and maxiter = 2,000. Intensities will use linear interpolation; labels and binary masks will use nearest-neighbor interpolation. Outside-field voxels will be zero. No retry or manual adjustment will be allowed.

   A registered visit will pass image QC only if NCC is at least 0.80, the largest absolute Euler angle is at most 10 degrees, and the translation-vector length is at most 10 mm. These are fixed operational thresholds, not clinically validated accuracy limits. Synthetic known-transform tests and released landmark pairs will be used to check the registration code before patient scoring. For pairs with released corresponding vessel landmarks, the student will report target-registration error without using those landmarks to tune the transform or remove individual points. Landmark checks will be reported only for pairs that actually have both scans and coordinates. The next-visit T1-c image may be used only to estimate the alignment needed to score the locked prediction; it will not enter model features, support generation, or tuning. This makes the evaluation retrospective rather than a live prospective forecast. Failed pairs will be excluded before target-label scoring, with reasons recorded. The passing patient/window list, protocol, code, and fold map will be frozen and hashed before any next-visit label voxels are opened. Once frozen, training and inner-validation labels may be read only within their assigned training folds; each outer-test prediction and its settings will be saved before its held-out next-visit label is loaded for outcome scoring.

5. **Define tissue states and the primary transition cohort.** The student will resample registered images to a 2 mm isotropic grid aligned to the middle visit. MRI intensities will be averaged during downsampling, and labels will use nearest-neighbor sampling. The released segmentation labels will be used as documented: labels 1 and 3 will define the operational core, label 2 will define nonenhancing T2-FLAIR abnormality, and label 4 will define resection cavity (Criscuolo et al., 2026; Yang, n.d.). Label 0 and every other value will be outside the operational core; this will not be interpreted as histologically normal tissue.

   For each three-visit window, the prior, middle, and next operational cores will be C₁, C₂, and C₃. F₁ and F₂ will be the label-2 masks at the prior and middle visits. The operational MRI foreground B₂ will be the union of finite, nonzero voxels in the middle-visit T1-c and FLAIR images; it is not a skull-stripped or validated anatomical brain mask. The origin-only scoring support will be S = B₂ union all nonzero voxels from the registered prior- and middle-visit label maps. This support will use history visits only and the same deterministic rule for every forecast method; no next-visit image or label will define S. Target-core voxels outside S will count as misses for every mask forecast. The middle-visit FLAIR-only risk tissue will be R₂ = F₂ outside a 2 mm dilation of the union of C₂ and the cavity. Newly appearing FLAIR tissue will be A₂ = R₂ outside a 2 mm dilation of F₁. Persistent FLAIR tissue will be P₂ = R₂ inside that dilation. These 2 mm margins will be fixed to reduce one-voxel registration and labeling-edge changes.

   Distance bands will use Euclidean distance in millimeters from each voxel center to the nearest C₂ voxel center, calculated with 2 mm isotropic spacing, at 2–10 mm, >10–20 mm, >20–30 mm, and >30 mm. A history-only window will contribute to the primary comparison only if C₂ is nonempty and at least one band contains at least 10 voxels in both A₂ and P₂. This rule will be applied without inspecting the next-visit label. The operational next-visit conversion outcome will be whether each voxel in A₂ or P₂ receives label 1 or 3 at visit 3. Labels 0, 2, and 4 will mean only that no core label was recorded for this endpoint; label 0 is undefined in the source and will not be interpreted as biological absence. The student will report the fraction of A₂ and P₂ receiving each next-visit label separately and repeat the descriptive rate comparison after excluding label-0 voxels. A patient/window with no recorded core-label conversion will remain and have a conversion rate of zero in both regions.

6. **Freeze patient-grouped validation.** The student will sort anonymized patient IDs, permute them using NumPy default_rng(20261103) (PCG64), divide the permuted IDs into five sequential array_split folds numbered 0-4, and assign every window from a patient to that patient's fold. Inner folds will use the same algorithm on the sorted outer-training IDs with seed 20261104 plus the outer-fold index (0-4). The inner-training set must contain at least 10 patients. If the eligible cohort cannot support these folds, the predictive analyses will be marked infeasible before target-label scoring. No feature scaling, model selection, volume fitting, or calibration will use an outer-test patient.

7. **Measure the primary biological transition.** Within each patient/window and distance band, the student will calculate the fraction of A₂ voxels that convert to core and the fraction of P₂ voxels that convert to core. For each band containing at least 10 voxels in both regions, the band difference will be the A₂ conversion fraction minus the P₂ conversion fraction. The band weight will be the smaller of the A₂ and P₂ voxel counts in that band, divided by the sum of these smaller counts across eligible bands. The window score will be the weighted mean of its band differences. Window scores will be averaged within each patient, and patients will receive equal weight in the cohort mean. The primary contrast will be calculated for every primary-evaluable patient, independent of whether any forecasting model succeeds.

8. **Test the secondary ADC prediction.** For each prior and middle scan, ADC will be divided by the median positive ADC value inside that visit's operational MRI foreground (the union of finite, nonzero T1-c and FLAIR voxels); the logarithm of the normalized ADC will be used. This is an intensity normalization rule, not a skull-stripping method or a claim that the foreground is anatomical brain. The current normalized ADC and its change from the prior scan will be measured only inside R₂. A scan pair with a missing, nonfinite, nonpositive, or incompatible ADC map, or with no positive ADC values in the operational foreground, will be unavailable for the ADC analysis and will not be used to alter the primary cohort.

   A prespecified L2 logistic regression will predict next-visit core-label conversion in R₂ using scikit-learn’s `LogisticRegression` with solver `lbfgs`, tolerance 1e-4, and a maximum of 1,000 iterations. The baseline model will use distance band, current FLAIR state (new or persistent), current robust-standardized FLAIR intensity, forecast interval, days since the most recent surgery known at the middle visit, radiation phase at that visit (not started, ongoing, completed, unknown), systemic-therapy status documented by that visit (no, yes, unknown), and indicators for radiation or systemic therapy received between the prior and middle visits. Unknown treatment history will remain an explicit category, not be imputed as no treatment. No treatment delivered after the prediction origin will be used as a predictor. FLAIR intensity will be robust-standardized within B₂ using its median and 1.4826 × median absolute deviation; a zero or nonfinite scale will make that scan unavailable for the secondary model. The extended model will add current log-normalized ADC and its change from the prior visit. The baseline and extended models will use the same ADC-complete windows, patients, and folds so their held-out Brier scores are paired. Features will be standardized on inner-training data only. No class weights will be used; training weights will be distributed equally by patient, then equally across that patient’s windows and at-risk voxels. The only tuning grid will be C ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}, selected by inner-fold mean patient-macro Brier score; exact ties will choose the smaller C. The secondary endpoint will be held-out patient-macro Brier score; positive improvement will mean baseline Brier minus extended-model Brier > 0. A 10,000-resample patient-cluster percentile bootstrap with seed 20261104 will provide its 95% interval. ADC-added prediction will be described as supported only if that interval’s lower bound is above zero; otherwise, no improvement will be claimed. A convergence failure will make this secondary model unavailable and its comparison incomplete, not alter the primary transition analysis. This tests radiographic prediction, not cell density or malignancy.

9. **Run the prespecified reaction-diffusion benchmark.** The benchmark will use the isotropic Fisher–Kolmogorov model ∂u/∂t = D∇²u + ρu(1−u), initialized from C₁, fitted to C₂, and forecast to visit 3 over the actual date intervals. The solver will be the project’s TensorFK explicit solver on the same 2 mm support grid, with a no-flux boundary, maximum time step 0.5 day, diffusion stability fraction 0.9, and reaction stability limit 0.05. No treatment-kill term will be used; the forecast will describe observed label evolution under recorded care.

   The primary parameter prior will be log-uniform, ρ ∈ [10⁻⁵, 10⁻¹] day⁻¹ and D ∈ [10⁻⁵, 10] mm²/day. A 21 × 21 coarse log grid will be refined over the region containing 90% posterior mass on an 11 × 11 grid. Each forecast will use 200 posterior draws and 256 deterministic surface anchors. For each rho-D pair, the PDE field from C1 to C2 will be ranked within S to match the observed C2 volume. At 256 deterministic C2 surface anchors, predicted-minus-observed signed-distance residuals will receive a normalized multivariate zero-mean Gaussian likelihood with covariance Sigma_ij = sigma_b^2 exp(-d_ij/ell) + tau_b^2 I. The joint posterior will be proportional to this likelihood times the log-uniform prior and log-grid quadrature weights, then normalized across the grid; 200 draws will be sampled with replacement from that discrete posterior. With only one follow-up mask after C1, rho and D may remain correlated and not separately identifiable, so forecasts rather than separate parameter values will be the prediction target. The boundary covariance will be fit by five-fold patient-held-out calibration within each outer-training set, with at least 8 calibration patients and at least 2 valid held-out residual patients. Calibration will use 256 deterministic surface anchors per held-out target and a patient-balanced semivariogram method of moments: nonnegative least squares will estimate σᵦ² and τᵦ² for each ℓ in {5, 10, 20} mm, and the ℓ with the lowest weighted RMSE will be selected. Initial values will be σᵦ = 0.8 mm, τᵦ = 0.3 mm, and ℓ = 6 mm. This is an effective residual covariance that includes model, registration, treatment, and label error; it is not scanner noise and does not model mean boundary bias. The one-decade-narrower sensitivity prior will use ρ ∈ [10⁻⁴, 10⁻²] and D ∈ [10⁻⁴, 1]; the one-decade-wider prior will use ρ ∈ [10⁻⁶, 1] and D ∈ [10⁻⁶, 100]. Sensitivity results will be descriptive and will not be used to choose a winning prior.

   The full PDE candidate will forecast one raw concentration field per posterior draw, rank that field within S to match the corresponding training-only volume draw, perturb the matched boundary using the fitted covariance and pre-generated seeds, average the 200 binary masks, and threshold the inclusion probability at 0.5. The training-only volume forecast will estimate log-volume slope from the two history masks, shrink it toward the equal-patient outer-training median using an inner-selected time constant from {30, 90, 180, 365} days, fit nonnegative log-volume predictive variance a + bT by patient-weighted nonnegative least squares on squared held-out training residuals, and generate 200 positive lognormal volume draws with seed 20261028. The boundary calibration seed will be 20261029 and the forecast seed will be 20261028. The held-out target will not set its volume draws, posterior, covariance, or settings.

10. **Use fixed forecast comparators.** Every mask comparator will use the same held-out windows, support, target mask, folds, requested volume draws where applicable, and boundary random numbers. The matched-volume no-PDE arm will be reported as a separate ablation and will not enter the seven-comparator pass family; the count model will be reported only on count metrics.

   - **Copy-last:** the middle core C₂ without growth or shrinkage.
   - **Fixed growth:** dilate C₂ by r = k × forecast days, with k ∈ {0, 0.01, …, 0.19} mm/day and a 20 mm maximum radius. Select k by maximum inner-validation mean patient-macro Dice using outer-training patients only; ties go to smaller k.
   - **Volume-matched persistence:** rank support voxels by signed distance from C₂, break ties by flat voxel index, and assign the shared history-only volume draws; this tests shape when requested volume is held constant.
   - **FLAIR-front forecast:** rank C₂ first, A₂ second, P₂ third, then remaining support by distance from C₂ and flat voxel index; assign the same volume draws. This converts the primary biological finding into a spatial forecast without fitting to the target.
   - **Two-history shape:** extrapolate the signed-distance change from C₁ to C₂ using factor f = min(T/(g+tau), m), where T is forecast days and g is history-gap days. The minimum usable history gap will be 1 day; if g is shorter, or C1 is empty/full, f will be set to 0. Select among tau in {0, 7, 28} days, m in {1, 4, 8}, and maximum signed-distance change in {10, 30} mm, plus f = 0, using outer-training patients’ inner-validation mean patient-macro Dice only. Each two-history draw will use the same training-only volume draw and boundary perturbation seed as the candidate. The f = 0 arm is volume-matched persistence. Inner settings within 0.005 mean patient-macro Dice will tie; select f = 0 first, then smaller m, larger τ, then smaller distance cap.
   - **PDE-only:** use the same raw PDE concentration draws but omit the candidate’s volume matching and boundary perturbation. Select the concentration cutoff c ∈ {0.05, 0.10, …, 0.50} by maximum inner-validation mean patient-macro Dice using outer-training patients only; ties go to the larger c. Threshold each raw draw at c, average the binary masks, and use inclusion probability ≥0.5. An empty result will remain empty and be scored by the fixed Dice rule.
   - **FiLM U-Net:** use binary input channels C₁, C₂, and support S, plus two conditioning inputs log(forecast days/91) and log(history days/91). The four-level 3D U-Net widths will be {16, 32, 64, 128}, with two 3×3×3 convolutions per level, instance normalization, and LeakyReLU slope 0.01. Each normalization layer will receive a zero-initialized 16-unit FiLM conditioning network. Training will use Dice plus binary cross-entropy, Adam learning rate 0.001, betas (0.9, 0.999), batch size 1, no weight decay, 300 epochs, last epoch retained, random axis flips, and axial 90-degree rotations. Three seeds per fold will be 20261102 + fold, +1000, and +2000; probabilities will be averaged, thresholded at 0.5, and clipped to S. No crop will be used. The U-Net will be a secondary forecast benchmark, not a biological cell classifier.

   The count-only secondary model will define a new core piece as a 26-connected component in C₃ outside a 2 mm dilation of C₂ with at least 2 voxels. A training-only Poisson GLM will use log(|C₂|) as predictor and log(forecast days) as offset. If the training total count is zero, the fixed fallback rate will be 0.5 divided by total training days. It will be scored only by Poisson deviance, count MAE, and Brier score for any new piece, with event probability 1 − exp(−predicted count); it will never be converted to a mask or scored by Dice.

11. **Apply failure and scoring rules.** Expected PDE failures will be limited to a typed forecast failure, an empty required history core, an unavailable training-only PDE nuisance fit, insufficient boundary-calibration patients or residuals, a nonfinite PDE field, or a forecast volume that is not strictly between zero and the support capacity. For these cases, the candidate, PDE-only, and matched-volume no-PDE arms that depend on that PDE call will use C₂ clipped to S; the patient will remain in the analysis, and the stage, type, and reason will be recorded. A valid empty PDE-only mask is not a failure and will be scored as empty. A missing training-only cutoff grid will make the forecast comparison family incomplete; it will not be silently replaced. If the FiLM U-Net is unavailable or produces nonfinite output, its score will be marked missing and the complete forecast-family claim will be incomplete; other arms will keep their own predictions. If the ADC model fails to converge, its secondary comparison will be incomplete. Registration failure, invalid inputs, nonfinite model inputs, changed hashes, or unexpected exceptions will stop the affected run as incomplete, with no fallback or silent patient removal. The primary biological transition analysis will remain separate from forecast-model failures. There will be no retry, post-result threshold change, or silent patient removal. Both-empty Dice will equal 1; one-empty Dice will equal 0. Target-core voxels outside S will count as misses for every mask method.

12. **Validate, time, and seal before target scoring.** Before opening target labels, the student will run synthetic tests at the released image dimensions and metadata-only scan-interval distribution. The tests will cover known registration transforms, landmark error calculation, support generation from history only, label transitions, patient-grouped folds, target isolation, patient-level weighting, model fallbacks, empty masks, result hashing, and end-to-end output. A full runtime pilot will include registration, every outer and inner fit, all specified forecasts, the U-Net, prior sensitivity runs, output writing, and one restart allowance. The project will proceed only if the projection is at most 1,000 wall-clock hours on the local computer. Otherwise, a dated scope amendment or a stop will be required before target-mask scoring.

   Before opening any next-visit label voxels, the student will freeze and hash the protocol, source code, exact package lock, hardware/thread settings, metadata eligibility list, fold map, image-QC list, support rule, treatment coding, seeds, and settings. Training and inner-validation labels may be used only inside their assigned training folds. Each outer-test prediction and setting will be saved before its target label is loaded. Each result will record permitted input hashes, cohort and window counts, exclusions, scan intervals, treatment summaries, registration metrics, landmark errors where available, patient-level primary and secondary scores, missing arms, fallbacks, and runtime. The outer-test results will be scored once and reported unchanged.

## Risk Assessment

## Data Analysis

### Primary biological outcome

For each patient/window and distance band b, let rAᵦ be the fraction of newly appearing FLAIR voxels A₂ that are labeled core at the next visit, and rPᵦ the corresponding fraction for persistent FLAIR voxels P₂. Let nAᵦ and nPᵦ be the history-only voxel counts. Include bands with nAᵦ ≥ 10 and nPᵦ ≥ 10. Define the band weight wᵦ = min(nAᵦ, nPᵦ) / Σᵦ min(nAᵦ, nPᵦ). The window-level primary measure will be Δ = Σᵦ wᵦ(rAᵦ − rPᵦ). Windows will be averaged within each patient; patients will have equal weight. Positive Δ means newly appearing FLAIR tissue converted to next-visit core labels more often than persistent FLAIR tissue at matched distances.

The primary outcome is an automated-label transition. A next-visit label 0, 2, or 4 will count as no recorded core label for the main operational rate; the undefined meaning of label 0 means this cannot be interpreted as confirmed biological non-conversion. The student will report the next-visit label distribution in A₂ and P₂ and a descriptive sensitivity comparison excluding label-0 voxels. Neither sensitivity result will replace the frozen primary endpoint.

The primary 95% confidence interval will use 10,000 patient-cluster bootstrap resamples with NumPy default_rng (PCG64), seed 20261104, and the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles using linear quantiles. Resampling will occur at the patient level, retaining all windows for each sampled patient. The primary hypothesis will pass only if the lower confidence bound is above zero, at least 35 patients are primary-evaluable, and half the interval width is no greater than 0.10. No voxel or scan will be treated as an independent patient.

This primary transition estimate is not a cross-validated model score: it uses the frozen history-defined regions for all primary-evaluable patients after cohort lock. Patient-grouped folds apply only to the ADC and forecast prediction analyses.

### ADC and forecast outcomes

For the ADC analysis, the secondary endpoint will be the patient-macro difference in held-out Brier score between the baseline transition model and the model with ADC features; positive values will favor adding ADC. Its 95% interval will use the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of 10,000 patient-cluster bootstrap resamples with seed 20261104. The student will claim improved held-out prediction only when the interval’s lower bound is above zero. This is a predictive test of imaging signal, not a histologic test of cellularity.

For the forecast benchmark, the endpoint will be equal-patient mean Dice between each forecast and the next-visit automated core mask. The student will report every paired patient-level candidate-minus-comparator Dice difference, its mean, and its interval. The secondary comparison family will include fixed growth, volume-matched persistence, FLAIR-front, two-history shape, PDE-only, FiLM U-Net, and copy-last. It will use 10,000 shared patient bootstrap resamples, seed 20261110, and a single-step max-|t| simultaneous 95% interval. For each bootstrap replicate, the student will divide each centered mean-difference deviation by that contrast’s bootstrap standard deviation (ddof = 1), take the 95th percentile of the maximum absolute statistic, and form simultaneous intervals. A zero or nonfinite standard deviation or missing comparator will make the secondary family incomplete. A complete claim that the candidate outperformed its required forecast comparators will be made only if all seven adjusted lower interval bounds are above zero; otherwise, the student will report each contrast without an overall superiority claim. This forecast-family rule will not change the primary biological pass result.

Secondary summaries will include transition rates by distance, ADC-change strata, scan interval, treatment timing, IDH status where permitted, core and FLAIR volumes, registration QC, available landmark error, exclusions, and fallback counts. Prior-range sensitivity results and the count-only results will be reported separately. These analyses will not change the primary endpoint or thresholds.

The project will interpret all results as predictions or transitions among the dataset’s automated MRI labels. It will not claim histologic infiltration, true tumor boundaries, a causal PDE effect, treatment benefit, clinical utility, or generalization to other hospitals.
