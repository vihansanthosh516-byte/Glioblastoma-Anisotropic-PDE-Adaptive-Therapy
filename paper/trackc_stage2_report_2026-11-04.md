## Test B (script 191): exact identities and gain-versus-burden checks

- **Asked.** Which exact statements follow from the equations, and where can a feedback rule gain at all?
- **Did it work.** Yes. Three lemmas and one proposition (`paper/trackc_lemmas_2026-11-04.md`), checked by 13 passing tests in a scratch copy (`tests/test_trackc_lemmas.py`) and by an independent read-only algebra check. With no crowding, no cost of resistance and no switching, front-loaded dosing gives a time to progression at least as long as any allowed schedule. So any gain of feedback in the full model has to come from crowding (starting burden B0), the cost c of resistance, or switching.
- **Weakest point.** It is a statement about the assumed equations. It does not show that real tumours behave this way.

## Test C (script 192): which assumed worlds reproduce the published standard arm?

Files: `output/trackc_stage2_C_calibration/{manifest.json,worlds.csv,kept_worlds.csv,results.json}`. Development seeds 20261801 (worlds) and [20261802, world] (patients). 500 worlds, 400 simulated patients each.

- **Asked.** The published standard arm (RTOG 0525, Gilbert 2013, J Clin Oncol 31:4085) has a median PFS of 5.5 months (95% CI 4.7 to 6.1, which is 143 to 186 days) from random assignment, with scans about every three months. The page of the paper (PMC3816958) was opened on 2026-11-04 and states 5.5 months (4.7 to 6.1) for the standard-dose arm and 6.7 months (6.2 to 7.7) for the dose-dense arm. For which settings of the parameter ranges does a simulated standard arm (6 courses, open loop, scan every 84 days, RANO-like progression call) land inside that interval?
- **Did it work.** Yes. 81 of 500 worlds (16.2%) pass (`results.json`, `base.n_pass`). The earlier ranges, unchanged, give a median of 240 days (`old_model.base.median_interp`), above the interval.
- **What it shows (simulation, exploratory).** In this model family the anchor constrains one axis strongly: the kill strength relative to growth (the m scale). The median kept world has an m-scale of 0.135 times the old range, so m is about 0.8 to 2.7 instead of 6 to 20. Of the 116 worlds with an m-scale of at least 0.5, only 2 pass (median PFS 175 and 184 days); the 116 worlds as a group have a median PFS of 196 to 321 days (5th to 95th percentile), mostly above the interval. Faster growth r shortens the median PFS and a higher B0 lengthens it (Spearman -0.53 and +0.45 against the world's median PFS). The pass fraction is lowest in the fastest-growth third (0.12), the highest-B0 third (0.13) and at nu = 0.04 (0.10); it is flat (0.14 to 0.19) across thirds of the f0 shift, the cost c and the switching rate mu. These are descriptive, uncorrected comparisons. In the kept worlds about one third of patients are called progressed at the first scan (S(84) about 0.67, 5th to 95th percentile 0.62 to 0.76).
- **Weakest points.**
  1. The set of consistent worlds depends on how progression is called, while the weak-kill finding survives. With a 1.25 factor instead of 1.4, 54 worlds pass but the Jaccard overlap with the base set is 0.03; with the old 1.2 x baseline rule (which also detects progression continuously instead of at scans), 30 pass and the overlap is 0.03. Scans every 56 or 112 days give overlaps of 0.86 and 0.68; the RANO floor (0, 0.1, 0.2 x baseline) leaves the pass set unchanged. In every variant the passing worlds have a median m-scale of 0.11 to 0.26, against 0.22 for all worlds, and few (1 to 7 per variant) have an m-scale of 0.5 or more (`worlds.csv`). So the weak-kill result holds under each rule tried, but its strength changes with the rule. The progression rule is an assumed translation of the trial rule and uses the true burden without measurement error.
  2. The pass rule depends on the median convention. The base rule uses the interpolated Kaplan-Meier median because a scan every 84 days puts the plain median on the grid 84, 168, 252 days. With the plain (step) median, 109 worlds pass; 46 of them are among the 81 (Jaccard 0.32). Of the 81, 46 have a step median of 168 days and 35 have 252 days. The step-rule passes also have weak kill (median m-scale 0.108; 1 of the 109 at 0.5 or more). Real progression dates are not on a scan grid, so the interpolated rule is the closer match, but it is a choice.
  3. The standard arm gives six courses to every patient not called progressed at the first scan. In RTOG 0525 the median number of adjuvant cycles in the standard arm was 3 and 37% received six or more (`paper/trackc_calibration_targets_2026-11-03.md`). The trial stopped treatment for toxicity and other reasons the model lacks. A run with 3 and 4 courses is planned (script 200).
  4. One published median fixes one condition, applied as a hard acceptance window of 143 to 186 days with no weight for distance from the centre. Many worlds pass and the anchor does not say which one is true. The trial population was selected (patients who progressed during chemoradiation were excluded); the model has no such exclusion.
  5. The kill strength is not anchored by any volume-change-per-cycle measurement (the search is recorded in `paper/trackc_calibration_targets_2026-11-03.md`, row 'Volume change per TMZ cycle': not found). The weak kill the anchor implies may only mean this model class needs a weak drug for everyone to produce a short PFS. A mixture of strong responders and non-responders could give the same median with a very different meaning for dosing; that family is planned (script 200).
  6. The model has no early deaths or drop-outs and no measurement error in the volumes; the early part of the PFS curve is not checked.
  7. 400 patients per world: a world near an edge of the interval can flip. Test D re-draws the kept worlds.

## Test D (script 193): do the kept worlds also pass the two negative controls?

Files: `output/trackc_stage2_D_negative_controls/{manifest.json,controls.csv,controlled_worlds.csv,results.json}`. Development seed [20261803, world]. 3,000 fresh simulated patients for each of the 81 kept worlds.

- **Asked.** Real trials found little or no gain from more drug: dose-dense against standard TMZ (RTOG 0525, PFS HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.75 to 1.00) and 12 against 6 cycles among patients still progression-free after cycle 6 (GEINO 14-01, PFS HR 0.99, 95% CI 0.71 to 1.38). Which kept worlds show a model hazard ratio inside both intervals?
- **Did it work.** Yes. Of 81 kept worlds, 78 still land in 143 to 186 days on fresh patients; the 3 that flipped were within 8 days of the lower edge on the first draw (1.9, 0.8 and 7.8 days). The dose-dense control is met by 65 worlds (80 at a drug-effect multiplier of 1.5, 48 at 3.0). The 12-versus-6 control is met by 41 worlds. 39 worlds pass everything and form the **controlled set** (`controlled_worlds.csv`). The kill rule (no world passes) did not fire.
- **What it shows (simulation, exploratory).** In this model family the controls select slower growth, weaker kill and more initial resistance: in the 41 landmark-passing worlds the mean log shift of r is -0.29 against +0.37 in the 40 others (Mann-Whitney p < 0.0001), the mean log m-scale is -2.29 against -1.48 (p < 0.0001), and the mean log f0 shift is 1.16 against 0.60 (p = 0.003). On the four axes where feedback dosing could matter there is no clear selection: the mean B0 centre is 0.314 against 0.315 (p = 0.94), nu 0.012 against 0.013 (p = 0.42), the cost c 0.125 against 0.106 (p = 0.19) and mu 0.021 against 0.013 (p = 0.09). The p-values are uncorrected and 41 against 40 worlds is a small sample, so "open" means "not shown to be constrained", not "shown to be free".
- **Weakest points.**
  1. The dose-dense arm is the standard arm with the drug term of the true dynamics multiplied by 2.0. That multiplies the kill of both populations and the induced switching. The ratio of drug exposure is assumed (the label doses of the dose-dense arm were not verified), and the pass count moves from 80 (1.5) to 65 (2.0) to 48 (3.0), so "more drug has little effect" is conditional on this choice.
  2. The model never reproduces the published landmark point estimate. Its hazard ratio for 12 against 6 courses has a 5th to 95th percentile of 0.26 to 0.89 across the 81 worlds (median 0.73) and is below 1 in all 81, while the trial point estimate is 0.99. The 41 passing worlds are inside the trial's wide interval (lower bound 0.71), not at its centre; that trial was phase II and not powered for medians (targets file). A second, non-randomised landmark estimate exists (Blumenthal 2017, PFS HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.98); 48 of the 81 worlds fall inside it. The spec names GEINO 14-01 as the control because it is randomised; Blumenthal is a sensitivity, and the choice is a degree of freedom.
  3. Both controls compare open-loop arms on the same simulated patients, so the Cox interval is not a valid confidence interval; the flags use the point estimate.
  4. The controlled worlds have weak kill by construction (test C), so contrasts between dosing rules may be small only because the drug does little. The mixture family (script 200) is the check.
  5. Toxicity, drop-out and death before progression are not modelled. Real trials include them.

## Internal review of tests C and D (three read-only Haiku checks, 2026-11-04)

- **Number check.** Every count, quantile, Jaccard overlap, Spearman value, third-bin pass fraction, control count and axis mean in the first version of the Test C and D sections reproduced from the CSV files. Wording defects were found and are fixed above: the 196 to 321 day range belongs to the 116 worlds, not to the 2 passers; the pass fraction is also lower for the fastest-growth and highest-B0 thirds, not only for nu; "winner's curse" had no support (the three flips were near the lower edge); the 41 against 40 comparison had no test.
- **Skeptic review.** Eleven findings. Verified and acted on: the 3-versus-6-course mismatch (weakest point C3), dependence on the median convention (C2; note that the skeptic's figure of 46 passes under the step median was wrong, the files give 109 with 46 shared), weak-drug degeneracy and the overclaim "the published data constrain growth, kill and resistant fraction" (rewritten), the second landmark estimate (D2), the dose multiplier also scaling switching (D1), the old-rule variant also changing detection resolution (C1), the selected trial population (C4), the hard acceptance window (C4) and uncorrected comparisons. Not acted on: the unused noise key (the arms are open loop; it is only passed to the simulator).
- **Literature scout.** No published objective response rate for adjuvant TMZ in newly diagnosed GBM and no TMZ-specific distribution of volume change were found. The nearest anchors are indirect: a pooled analysis of the AVAglio trial (PMC6178278, Neuro-Oncology 2018, first author not confirmed on the page) reports, for the placebo arm (radiotherapy plus TMZ, no bevacizumab), partial response 12.1% and complete response 1.6% during chemoradiation under volumetric RANO (more than 65% fall in enhancing volume); and a recurrent-GBM control arm (PMC2667826) had 9.6% partial responses under Macdonald criteria. Neither is an adjuvant-TMZ benchmark. Script 199 computes the model's prediction of the share of deep responders so that this comparison can be made with the mismatch stated.
