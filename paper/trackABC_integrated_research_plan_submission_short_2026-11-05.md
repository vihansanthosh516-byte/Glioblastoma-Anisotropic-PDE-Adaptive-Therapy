# Research Plan

## Project Title

**Integrating Tumor Biology and Longitudinal MRI for Glioblastoma Progression Modeling and In-Silico Therapy Optimization**

## Rationale

Glioblastoma can contain distinct cell states, extend beyond the enhancing tumor, and change during treatment. Standard care includes temozolomide given in repeated courses (Stupp et al., 2005). Single-cell RNA sequencing can measure molecular patterns (Yu et al., 2020). Serial MRI, T2-FLAIR, and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps can describe radiographic changes over time (Akbari et al., 2016; Hoff et al., 2021). Recent work has mapped recurrence-prone tissue in the peri-enhancing zone, tested mid-treatment ADC-based maps of regions that later recur, and combined mathematical tumor models with reinforcement learning (Bertalan et al., 2026; Moore-Palhares et al., 2025; Rezvanjou et al., 2026; Q. Liu et al., 2025; Z. Liu et al., 2025). Same-patient molecular-imaging work is also established: Hu et al. (2021) linked 95 image-localized biopsies from 25 untreated glioblastoma patients to spatially matched multiparametric MRI to model regional EGFR amplification and prediction uncertainty, then evaluated a separate set of 24 biopsies from 7 high-grade-glioma patients. A 2026 prospective pilot used machine-learning infiltration maps to guide radiation dose escalation in 20 patients; the comparative analysis used historical matched controls, and the report also described radiation necrosis in 47% of treated patients versus 12% of controls, so its survival findings remain preliminary and are not randomized evidence (Akbari et al., 2026). A 2026 systematic review identified 21 neuro-oncology digital-twin studies, including 12 on glioblastoma or high-grade glioma; it found that multimodal data fusion and external validation remained limited. A separate 2026 review specifically describes MRI-centered integration of molecular, pathology, and treatment-history data as a current glioblastoma research direction. These reviews identify field needs; they do not establish this project’s uniqueness (Singh et al., 2026; Zadeh-Shirazi et al., 2026). Other direct precedents include Bayesian MRI-informed, risk-aware radiotherapy planning demonstrated in a 100-patient in-silico high-grade-glioma cohort; weekly MRI data assimilation with a two-species reaction-diffusion model in 21 patients; a patient-specific MRI/ADC treatment-response and radiotherapy-target framework in 21 patients; PDE-constrained spatiotemporal radiotherapy optimization; Q-learning for temozolomide scheduling in virtual glioblastoma; and a recent preprint combining reaction-diffusion, learned residual correction, recursive updating, and treatment scheduling on patient-data-informed synthetic trajectories (Chaudhuri et al., 2023; Miniere et al., 2025; Ty et al., 2026; Kao et al., 2026; Ebrahimi Zade et al., 2020; Liu, Trimboli, & Li, 2026, preprint; Corwin et al., 2013; Z. Liu et al., 2025). Uncertainty and robustness analysis for patient-specific glioblastoma response metrics also have prior precedent (Hawkins-Daarud et al., 2019). TumorTwin is a published modular Python framework for patient-specific imaging-based tumor models, longitudinal MRI calibration, and high-grade-glioma treatment-response simulation, so those software components are not novel by themselves (Kapteyn et al., 2026). The project will therefore not claim that multimodal therapy modeling, MRI forecasting, radiogenomics, digital twins, mechanistic-learning hybrids, uncertainty analysis, or adaptive scheduling is new by itself. Its candidate methodological question will be whether explicitly uncertain, unlinked molecular and MRI-derived signals change treatment-policy value and worst-case regret across shared held-out virtual worlds. This targeted review has not established that this narrower design is unique; the project will treat it as an open question, not a novelty claim.

The project will make the Duke patient MRI analysis its main empirical study. It will test whether newly FLAIR-labeled peritumoral regions receive an automated necrotic- or enhancing-core label at the next visit more often than persistent FLAIR-labeled regions at the same distance from the current core. It will also test whether local ADC measurements improve prediction of that transition. Related studies already examine peritumoral imaging and later recurrence; the narrower question here is the three-visit, distance-matched comparison of newly and persistently FLAIR-labeled regions under observed care. These outcomes will describe transitions between automated MRI labels. They will not prove that a region contains invading tumor cells.

The three tracks will then form one connected computational framework. Track A will provide previously generated donor-held-out molecular prediction summaries as simulation inputs. Track B will provide patient-level MRI transition and ADC observations from the Duke cohort. Track C will represent tumor growth, treatment suppression, and resistant cells. Because the source cohorts have no shared patient identifiers, I will not merge people or assign Track A measurements to Duke patients; the title’s “tumor biology” component will enter only as a prespecified scenario signal derived from the exploratory A5 summary, not as a measured Duke-patient biomarker or resistance assay. Instead, I will build virtual patients and explicitly vary the assumed relationships among the molecular signal, MRI signal, and drug resistance. This design will show when combining these signals helps a simulated treatment policy and when uncertain or misleading links remove that benefit.

The mathematical contribution will be a paired, out-of-grid stress test of whether dependence-aware belief updating changes simulated treatment utility relative to the same controller with conditional independence. I will vary signal reliability, factor loadings, residual dependence, and likelihood mismatch, then compare dependence-aware and robustness-aware policies with single-channel, no-signal, and fixed-schedule controls. The primary contrast will use unique virtual-world IDs; patient and measurement-noise replicates will remain nested within each world. Track A and Duke data will not share participants. Their summaries will inform permitted marginal signal ranges only; every molecular-to-resistance relationship will remain an explicit simulation assumption. I will report all development regimes, every held-out anchor, and any harmful regime. This finite scenario study will not prove a general theorem, patient treatment benefit, or novelty by itself. The Duke MRI transition analysis will remain a separate patient-derived description of automated label transitions.

## Research Question, Hypotheses, Engineering Goals, and Expected Outcomes

### Research Questions

**Central mathematical question:** Across two aggregate-constrained simulator families and genuinely out-of-grid virtual worlds, when will a controller that models dependence between a baseline biology-like report and MRI-like evidence improve or harm simulated treatment utility compared with the same controller assuming conditional independence? How will results change under single-channel, no-signal, and signal-model-mismatch controls?

1. **Primary patient question:** At the same physical distance from the existing core, will newly FLAIR-labeled nonenhancing regions have a higher next-visit automated necrotic/enhancing core-label rate than persistent FLAIR-labeled regions?
2. **ADC question:** Will current ADC and its change over time improve patient-held-out prediction of that transition beyond the prespecified MRI and clinical baseline?
3. **Integrated simulation question:** In held-out worlds with nonzero residual dependence, will a dependence-aware controller improve paired worst-endpoint utility over an otherwise identical conditional-independence controller when its assumed signal model is correctly specified? How will the difference change under wrong-dependence and wrong-sign stress tests? The world-level q value will remain an illustrative setting for a synthetic report; it will not be treated as a patient assay accuracy, resistance measure, or biological link.
4. **Robustness question:** Across the 27 development combinations and five out-of-grid evaluation anchors, how will the fused policies help, tie, or harm as signal reliability, factor loadings, and residual dependence change?

### Hypotheses

1. **Primary Duke hypothesis:** Under observed care, at matched physical distances from the middle-visit core, newly FLAIR-labeled regions will have a higher next-visit automated core-label rate than persistent regions; I will report prior-label composition and the prespecified prior-core/cavity sensitivity.
2. **Secondary ADC hypothesis:** Adding current normalized ADC and its change from the prior visit will lower patient-held-out Brier score for predicting next-visit core-label conversion.
3. **Virtual-patient hypothesis:** In correctly specified held-out worlds with nonzero residual dependence, dependence-aware fusion will have higher mean worst-endpoint utility than the otherwise identical conditional-independence controller. This directional hypothesis may fail; wrong-dependence and wrong-sign worlds may favor the simpler controller. The A5-derived q value will remain an illustrative synthetic-report setting, not an assay estimate or resistance measure.
4. **Robustness hypothesis:** The robustness-aware controller will have lower worst-case regret than fixed-prior fusion across all five out-of-grid evaluation anchors, measured using the frozen utility. This directional hypothesis may fail.

These are directional hypotheses. I will report the measured results even if they do not support them.

### Engineering Goals

1. Build an auditable Duke pipeline that links consecutive patient visits, checks MRI registration, measures tissue-label transitions, and keeps all visits from each patient in one validation fold.
2. Use the frozen Track A A5 donor-level values only as analyst-selected, world-level reliability scenarios for a synthetic binary report of a Core-like/Periphery-like state. I will vary the assumed association between that state and simulated resistance separately; A5 will not be interpreted as a resistance assay.
3. Evaluate a two-population tumor and drug-suppression model across prespecified parameter ranges before comparing treatment policies.
4. Build virtual patients from separate Track A and Track B signal distributions and Track C tumor and treatment states without matching people across cohorts. I will make each cross-source association an explicit simulation assumption rather than an estimate from linked patient data.
5. Quantify the incremental value of the fused belief-state controller against development-selected fixed and single-modality policies, alongside no-information controls, using shared virtual patients, scan schedules, noise, and treatment limits.
6. Save the data flow, exclusions, settings, seeds, software versions, code fingerprints, model failures, and all primary and secondary results.

### Expected Outcomes

The Duke analysis will estimate whether newly appearing FLAIR-labeled regions will show higher, similar, or lower next-visit core-label conversion than persistent regions. The ADC analysis will test whether adding ADC features will improve held-out prediction. The simulation will compare fusion under different assumptions and may show benefit, no advantage, or harm. I will report each result by its evidence source: patient MRI analysis or virtual-patient simulation.

## Procedures

1. **Confirm permission and protect data.** Before analysis, I will verify that the Duke agreement and school or competition conditions allow the planned processing and reporting. If terms do not permit a step, I will omit it before data access. I will verify the archive checksum and NIfTI headers before reading voxel arrays, and I will stop on a failed hash or invalid header. I will store restricted data and patient-level outputs locally in access-controlled storage. I will not identify patients, contact them, or upload restricted files or predictions.

2. **Select the Duke cohort from metadata.** Before image or mask reads, I will use permitted metadata to select glioblastoma records whose released IDH field is “Negative.” I will report this only as the dataset-coded field, not as comprehensive IDH1/2 wildtype status. I will form windows from every eligible set of three adjacent visits with T1-c, FLAIR, and segmentation data; I will not skip visits. I will sort by relative scan date, require dates to be finite and increasing, record scan intervals and treatment events, and exclude unresolved windows before voxel reads. Because the authors report that extracted dates may be off by several days, I will repeat interval- and treatment-phase classifications under ±3- and ±7-day date-jitter stress scenarios; these will be analyst-selected stress bounds, not verified error limits. I will check prior-use overlap where permitted and will not claim independent confirmation if overlap cannot be established. I will retain surgery-between-visits windows in the primary analysis and report their exclusion as a sensitivity analysis. The study will estimate changes under observed care and will be retrospective.

3. **Register images and define labels and support.** I will rigidly align prior- and next-visit T1-c images to the middle-visit T1-c using image intensities only, then apply each transform to the other modalities and masks. I will accept registrations only if normalized cross-correlation is at least 0.80, the largest absolute Euler angle is at most 10 degrees, and translation length is at most 10 mm; these will be operational screens, not validated accuracy limits. I will not retry or manually adjust failures. Synthetic known-transform tests will check implementation. Released vessel-landmark errors will be reported only for visit pairs with matching landmarks; they will not be assumed available for every adjacent pair. I will use the released 1 mm resampled grid for the primary MRI transition analysis and run a prespecified 2 mm sensitivity analysis. Secondary PDE forecasts will use a 2 mm grid. Labels 1 and 3 will define the operational core, label 2 the FLAIR abnormality, and label 4 the cavity; I will not treat label 0 as normal tissue. I will define origin support from the prior and middle visits only. This will be an operational support region, not a validated brain mask. The next-visit image will be used only for evaluation alignment and frozen QC; it may determine technical registration eligibility, but not support, features, tuning, or forecast generation. QC failures will exclude the whole window from every arm before target labels are read. Target-core voxels outside support will count as misses.

4. **Measure the primary MRI transition.** Within the middle-visit label-2 FLAIR region, I will exclude a 2 mm margin around the operational core and cavity. I will call a region newly FLAIR-labeled if it is outside a 2 mm dilation of prior-visit label 2, and persistent if it is inside. “Newly FLAIR-labeled” will not mean newly abnormal tissue: I will report the mapped prior-label composition and run a sensitivity excluding voxels previously labeled core (1 or 3) or cavity (4). I will compare the two region types within 1 mm distance strata from 2 to 30 mm from the middle-visit core, retaining only strata with at least 80 mm³ of each region type. I will average stratum differences using the smaller of the two volumes, then average windows within each patient and weight patients equally. Regions beyond 30 mm will be descriptive. This overlap rule will reduce residual distance imbalance left by broad distance bands. I will set strata and exclusions without viewing next-visit labels. Labels 1 or 3 will count as a recorded automated core label, not histologic tumor conversion; I will report necrotic and enhancing labels separately as descriptive outcomes. I will use 10,000 patient-cluster bootstrap resamples. I will report scan intervals and treatment events, plus prespecified sensitivities by scan interval, recorded treatment status, and exclusion of surgery between the middle and next visit. I will run a label-free precision check before opening next-visit labels. I will stop inference below 20 eligible patients; with 20–34 patients or inadequate precision, I will report feasibility only. I will treat the hypothesis as supported only with at least 35 evaluable patients, interval half-width no greater than 0.10, and a lower confidence bound above zero.

5. **Test ADC and run secondary forecasts.** I will compare a prespecified MRI/clinical logistic model with the same model plus current normalized ADC and ADC change, using five patient-grouped folds and training patients only for feature processing and model selection. The baseline will include distance, new-versus-persistent status, FLAIR intensity, scan interval, and recorded treatment history. I will report held-out patient-macro Brier scores and calibration summaries. This retrospective analysis will not be described as a prospective tool. Separately, I will compare the reaction-diffusion PDE with copy-last, fixed growth, volume-matched persistence, FLAIR-front, two-history shape, PDE-only, and a time-conditioned FiLM U-Net. All arms will use the same patients, folds, support, and targets; fitted settings will use training patients only. This forecast benchmark will remain secondary. A candidate will count as better only if every adjusted lower confidence bound for its paired Dice difference versus a required mask comparator is above zero. A count-only model will be evaluated only with Poisson deviance, count MAE, and Brier score for any new piece, never as a mask.

6. **Build the molecular and drug-suppression inputs.** I will use the six frozen Track A A5 donor-level balanced-accuracy values only as six possible world-level values q for the assumed probability that one synthetic binary report correctly identifies a synthetic Core-like/Periphery-like state. I will hold q fixed across every patient in a world, never draw it per patient, and not treat it as a clinical test estimate or resistance measure. The relationship between the synthetic state and resistance will be varied separately. Development worlds will include uninformative and prespecified asymmetric sensitivity/specificity channels; held-out worlds will use q equal to the equal-donor mean A5 value. I will model sensitive and resistant populations, growth, generic treatment suppression, and assumed switching or reversion. A modeled course will not represent a clinical dose or drug regimen. I will analyze a single-response family and a mixture-response family over the full prespecified parameter ranges, without discarding worlds because their simulated outcomes do not match a trial summary. The model will report time to modeled progression under both fixed progression rules, not clinical progression-free survival: it will not simulate death. I will show RTOG 0525 and GEINO 14-01 summaries as context only, and I will not use their PFS medians or hazard ratios as direct calibration gates. I will report results across every prespecified world and label conclusions as conditional on the simulator and parameter ranges.

7. **Construct virtual patients and compare policies.** I will combine separate Track A, Track B, and Track C distributions without matching people across datasets. I will map each simulator resistance fraction f0 through the fixed log-uniform reference CDF over the declared f0 envelope to obtain Z_R = Φ⁻¹(F_ref(f0)); I will draw only E_B and E_I as independent standard-normal variables. I will form Z_B = a Z_R + sqrt(1−a²) E_B and Z_I = b Z_R + sqrt(1−b²)[d E_B + sqrt(1−d²) E_I], define C = 1[Z_B ≥ 0], and generate molecular report M with world-level q. I will treat a and b as factor loadings and d as a residual-factor coefficient, not empirical cohort correlations; I will report induced prevalence and associations for each world. I will calibrate MRI-score location and scale using only the permitted training/history template. I will use a,b,d ∈ {−0.5, 0, 0.5} for 27 development scenarios. Independent setting (0,0,0) will remain a development negative control, not a held-out anchor. Five evaluation anchors will be outside that grid: strong aligned dependence (0.75,0.75,0.75); complementary residual dependence (0.75,0.75,−0.75); reversed molecular loading (−0.75,0.75,0.50); reversed MRI loading (0.50,−0.75,−0.50); and both loadings reversed (−0.75,−0.75,0.75). The first two will form the correctly specified nonzero-dependence primary set. I will preserve unique parameter-world IDs and paired random streams. For the correctly specified primary contrast, the dependence-aware likelihood will use the matching predeclared population-level q, a, b, and d; it will not receive hidden states or tumor parameters. In mismatch tests, I will freeze the policy likelihood before evaluation and conceal the generating scenario. I will compare it with an otherwise identical conditional-independence controller that sets d=0, plus no-signal, single-channel, fixed-schedule, and robustness-aware policies. I will implement repeated MRI-like observations at each scheduled scan and update the likelihood once per observation; synthetic tests will check missing scans, no double-counting, and future-observation isolation before outcome evaluation. The current tabular Q-learning policy will be labeled a burden-only reference. I will add a same-input Q-learning arm only if I implement and test molecular/MRI state inputs before protocol freeze; otherwise, I will exclude it from same-information superiority claims. All policies will share virtual patients, scans, noise, and treatment limits. I will use a five-day course penalty as an assumed utility weight and repeat policy selection at 0 and 10 days as secondary sensitivities. Simulated courses will represent generic drug suppression, not clinical treatment or toxicity.
8. **Apply stop rules and report results.** I will use the predeclared fallback for expected model failures and record each reason; I will not silently remove patients. On development simulations, I will double particle and prediction-draw counts and halve the integration step. If the primary policy contrast changes direction, I will report numerical instability and make no confirmatory simulation claim. I will report Monte Carlo uncertainty across held-out worlds. Missing required outputs, nonfinite values, or changed protocol hashes will make the comparison incomplete or stop it. Before scoring, I will run a full-size synthetic timing pilot that includes the 1 mm transition pipeline and all model arms; I will stop or amend the plan if projected runtime exceeds 1,000 hours. I will save predictions and settings before opening held-out targets, hash the protocol, code, folds, permitted inputs, and cohort, and report patient MRI findings separately from simulated treatment outcomes. I will report positive, null, or negative results and will not present simulation as treatment evidence for real people.

Potential risks will include disclosure of health information, use outside the dataset agreement, and confusion between simulated outputs and clinical evidence. I will keep the Duke archive, images, masks, landmarks, patient-level tables, and predictions in access-controlled local storage. I will not try to identify patients or contact them. ## Data Analysis

### Primary patient-data analysis

I will treat the patient MRI transition and virtual-patient policy comparison as separate primary components with separate conclusions; I will not combine them into one pass/fail claim. The primary patient estimate will be the equal-patient mean of within-window, 1 mm distance-stratified differences in next-visit automated core-label rate between newly FLAIR-labeled and persistent regions. Only strata with at least 80 mm³ of both region types will contribute; each stratum will receive the smaller-region-volume weight. The 95% interval will use 10,000 patient-cluster percentile-bootstrap resamples. Patients, not voxels, visits, or regions, will be units of inference. I will treat the patient rule as met only if at least 35 patients are evaluable, the interval half-width is no greater than 0.10, and the lower confidence bound is above zero. If fewer patients qualify or precision is wider, I will report feasibility only.

The ADC analysis will report paired held-out patient-macro Brier scores for the baseline and ADC-extended models. The Track B forecast comparison will report patient-macro Dice and simultaneous paired confidence intervals for every required comparator. Neither secondary analysis will change the primary transition rule.

### Integrated virtual-patient analysis

For each virtual patient, I will calculate restricted mean time to progression at 365 and 730 days under two predeclared outcomes: (1) true simulated burden first reaching 1.2 times starting burden, and (2) a scan-based RANO-like call at 84-day scans, when burden reaches 1.4 times the greater of the prior scan nadir or 0.10 times starting burden (Ellingson et al., 2017). Restricted mean time will be the average of min(time to progression, horizon). The second endpoint will be a model approximation, not a formal RANO assessment.

I will evaluate both progression endpoints on one treatment trajectory. I will stop therapy in the simulator only after two consecutive scheduled noisy MRI-like volume readings cross the 1.4-times observed-nadir threshold with a 0.10-times observed-baseline floor. A missing scheduled reading will reset confirmation; a non-scan cycle will not count. This is an assumed stop proxy, not formal RANO. Hidden progression times will be outcomes only. I will apply the same rule to policy training, MPC planning, schedule selection, and evaluation; if an arm cannot use it, I will not compare outcomes. Latent-oracle stopping will appear only as a labeled sensitivity analysis. For patient i, I will define Tᵢ,base = min(time to simulated burden progression, 730 days), Tᵢ,RANO = min(time to the scan-based RANO-like event, 730 days), and Uᵢ = min(Tᵢ,base, Tᵢ,RANO) − 5 × Cᵢ, where Cᵢ is the number of courses delivered by day 730 on that policy-specific trajectory. I will average Uᵢ within each virtual world and compare paired world means. Thus the primary utility will be the mean of patient-level worst-endpoint utility, not the lower of the two cohort-average RMST values. The primary contrast will be paired ΔU = U_dependence-aware − U_conditional-independence over unique world IDs in the two correctly specified nonzero-dependence anchors and both response families. I will use simultaneous 95% max-|t| intervals across the two families; both lower bounds must exceed zero to support a dependence-model advantage. Otherwise, I will report the estimate and uncertainty without a superiority claim. I will report endpoint-specific RMST differences and policy-specific course counts. Wrong-dependence, wrong-sign, single-channel, no-signal, and shuffled-signal controls will be prespecified robustness analyses across all five anchors. Bootstrap resamples will draw unique world IDs and keep all patients and noise replicates within a sampled world together. I will report regret against the best mean utility among predeclared deployable policies and the worst regret across the five anchors. I will report all 27 development scenarios, every held-out anchor, and both endpoints. The held-out fixed-schedule frontier will remain descriptive. I will reselect policies at 0- and 10-day penalties as preference sensitivities and report ranking changes. No favorable anchor will be selected after evaluation.
