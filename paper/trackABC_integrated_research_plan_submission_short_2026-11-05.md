# Research Plan

## Project Title

**Integrating Tumor Biology and Longitudinal MRI for Glioblastoma Progression Modeling and In-Silico Therapy Optimization**

## Rationale

Glioblastoma varies across tumor regions and can change during treatment. MRI can show changes in tumor-associated signals over time, while molecular measurements can describe differences in cell states. Mathematical models can represent tumor growth, treatment suppression, and drug-resistant cells. Prior research has studied MRI-based recurrence risk and mathematical-model-informed reinforcement learning for glioblastoma treatment scheduling, so this project will not claim that any one ingredient is new by itself.

This project will connect three research tracks without treating their datasets as if they came from the same people. Track A will supply frozen donor-level molecular prediction summaries; its mixed-cell Core/Periphery scores will not be treated as validated drug-resistance measurements. Track B will estimate associations between longitudinal MRI labels and ADC measurements in the restricted Duke dataset. Track C will model drug-sensitive and drug-resistant populations under assumed treatment suppression. The central mathematical contribution will be a held-out, scenario-based value-of-information simulation: it will test how hypothetical molecular and MRI/ADC signals change simulated outcomes when their links to resistance are uncertain. The patient MRI analysis and simulated treatment results will remain separate. The project will not establish a patient-level molecular-to-MRI link or provide treatment recommendations.

## Research Question, Hypotheses, Engineering Goals, and Expected Outcomes

### Research Questions

**Central mathematical question:** Using separate, unlinked molecular and MRI cohorts, how will assumptions about the signals’ relationships with latent drug resistance change the fused policy’s 730-day restricted mean time to simulated progression and treatment-course use, compared with the best development-selected fixed schedule and single-modality policy on held-out virtual patients?

1. **Patient MRI question:** Under observed care, will newly appearing nonenhancing FLAIR-labeled regions receive a next-visit automated necrotic or enhancing MRI label more often than persistent regions at the same distance from the current core?
2. **ADC question:** Will current ADC and its change from the prior visit improve patient-held-out prediction of that transition?
3. **Treatment-simulation question:** Under the predeclared moderate-concordance assumption, will the fused policy improve simulated progression time without greater treatment-course use than both development-selected comparators?
4. **Robustness question:** How will the paired differences change under independent, weak, moderate, and reversed signal-dependence assumptions?

### Hypotheses

1. Newly appearing FLAIR-labeled regions will have a higher next-visit automated core-label conversion rate than distance-matched persistent regions under observed care.
2. Adding ADC features will lower held-out patient-macro Brier score compared with the MRI and clinical baseline.
3. Under moderate concordance, the fused policy will have greater 730-day restricted mean time to simulated progression than both the best fixed schedule and best single-modality policy, without using more courses, under both progression definitions and both calibration families.
4. The simulated advantage of fusion will vary with the assumed signal-to-resistance dependence. I will test whether it will shrink, disappear, or reverse across the predeclared scenarios; I will not treat any scenario as an estimated biological relationship.

The study will report results that support, fail to support, or contradict these hypotheses.

### Engineering Goals and Expected Outcomes

The project will build an auditable patient-level MRI pipeline, quantify ADC’s additional predictive value, and connect the resulting signal distributions to a two-population simulator constrained by aggregate trial outcomes. It will compare fused, fixed, single-modality, and no-information policies on the same virtual patients. The analysis will test whether fusion improves, leaves unchanged, or worsens performance under different assumptions. I will label each finding as patient MRI evidence or conditional simulation evidence.

## Procedures

1. **Confirm permission and protect data.** Before analysis, I will verify that the Duke agreement and school or competition conditions allow the planned processing and reporting. If terms do not permit a step, I will omit it before data access. I will verify the archive checksum and NIfTI headers before reading voxel arrays, and I will stop on a failed hash or invalid header. I will store restricted data and patient-level outputs locally in access-controlled storage. I will not identify patients, contact them, or upload restricted files or predictions.

2. **Select the Duke cohort from metadata.** Before image or mask reads, I will use permitted metadata to select glioblastoma records whose released IDH field is “Negative.” I will report this only as the dataset-coded field, not as comprehensive IDH1/2 wildtype status. I will form windows from every eligible set of three adjacent visits with T1-c, FLAIR, and segmentation data; I will not skip visits. I will sort by relative scan date, require dates to be finite and increasing, record scan intervals and treatment events, and exclude unresolved windows before voxel reads. I will check prior-use overlap where permitted and will not claim independent confirmation if overlap cannot be established. I will retain surgery-between-visits windows in the primary analysis and report their exclusion as a sensitivity analysis. The study will estimate changes under observed care and will be retrospective.

3. **Register images and define labels and support.** I will rigidly align prior- and next-visit T1-c images to the middle-visit T1-c using image intensities only, then apply each transform to the other modalities and masks. I will accept registrations only if normalized cross-correlation is at least 0.80, the largest absolute Euler angle is at most 10 degrees, and translation length is at most 10 mm; these will be operational screens, not validated accuracy limits. I will not retry or manually adjust failures. Synthetic known-transform tests will check implementation. Released vessel-landmark errors will be reported only for visit pairs with matching landmarks; they will not be assumed available for every adjacent pair. I will use the registered 1 mm grid for the primary MRI transition analysis and run a prespecified 2 mm sensitivity analysis. Secondary PDE forecasts will use a 2 mm grid. Labels 1 and 3 will define the operational core, label 2 the FLAIR abnormality, and label 4 the cavity; I will not treat label 0 as normal tissue. I will define origin support from the prior and middle visits only. This will be an operational support region, not a validated brain mask. The next-visit image will be used only for evaluation alignment, not for support, features, tuning, or forecast generation. Target-core voxels outside support will count as misses.

4. **Measure the primary MRI transition.** Within the middle-visit label-2 FLAIR region, I will exclude a 2 mm margin around the operational core and cavity. I will classify regions as newly appearing if outside a 2 mm dilation of prior-visit label 2, and persistent if inside. I will use distance bands of 2–10, >10–20, >20–30, and >30 mm from the middle-visit core. At 1 mm resolution, a band will qualify only if each history-defined region contains at least 80 mm³, preserving the physical-volume threshold of 10 voxels on a 2 mm grid. I will apply this rule without viewing next-visit labels. Labels 1 or 3 will count as a recorded automated core label; this will not mean histologic tumor conversion. I will calculate the new-minus-persistent difference by band, weight each band by the smaller region volume, average bands and windows within each patient, and weight patients equally. I will use 10,000 patient-cluster bootstrap resamples. I will report scan intervals and treatment events. The primary estimate will describe association under observed care; I will report prespecified sensitivities by scan interval and recorded treatment status, and after excluding windows with surgery between the middle and next visit. I will run a label-free precision check across plausible conversion rates before opening next-visit labels; I will keep the thresholds fixed. These are practical decision thresholds, not a power calculation. I will stop inference below 20 eligible patients; with 20–34 patients or inadequate precision, I will report feasibility only. I will consider the hypothesis supported only with at least 35 patients, interval half-width no greater than 0.10, and a lower confidence bound above zero.

5. **Test ADC and run secondary forecasts.** I will compare a prespecified MRI/clinical logistic model with the same model plus current normalized ADC and ADC change, using five patient-grouped folds and training patients only for feature processing and model selection. The baseline will include distance, new-versus-persistent status, FLAIR intensity, scan interval, and recorded treatment history. I will report held-out patient-macro Brier scores and calibration summaries. This retrospective analysis will not be described as a prospective tool. Separately, I will compare the reaction-diffusion PDE with copy-last, fixed growth, volume-matched persistence, FLAIR-front, two-history shape, PDE-only, and a time-conditioned FiLM U-Net. All arms will use the same patients, folds, support, and targets; fitted settings will use training patients only. This forecast benchmark will remain secondary. A candidate will count as better only if every adjusted lower confidence bound for its paired Dice difference versus a required mask comparator is above zero. A count-only model will be evaluated only with Poisson deviance, count MAE, and Brier score for any new piece, never as a mask.

6. **Build the molecular and drug-suppression inputs.** I will use frozen Track A A5 donor-level summaries only as one hypothetical simulation input. A5 will not be treated as a validated molecular biomarker, drug-response measure, or estimate of sensitivity and specificity for resistance. In the primary simulation, I will use each score as a hypothetical probability of a correct signal under a balanced latent class and equal sensitivity and specificity. I will vary sensitivity and specificity independently over {0.50, 0.65, 0.80, 0.90, 1.00}, include an uninformative channel, and report asymmetric cases. I will model sensitive and resistant populations, growth, generic treatment suppression, and assumed switching or reversion. A modeled course will not represent a clinical dose or drug regimen. I will constrain two simulator families with aggregate trial outcomes: a single-response family and a mixture-response family. Each family will need at least 30 worlds that meet prespecified ranges: 143–186 days for median progression-free survival, 0.75–1.00 for the dose-dense hazard ratio, and 0.71–1.38 for the 12-versus-6-course hazard ratio. These checks will constrain simulated worlds, not validate individual predictions or treatment benefit.

7. **Construct virtual patients and compare policies.** I will combine separate Track A, Track B, and Track C distributions without matching people across datasets. I will use predeclared dependence assumptions: independent (correlations 0), weak (0.25), moderate (0.50), and reversed molecular-to-resistance dependence (−0.25, with other links at 0.25). These will be analyst-selected, not estimated biological correlations. A 500-particle belief-state controller will use the moderate prior in every scenario, evaluate 16 posterior draws across three future 28-day cycles, and apply only that first three-cycle block before replanning. It will not receive the hidden scenario or tumor parameters. I will compare it with development-selected fixed and single-modality policies, plus Track-C-only, late-fusion, Q-learning, no-information, and signal-shuffle controls. All policies will receive the same virtual patients, scan schedules, observation noise, and maximum 12 courses. A five-day course penalty will be a mathematical utility assumption, not a measured toxicity or time cost. I will tune policies on development simulations, freeze the protocol and seeds, then evaluate 1,000 held-out worlds per dependence scenario once. Non-moderate scenarios will be reported as robustness analyses; the primary simulated claim will be conditional on the moderate scenario.

8. **Apply stop rules and report results.** I will use the predeclared fallback for expected model failures and record each reason; I will not silently remove patients. On development simulations, I will double particle and prediction-draw counts and halve the integration step. If the primary policy contrast changes direction, I will report numerical instability and make no confirmatory simulation claim. I will report Monte Carlo uncertainty across held-out worlds. Missing required outputs, nonfinite values, or changed protocol hashes will make the comparison incomplete or stop it. Before scoring, I will run a full-size synthetic timing pilot that includes the 1 mm transition pipeline and all model arms; I will stop or amend the plan if projected runtime exceeds 1,000 hours. I will save predictions and settings before opening held-out targets, hash the protocol, code, folds, permitted inputs, and cohort, and report patient MRI findings separately from simulated treatment outcomes. I will report positive, null, or negative results and will not present simulation as treatment evidence for real people.

## Risk Assessment

The project will use de-identified MRI records under restricted access. Risks will include accidental disclosure and use beyond the data agreement. I will use access-controlled local storage, will not attempt re-identification, and will share only outputs allowed by the agreement and school or competition rules. No person will receive a diagnosis or treatment recommendation, and no drug will be administered. The simulator will not assess toxicity or quality of life. I will describe automated labels as MRI labels, not verified tumor boundaries.

## Data Analysis

I will treat the patient MRI transition and virtual-patient policy comparison as separate primary components with separate conclusions; I will not combine them into one pass/fail claim. For the patient transition analysis, I will report the equal-patient difference in next-visit automated core-label rate between newly appearing and persistent FLAIR-labeled regions under observed care, with a 95% patient-cluster bootstrap interval. For ADC, I will report paired held-out patient-macro Brier scores and calibration summaries. For the secondary mask forecasts, I will report patient-macro Dice and simultaneous paired intervals for each required comparator.

For simulation, I will calculate 730-day restricted mean time to progression under two outcomes: first reaching 1.2 times starting burden, and a scan-based approximation at 84-day scans that calls progression when burden reaches 1.4 times the greater of the prior scan nadir or 0.10 times starting burden. This will be a model approximation, not formal RANO. I will define ΔRMST as fused-policy RMST minus comparator RMST and Δcourses as fused-policy course use minus comparator course use. Positive ΔRMST will favor fusion; Δcourses at or below zero will indicate no greater simulated course use, not lower toxicity.

In the moderate-concordance scenario, I will compare fusion with the development-selected fixed schedule and single-modality policy using simultaneous 95% max-|t| bootstrap intervals across 16 contrasts: eight ΔRMST contrasts and eight endpoint-specific Δcourse contrasts across both progression definitions and both calibration families. The primary simulation rule will require every adjusted ΔRMST lower bound to exceed zero, every adjusted Δcourse upper bound to be at or below zero, and successful calibration in both families. I will label independent, weak, and reversed scenarios as robustness analyses, not additional confirmatory tests. Bootstrap resampling will preserve each simulation world and its related observations as one unit. I will report whether conclusions remain stable under the predeclared numerical convergence checks.
