# Search finds no exact hybrid, but strong threats remain

No released method found in this search combines all four parts of the planned Track B B2.1 design: multi-timepoint patient MRI, a low-dimensional tissue-aware reaction-diffusion prior, a training-only expected-additions estimate, and a residual shrunk by its stability. The nearest real-patient PDE work, GliODIL, forecasts recurrence from one pre-operative timepoint and has no neural residual ([GliODIL arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05063v4)). The nearest PDE-plus-residual architecture was tested only on 387 synthetic trajectories ([arXiv 2607.13877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13877), snippet). Three threats limit any gap claim. In PREDICT-GBM, a plain U-Net reaches 79.37% enhancing-recurrence coverage, against 78.91% for GliODIL ([PREDICT-GBM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13360)). No paper read reports persistence (copy-last-mask) Dice by horizon. Petersen's Query Volume Dice gives the true future size to the scorer ([arXiv 2106.12917 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.12917v2)). Glioma papers predict new-lesion location as voxel risk maps, but this search found no glioma model that forecasts an expected count of new lesions. Count forecasting exists in MS and in brain metastases ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v172/durso-finley22a.html); [PMC8008166](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8008166)). The evidence is weak. The notes came from about 41 tool calls in total (14, 13, 7 and 7 across four topics), almost all at the abstract or snippet level, with one full-text PDE read. Not finding a method is not evidence that it does not exist, so no "first" claim is supported. Whether any gap is novel is subjective. Nothing here supports a validated digital twin or any clinical benefit.

## Two benchmarks fix the bar: GliODIL and PREDICT-GBM

The closest real-patient PDE work does not forecast from a time series. **GliODIL** (Balcerak et al., Nat Commun 2025) fits a discrete PDE with a data loss to one pre-operative scan (T1, T1Gd, FLAIR, T2, atlas WM/GM maps, and FET-PET in 58 patients). It scores the result as coverage of the later recurrence by an iso-volumetric treatment volume. Coverage rose from **63.59% to 67.80%** in 152 patients without PET, and from 70.04% to 72.94% in the 58 with PET ([GliODIL arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05063v4)). The weakest point: one timepoint makes the inverse problem ill-posed, the spread across patients is large, and some patients did worse than the standard plan. Its deviation from the PDE sits in the fitted field, not in a learned residual. The code is at github.com/m1balcerak/GliODIL; the license is UNVERIFIED. **PREDICT-GBM** is a multi-centre benchmark of 243 patients on the abstract page and 255 in a snippet, a discrepancy left unresolved. It uses the same fixed-volume scoring, and there the pure U-Net ties GliODIL within 0.5 percentage points ([PREDICT-GBM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13360)). The code is at github.com/BrainLesion/PredictGBM. Split details are UNVERIFIED. The implication: a hybrid must beat both a learned-only and a PDE-only baseline, not just the PDE.

The mechanistic longitudinal line of Hormuth and Yankeelov forecasts true future scans but has no learned part. In 9 patients, a model calibrated before and during radiotherapy predicted 3-month enhancing volume with a **median error of -2.5%** ([Hormuth 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8055874)). A 2025 study reports Dice above 0.87 during radiotherapy and above 0.74 after it (snippet; n UNVERIFIED) ([Hormuth 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12815495)). No code release was found for this group. Several other PDE-learning works solve the inverse problem from a single snapshot and do not forecast. These are Learn-Morph-Infer ([arXiv 2111.04090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04090v3)), the GBM PINN ([arXiv 2311.16536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16536)), Lipková's Bayesian calibration ([arXiv 1807.00499](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.00499v3)) and Subramanian's ensemble inversion in 216 GBM ([arXiv 2106.06016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.06016.pdf)). A CNN-corrected reaction-diffusion model forecasts spread only on synthetic tumors placed in real brains ([arXiv 2111.13404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13404)).

## Pure-DL forecasters report about 0.7 Dice without a persistence baseline

Pure deep-learning comparators report future-mask Dice of about 0.68 to 0.76. The test sets are tiny or the metric is oracle-assisted. **TaDiff** reports DSC 0.719 ± 0.13 on just **5 test patients**, compared only with its own ablations. Its Dice falls from **0.85 at about 0.5 months to 0.46 at 24 months or more**, so pooled Dice depends on the mix of intervals ([TaDiff](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05406)). Kebaili et al. report 0.683–0.736 on 7 test patients and 0.759 on a private set of 5; about half of the follow-up labels are nnU-Net auto-segmentations ([arXiv 2509.10824](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10824v1)). Neither paper reports a copy-last-mask baseline. GP-GAN is said to reach Dice 78.97% with Jaccard 88.26%, but Jaccard cannot exceed Dice, so these figures are inconsistent and UNVERIFIED ([GAN review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07081)).

Petersen's work is the only line found that uses a no-change reference, and its metric is the main scoring threat. Query Volume Dice picks the sample whose volume matches the true future volume, which gives future size to the scorer. Under that metric, the 2021 model scores **0.714 against a mean consecutive-scan overlap of 0.67**, a margin of about 0.04 (our inference, not reported as a test) ([arXiv 2106.12917](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.12917v2)). The 2019 model beat its "B to B" no-change bound only on 13 large-change cases ([ar5iv 1907.04064](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.04064)). **No paper read reports persistence Dice by horizon.** It follows that a reported Dice near 0.72 at short horizons does not clearly beat persistence (UNVERIFIED for each cohort). A Track B comparison should report plain Dice, with no size oracle, next to persistence Dice in each horizon bin.

## New-lesion counts are forecast in MS and metastases, not glioma

Glioma work predicts where recurrence will appear, inside the peritumoral edema, from pre-operative imaging, and scores the result as a voxel or region map. Metz et al. reached AUC about 0.9 with free-water-corrected DTI in 35 patients ([PMC7140058](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7140058/)). Kwak et al. report a high-risk vs low-risk odds ratio of 8.62 under leave-one-site-out validation (conference value, snippet) ([SPIE](https://astronomicaltelescopes.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/journal-of-medical-imaging/volume-11/issue-05/054001/Predicting-peritumoral-glioblastoma-infiltration-and-subsequent-recurrence-using-deep-learningbased/10.1117/1.JMI.11.5.054001.full)). Neither scores lesion-wise detection or count error. Background rates make a count target sparse: multifocal recurrence occurs in **4–14%** of GBM ([DGNC 2017](https://www.egms.de/static/en/meetings/dgnc2017/17dgnc195.shtml)).

Count forecasting is established in nearby diseases. Durso-Finley et al. forecast future new or enlarging T2 lesion counts in MS from 1817 MRIs from proprietary trials, scored by average precision on a binarized count ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v172/durso-finley22a.html)). Brain-metastasis velocity models forecast the rate of new metastases ([PMC8008166](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8008166)), and a nomogram built on 1354 patients across 8 centres estimates distant brain failure from clinical inputs ([PMC5667906](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5667906)). A direct query for "expected number" of new glioma lesions returned only calibration work at the voxel-probability level ([arXiv 2609.12435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12435); [arXiv 2509.10824](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.10824)). One of those, arXiv 2609.12435 on MU-Glioma-Post, should be checked for overlap with this project's own work before anyone cites it.

## The stability-shrunk residual has parts in the literature but no glioma assembly

Each part of the proposed residual exists somewhere, but this search did not find them combined in a glioma model. A Bayesian discrepancy term for glioblastoma progression reduces parameter spread ([arXiv 2509.18998](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.18998); authorship UNVERIFIED). Boosting for PINNs applies shrinkage coefficients to staged residual corrections ([arXiv 2607.23940](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23940)). Stability selection is used for PDE term identification ([arXiv 2605.11524](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11524)). Perturbation ensembles after registration appear as a robust ADC biomarker, not as a growth forecast ([ISMRM 2012](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/12MProceedings/PDFfiles/0185.pdf)). SoC-DT benchmarks a "PDE prior with neural correctors"; its details are UNVERIFIED and it should be read first ([arXiv 2510.03287](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03287)). Studies of input perturbation for glioma PDEs mostly treat mask noise as likelihood noise ([PMC6633916](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6633916)). Lê's thesis sampled plausible segmentations, but its effect size is UNVERIFIED ([HAL](https://dumas.ccsd.cnrs.fr/ASCLEPIOS/tel-01376688v2)). No study found reports how forecast Dice changes when tissue maps, DTI or registration are perturbed. The steelman view is that the narrow combination may be unreported only because each part is routine. It may also exist in work these searches did not reach.

## Conclusion

A narrow gap may remain, but it is unconfirmed and small, and a result counts only if it holds on fair scoring. In practice, the B2.1 design must beat four baselines: persistence, a PDE-only model, a pure U-Net (the PREDICT-GBM tie), and a training-only count baseline such as a fold-mean rate. It must do so on held-out patients, with plain Dice reported by horizon and no future-size oracle. If the hybrid only ties the U-Net, as GliODIL did, the PDE prior adds interpretability, not accuracy, and the write-up should say so.
