# Track B FLAIR-transition prior-art update (2026-11-07)

## Scope and status

This targeted primary-source check concerns the proposed Duke question: whether newly appearing nonenhancing FLAIR regions differ from persistent regions in later core-label transition, and whether local ADC trajectory adds predictive information. No patient data were read or scored. The search is not systematic and does not establish priority.

## Closest sources checked

- [Bertalan et al. (2026)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41791182/) retrospectively mapped later contiguous T1 enhancement back to baseline peri-enhancing regions, separated regions by baseline FLAIR signal, and compared ADC and CBV in recurrence-prone versus remaining regions. Their final analysis included 46 patients with local recurrence. This is close precedent for spatially matched later enhancement and ADC analysis, although the abstract does not describe a newly appearing versus persistent FLAIR comparison or the proposed repeated-visit ADC trajectory. The full text was inaccessible in this check; inspect it before making a precise novelty claim.
- [Matsuda et al. (2022)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/nmc/62/1/62_oa.2021-0182/_article/-char/en) studied 21 patients and compared preoperative ADC in FLAIR regions according to later enhancement. Regions removed at surgery were separately identified. This predates any broad claim that peritumoral ADC predicts where enhancement later appears. The accessible source supplied the abstract; detailed methods remain to be checked.
- [Zhang et al. (2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12883-026-05473-5), published online October 8, used longitudinal T1CE, FLAIR and ADC to quantify voxel-level changes in imaging-defined habitats and relate them to recurrence. The available publisher page is an early accepted version with abstract only, and the authors describe a retrospective single-center study without external validation. The abstract does not establish that it tests the proposed new-versus-persistent FLAIR contrast.

## Consequences for the Duke proposal

An additional targeted search found older related longitudinal work. [Lutz et al. (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24115401/) examined ADC histograms in glioblastoma with T2 progression versus stable T2 signal and discussed subsequent T1 progression. [Gupta et al. (2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21596805/) studied newly appearing restricted-diffusion lesions and later spatially concordant enhancement in treated glioblastoma. These are close conceptual precedents for serial diffusion and lesion evolution, not demonstrated matches to the proposed voxelwise new-versus-persistent FLAIR and distance-matched design. The accessible results here are abstracts or indexed excerpts; full methods, cohort overlap, and exact comparison units remain unverified.

Claims of first using longitudinal FLAIR, ADC, spatial recurrence mapping, or imaging habitats are unsupported. A possible narrower contribution is the predeclared, distance-matched comparison of **newly appearing versus persistent** FLAIR regions, with a separately tested incremental ADC trajectory. Its novelty remains unverified until full methods and citation relationships are checked. Automated labels and enhancement are imaging endpoints; they do not establish malignant-cell infiltration or a new biological mechanism.

Before using these sources to change a protocol, verify their full methods and denominators, check whether their cohorts overlap Duke or prior project data, and specify time origin, treatment context, registration, distance matching, patient-level validation and confounder handling. Any later protocol change must be dated and distinguish development from held-out scoring. A negative result remains reportable.
