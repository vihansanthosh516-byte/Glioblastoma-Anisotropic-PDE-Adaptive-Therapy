# Track B FLAIR-transition prior-art update (2026-11-07)

## Scope and status

This targeted primary-source check concerns the proposed Duke question: whether newly appearing nonenhancing FLAIR regions differ from persistent regions in later core-label transition, and whether local ADC trajectory adds predictive information. No patient data were read or scored. The search is not systematic and does not establish priority.

## Closest sources checked

- [Bertalan et al. (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12993171/) retrospectively mapped later contiguous T1 enhancement back to baseline peri-enhancing regions, separated regions by baseline FLAIR signal, and compared ADC and CBV in recurrence-prone versus remaining regions. The full methods were read through the [Europe PMC full-text XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12993171/fullTextXML). The final 46-patient, single-center analysis selected IDH-wildtype cases with local recurrence within 12 months; baseline imaging was treatment-naive and preoperative, while the second image was the first recurrence scan. The study did not implement the proposed new-versus-persistent FLAIR comparison across two history visits. It used rigid and affine registration, expert review, and occasional manual refinement, which included tumor boundaries. Its reported regional comparisons are descriptive and do not by themselves validate the Duke forecasting procedure. The institution differs from Duke; patient-level independence is not established by that fact alone.
- [Matsuda et al. (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8754683/) studied 21 patients and compared preoperative ADC in FLAIR regions according to later enhancement. The [full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8754683/fullTextXML) reports 1,844 regions, of which 186 later enhanced, 1,258 remained stable, and 400 were removed at surgery. All patients underwent gross total resection and chemoradiotherapy; one had an IDH1 mutation. Its reported ADC ROC analysis pooled regions, so the 1,844 regions must not be represented as 1,844 independent patients. This predates any broad claim that peritumoral ADC predicts where enhancement later appears; it does not establish a two-history new-versus-persistent FLAIR comparison.
- [Zhang et al. (2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12883-026-05473-5), published online October 8, used longitudinal T1CE, FLAIR and ADC to quantify voxel-level changes in imaging-defined habitats and relate them to recurrence. The available publisher page is an early accepted version with abstract only, and the authors describe a retrospective single-center study without external validation. The abstract does not establish that it tests the proposed new-versus-persistent FLAIR contrast.

## Consequences for the Duke proposal

An additional targeted search found older related longitudinal work. [Lutz et al. (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24115401/) examined ADC histograms in glioblastoma with T2 progression versus stable T2 signal and discussed subsequent T1 progression. [Gupta et al. (2011)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4040369/) studied restricted-diffusion lesions and later spatially concordant enhancement in treated glioblastoma. Indexed full-text excerpts describe a selected cohort of 27 patients with nonenhancing low-ADC lesions, after exclusions including hemorrhage, immediate postoperative changes and clinical ischemia. This is not an unselected forecast population. These are close conceptual precedents for serial diffusion and lesion evolution, not demonstrated matches to the proposed voxelwise new-versus-persistent FLAIR and distance-matched design. The Gupta details here come from indexed excerpts; its full methods and exact comparison units, Lutz's full methods, and cohort overlap remain unverified.

Claims of first using longitudinal FLAIR, ADC, spatial recurrence mapping, or imaging habitats are unsupported. A possible narrower contribution is the predeclared, distance-matched comparison of **newly appearing versus persistent** FLAIR regions, with a separately tested incremental ADC trajectory. Its novelty remains unverified until full methods and citation relationships are checked. Automated labels and enhancement are imaging endpoints; they do not establish malignant-cell infiltration or a new biological mechanism.

Before using these sources to change a protocol, verify the remaining full methods and denominators, check whether their cohorts overlap Duke or prior project data, and specify time origin, treatment context, registration, distance matching, patient-level validation and confounder handling. The Bertalan study's selection on observed recurrence also differs from a forecast cohort that includes patients without subsequent conversion; preserve that distinction. Any later protocol change must be dated and distinguish development from held-out scoring. A negative result remains reportable.
