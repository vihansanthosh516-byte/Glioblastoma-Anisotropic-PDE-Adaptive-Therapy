## What it is
A single-author science-fair project (regional fair, February 2027) in computational biology of glioblastoma. This part, Track C, is a computer simulation of temozolomide (TMZ) dosing. It uses no patient data. The simulated tumour has drug-sensitive and drug-resistant cells that compete (logistic growth). A course lasts 5 days inside a 28-day cycle. Scans are every 84 days. At most 12 courses are allowed.

A first version (October 2026) used a toy world that did not match real glioblastoma. Its best result, a gain of +24.7 days in time to progression over the best simple rule (95% CI 19.6 to 29.8; `output/trackc_sealed_containment/sealed_results.json`, ledger C4), rested on assumed numbers, and its stricter sealed hypothesis H1 failed in scripts 169, 171 and 173. This restart asks a narrower question. When the simulation is forced to match published trial numbers, what does it say about dosing? (a) How strong may the drug be? (b) Can any dosing rule that reads scans beat the best fixed schedule that uses the same mean number of courses? (c) Does the answer depend on how progression is called?

Everything is simulation on assumed parameter ranges, run on development seeds. No claim of clinical benefit, patient-specific effect or digital twin is made. No paper yet.

## Glossary
- **World**: one draw of the model's parameter ranges (500 per configuration). Patients are drawn inside a world. A world is "controlled" when it passes tests C and D (Results 1 and 2).
- **The model** (`src/trackc_sim.py`): sensitive cells and resistant cells grow logistically towards a shared capacity of 1 and compete. While the drug is given (the first 5 days of a 28-day cycle, one "course") it kills sensitive cells at rate k = m r and resistant cells at rate eps k. Every range is an assumption.
- **r**: growth rate per day (earlier range 0.001 to 0.05; a world shifts it). **m**: ratio of the kill rate to the growth rate of sensitive cells (earlier range 6 to 20). **f0**: share of the starting burden that is resistant (earlier range 0.001 to 0.1). **eps**: residual kill of resistant cells as a fraction of the sensitive kill (0 to 0.2). **c**: growth cost of resistance (fixed per world, 0 to 0.25). **B0**: starting burden as a share of capacity (a world sets the centre; the range is plus or minus 0.10).
- **Kill scale**: the factor by which a world multiplies the earlier range of m. Scale 1 is the earlier range; a scale of 0.135 gives m between about 0.8 and 2.7. "Strong kill" means a scale of 0.5 or more (a cut chosen after the replicate). In the mixture family the scale belongs to the non-responders (sampled between 0.02 and 0.3); responders keep the range 6 to 20, and their share q is a world parameter. The responder class is a threshold on m.
- **Standard arm**: six courses, one per cycle (`soc6`). In the mixed-cap configurations: 3 courses for 63% and 6 courses for 37% of the patients (`std_capmix`, my reading of two numbers from the trial report).
- **Fixed schedules** are set before treatment and ignore scans (open loop). `frontK`: a course in each of the first K cycles. `paced2`, `paced3`: a course every second or third cycle, up to 12 courses. `best_fixed`: the best of 56 open-loop schedules, chosen on separate patients.
- **Scan-reading rules** (scans every 84 days): `raw_*` switch the drug on and off against the first scan reading; `nadir_kK_onA` give K induction courses, then treat while the scan shows at least A times the smallest earlier reading; `resp_kK_thT` (response-guided) give K induction courses, then continue only while every scan shows at most T times the first reading, and stop for good at the first failure. In test E the readings carry noise; tests G, H and M are open loop and noise-free.
- **RANO-like call**: at a scan, the true burden is at least 1.4 times the smallest earlier scanned value (floor 0.1 times baseline). **Baseline-referenced call**: the true burden reaches 1.2 times baseline, detected continuously. After a call the drug stops and nothing more is given.
- **Restricted mean time**: the mean of min(time to progression, horizon), with a horizon of 365 or 730 days. A "gap" is a difference of restricted mean times.
- **Informed controller**: for each patient, the best of 56 open-loop schedules, chosen with the true parameters. It cannot be built; it measures room.
- **Frontier, equal drug use**: the upper concave envelope of the (mean courses, restricted mean time) points of the fixed schedules. The gap of an arm is its restricted mean time minus the frontier at the arm's own mean number of courses.
- **Paired tail**: the mean of the worst 10% of the per-patient differences in restricted time between an arm and the standard arm.

## Scope and statistics
- **Published targets** (`paper/trackc_calibration_targets_2026-11-03.md`): standard-arm median progression-free survival (PFS) 5.5 months, 95% CI 4.7 to 6.1, which is 143 to 186 days (RTOG 0525, Gilbert 2013); dose-dense against standard TMZ, hazard ratio 0.87, 95% CI 0.75 to 1.00 (same trial); 12 against 6 cycles among patients still progression-free after cycle 6, hazard ratio 0.99, 95% CI 0.71 to 1.38 (GEINO 14-01, Balaña 2020, a phase II trial).
- **Worlds.** A world is one draw of the model parameters from assumed ranges (500 per configuration). A world is "controlled" if it reproduces the standard-arm median and passes both negative controls and still does so on fresh simulated patients.
- **Five configurations.** `single` (first run), `single_rep` (same family, fresh seeds), `mixture` (a fraction of responders with strong kill, the rest weak; chosen after the single-family result), `single_mix` and `mixture_mix` (the standard arm is limited to 3 courses for 63% and 6 courses for 37% of the patients, my reading of two numbers in the trial report).
- **Two ways to call progression, both reported.** RANO-like: true burden at least 1.4 times the smallest earlier scanned value, checked only at scans. Baseline-referenced: at least 1.2 times baseline, detected continuously. Which one is primary is an open decision in the design.
- **Scores.** Restricted mean time to progression up to 365 or 730 days, paired across arms on the same simulated patients. "Equal drug use" means: compare an arm with the best fixed schedule at the arm's own mean number of courses (upper concave envelope of the fixed schedules; the gap is the arm minus that envelope; the envelope also credits mixing fixed schedules across patients, which is generous to fixed dosing).
- **Statistics.** p-values are descriptive and not corrected for the number of looks. With 11 worlds the smallest possible two-sided sign-test p-value is 0.001. The progression endpoint is computed on the true burden. In test E the scan-reading rules see noisy readings (noise sd 0.05, 0.10 or 0.20, drawn per patient); tests G, H and M are open loop and noise-free.
- **Seeds.** All runs use development seeds from a registry (`output/trackc_seed_registry.json`). The earlier test sets (H-2, H-3) and the sealed seeds of scripts 167 to 173 are spent. No sealed run has been done in this restart, and I recommend none for now. A sealed run would need my decision and an amendment of the 2026-10-31 stop rule (design section 6, item 1), and the weak points listed below would have to be settled first.

## Result 1 (test C: which worlds reproduce the standard arm)
Of 500 sampled worlds, 81 (16.2%) reproduce the standard-arm median in the first run and 74 (14.8%) in a replicate with fresh seeds. The passing worlds have weaker kill than the average world: the median kill scale (see the glossary) is 0.135 against 0.223 for all 500 worlds, and 2 of the 116 worlds with a kill scale of 0.5 or more pass in the first run (8 of 116 in the replicate, whose passers have a median scale of 0.151). The earlier ranges, unchanged, give a median of 240 days with 6 courses. Source: `output/trackc_stage2_C_calibration/results.json`, `..._rep/results.json`; ledger C6, C11.
Weakest points: the median alone does not force a weak drug, and the result depends on how many courses a patient can get. With 3 courses for everyone, 170 worlds pass and 58 of them (34%) have a kill scale of 0.5 or more (with 4 courses: 42 of 150; with 6 courses: 8 of 74, replicate), and the unchanged earlier ranges give a median of 157 days, inside the window. The sampling error of these medians is about 10 days (ledger C12). With the mixed cap, 121 of 500 pass and 40 of the 115 strong-kill worlds are among them (ledger C16). The passing set also changes with the progression rule (overlap 0.03 between the 1.4 and 1.25 factors) and with the median convention; calibration alone admits strong-kill worlds in several variants (replicate: 11 of 62 passers with the 1.25 factor, 3 of 45 with the old 1.2-times-baseline rule); what removes them is the second step, the negative controls (Result 2). One published median fixes one condition: a second point of the standard-arm curve (a PFS rate at 6 or 12 months) was not found in a verified source (targets file, section H item 8), so the shape of the simulated curve is not checked. So "the drug is weak in the controlled worlds" is a statement about this model class, this exposure assumption and the two controls, not about glioblastoma.

## Result 2 (test D: negative controls)
Of the worlds that pass test C, 39 (first run) and 34 (replicate) also give model hazard ratios inside both published intervals and stay in the window on fresh patients. The 12-versus-6 control binds: 41 of 81 kept worlds meet it. In the controlled worlds the median kill scale is 0.046 to 0.100 across the five configurations (for the mixture family: the non-responders), and none of the 129 controlled worlds has a scale of 0.5 or more (largest 0.445, in the single family; the non-responder scale of the two mixture configurations is sampled only between 0.02 and 0.3, so there the statement holds by construction; ledger C7, C11, C17 and the `controls.csv` files). The other published landmark estimate (Blumenthal 2017, hazard ratio 0.80, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.98, observational) admits 3 to 16 more worlds per configuration and leaves this unchanged: 1 of 168 worlds has a kill scale of 0.5 or more (post hoc; ledger C23). Source: `output/trackc_stage2_D_negative_controls*/controls.csv`.

The dose-dense arm multiplies the drug term by 2 (assumed; 80, 65 and 48 worlds pass at multipliers 1.5, 2.0 and 3.0). The Cox interval of a model hazard ratio is not a confidence interval.
