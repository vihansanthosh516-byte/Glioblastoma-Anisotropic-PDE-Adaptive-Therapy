# Overview for an independent rating (Track C, treatment-policy simulation), 2026-11-04

You know nothing about this project. Rate it honestly. I want hard feedback, not praise. Each result gives its source file; interim numbers are marked. This is a dated snapshot written while some runs were still going (listed under "Pending"). `START_HERE.md` governs current status; the full text is `paper/trackc_stage2_report_2026-11-04.md`; the numbered findings are rows C1 to C26 of `vault/PAPER_FINDINGS_LEDGER.md`.

## What it is
A single-author science-fair project (regional fair, February 2027) in computational biology of glioblastoma. This part, Track C, is a computer simulation of temozolomide (TMZ) dosing. It uses no patient data. The simulated tumour has drug-sensitive and drug-resistant cells that compete (logistic growth). A course lasts 5 days inside a 28-day cycle. Scans are every 84 days. At most 12 courses are allowed.

A first version (October 2026) used a toy world that did not match real glioblastoma. Its best result, a gain of +24.7 days in time to progression over the best simple rule (95% CI 19.6 to 29.8; `output/trackc_sealed_containment/sealed_results.json`, ledger C4), rested on assumed numbers, and its stricter sealed hypothesis H1 failed in scripts 169, 171 and 173. This restart asks a narrower question. When the simulation is forced to match published trial numbers, what does it say about dosing? (a) How strong may the drug be? (b) Can any dosing rule that reads scans beat the best fixed schedule that uses the same mean number of courses? (c) Does the answer depend on how progression is called?

Everything is simulation on assumed parameter ranges, run on development seeds. No claim of clinical benefit, patient-specific effect or digital twin is made. No paper yet.

## Scope and statistics
- **Published targets** (`paper/trackc_calibration_targets_2026-11-03.md`): standard-arm median progression-free survival (PFS) 5.5 months, 95% CI 4.7 to 6.1, which is 143 to 186 days (RTOG 0525, Gilbert 2013); dose-dense against standard TMZ, hazard ratio 0.87, 95% CI 0.75 to 1.00 (same trial); 12 against 6 cycles among patients still progression-free after cycle 6, hazard ratio 0.99, 95% CI 0.71 to 1.38 (GEINO 14-01, Balaña 2020, a phase II trial).
- **Worlds.** A world is one draw of the model parameters from assumed ranges (500 per configuration). A world is "controlled" if it reproduces the standard-arm median and passes both negative controls and still does so on fresh simulated patients.
- **Five configurations.** `single` (first run), `single_rep` (same family, fresh seeds), `mixture` (a fraction of responders with strong kill, the rest weak; chosen after the single-family result), `single_mix` and `mixture_mix` (the standard arm is limited to 3 courses for 63% and 6 courses for 37% of the patients, my reading of two numbers in the trial report).
- **Two ways to call progression, both reported.** RANO-like: true burden at least 1.4 times the smallest earlier scanned value, checked only at scans. Baseline-referenced: at least 1.2 times baseline, detected continuously. Which one is primary is an open decision in the design.
- **Scores.** Restricted mean time to progression up to 365 or 730 days, paired across arms on the same simulated patients. "Equal drug use" means: compare an arm with the best fixed schedule at the arm's own mean number of courses (upper concave envelope of the fixed schedules; the gap is the arm minus that envelope; the envelope also credits mixing fixed schedules across patients, which is generous to fixed dosing).
- **Statistics.** p-values are descriptive and not corrected for the number of looks. With 11 worlds the smallest possible two-sided sign-test p-value is 0.001. The true burden is used, with no measurement error.
- **Seeds.** All runs use development seeds from a registry (`output/trackc_seed_registry.json`). The earlier test sets (H-2, H-3) and the sealed seeds of scripts 167 to 173 are spent. No sealed run has been done in this restart, and I recommend none.

## Method rules
The design (`paper/trackc_anchored_spec_2026-11-04.md`) was committed before the runs. Each run writes a manifest first (git commit, code hashes, seeds from the registry, clean-tree flag). I read each result back from disk before writing it down. Tests run in a scratch copy of the committed code. Two read-only checkers and one hostile reader compared the report with the files; they found wording errors and I corrected them. Post hoc choices are listed at the end.

## Result 1 (test C: which worlds reproduce the standard arm)
Of 500 sampled worlds, 81 (16.2%) reproduce the standard-arm median in the first run and 74 (14.8%) in a replicate with fresh seeds. The passing worlds need a weak drug: the median kill scale is 0.135 against 0.223 for all worlds, and only 2 of the 116 worlds with a kill scale of 0.5 or more pass. The earlier ranges, unchanged, give a median of 240 days with 6 courses. Source: `output/trackc_stage2_C_calibration/results.json`, `..._rep/results.json`; ledger C6, C11.
Weakest points: the result depends on how many courses a patient can get. With 3 courses for everyone, 170 worlds pass and 58 of them have a kill scale of 0.5 or more (with 6 courses: 74 and 8), and the unchanged earlier ranges give a median of 157 days, inside the window. The sampling error of these medians is about 10 days (ledger C12). With the mixed cap, 121 of 500 pass and 40 of 115 strong-kill worlds pass (ledger C16). The passing set also changes with the progression rule (overlap 0.03 between the 1.4 and 1.25 factors) and with the median convention; the weak-kill statement holds in every variant tried. One published median fixes one condition: a second point of the standard-arm curve (a PFS rate at 6 or 12 months) was not found in a verified source (targets file, section H item 8), so the shape of the simulated curve is not checked. So "the drug must be weak" is a statement about this model class and this exposure assumption, not about glioblastoma.

## Result 2 (test D: negative controls)
Of the worlds that pass test C, 39 (first run) and 34 (replicate) also give model hazard ratios inside both published intervals and stay in the window on fresh patients. The 12-versus-6 control binds: 41 of 81 kept worlds meet it. In the controlled worlds the median kill scale is 0.046 to 0.100 across the five configurations (for the mixture family: the non-responders), and none of the 129 controlled worlds has a scale of 0.5 or more (largest 0.445; ledger C7, C11, C17 and the `controls.csv` files). The other published landmark estimate (Blumenthal 2017, hazard ratio 0.80, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.98, observational) admits 3 to 16 more worlds per configuration and leaves this unchanged: 1 of 168 worlds has a kill scale of 0.5 or more (post hoc; ledger C23). Source: `output/trackc_stage2_D_negative_controls*/controls.csv`.
Weakest points: the model's hazard ratio for 12 against 6 courses is below 1 in all kept worlds (median 0.73 in the first run) and never near the trial's 0.99; the controlled worlds have model hazard ratios of 0.71 to 0.92, so they are the least bad worlds and not good matches. The dose-dense arm multiplies the drug term by 2 (assumed; 80, 65 and 48 worlds pass at multipliers 1.5, 2.0 and 3.0). The Cox interval of a model hazard ratio is not a confidence interval.

## Result 3 (response depth, MGMT anchor, numerics)
The controlled single-family worlds predict no patient with a 65% fall in tumour burden (0.0 in all 39 worlds), while the earlier ranges give 44.6%. The mixture family predicts 3.5% (range over 11 worlds 0.1% to 11.0%), and the mixed-cap mixture 3.3% (ledger C9, C15, C18). Published response rates under adjuvant TMZ are not on file in verified form (a literature scout suggested about one patient in ten; I did not open those pages), so this is a direction check and not a validated number. For the MGMT-stratified published medians, only 5 of 110 kept mixture worlds match them together with the methylated fraction, and none of the 11 controlled worlds does; in the mixed-cap mixture 6 of 151 and none of 20 (ledger C14, C19). The mapping "responder = methylated" is an assumption. A smaller integration step (0.10 instead of 0.25 days) changes no progression call in any controlled world (ledger C10, C20).

## Result 4 (policy contrasts and the frontier at equal drug use)
Arms: the standard six courses; fixed schedules; raw on/off rules; nadir rules (a course unless the burden has fallen); response-guided stopping rules; and an "informed" controller that knows every patient's parameters and picks the best of 56 open-loop schedules per patient. The informed controller cannot be built; it measures the room above fixed dosing.
RANO-like rule, 730 days, median gap to the best fixed schedule at equal mean courses:
- `single` (39 worlds): nadir rules -1.3, -1.8 and -2.9 days (below in 37, 37 and 39 of 39 worlds); raw rules 0.0; informed +29.5 days at 2.7 courses (ledger C22).
- `single_rep` (34 worlds): nadir rules -1.40, -1.29 and -2.45 days; raw rules 0.00; informed +29.7 days at 2.9 courses (ledger C25).
- `single_mix` (25 worlds, mixed-cap reference arm): nadir rules -1.08, -2.13 and -3.57 days; raw rules 0.00; informed +31.1 days at 2.9 courses (ledger C26).
- `mixture` (11 worlds): all nine scan-reading rules below the frontier in 11 of 11 worlds (-8.2 to -20.3 days); informed +40.9 days at 2.4 courses, in 10 of 11 worlds (ledger C21).
Baseline-referenced rule, 730 days: the informed controller is only +0.15 days (`single`), +0.27 days (`single_rep`), +0.31 days (`single_mix`) and +5.1 days (`mixture`) above the frontier, and the best scan-reading rule is level with it. At 365 days (baseline-referenced rule) in the mixture family two early-stopping rules are +0.9 and +1.2 days above it (above in 11 and 9 of 11 worlds; uncorrected p = 0.001 and 0.004); the fixed-schedule frontier is sparse, so every gap above it is an upper bound.
Reading: no scan-reading rule tested beats fixed dosing at equal drug use. The only large room is for a controller that knows the patient's parameters, and its size depends on the progression rule (+29.5 to +40.9 days under the RANO-like rule, +0.15 to +5.1 days under the baseline-referenced rule). Source: `output/trackc_stage2_E_regime_map*/results.json`, `output/trackc_stage2_E_frontier*/frontier.json`.
Weakest points: simple rules only. The belief-based (particle-filter) rules of the first version were not run on these worlds. The frontier is built from about a dozen fixed schedules, so every gap above it is an upper bound. The informed controller uses the true parameters and true burden. In every world of the two single-family runs (73 worlds) the best fixed schedule is the front-loaded `front12`, so in those worlds more drug early always helps (in the mixture family it is front-loaded in 4 of 11 worlds and a spaced schedule in the others); that is consistent with the controls only because their model hazard ratios reach 0.92. The RANO-like rule uses the smallest earlier scan and is checked only at scans, so a controller that knows the patient can shape what the scans show; whether the large headroom is better disease control or a later call is the question of test M.

## Result 5 (test H: which patient-level quantity is worth knowing)
Mixture family, 11 controlled worlds, open loop, RANO-like rule, 730 days, cost of 0, 5 and 10 days of restricted time per course (assumed grid). Knowing every patient's parameters beats the best single fixed schedule by a median of 14.3, 25.1 and 34.9 days at the three costs. Knowing the responder class captures a median share of 0.50, 0.21 and 0.52 of that; the kill strength m alone 0.38, 0.20 and 0.49; the growth rate r alone 0.19, 0.09 and 0.18; the initial burden, the initial resistant fraction and the residual kill of resistant cells alone nothing (median gain -0.4 to 0.0 days). Source: `output/trackc_stage2_H_value_of_information_mixture/results.json`; ledger C24.
Weakest points: each quantity is known exactly and at once; the share is not monotone in the cost and I did not find out why; the responder class is an assumed split and is defined from the kill strength m, so class and m carry similar value by construction; group policies carry selection noise; open loop only; RANO-like rule only, so it describes the RANO-like headroom; controlled (weak-kill) worlds only; 11 worlds.

## Negative or failed findings
- The sealed claims of the first version failed: the stricter hypothesis H1 failed in scripts 169, 171 and 173; the worst-decile comparison used there is biased against feedback rules at few noise repeats (about 1 to 4 days), but the deficit remains at 16 repeats (ledger C4, C5).
- In worlds that match the published numbers, no scan-reading rule beats the best fixed schedule at equal drug use (Result 4).
- The weak-kill requirement depends on the course cap (Result 1). I cannot say the drug is weak in patients.
- The mixture family does not match the MGMT-stratified published medians in any controlled world (Result 3).
- The model never reproduces the point estimate of the 12-versus-6 trial (0.99); its hazard ratios are below 1.
- The controlled single-family worlds predict almost no deep responders.

## Post hoc choices (made after results were seen)
The kill-scale cut at 0.5 (after `single_rep`); the course-cap variants and the mixed cap (after `single_rep`); the mixture family (after the single-family result); the response-guided arms and the frontier question (after the first regime map); the second progression endpoint in the frontier text (after a number check found it missing); the Blumenthal sensitivity (after a hostile read); test M (after the frontier was read under both endpoints). The original test H (how many washout durations place the switching rates) was replaced before any result of the new H.
