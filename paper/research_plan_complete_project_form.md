# Final Research Plan: Three Hybrid Methods for Glioblastoma

This is one proposed project with three equally important, separate tracks. Each track creates and evaluates a hybrid method for a different GBM problem. The tracks do not share patients or form a patient-level digital twin. The plan describes the full intended work; earlier exploratory analyses already exist and are disclosed below and in the companion protocol. No result or novelty claim is assumed in advance.

## Rationale

Glioblastoma varies across patients, within tumors, and during treatment. Single-cell prediction can mistake donor identity or cell composition for tumor-region biology; MRI growth estimates can be unstable when visits are sparse, tumors shrink, and boundaries are uncertain; simulated treatment policies can appear effective because of favorable assumptions about drug response. Existing work already classifies GBM core versus periphery cells (Lopes & Vinga, 2020), tests cross-donor annotation (Fischer et al., 2024; Zhong et al., 2024), fits image-based reaction-diffusion models (Hormuth et al., 2021; Subramanian et al., 2020), models spatial segmentation uncertainty (Monteiro et al., 2020), forecasts post-treatment tumor-state proxies on the MU collection (Jung & Shin, 2026, preprint), and studies model-informed GBM treatment control (Liu et al., 2025; West et al., 2023). The project asks whether carefully combined methods add measurable value in these settings; none is assumed to be novel or better before testing.

Track A combines sparse gene selection, an inductive expression graph, donor-robust training, and confidence-based selective prediction. Track B combines a screened graph-diffusion spatial prior, an MRI residual, and a deterministic sensitivity gate to rank next-visit additions to source-provided tumor-core annotations. Track C combines a sensitive/resistant tumor model, particle-filtered resistance estimates, and a dose-limited on/off policy selected for lower-tail outcomes. Each full method is compared with its components and prespecified alternatives. The exact combinations are candidate contributions; a focused prior-art search is required before claiming novelty.

## Research Question and Hypotheses

**Research question:** Do three task-specific hybrid methods improve prediction or decision-making over their component methods and strongest prespecified, directly comparable alternatives when evaluated on held-out donors, patients, or simulated parameter scenarios?

**Track A hypothesis:** I hypothesize that sparse gene selection, inductive graph smoothing, and smooth worst-donor training together will reduce class-balanced held-out donor risk-versus-coverage area compared with each component ablation and the prespecified expression-based baselines.

**Track B hypothesis:** I hypothesize that a screened graph-diffusion prior combined with an MRI residual attenuated by fixed one-voxel boundary-perturbation sensitivity will improve patient-macro ROC AUC by at least 0.02 for ranking next-visit additions to supplied NETC+ET annotations in UCSD-PTGBM v3, compared with the strongest non-full model selected using MU development-only patient-grouped cross-validation.

**Track C hypothesis:** I hypothesize that a particle-filtered, resistance-aware on/off controller selected for lower-tail outcomes will increase worst-noise-level lower-decile simulated time to progression over its ablations and prespecified dose-limited baselines in the stated simulator; if rankings vary across scenarios, I will report that no single policy is preferred.

## Procedures

### Shared procedure

1. Make a source manifest before modeling. Record each release, exact files, checksums, data terms, identifiers, labels, preprocessing, and planned role. Do not join patients across sources without a documented crosswalk.
2. Record known prior exposure: the project has already examined UCSC donor/cell data, MU scans and splits, RHUH recurrence data, LUMIERE data, and MU MGMT analyses. All results on these same sources are exploratory, even when a new split or metric is used. Freeze the previously examined case and split list from the repository before new scoring.
3. Keep the three tracks separate. Use donor, patient, or simulated parameter scenario as the evaluation unit. Fit preprocessing, feature selection, tuning, calibration, and policy selection on training units only.
4. Freeze each track’s primary measure, baselines, eligibility rules, and failure rules before opening outer-fold scores. Compare all methods on the same cases and information. Report failed fits, exclusions, and non-estimable quantities.
5. For each track, calculate paired 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals over its independent evaluation units, with a max-T adjustment across that track’s prespecified comparisons. A within-study advantage is supported only when the adjusted interval for improvement is above zero against every required component ablation and baseline and the track’s data gates pass. Otherwise report the estimate and uncertainty as inconclusive. Small sample size and prior data exposure limit claims; no clinical effect or award outcome is assumed.

### Track A — donor-robust molecular and spatial prediction

1. Inventory the downloaded atlas files, assays, measured genes, source labels, donor/sample identifiers, and reuse terms. Resolve aliases only from source documentation and preserve the crosswalk. Do not guess whether two sample identifiers are the same donor.
2. Use source-labeled GBM Core versus GBM Periphery cells as the primary target. Exclude records without traceable target labels or donor identity. Report donor and class counts before fitting. Run the descriptive nested leave-one-donor-out evaluation only if at least six donors have both target classes. Make no inferential superiority claim unless at least ten eligible donors remain; below that, show donor-level estimates and label uncertainty as too large for a confirmatory claim.
3. Make outer leave-one-donor-out folds and inner donor-held-out folds. Keep all cells from a donor in one fold. Fit normalization, gene selection, graph construction, hyperparameter selection, and calibration on training donors only.
4. Fit the full hybrid: a sparse-gated softmax classifier, a symmetric 15-nearest-neighbor graph among training cells, a normalized pairwise probability-smoothness penalty, and a smooth worst-donor cross-entropy objective. For a held-out cell, blend its classifier probabilities with predictions from its nearest training cells. Do not connect held-out cells to one another or use donor identity, clinical outcome, or held-out label as a feature.
5. Compare with component ablations: remove the sparse penalty; remove graph smoothing and neighbor blending; replace the smooth worst-donor loss with the mean donor loss. Compare with multinomial logistic regression, a 15-nearest-neighbor vote, linear SVM, and gradient-boosted trees, using the same donor folds and a fixed inner-fold tuning budget. Add scANVI only if its training inputs and annotations can be fit within each training fold and a CPU pilot fits the compute budget. Select any primary baseline using inner training folds only; never choose it from outer-test scores. Include the existing attention model only if its inputs, folds, and tuning budget can be refit without using label-selected genes outside folds. Use cell-type-only and library/batch-metadata models as leakage diagnostics, not primary expression methods; report results within cell types when enough donors are available.
6. Rank each held-out cell by maximum predicted class probability. For each donor and each true class, calculate error among the retained cells at coverages 50% to 100%; if no cell of a class is retained at a coverage point, set that class's risk to 1. Average the two class-specific risks, integrate over coverage by the trapezoid rule, then average equally over donors. This class-balanced risk-versus-coverage area is the primary outcome; lower is better. Do not tune an abstention threshold for this threshold-free endpoint. Report full-coverage balanced accuracy, calibration, and within-cell-type and within-library results as secondary checks. When fewer than ten donors qualify, these are descriptive estimates only.
7. Test spatial transfer only as a separate secondary endpoint if the source provides measured spatial labels, specimen identity, assay-compatible genes, and permitted reuse. Hold out whole specimens. Do not use pseudozone predictions as spatial truth.
8. Stop scoring if donor identity, target-label meaning, or permitted data use cannot be established. Treat unstable donor rankings or failure to beat baselines as valid outcomes.

### Track B — next-visit tumor-core annotation-change ranking

1. Use MU-Glioma-Post only to fit the method and select the primary comparator. The cohort and prior splits were examined earlier, so all MU scores are development results. Use the exact TCIA UCSD-PTGBM v3 clinical workbook and CC BY 4.0 image packages for one locked external patient test. Include exact primary-diagnosis Glioblastoma rows, sort visits by numeric source suffix, reject duplicate visits, and stop if recorded acquisition-to-anchor chronology contradicts that order.
2. Select at most the first adjacent pair per patient with one standard segmentation and all four MRI modalities at both visits, with millimetre spatial units, matching within-visit and between-visit grids, and orthogonal 1 mm spacing. Eligibility depends only on metadata, files, and geometry, never on the size or direction of label change. Use the earlier visit as origin and the next available visit as target; report the actual interval and do not call this a fixed-day forecast.
3. Define the target as new NETC+ET voxels in the next standard segmentation. In the origin scan, use the full nonzero skull-stripped brain support as the risk set, excluding current NETC+ET and resection cavity. Report additions outside that risk set. Treat all results as prediction of source-annotation changes, not biological growth or clinical progression.
4. Build the spatial prior by solving a screened six-neighbor graph-diffusion equation on current brain support, with MRI-contrast conductance, a fixed permeability increase in current SNFH, and the current cavity blocked. Fit a ridge-logistic MRI residual from seven current-scan features; attenuate this residual using a fixed sensitivity gate based on recomputing the spatial prior after one-voxel erosion and dilation of the current core. This gate is a sensitivity heuristic, not calibrated uncertainty. Use no earlier transition, future interval, future count, or UCSD data in fitting.
5. Select the strongest non-full comparator among the prespecified ungated hybrid, MRI-only, diffusion-only, distance-only, and no-change scores by five-fold patient-grouped cross-validation on MU only. Fit the final model on eligible MU development patients. Commit the protocol and scoring code before reading UCSD segmentation arrays; verify the pinned workbook checksum; create a one-shot run marker; hash the selected MRI and segmentation files before scoring and verify they remain unchanged. Report patient-macro AUC and a paired patient bootstrap interval for the full model minus the selected comparator, along with the prespecified interval strata, cohort flow, zero-addition cases, failed pairs, and additions outside the risk set.
6. Count added value only if the lower bound of the 95% patient-bootstrap interval for the mean AUC gain exceeds 0.02. This project-set margin is not a clinical minimum important difference, and the metadata maximum of 48 repeat-eligible GBM patients is not a power calculation. If the criterion fails or the evaluable cohort is small, report no demonstrated gain or an imprecise result. Do not claim biological tumor growth, clinical utility, novelty, or superiority to all published methods.

### Track C — robust virtual treatment control

1. Use the existing two-compartment sensitive/resistant model with carrying capacity K=1. Use daily numerical integration with a 0.1-day step, but make treatment decisions at the start of each 28-day cycle. A treated cycle gives a five-day course at its start. Use assumed parameter ranges: growth rate ρ∈[0.001,0.05] day⁻¹, kill rate κ∈[0.02,0.08] day⁻¹, initial resistant fraction fR∈[0.001,0.1], resistance cost c∈[0,0.25], residual drug effect ε∈[0,0.2], and initial burden B0∈[0.05,0.5]. Use log-uniform draws for ρ, κ, and fR, and uniform draws for c, ε, and B0. These are virtual assumptions, not patient or drug measurements.
2. Define simulated progression as the first day true burden B(t) reaches 1.2B0, capped at a 1,456-day horizon. Since B0≤0.5 and K=1, the threshold is below K; verify reachability without treatment in every generated scenario. This is an in-simulator endpoint, not RANO or a clinical response rule. Give every policy a maximum of 12 five-day courses over the full horizon.
3. Observe one noisy log-burden value at each 28-day cycle start: yj=log B(tj)+εj, with independent εj∼N(0,σy²) and σy∈{0.05,0.10,0.20}. Use zero noise only for exact simulator checks. Generate 400 training, 200 filter-validation, and 2,000 held-out parameter scenarios. Evaluate each parameter scenario at all three noise levels with two noise replicates, using the same scenario IDs and observation streams for every policy. Keep all noise replicates of a scenario together in five-fold grouped cross-validation. Add four separate 1,000-scenario misspecification suites, using σy=0.10 and no tuning on those suites.
4. Build a 500-particle filter for hidden burden, resistant fraction, and static parameters. Resample systematically when effective sample size falls below 250. Apply Liu–West regularization with shrinkage 0.98 to standardized static-parameter particles, reflect proposals at prior bounds, and preserve current burden and resistant fraction after parameter jitter. On the separate filter-validation scenarios, require 80–97% coverage for nominal 90% intervals and, across five filter seeds, posterior resistance-tail probabilities within 0.10 at 90% or more of decision times before making a resistance-estimation claim.
5. Build the full controller as an on/off hysteresis rule. At each cycle start, compare filtered burden with the posterior reference burden from cycle zero. If treatment is off, start a five-day course when the ratio reaches a_on; if treatment is on, continue a course next cycle until the ratio falls to a_off. When posterior probability that fR≥b reaches 0.80, use threshold a_R instead of a_on. Tune a_on, a_off, a_R, and b on training scenarios; include a_R values both below and above a_on so the model determines the direction of the resistance mode. Enforce a_off below both possible start thresholds. Select thresholds by the five-fold out-of-fold lower-decile mean time-to-progression objective across all three noise levels.
6. Compare the hybrid with: filtered burden-only hysteresis; raw-observation burden-only hysteresis; a resistance-only mode; a fixed front-loaded schedule; an evenly paced schedule; the best periodic schedule selected on training scenarios; a threshold-based adaptive-therapy rule adapted from Zhang et al. (2017) to the same observation cadence and budget; mean-time threshold selection; and a risk-sensitive PPO policy with the same observations, resistance summaries, course history, remaining budget, and elapsed-cycle information. Train five PPO seeds and select seed/checkpoint on training-only out-of-fold performance. A true-state optimized schedule may be shown only as an unattainable upper-bound illustration, not as a competing policy. If the filter fails its calibration gate, retain the policy comparison but make no claim that hidden resistance was estimated reliably.
7. Before opening the 2,000 held-out scenarios, freeze the simulator, policy grids, seeds, and comparator selection. Compare policies on common scenarios and noise streams. The primary outcome is the minimum across the three noise levels of the held-out lower-decile mean (CVaR at 10%) of scenario-level time to progression, after averaging the two noise replicates within each scenario and noise level. Higher is better. Report per-noise CVaR, capped fraction, median time, burden integral, resistant fraction, and total courses secondarily. Report all four misspecification suites separately; call the policy robust across these tested departures only if its adjusted paired interval favors the strongest training-selected baseline in all four suites.
8. Use the prespecified strata fR,0<0.01 versus ≥0.01 and c<0.125 versus ≥0.125. A ranking reversal is reported only when paired intervals exclude zero in opposite directions in at least two strata. A within-simulator advantage requires the adjusted paired interval to favor the hybrid over each required component and baseline. Otherwise report scenario-dependent rankings or no preference. No result implies that a real drug works or that a treatment policy helps patients.

## Risk Assessment

This is secondary analysis of de-identified public files and computer simulation. No wet-lab materials, patient contact, or treatment recommendations are involved. Follow each source’s data terms; keep data local unless cloud transfer is explicitly allowed. Do not attempt re-identification or link patients across sources without documentation. Any result is a research model result, not medical advice.

## Data Analysis

Track A uses donor-level risk-versus-coverage area (lower is better). Track B uses the paired difference in patient-specific ROC AUC for ranking next-visit source-annotation additions, averaged equally across patients with both target classes; uncertainty is estimated by resampling patients. The primary comparison is against the non-full model selected only on MU development data. Track C uses worst-noise-level lower-decile simulated time to progression under a common dose budget (higher is better). Track B meets its project-added-value rule only if the two-sided 95% patient-bootstrap interval lower bound exceeds 0.02 AUC. This external score cannot establish clinical validity, fixed-horizon performance, adequate power, or superiority to all published methods.
