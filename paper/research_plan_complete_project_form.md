# Final Research Plan: Three Hybrid Methods for Glioblastoma

This is one proposed project with three equally important, separate tracks. Each track creates and evaluates a hybrid method for a different GBM problem. The tracks do not share patients or form a patient-level digital twin. The plan describes the full intended work; earlier exploratory analyses already exist and are disclosed below and in the companion protocol. No result or novelty claim is assumed in advance.

## Rationale

Glioblastoma varies across patients, within tumors, and during treatment. Single-cell prediction can mistake donor identity for tumor biology; MRI growth estimates can be unstable when visits are sparse and tumor boundaries are uncertain; and simulated treatment policies can appear effective because of favorable assumptions about drug response. Existing research already addresses donor-held-out single-cell annotation, reaction-diffusion tumor modeling, spatial segmentation uncertainty, and model-informed treatment control. The project therefore asks whether carefully combined methods add measurable value in these three settings, rather than treating any one model family as new by itself.

Track A combines sparse gene selection, an inductive expression graph, donor-balanced training, and selective prediction. Track B combines a reaction-diffusion model, a training-derived joint prior for growth parameters, and a spatially correlated boundary-error model. Track C combines a sensitive/resistant tumor model, a particle filter for hidden resistance, and a dose-limited policy optimized for poor outcomes across uncertain scenarios. Each full method is compared with its components and prespecified alternatives. The exact combinations are candidate contributions; focused prior-art review is required before claiming novelty.

## Research Question and Hypotheses

**Research question:** Do three task-specific hybrid methods improve prediction or decision-making over their component methods and prespecified alternatives when evaluated on held-out donors, patients, or simulated parameter scenarios?

**Track A hypothesis:** I hypothesize that sparse gene selection, inductive graph smoothing, and smooth worst-donor training together will reduce held-out donor risk-versus-coverage area compared with each component ablation and the prespecified expression-based baselines.

**Track B hypothesis:** I hypothesize that integrating a joint growth-parameter distribution with spatially correlated boundary uncertainty will improve held-out agreement with supplied tumor masks, measured by a spatial variogram score, over the same PDE without those components and over matched probabilistic persistence and volume-trend forecasts.

**Track C hypothesis:** I hypothesize that a particle-filtered two-trigger controller optimized for lower-tail outcomes will increase worst-noise-level lower-decile simulated time to progression over its ablations and prespecified dose-limited baselines in the stated simulator; if rankings vary across scenarios, I will report that no single policy is preferred.

## Procedures

### Shared procedure

1. Make a source manifest before modeling. Record each release, exact files, checksums, data terms, identifiers, labels, preprocessing, and planned role. Do not join patients across sources without a documented crosswalk.
2. Record known prior exposure: the project has already examined UCSC donor/cell data, MU scans and splits, RHUH recurrence data, LUMIERE data, and MU MGMT analyses. All results on these same sources are exploratory, even when a new split or metric is used. Freeze the previously examined case and split list from the repository before new scoring.
3. Keep the three tracks separate. Use donor, patient, or simulated parameter scenario as the evaluation unit. Fit preprocessing, feature selection, tuning, calibration, and policy selection on training units only.
4. Freeze each track’s primary measure, baselines, eligibility rules, and failure rules before opening outer-fold scores. Compare all methods on the same cases and information. Report failed fits, exclusions, and non-estimable quantities.
5. For each track, calculate paired 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals over its independent evaluation units, with a max-T adjustment across that track’s prespecified comparisons. A within-study advantage is supported only when the adjusted interval for improvement is above zero against every required component ablation and baseline and the track’s data gates pass. Otherwise report the estimate and uncertainty as inconclusive. Small sample size and prior data exposure limit claims; no clinical effect or award outcome is assumed.

### Track A — donor-robust molecular and spatial prediction

1. Inventory the downloaded atlas files, assays, measured genes, source labels, donor/sample identifiers, and reuse terms. Resolve aliases only from source documentation and preserve the crosswalk. Do not guess whether two sample identifiers are the same donor.
2. Use source-labeled GBM Core versus GBM Periphery cells as the primary target. Exclude records without traceable target labels or donor identity. Report donor and class counts before fitting. Require at least eight donors with both target classes to run the planned nested leave-one-donor-out comparison; if fewer are available, report a feasibility result only. A superiority claim additionally requires at least ten eligible donors.
3. Make outer leave-one-donor-out folds and inner donor-held-out folds. Keep all cells from a donor in one fold. Fit normalization, gene selection, graph construction, hyperparameter selection, and calibration on training donors only.
4. Fit the full hybrid: a sparse-gated softmax classifier, a symmetric 15-nearest-neighbor graph among training cells, a normalized pairwise probability-smoothness penalty, and a smooth worst-donor cross-entropy objective. For a held-out cell, blend its classifier probabilities with predictions from its nearest training cells. Do not connect held-out cells to one another or use donor identity, clinical outcome, or held-out label as a feature.
5. Compare with three matched ablations: remove the sparse penalty; remove both graph training and graph smoothing; replace smooth worst-donor loss with cell-weighted loss. Also compare with multinomial logistic regression using the same expression features. Include the existing attention model only if its inputs, folds, and tuning budget can be matched. Use donor-composition and cell-type/library metadata predictors only as leakage diagnostics, not as primary expression features.
6. Rank each held-out cell by maximum predicted class probability. The primary outcome is donor-averaged area under the risk-versus-coverage curve from 50% to 100% coverage; lower is better. Do not tune an abstention threshold for this threshold-free endpoint. Report macro-F1, calibration, and within-cell-type and within-library results as secondary checks.
7. Test spatial transfer only as a separate secondary endpoint if the source provides measured spatial labels, specimen identity, assay-compatible genes, and permitted reuse. Hold out whole specimens. Do not use pseudozone predictions as spatial truth.
8. Stop scoring if donor identity, target-label meaning, or permitted data use cannot be established. Treat unstable donor rankings or failure to beat baselines as valid outcomes.

### Track B — uncertainty-aware tumor growth forecasting

1. Build a patient-by-visit manifest from MU-Glioma-Post. Verify GBM diagnosis, scan dates, image-mask alignment, physical voxel spacing, label meaning, treatment context, and duplicate or missing visits. Define the tumor-volume rule once, before fitting. Keep all visits from a patient in one outer fold.
2. Require at least three ordered masks for a forecast. Use the first two masks to forecast the third and then use rolling-origin forecasts for later visits. One interval does not identify growth rate and diffusion separately: integrate their joint uncertainty using a prior fitted only on training patients, and make no separate patient-specific biological claim from that first forecast. Use additional intervals only for a separately gated parameter analysis.
3. Before patient scoring, generate at least 100 synthetic longitudinal mask series at observed visit intervals. Include spatial boundary displacement with standard deviations 0, 2, and 4 mm and correlation lengths 2, 5, and 10 mm, plus anisotropic and misspecified-growth cases. Test forecast calibration, parameter recovery where multiple intervals are available, numerical stability, failures, runtime, and peak memory. Freeze any resource-based adjustment before outer scores are opened.
4. Model latent concentration with the Fisher-Kolmogorov equation, K=1, initial concentration 1 inside the first mask and 0 outside, isotropic D in the primary fit, a 2 mm grid, a 0.05-day time step, a 30 mm padded domain, no-flux crop boundaries, and a visible contour at c=0.5. Keep D at or below 10 mm²/day so the stated explicit solver remains within its diffusion stability limit. Do not expand this bound after examining held-out fits.
5. Use a training-only empirical-Bayes prior over log growth rate and log diffusion. Combine it with a 21-by-21 logarithmic joint parameter grid and local 11-by-11 refinement. Fit a spatial Gaussian boundary-discrepancy model on 256 fixed surface anchors. Estimate its covariance settings from cross-fitted, rolling-origin training-patient forecast residuals, with each target mask excluded from fitting, and evaluate the same spatial process at forecast surfaces. Use grid-weighted parameter draws and correlated boundary-field draws; do not depend on a local Hessian approximation.
6. Compare the full method with: the same PDE using independent boundary errors; the PDE mean forecast with the same calibrated correlated-error wrapper; persistence; and a time-gap-aware volume-trend mask. Convert every deterministic baseline into a probability distribution using the same training-derived error wrapper. The volume-trend mask is made by shifting the prior mask’s signed-distance surface until its volume matches the linear volume extrapolation from prior scans, including possible shrinkage.
7. Score all eligible cases, including cases where observed volume shrinks. The PDE-only model may fail on shrinkage; do not remove such cases after seeing the results. Count numerical or non-estimable fits and use the prespecified persistence-plus-error fallback for a failed PDE forecast so every eligible patient remains in the primary comparison.
8. The primary outcome is a spatial variogram score on a fixed, distance-stratified set of voxel pairs within a 10 mm band of the supplied held-out boundary; lower is better. Use the held-out mask only to define the scoring locations and score, never as a model input. Report marginal Brier score, Dice, surface distance, volume error, interval coverage, and shrinkage-stratum performance secondarily. This evaluates agreement with supplied masks, not the true boundary or scanner noise.
9. Validate synthetic forecasts and parameter recovery. For separate ρ,D interpretation, require at least two fitting intervals, median absolute relative error at most 25% for each parameter, 80–97% coverage of nominal 90% parameter intervals, and no more than 10% of complete fits with any parameter bound hit. For forecast uncertainty, require 80–97% coverage of nominal 90% predictive intervals. These are project gates, not clinical standards. If a gate fails, report it and withhold the affected parameter interpretation; do not drop the patient from predictive scoring.
10. Keep the previously explored RHUH-GBM 15 mm recurrence-localization analysis as a secondary, retrospective spatial endpoint only. Use the public brain-extracted files if terms and paired labels are verified; call it localization at the observed recurrence interval, not prospective recurrence-time prediction. Keep the recurrence mask out of fitting and tuning for its patient. LUMIERE is excluded unless its dataset and non-commercial terms are reconciled.

### Track C — robust virtual treatment control

1. Use the existing two-compartment sensitive/resistant model with K=1, daily treatment decisions, and 0.1-day integration. Let B=S+R. Keep the inherited virtual ranges for growth, drug effect, resistance cost, residual effect, and resistant fraction, but restrict initial B/K to 0.08–0.65 so the 1.4-times-running-nadir endpoint is reachable below carrying capacity. These are simulation assumptions, not patient measurements.
2. Observe y=log(B)+Gaussian noise with positive standard deviations 0.05, 0.10, and 0.20. Use zero noise only for exact-simulator checks. Make 256 Latin-hypercube training scenarios and 512 disjoint evaluation scenarios. Use five-fold scenario-grouped cross-validation within the 256 training scenarios to select thresholds, horizon, PPO seed, and checkpoint. Evaluate every parameter scenario at all three noise levels with five noise replicates at each level, using identical scenario IDs and observation streams for every policy.
3. Build a 500-particle filter for hidden resistance and static model parameters. Resample systematically when effective sample size falls below 250. Apply Liu–West regularization with shrinkage 0.98 to standardized parameter particles, reflect proposals at prior bounds, and preserve current total burden and resistant fraction when reconstructing S and R after parameter jitter. Require 80–97% coverage for nominal 90% particle intervals and, across five filter seeds, posterior tail-probability range at most 0.10 at 90% or more of validation decision times before interpreting a policy.
4. Trigger treatment if observed burden relative to its running observed nadir, exp(y_t−min(y_s for s≤t)), reaches a in {1.05, 1.10, 1.20, 1.30}, or if posterior probability that resistant fraction reaches b in {0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35, 0.50} is at least 0.80. The true simulated progression endpoint is B_t ≥ 1.4 times its running true nadir. This is a simulator endpoint, not RANO or a clinical response rule.
5. Give every policy the same total active-dose-day budget: the number of active days in a repeated 5-days-on/23-days-off schedule over the frozen horizon. Compare the hybrid against burden-only and resistance-only triggers, a fixed 5/23 schedule, a point-estimate filter ablation, mean-time optimization, and a risk-sensitive PPO policy. Train and select every adaptive policy on the same training scenarios; give PPO the same noisy observations, dose history, posterior summaries, remaining dose budget, and elapsed-horizon information. Use five training seeds and select by training-only validation.
6. Select the horizon from 500, 1,000, and 1,500 days on training scenarios alone: use the shortest horizon where fewer than 90% remain progression-free under the fixed schedule. Stop the clock at progression or that horizon and record censoring. Before the full run, profile the frozen workflow on a training-only pilot; use Kaggle only if terms and compute allow.
7. The primary outcome is the minimum, across the three noise levels, of the held-out lower-decile mean time to progression (CVaR at the lower 10%) across evaluation scenarios; higher is better. Average five noise replicates within each scenario and noise level before calculating the tail mean. Report each noise level separately, capped fraction, median time, burden integral, resistant fraction, and cumulative dose as secondary outcomes.
8. Evaluate all policies on the same held-out scenarios and noise streams. A universal policy preference is supported only if the adjusted paired interval shows improvement over every required component and baseline and there is no reversal across the prespecified parameter strata. Otherwise report scenario-dependent rankings or no preference. Claims apply only to the stated simulator and parameter distributions, not to human treatment.

## Risk Assessment

This is secondary analysis of de-identified public files and computer simulation. No wet-lab materials, patient contact, or treatment recommendations are involved. Follow each source’s data terms; keep data local unless cloud transfer is explicitly allowed. Do not attempt re-identification or link patients across sources without documentation. Any result is a research model result, not medical advice.

## Data Analysis

Track A uses donor-level risk-versus-coverage area (lower is better). Track B uses a spatial variogram score against supplied held-out masks (lower is better), with Brier score secondary. Track C uses worst-noise-level lower-decile simulated time to progression under a common dose budget (higher is better). For each track, use paired 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals at the donor, patient, or scenario level and adjust across the prespecified comparisons. The full hybrid is supported as better only if its adjusted interval favors it over every required ablation and baseline and all eligibility and validation gates pass. If an interval crosses zero, a gate fails, or rankings reverse, report the limitation and the result as inconclusive or scenario-dependent.
