# Research Plan

## Project Title

**Integrating Tumor Biology and Longitudinal MRI for Glioblastoma Progression Modeling and In-Silico Therapy Optimization**

## Rationale

Glioblastoma varies across tumor regions and over time. Single-cell RNA sequencing can measure molecular patterns, while serial MRI and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps can describe radiographic change. Mathematical models can represent tumor growth, treatment suppression, and resistant populations. Standard care includes radiotherapy and temozolomide (Stupp et al., 2005). Prior studies have already combined tumor-growth models, imaging, molecular features, digital twins, and treatment scheduling (Akbari et al., 2016; Bhattacharya et al., 2025; Chaudhuri et al., 2023; Ebrahimi Zade et al., 2020; Q. Liu et al., 2025; Zimmer et al., 2025; Z. Liu et al., 2025). This project will not claim that broad multimodal treatment modeling is new or that its method is best.

The three tracks will connect through one virtual-patient simulation, not through patient matching. Track A will contribute a fixed summary of an exploratory mixed-cell expression classifier. Track B will contribute only aggregate MRI summaries that the accepted data-use terms permit. Track C will represent tumor growth, treatment suppression, and resistance mathematically. The available cohorts do not share patient identifiers, and neither Track A labels nor Track B MRI labels measure drug resistance directly. Every connection among molecular-like signals, MRI-like signals, and simulated resistance will therefore be an explicit assumption.

The study will test a narrow methodological question: whether a controller that allows residual dependence between imperfect molecular-like and MRI-like signals can improve simulated treatment utility over a matched controller that assumes conditional independence. Fresh synthetic worlds will include informative, uninformative, weak, reversed, and misspecified signal relationships. Scientifically, the comparison will test how uncertainty about signal dependence affects a mathematical treatment policy. Its possible future societal value will be to guide what should be validated next; this simulation will not recommend or select treatment for any person. A positive result will apply only to the stated simulator and assumptions. A null or harmful result will also be reported.

## Research Question, Hypotheses, Engineering Goals, and Expected Outcomes

### Research Questions

**Central question:** Across fresh, independently generated synthetic glioblastoma-like worlds, will a finite-set dependence-aware treatment controller achieve higher prespecified robust utility than a matched finite-set conditional-independence controller, when both receive the same molecular-like and MRI-like observations and have the same treatment options?

1. How will the paired utility difference change when the simulated signals are informative, uninformative, weakly related, reversed, or misspecified?
2. How will the utility and course-use tradeoff change across the prespecified signal and tumor-response scenarios?
3. How sensitive will the policy ranking be to assumed drug suppression, resistance, scan loss, observation noise, and the cost assigned to modeled treatment courses?

### Hypotheses

1. **Primary simulation hypothesis:** In the predeclared correctly specified nonzero-dependence scenarios, the dependence-aware controller will have higher equal-world mean robust utility than its matched conditional-independence controller. This hypothesis may fail.
2. **Negative-control hypothesis:** When both channels are uninformative (`q_signal = 0.5` and `a = b = d = 0`) or their assumed relationship is reversed, multimodal fusion will not consistently outperform the no-signal or single-signal controls. The project will report cases where fusion harms performance.

### Engineering Goals

1. Verify the provenance, hashes, definitions, and permitted use of fixed Track A and Track B summaries without reopening expression or patient image and mask values.
2. Build one auditable synthetic pipeline with separate molecular-like and MRI-like observation channels, sensitive and resistant tumor compartments, treatment suppression, and constrained policies.
3. Compare policies on identical virtual worlds, signal draws, observation-noise streams, scan schedules, and course budgets.
4. Separate development, calibration, and evaluation data and reserve fresh evaluation-world seeds before outcome scoring.
5. Report prior patient analyses separately from the new simulation and make no patient-specific treatment, causal, or clinical-benefit claim.

### Expected Outcomes

The study will estimate where dependence-aware fusion helps, ties, or harms under the declared simulator. It may show a conditional advantage, no measurable advantage, or worse performance. The result will not establish a validated resistance biomarker, clinical treatment benefit, or superiority over methods that were not compared fairly.

## Procedures

1. **Verify permissions and fix the permitted inputs.** I will retain the Duke access agreement and school and competition approval conditions. I will verify that every archived Track A or Duke summary used in this simulation is allowed by those terms. I will use only the fixed Track A donor-level summary and permitted aggregate Duke summaries. I will not reopen expression data, patient images, masks, ADC maps, or patient-level files, and I will not join people across datasets. If a summary is not permitted, I will omit it and use the predeclared synthetic scenario.

2. **Generate the two synthetic signal channels.** I will represent Track A with a hypothetical binary molecular-like report. Its assumed accuracy, `q_signal`, will use these A5 donor-level balanced accuracies in donor order GS1, GS2, GS3, GS5, GS6, and GS13: 0.7485868503, 0.6894338381, 0.4587969245, 0.8339715268, 0.4996343158, and 0.8413786704. These will be stress-test assumptions, not measured resistance-assay accuracies. I will draw one value uniformly per primary world and hold it constant across that world's virtual patients. I will generate a continuous MRI-like baseline score from the declared dependence model, using only a permitted history-only summary to set its scale; otherwise I will use a standard-normal score. Later scan readings will come from simulated burden, not Duke images. The two datasets will never be patient-matched.

3. **Generate synthetic tumor-response worlds.** I will use sensitive and resistant burdens `S` and `R`, total burden `B = S + R`, and normalized capacity 1. The equations will be `dS/dt = rS[1 − (S + a_rsR)] − mruS − μuS + ν(1 − u)R` and `dR/dt = r(1 − c)R[1 − (a_srS + R)] − εmruR + μuS − ν(1 − u)R`, with `a_rs = a_sr = 1`, `k = mr`, and initial states `S(0) = (1 − f0)B0`, `R(0) = f0B0`. I will generate worlds by Latin-hypercube sampling. The single-response family will use `log_r_shift ∈ [−0.7, 0.9]`, `log_m_scale ∈ [ln(0.05), 0]`, `log_f0_shift ∈ [−0.7, 2.3]`, and `B0_center ∈ [0.12, 0.55]`. The mixture family will replace the `m` scale with `log_m_scale_non ∈ [ln(0.02), ln(0.30)]` and responder fraction `π_response ∈ [0.1, 0.6]`; responders will use `m = 6–20`, while nonresponders will use the weak scaled range. Both families will use `μ, ν ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.04}` day⁻¹. Within worlds, I will draw `r = 0.001–0.05`, `m = 6–20`, and `f0 = 0.001–0.1` on log scales, apply the world scales, cap `f0` at 0.9, draw `B0` uniformly from `B0_center ± 0.10`, and draw patient-level `c ∈ [0,0.25]` and `ε ∈ [0,0.2]`. Patient parameters will use Latin-hypercube draws; response-class assignments will use a separate random stream. I will integrate with RK4 at 0.25-day steps and compare with 0.05-day steps. Cycles will be 28 days, a modeled course will treat for five days, and the budget will be at most 12 courses over 1,456 days.

4. **Compare matched treatment controllers.** The primary candidate will be a finite-set dependence-aware minimax controller; the comparator will use the same model support with residual dependence fixed at `d = 0`. The candidate support will combine `q_signal` values above plus 0.5 with every `a`, `b`, and `d` combination in `{−0.5, 0, 0.5}` (at most 189 models before duplicate removal). The primary evaluation anchor will be `(a,b,d) = (0.5,0.5,0.5)`; the full no-information control will use `q_signal = 0.5` and `a = b = d = 0`. I will also test `q_signal = 0.5` at the primary anchor as a molecular-channel null and report out-of-grid anchors separately. Each model will use a shared prior particle cloud, 500 filter particles, and 16 common stratified prediction draws. At each scan, the controller will score all eight treatment/no-treatment patterns over three 28-day cycles, repeat each pattern over a six-cycle (168-day) prediction window with no terminal value, apply the first three-cycle pattern, and replan. The main scan interval will be 84 days. Treatment will stop after two consecutive noisy readings reach at least 1.4 times the lowest earlier observed volume, with a floor of 0.10 times the observed starting volume; a missed scan will reset confirmation. This is a synthetic stop rule, not formal RANO. I will report no-signal, single-signal, fixed-schedule, scan-based, and burden-only Q-learning controls as secondary references. Scan intervals of 28, 56, and 112 days and 10% independent visible scan loss will be separate sensitivities; the MPC will use the same fixed scan-loss assumption and common missingness draws across candidate patterns.

5. **Separate development, calibration, and evaluation.** I will use disjoint virtual-patient cohorts and distinct registered seeds for model development, course-use calibration, and evaluation. I will use the 27 `a/b/d` combinations in `{−0.5, 0, 0.5}` for development and keep the single-response and mixture-response families separate. A fresh seed audit and outcome-blind timing/precision pilot will set cohort sizes and world count before evaluation. The current registry supports at most 14 six-scenario-seed world blocks before accounting for policy seeds, multiple response families, and secondary settings; this is only an upper bound, not a sample-size justification. If all required seeds cannot be reserved or the 14-day interval half-width target cannot be met, I will report feasibility only. I will check numerical convergence and compare only with published aggregate targets that match the simulated endpoint, population, and time origin. Before evaluation, I will validate the driver and interval method on synthetic records with known effects and freeze the settings, seeds, code, and analysis rules.

6. **Score and report the locked comparison.** I will measure the paired difference in robust utility between the primary controllers within each independent world and weight worlds equally. I will form simultaneous max-T intervals across the two primary response-family contrasts using 10,000 world-level bootstrap resamples. A positive result will require both adjusted lower bounds to exceed zero and the prespecified precision target to be met. I will report both progression times, course counts, censoring, per-world values, all anchors and sensitivities, failed fits, missing arms, and negative results. I will not change settings after evaluation outcomes are generated or make a patient-benefit or clinical-treatment claim.

## Risk Assessment

The new phase will use computer simulation and fixed, permitted aggregate records. It will not recruit or contact people, administer treatment, or provide clinical recommendations. Main risks will be misuse of restricted data, disclosure of sensitive patient information, and readers mistaking a simulation for medical evidence. I will keep Duke materials in access-controlled local storage, follow the exact accepted agreement, and share only outputs allowed by that agreement. No new patient image, mask, or ADC value will be read under this plan. Simulated treatment suppression will represent mathematical assumptions, not medical instructions or evidence of patient benefit. Long computations will run on a ventilated surface with the computer connected to power.

## Data Analysis

### Fixed Track A and Track B inputs

I will report the archived Track A and Track B results only as prior exploratory evidence, with their original denominators and limitations. The A5 result will retain its failed five-of-six donor rule. Its mixed-cell expression and MRI-marked labels will not be interpreted as malignant-cell truth or as evidence of resistance. The Duke cohort and target outcomes have already been examined; I will not calculate a new patient-level estimate or claim confirmation from them. I will use permitted aggregate summaries only to define clearly labeled synthetic scenario ranges.

### Primary in-silico comparison

For each policy's simulated treatment path, I will calculate two latent-burden progression times through 730 days: (1) the interpolated time when true burden first reaches 1.2 times its starting burden, and (2) the first scheduled scan when true burden reaches 1.4 times the larger of the prior true-burden scan nadir or 0.10 times starting burden. The second is a latent-burden, scan-sampled RANO-like mathematical proxy, not a clinical RANO assessment (Ellingson et al., 2017). The two noisy observed readings will determine the treatment-stop trigger; they will not replace these latent progression outcomes. For each policy, I will define utility as the smaller of those two progression times, each restricted at 730 days, minus five simulated days per course delivered through day 730. The five-day penalty will be a declared model preference and will be tested at 0 and 10 days per course.

The primary contrast will be the paired utility difference between the finite-set dependence-aware controller and the matched `d = 0` controller. Within each world, I will average paired patient differences; I will then weight independent worlds equally. The primary family will contain the primary signal anchor in both response families. Before evaluation, I will resample the independent worlds with replacement 10,000 times. For each resample and each primary contrast, I will calculate the studentized deviation from the observed world-mean contrast using that contrast's bootstrap standard deviation. I will use the 95th percentile of the maximum absolute studentized deviation across the two response-family contrasts as the simultaneous critical value and form max-T intervals. I will validate this procedure on synthetic records with known effects before the evaluation run and freeze the bootstrap seed in the protocol. The target will be a 95% interval half-width of at most 14 simulated days for each primary contrast; this is a Monte Carlo precision target, not a clinically meaningful difference. World count and precision will be set using separate development worlds, without using evaluation outcomes. If the seed capacity or precision target cannot be met, I will report feasibility only. A positive result will require both adjusted lower confidence bounds to exceed zero. If precision fails or any required arm is missing, I will report the comparison as inconclusive or feasibility-only. If model calibration is insufficient, I may still report a within-simulator comparison but will not claim clinical calibration or therapy benefit.

I will report both progression times, treatment-course counts, censoring, per-world differences, anchor-specific results, and all sensitivity results. I will show independent, weak, reversed, and misspecified signal relationships separately. The independent uncertainty unit will be the generated world, not the nested virtual patient, scan, or noise draw. I will make no patient-benefit, causal, or generalization claim.

## Additional Project Information

This will be a continuation project. The new procedures above will be prospective. The following completed work will be disclosed as prior work and will not be presented as newly performed.

### Track A: molecular and spatial-expression analyses

The project previously analyzed Yu et al. 2020 (GSE117891) using six frozen donors: GS1, GS2, GS3, GS5, GS6, and GS13. The A5 pipeline used exact metadata joins, a minimum of 1,000 UMIs and 500 detected genes per cell, training-fold-only feature processing, and nested leave-one-donor-out validation. The candidate had lower mean class-balanced AURC than L2 logistic regression (0.1479 versus 0.1764) but won in only four of six donors, so it failed the frozen five-of-six rule. Its mean Brier score was worse (0.2335 versus 0.1944); removing the gene gate produced nearly the same mean AURC (0.1473). The analysis involved mixed tumor and peritumoral cells with author-supplied MRI-marked point labels, not malignant-cell-only ground truth. The eight-donor sensitivity run was not performed.

Earlier random-cell validation showed donor leakage: patient-level balanced accuracy was 0.638, close to the cells-per-patient control at 0.646. A seven-donor composition audit found that region was confounded with library and cell composition. A sealed same-cell-type analysis and candidate-model controls did not establish malignant-cell-specific prediction. Three sealed secondary analyses of SenMayo senescence, OXPHOS, and TAM activation failed their adjusted success rules. Calibration curves, within-library analysis, scANVI, and attention analysis remain deferred and were not run in A5. Spatial and public-cohort screens did not identify a cohort passing all donor-count, region-map, label, reuse-term, and independence gates. Track A will make no external-confirmation claim. The local Table S1 marker records no reported IDH1 R132 mutation for four additional donors but does not establish comprehensive IDH1/2-wildtype status.

### Track B: longitudinal MRI analyses

The project previously built synthetic-only MU and LUMIERE pipelines, timing pilots, fixed-growth and learned-baseline comparators, and a guarded LUMIERE runner. No MU or LUMIERE patient masks were scored. LUMIERE source-label, support, and derived-output questions remained unresolved, so LUMIERE will not contribute patient outcomes to the present simulation. Prior-art screening identified related PDE-based digital-twin and learned tumor-forecasting work, including SoC-DT and PREDICT-GBM (Bhattacharya et al., 2025; Zimmer et al., 2025); the project will not claim broad PDE-plus-learned-model integration as new.

A Duke patient-mask analysis was completed on 2026-11-04. It used a 10-mm prior-FLAIR dilation, excluded regions within 10 mm of the current core, and summarized three distance bands. The recorded primary estimate was −2.2 × 10⁻⁶, with a 95% interval from −0.003675 to 0.004071. Destination-label, label-0, distance, interval, and registration summaries were also examined after target access. These outcomes are exposed exploratory evidence, not untouched confirmation. A preliminary audit found that target voxels outside the resampled field of view were set to zero; the effect of this coverage issue is unknown. A separate ADC preflight is marked incomplete and does not establish the status of every possible ADC analysis. The current Duke cohort and outcomes will not be rescored or retuned in this plan. The exact data-use terms and rights to create or share derived summaries will be checked before any summary is used.

### Track C: mathematical simulation and treatment-policy work

The project previously developed a two-population growth and treatment-suppression simulator, particle-filter methods, fixed and adaptive treatment policies, signal-generation prototypes, and synthetic benchmark code. Earlier simulations produced mixed or null policy comparisons. Scan-responsive policies often did not exceed the best fixed schedule at equal course use; oracle policies sometimes performed better but relied on hidden true parameters and were not feasible controllers. Results depended on the progression endpoint, response family, and assumed parameter ranges. Prior signal-recovery diagnostics also found that the dependence parameters `a`, `b`, and `d` were weakly identifiable from the two observed channels: when `q_signal = 0.5`, `d` was unidentified, and finite-sample recovery of the sign of `d` was weak at cohort sizes 18 and 28. A predeclared predictive-recovery gate failed. These were synthetic development findings, not policy-benefit results; they motivate testing a finite-set minimax controller instead of relying on a single fitted dependence value. Signal-action and policy-mechanics tests did not establish treatment benefit. No sealed Stage 3 evaluation was authorized.

The current Track C independent-world benchmark remains a development draft. It has not fixed all anchors, world counts, precision targets, inference settings, seed allocation, or runtime limits. The current seed preflight supports at most 14 six-seed world blocks before other stream needs; that may not provide adequate precision. Previously exposed seeds and outcomes will remain development data. Any future evaluation will use only fresh audited seed/world IDs after the protocol and code are frozen. Trial calibration is limited by differences in population, time origin, endpoint, and death handling; prior calibration failures will remain in the record.

I will retain the exact Duke access agreement and school and competition approval conditions with the project records. I will not recruit or contact participants, administer treatment, or use the simulator for clinical decisions. If the competition requires continuation documentation, I will provide the prior abstract and plan separately.
