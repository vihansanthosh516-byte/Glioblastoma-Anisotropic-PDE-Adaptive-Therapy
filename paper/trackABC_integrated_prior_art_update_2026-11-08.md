# Integrated Track A/B/C prior-art check (2026-11-08)

## Scope

This is a targeted check of the chosen project framing, **“Integrating Tumor Biology and Longitudinal MRI for Glioblastoma Progression Modeling and In-Silico Therapy Optimization.”** It checks primary papers and dataset records surfaced by searches on glioblastoma drug resistance, longitudinal MRI, mechanistic tumor modeling, and therapy optimization. It is not a systematic review and does not establish that the exact method is unique.

## Current project evidence relevant to the title

- **Track A:** the exploratory score uses 119 of 121 exact-mapped genes from an in-vitro TMZ-viability-associated panel. Its six-donor regional directions split 3–3, with no consistent Core-versus-Periphery shift. The score is not a validated response predictor or a measured patient drug response. See the [mapping amendment](trackA_tmz_response_119of121_amendment_2026-11-08.md) and local, uncommitted aggregate [result record](../output/trackA_tmz_response_proxy_119of121/result.json).
- **Track B:** Duke MRI progression and recurrence data do not supply a matched Track A expression or direct drug-response record. The analyzed Duke cohort is already exposed within this project. CFB-GBM is a separate annotation-change side study with unresolved scoring gates; it is not an untouched confirmation cohort.
- **Track C:** the integrated controller receives synthetic molecular-like and MRI-like signals. Their sensitivity, bias, missingness, and residual dependence are assumptions; current A/B data do not calibrate them jointly or link them to the same patients. Policy outcomes have not been evaluated under the integrated v2 protocol.

## Follow-up: longitudinal FLAIR and ADC recurrence mapping (2026-11-09)

The next-visit FLAIR-transition question and secondary ADC analysis were checked against recent and directly related primary studies.

| Study | What it measured | Relevance and limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Jamora et al. (2026), serial MR-Linac imaging.** In a prospective cohort of 33 patients, the authors tracked T2/FLAIR abnormality volume and maximum migration during chemoradiation; 28 had at least six months of follow-up and 12 were early progressors. Greater volume expansion and migration were associated with early progression. The daily imaging protocol did not include contrast, and the FLAIR ROI included the resection cavity. ([Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13276516/)) | This preempts a broad claim that longitudinal FLAIR volume or migration is a new progression signal. It does not test the Duke proposal's distance-matched, next-visit transition from newly appearing versus persistent FLAIR labels into a later core label. The small cohort and treatment-phase endpoint differ from Duke's post-recurrence scans. |
| **Moore-Palhares et al. (2025), serial ADC and spatial recurrence.** In a prospective observational cohort of 80 patients, ADC was measured at planning, radiation fractions 10 and 20, and one month after chemoradiation. The authors defined recurrent GTV as the overlap between the original GTV and later recurrence; temporal ADC increases were smaller in recurrent regions and predicted GTV failure. ([Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2025.03.028)) | This preempts a broad claim that temporal ADC change can identify spatially recurrence-prone or treatment-resistant MRI regions. “Resistant” was operationalized by later recurrence overlap, not measured by a direct drug assay. It studies within-GTV treatment response, not the proposed Duke peritumoral FLAIR-to-core transition. |
| **Rezvanjou et al. (2026), pixel-wise recurrence mapping.** ADC-based deep-learning models generated maps of later-recurrence-overlapping regions inside the GTV. The study used 64 in-house fraction-10 cases plus 40 external pre-treatment cases for training/validation and held out 16 in-house fraction-10 cases for testing. ADC-only performance was 0.69 ROC AUC and 0.80 PR AUC; performance was weaker for small or sparse recurrence regions. ([Primary article](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6694/18/21/3465)) | This is close prior art for MRI-based spatial recurrence and recurrence-defined “resistance” mapping. It does not establish pharmacologic resistance and does not test the Duke new-versus-persistent, distance-matched FLAIR transition. Its holdout is small, and the external cohort was used as auxiliary training data rather than as an external test. |

**Implication for Track B:** broad claims about novel ADC-based spatial recurrence prediction or FLAIR migration analysis are preempted. The narrower Duke transition endpoint remains different from these studies, but that distinction alone does not establish novelty. Because the Duke cohort and endpoint have already been examined, this analysis remains exploratory. MRI label transitions and recurrence-defined regions must not be described as direct tumor-cell or drug-response measurements.

## Follow-up: direct drug-response and MRI links (2026-11-09)

This targeted follow-up checked primary papers that connect tumor biology, MRI, and temozolomide (TMZ) response. It narrows the defensible novelty claim further.

| Study | What it directly measured or modeled | Relevance and limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Moniz Garcia et al. (2026), MRI-guided regional drug vulnerability in GBM.** The study enrolled six treatment-naïve IDH-wildtype patients and collected two contrast-enhancing cores and two non-enhancing margins per patient under MRI/neuronavigation guidance. It paired regional WES/RNA-seq with functional drug tests in patient-derived 3D model systems. A 15-drug panel found region-associated vulnerabilities; the sequencing data are available from the authors on request under institutional policy. ([Primary article](https://academic.oup.com/neuro-oncology/article/28/9/2136/8708331); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42296327/)) | This is a direct precedent for integrating MRI-defined tumor regions, molecular profiling, and drug response. It strongly preempts broad claims that spatial MRI–biology–therapy integration is new. Its six-patient study is preoperative and region-based; it does not test longitudinal post-recurrence forecasting or the current dependence-aware controller. |
| **Zhao et al. (2026), preoperative T2 radiomics in glioma.** The study included 576 patients across training, retrospective validation, and prospective observational cohorts. It combined preoperative T2 radiomics with transcriptomic and clinical data where available, and used single-cell and laboratory assays to interpret radiomic features. Its 17-feature score predicted survival; TMZ chemosensitivity was inferred from observational survival comparisons between radiotherapy and chemoradiotherapy groups, not from a direct ex-vivo TMZ assay or randomized treatment assignment. The prospective cohort was too small for its own survival treatment-comparison analysis and was pooled with retrospective patients for that analysis. ([Primary article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12885-026-15726-8)) | This is a close precedent for MRI, tumor biology, prognosis, and TMZ-related treatment guidance. It uses preoperative T2 imaging and a glioma cohort, not the current Duke post-recurrence longitudinal endpoint or Track C controller. Its observational treatment comparison does not establish causal TMZ benefit. |
| **Yang et al. (2023), patient-derived pharmacogenomic profiling in IDH-wildtype GBM.** TMZ response was measured by in-vitro drug screening of patient-derived glioma stem-like cells. The primary cohort included 69 patients; a multisector cohort included 52 tumor sectors from 18 cases, and 8 of those 18 patients had both resistant and sensitive sectors. The newly sequenced datasets are in controlled-access EGA accession EGAS00001006989. ([Primary article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13073-023-01165-8); [EGA record](https://ega-archive.org/studies/EGAS00001006989)) | This directly establishes that patient-derived TMZ response and intratumoral response heterogeneity have been studied. The article does not establish a patient-level match to the Duke MRI cohort or validate the current Track A score against Duke drug response. |
| **Ntafoulis et al. (2023), ex-vivo TMZ assay with follow-up MRI illustrations.** The study measured TMZ dose-response in patient-derived GBM cultures and presented follow-up MRI for six selected patients: three assay-predicted responders and three predicted nonresponders. ([Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41416-023-02402-y)) | This is a small direct precedent for linking an ex-vivo drug-response assay to later MRI observations. Six illustrative cases do not validate an MRI response model, establish a generalizable molecular-to-imaging mapping, or provide a matched external dataset for the current project. |

**Implication for this project:** the literature now includes a direct six-patient precedent combining MRI-guided core/margin sampling, regional molecular profiling, and patient-derived drug testing. It also includes MRI-based TMZ-guidance modeling and direct ex-vivo TMZ studies with follow-up MRI. The current project still has no verified patient-level join among Track A expression, Track B Duke MRI, and a measured drug-response assay. Track A's 119-gene score therefore cannot be called patient-specific resistance or used to calibrate Duke or Track C. Track C remains an assumption-driven simulation, not a patient treatment recommendation.

## Follow-up: dependence-aware fusion and GBM control (2026-11-09)

This check focused on the narrower proposed Track C comparison: a joint likelihood for dependent molecular-like and MRI-like observations versus a conditional-independence controller, followed by therapy-policy evaluation.

| Study | What it did | Relevance and limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Chen and Ansari (1998), adaptive fusion of correlated local decisions.** They derived a maximum-a-posteriori fusion rule for dependent sensor observations and local decisions, adapted conditional probabilities using reinforcement learning, and simulated how inter-sensor correlation affected error. ([Author-hosted paper](https://web.njit.edu/~ansari/papers/SMC98.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/5326.669570)) | Correlation-aware fusion and reinforcement-learning-based adaptation are established methods. This predates the project by decades. It is a sensor-detection study, not a GBM or therapy-policy study, so it does not directly test this project's specific simulator comparison. |
| **Lu et al. (2016), copula-based track-to-track fusion.** The authors model dependent local sensor estimates in a Bayesian fusion framework and evaluate consistency in simulations. ([Primary article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2016/3751959)) | Further evidence that modeling dependence in joint observation fusion is not itself new. The task is distributed target tracking, not oncology or adaptive treatment. |
| **Volovăț et al. (2026), AI-guided GBM morphodynamic states.** The study used four preoperative MRI sequences from 494 BraTS 2020 cases, inferred neural-ODE flows from cross-sectional population variation, and explored deterministic control and SAC. The authors state the data are not longitudinal; SAC moved trajectories toward a target but did not meet their success criterion and remained below deterministic control. Dynamic attractor labels did not improve the age-plus-resection C-index (about 0.63). ([Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12785452/); [journal version](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4418/16/1/139)) | This is close GBM precedent for MRI-based latent dynamics and simulation control. It is not evidence of observed longitudinal evolution, molecular/drug-response calibration, or a clinically validated therapy policy. Its weaker SAC result also shows why the deterministic baseline must remain. |

**Implication:** do not describe dependence-aware fusion, reinforcement learning, or MRI-based GBM control as individually new. The narrow candidate contribution is only a simulator-specific comparison of whether an assumed residual dependence between two explicitly synthetic signals changes the performance of a predeclared therapy policy, against a matched independence assumption and simple controls. That exact comparison is not established as novel by this targeted search; it remains untested, assumption-dependent, and nonclinical.

## Novelty assessment

The **broad title describes an established combination of research areas**. Moniz Garcia et al. provide a direct MRI-guided regional biology-and-drug-testing precedent; Zhao et al. add MRI–biology–TMZ-guidance modeling; recent ADC and FLAIR studies already map spatial recurrence or progression from longitudinal MRI. Mechanistic glioma models, multimodal MRI, progression forecasting, resistance-aware therapy models, and reinforcement-learning schedule optimization also have primary-source precedents. The project must not claim that any one of those components is unprecedented.

The narrowest plausible contribution remains a **simulator-specific assumption-sensitivity comparison**, not a new fusion or control algorithm: whether an assumed residual dependence between molecular-like and MRI-like signals changes a predeclared therapy policy's simulated performance versus a matched conditional-independence model. Correlation-aware fusion and reinforcement learning for correlated observations are established methods, while GBM MRI control has also been explored. The exact Track C comparison is not proven novel. It currently has three major limits: the cross-modal signal relationship is not measured in matched patients; signal accuracy and dependence are assumptions; and no integrated v2 policy-outcome comparison has been run. Frame this as simulation-method evaluation, not empirical patient-level integration or clinical therapy benefit.

## Next research gate

Do not run research-scale policy outcomes until the integrated estimand, virtual-cohort sizes, seed table, runtime cap, research-scale driver, and a statistically valid uncertainty plan are frozen together. The existing max-T and Student-t candidates failed outcome-blind coverage screens. Under the current bounded mean estimand, Hoeffding requires 27,907 worlds per family for the 14-day target; the saved timing extrapolation is about 922 days even at tiny cohorts. A replacement must be independently reviewed and validated without policy outcomes. Do not relax precision, select a method, or change the estimand because it produces a favorable result.

## Search limits

Searches were targeted, not exhaustive. The primary-source pages for the M4RL paper, data-assimilation paper, and PINN paper were blocked or exposed only abstract/search-rendered content during this check. CFB-GBM v2.0's article page exposed its abstract; the TCIA page was discoverable but failed to open directly. The fusion and GBM-control follow-up checked the full author-hosted 1998 paper and the accessible 2026 article, but did not search every related control, sensor-fusion, or oncology venue. This review supports a bounded prior-art assessment, not a “first” claim.
