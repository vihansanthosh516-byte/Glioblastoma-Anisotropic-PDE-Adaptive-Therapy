# Track C: peer-only model averaging for paired baseline signals

**Status: synthetic method prototype; frozen signal-prediction gate not passed at n=18 or n=28; no policy outcomes or patient data evaluated.** The project title remains **“Integrating Tumor Biology and Longitudinal MRI for Glioblastoma Progression Modeling and In-Silico Therapy Optimization.”** Track A and Duke are not linked to this Track C simulator.

## Proposed method

The signal model has four shared parameters, `q`, `a`, `b`, and `d`. A fixed 81-point grid supplies the candidate parameter settings. For each virtual patient *i*, the method calculates model weights from the paired baseline signals of all other virtual patients. It integrates each candidate model over the declared reference distribution for `f0`. It then averages patient *i*'s conditional signal likelihood over those peer-only weights and uses the mixture to update that patient's `f0` particles.

The method uses no latent resistance values, future simulator outcomes, or treatment responses to estimate the peer weights. It assumes all patients in a synthetic cohort share one signal-generating model. It is a cohort-calibrated simulation method, not a validated clinical biomarker workflow.

## Evidence and limits

This implementation is not a result that the method is better, unique, or biologically correct. The simulator chooses the molecular-to-resistance and MRI-to-resistance relationships. Current finite-sample recovery results show weak recovery at sample sizes comparable to the project's small virtual cohorts; see [`trackc_finite_sample_recovery_protocol_2026-11-06.md`](trackc_finite_sample_recovery_protocol_2026-11-06.md) and [`output/trackc_finite_sample_signal_recovery/diagnostic.json`](../output/trackc_finite_sample_signal_recovery/diagnostic.json). The new method must therefore pass a prospective held-out signal-prediction diagnostic before any treatment-policy claim is tested.

The broader frame is not new by itself. Prior work already describes MRI-personalized digital twins and risk-aware radiotherapy optimization in high-grade glioma ([Chaudhuri et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10598726/)). A 2025 primary study uses longitudinal multiparametric MRI to personalize a family of biology-based high-grade glioma models, averages model weights, and selects the highest-weight model for cohort-level error analysis ([Hormuth et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12815495/)). Two 2026 primary studies further overlap with the project title: one uses baseline and week-three quantitative MRI from 15 patients to target dose boosts to proliferative regions and compares adapted plans with standard care in patient-calibrated tumor models ([Miniere et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42562016/)); another combines longitudinal MRI, tumor-response modeling, and ADC-derived maps to forecast treatment response and identify adaptive radiotherapy targets in 21 patients ([JCO Clinical Cancer Informatics, 2026](https://ascopubs.org/doi/pdfdirect/10.1200/CCI-25-00336)). These overlaps mean the project must not claim that multimodal digital twins, MRI-informed therapy planning, tumor-model averaging, or image-guided adaptation are new. A possible narrow contribution would be a controlled test of peer-calibrated dependence-aware baseline signals versus matched alternatives, but this run failed its small-cohort gate and exact prior-art coverage for that intersection remains unverified.

## Next gate, before treatment-outcome evaluation

The frozen synthetic leave-one-out predictive-score diagnostic ran on 2026-11-06. It covered 2,100 cohorts: seven truth anchors, three `f0` ranges, four sample sizes, and 25 root seeds. The JSON records 84 truth-by-range-by-size summaries, all 2,100 cohort scores, the pre-run code hashes, and the frozen protocol hash: [`output/trackc_loo_predictive_check/diagnostic.json`](../output/trackc_loo_predictive_check/diagnostic.json).

**Primary result:** the advancement gate was **not passed**. At n=18, LOO minus the uniform-grid mixture averaged +0.046895 nats per patient (one-sided 98.75% lower bound +0.035296), but LOO minus matched conditional independence averaged −0.019283 (lower bound −0.029323). At n=28, the corresponding values were +0.050212 (lower bound +0.039700) and −0.008081 (lower bound −0.014564). The frozen rule required all four lower bounds above zero; two were below zero. At n=100 and n=500, mean LOO-minus-matched-independence differences were +0.013581 and +0.019489, respectively. Those larger-sample results are descriptive and do not rescue the failed n=18/n=28 gate.

**Interpretation and weakest point:** peer-only model averaging beat the fixed uniform mixture on average, but it did not beat the matched conditional-independence comparator at cohort-like sample sizes. That comparator receives the generating `q`, `a`, and `b`, so it has oracle nuisance-parameter information; the benchmark is deliberately difficult for the learned method. The simulation also gives the estimator the correct `f0` range and includes generating anchors in its grid. These choices make this a favorable, correctly specified simulation, not evidence of real biology. No treatment-policy comparison or patient analysis ran. The 20261905–20261929 roots are now spent for development and must not be reused to tune this method.

This candidate will not advance as the primary policy method under the frozen rule. A future policy study would need a new, separately frozen question and unused seeds; it must not present this failed gate as a win. Any positive result would remain an in-silico result under the selected simulator assumptions, not evidence of clinical benefit.
