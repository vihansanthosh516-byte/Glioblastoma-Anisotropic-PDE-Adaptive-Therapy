# Anchor resistance signals to drug response

As currently defined, **Track A and Track B cannot directly measure glioblastoma drug resistance**. Track A predicts a sampled region from mixed-cell gene expression; Track B records MRI changes and recurrence. Neither supplies a verified, patient-matched measurement of response to a named drug. Both tracks can contribute carefully labeled resistance-associated proxies, and both can motivate channels in a synthetic treatment-policy simulation. The simulation can test decisions under stated resistance assumptions, but it cannot turn those proxies into measured patient resistance or evidence that a treatment works for patients. A direct resistance-centered study needs a drug-response reference, such as patient-derived dose-response testing or an adjudicated clinical outcome linked to documented temozolomide exposure.

## Track A identifies regional expression and resistance-associated markers

Yu et al. used MRI-guided, multi-sector sampling to study glioma heterogeneity. The project’s frozen A5 endpoint uses Tumoral/Core versus Peritumoral/Periphery labels. That is a spatial-label prediction task, not a drug challenge or response measurement. The source study does not report temozolomide (TMZ) exposure, a TMZ dose-response assay, or patient-level TMZ response as an endpoint for the A5 donors ([Yu et al. 2020](https://academic.oup.com/nsr/article/7/8/1306/5849029); [GEO GSE117891](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE117891)).

A later supplement lists MGMT-promoter methylation for the six A5 donor IDs: four marked methylated and two marked “not methylated.” It does not supply matched TMZ response, survival, or ex-vivo sensitivity for those six donors; the assay details and match between each MGMT measurement and sampled region also remain unverified in the reviewed table ([Feng et al. 2023](https://journal.hep.com.cn/fmd/EN/10.1007/s11684-022-0936-z); [Supplementary Table S5](https://journal.hep.com.cn/fmd/fileup/2095-0217/SUPPL/20230110084242_1.pdf)). MGMT status is a clinically relevant biomarker: a randomized trial found promoter methylation associated with greater benefit from TMZ-containing treatment. But a biomarker is not a direct measurement of how much an individual tumor’s cells respond to TMZ ([Hegi et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa043331)).

Direct cellular sensitivity studies instead expose patient-derived glioblastoma cultures to a TMZ concentration series and measure outcomes such as viability, area under the dose-response curve, or IC50. Published studies show this design and relate measured sensitivity to molecular features and, separately, clinical outcomes ([Ntafoulis et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10575865/); [Wang et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10010007/)). A defensible A extension could apply one frozen, externally published TMZ-response-associated signature to the existing Core and Periphery expression data, summarize paired differences by donor, and show MGMT groups descriptively. It must be called a **resistance-associated expression proxy**. It must not rename A5’s classifier or interpret its AURC as resistance-test accuracy. With six donors, the secondary biomarker analysis would remain exploratory.

## Track B records post-treatment imaging patterns, not TMZ-specific resistance

The Duke Zenodo record describes 61 patients, 3–18 MRI sessions per patient, six MRI modalities, automated tumor segmentations, and relative treatment-date metadata. Its linked README defines recurrence using either an enhancing-volume increase greater than 40% from a postoperative tumor-volume nadir or radiologist-determined clinical progression. This supports a longitudinal radiographic recurrence/progression phenotype; the public description does not establish a direct drug-sensitivity assay or a uniform, TMZ-specific response endpoint ([Duke Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/20548528); [dataset README](https://github.com/deshanyang/GBM-DIR-QA)).

The distinction matters because the README says treatment and clinical dates were automatically extracted and not individually validated, and scan sessions are not already co-registered. RANO 2.0 also notes that pseudoprogression occurs in up to 30–40% of glioblastoma patients during the first 12 weeks after chemoradiotherapy; suspected progression in clinically stable patients during that period needs confirmation. Therefore, progression on MRI is a **clinical or radiographic proxy** for treatment failure only after treatment exposure, scan timing, registration, and adjudication are checked. It does not by itself establish resistance to TMZ rather than radiation effect, surgery, another treatment, or disease biology ([RANO 2.0](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10860967/)).

For a future B analysis to use a TMZ-specific clinical proxy, it would need a verified patient-level link between each scan and the named regimen, treatment dates and duration, and a predeclared clinical progression rule with confirmation or pathology support. It would also need relevant treatment and imaging covariates, including radiation, surgery, corticosteroids, and the pseudoprogression window. Until those gates pass, the accurate endpoint is **MRI-defined recurrence or core-label transition**, not “TMZ resistance.” The Duke record lists CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 and a separate Duke custom-license route; the accepted terms for the downloaded copy control analysis and sharing of derived outputs ([Duke Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/20548528)).

## A shared resistance target requires matched patients and outcomes

Separate A and B cohorts cannot identify how expression and MRI jointly relate to resistance in the same person. Their marginal patterns do not determine the cross-modal dependence, and A5’s regional-classification score cannot supply the molecular channel’s resistance accuracy. Likewise, a Duke recurrence pattern cannot calibrate an MRI channel’s ability to detect TMZ resistance. Setting those links in the simulator is a modeling assumption, not an empirical estimate.

IvyGAP is a closer published MRI–RNA bridge: a radiomics/transcriptomics analysis matched 28 patients, but used approximate mapping between sampled anatomy and MRI compartments and lacked detailed public treatment history. A separate co-registered MRI/pathology/RNA analysis had only five patients with the relevant spatially matched RNA data. These studies link imaging and tumor biology; they do not provide the needed drug-specific resistance endpoint ([IvyGAP radiomics/transcriptomics study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13114458/); [Park et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11726254/)). Thus, the review did not establish a ready-to-use cohort containing spatial or single-cell tumor biology, serial MRI, named drug exposure, and validated drug response for the same patients. This is a bounded search result, not proof that no such resource exists.

## Build an honest bridge and define its claim

For the current project, preserve A5 as region prediction and report B as an exploratory MRI-transition or recurrence-pattern analysis. If added, A’s published resistance-signature score and B’s MRI progression phenotype should remain separate proxy analyses with their own uncertainty and validity checks. The integrated simulation can then define one latent resistant state and generate molecular-like and MRI-like observations from it under an explicit grid of signal quality, bias, missingness, and residual dependence. Since current A and B data cannot estimate the joint relationship to drug response, those channel settings must remain synthetic assumptions unless a matched validation cohort supplies calibration.

The benchmark should compare the dependence-aware controller with a matched conditional-independence controller, plus no-signal and single-channel controls, using the same virtual patients, noise, treatment choices, and scan schedules. It should freeze world and patient counts through outcome-blind precision planning, reserve distinct development and evaluation seeds, set a runtime cap from a synthetic pilot, and use a complete driver that records every arm, failure, exclusion, and output hash. Report both progression endpoints and the utility contrast at the independent-world level. A positive result would support only this claim: **under the stated simulated resistance and signal assumptions, one controller had better simulated utility than its specified comparators**. It would not show patient-specific resistance measurement, clinical treatment benefit, or a personalized therapy recommendation.

If the scientific goal must be to measure patient drug resistance, add or replace data with a cohort that links the same patients’ tumor tissue, serial MRI, documented TMZ exposure, and either direct ex-vivo TMZ dose-response or a validated, treatment-specific clinical response endpoint. Use patient-grouped validation and compare RNA-only, MRI-only, clinical-only, and combined models. Until such a dataset is verified, the strongest honest description is **resistance-associated molecular and radiographic proxies connected through an assumption-based virtual-patient model**.
