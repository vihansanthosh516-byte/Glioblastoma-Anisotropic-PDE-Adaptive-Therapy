# Integrated Track A/B/C novelty update (2026-11-06)

## Status and scope

This is a targeted primary-source update, not an exhaustive systematic review. It does not establish that the project is first, best, or clinically useful. No patient data were read and no outcome benchmark was run for this update.

## Closest prior work

1. **MRI-informed glioma digital twin and treatment optimization.** Chaudhuri et al. developed a predictive digital twin for high-grade glioma using MRI-informed mechanistic modeling, uncertainty quantification, and risk-based radiotherapy-regimen optimization. This covers the broad claim that an MRI-calibrated tumor model can inform personalized therapy under uncertainty.
2. **Longitudinal multiparametric MRI and response forecasting.** A 21-patient high-grade-glioma data-assimilation study used diffusion MRI, T1, T2/FLAIR, and repeated treatment-time observations to predict chemoradiation response and discuss dynamic treatment planning. A 2026 JCO Clinical Cancer Informatics report also describes patient-specific, multiparametric-MRI-based forecasts of tumor-cell density during and after radiotherapy.
3. **Glioblastoma biology plus drug scheduling with reinforcement learning.** Liu et al. (2025) introduced M4RL: a multiscale glioblastoma tumor-cell/TAM model with molecular signaling and cytokine/drug diffusion, a Fokker–Planck/PINN surrogate, and actor-critic reinforcement learning for CSF1R-inhibitor and IGF1R-inhibitor schedules. The paper reports verification using spatial-transcriptomic data. This directly rules out a broad claim that combining GBM biology, mechanistic modeling, and AI drug scheduling is itself new.
4. **GBM world models that connect treatment and future imaging.** Wang et al. (2026) describe Brain-WM, an arXiv preprint that jointly predicts next-step treatment and future MRI from longitudinal GBM data. Its public repository lists LUMIERE, MU-Glioma-Post, and UCSF-ALPTDG as internal cohorts and UCSD-PTGBM as external validation. This makes a broad “integrated GBM model for treatment planning and MRI forecasting” claim especially weak. The paper is a preprint; its reported performance has not been independently reproduced in this project.
5. **Reusable patient-specific digital-twin software.** TumorTwin (2025) presents a public Python framework for composing patient data, models, solvers, and optimization, demonstrated on simulated high-grade-glioma growth and radiotherapy response. A 2026 systematic review also finds many neuro-oncology digital-twin studies, most focused on GBM/HGG. “Building a GBM digital twin” is therefore not a defensible novelty claim by itself.
6. **Robust treatment policies under imperfect biomarkers and model shift.** An October 2026 preprint develops robust contextual reinforcement learning for metastatic prostate-cancer adaptive therapy under measurement error and disease-dynamics shifts. An August 2026 preprint studies adaptive therapy under parameter, structural, and biomarker-measurement uncertainty, including cases where model misspecification changes conclusions. These are different diseases, but they substantially overlap the general robustness idea.

## What this means for this project

- The umbrella title can remain, but the current tracks do not yet form a patient-level multimodal model. Track A and Track B patient data are not linked to the Track C virtual patients. The new signal-to-resistance map is an analyst-chosen simulation assumption, not a relationship measured in those cohorts.
- No evidence currently supports “highest,” “best,” “first,” or a guaranteed positive result. A unit-test pass establishes implementation consistency, not a benefit from the policy.
- The strongest current candidate is a narrower methods question: **Across held-out simulated GBM worlds, when does dependence-aware belief updating between a baseline biology-like report and MRI-like evidence improve or harm worst-endpoint treatment utility relative to the same controller assuming conditional independence?** This remains a hypothesis, not a positive result or verified novelty claim. The current code conditions once on joint baseline molecular-like and MRI-like signals; the filter separately assimilates repeated simulated noisy log-burden scans. A time-varying joint MRI/ADC signal-likelihood model is planned but not implemented. The revised plan defines nonzero-dependence primary anchors outside the development grid and makes the d=0 ablation identical in all other policy settings. Comparisons with Brain-WM and robust-observation POMDP work may be conceptual only when inputs, actions, or simulators cannot be matched fairly.
- A candidate must do more than concatenate signals or add a reliability gate. The proposed primary contrast will compare dependence-aware and conditional-independence controllers with identical inputs, priors, particles, actions, utility, and random streams. Single-channel, no-signal, wrong-sign, wrong-dependence, and shuffled-signal controls will map benefit and harm. The current Q-learning policy receives burden history only; it will remain a no-signal reference unless a same-input version is implemented and tested. The plan now treats a,b,d as factor coefficients rather than empirical correlations, uses genuinely out-of-grid evaluation anchors, and keeps patient/noise repeats nested within unique virtual-world IDs.
- A scratch run passed 68 focused synthetic tests after adding checks that q=0.5 removes molecular information, d=0 factorizes the joint likelihood, and nonzero d changes only the joint—not either single-channel—likelihood. These tests establish likelihood invariants, not policy superiority or biological validity.
- The existing track data do not form a linked multimodal patient cohort. Track A can inform only an explicitly assumed molecular-like signal range; Track B can inform only permitted MRI/ADC observation characteristics; Track C supplies the assumed drug-suppression simulator. The molecular-to-resistance relationship remains simulated. The umbrella title can stay, but the protocol must say this clearly.
- To keep the result informative rather than positivity-seeking, evaluate all predeclared signal regimes, including independent, noisy, shuffled, wrong-sign, and mismatched-likelihood controls. Report both progression endpoints, treatment-course use, mean and lower-tail outcomes, and the full regime map. Do not choose a winning regime after seeing evaluation outcomes.

## Additional prior-art correction and implementation gate (2026-11-06)

The targeted follow-up makes the generic reliability-fusion claim weaker than the earlier draft suggested:

- Sun and Sheng's **QA-MoE** (IJCAI 2026) scores modality quality, selects a subset of modalities, and stabilizes routing under noisy and missing inputs. It is evaluated for ADNI staging and MIMIC-IV length of stay, not GBM treatment. This is direct prior art against claiming that uncertainty-based modality selection or reliability gating is itself new.
- Huang et al.'s **contextual-discounting evidential fusion** learns modality reliability and combines multimodal medical-image evidence; experiments include PET/CT lymphoma and multi-MRI brain-tumor segmentation. It is direct prior art against generic reliability-aware medical-image fusion.
- **Robustness Analysis of POMDP Policies to Observation Perturbations** studies policy value under misspecified observation models and gives robust-policy methods in robotics and operations-research examples. It is adjacent prior art against claiming robust observation-model planning is new.
- **MORE-CLEAR** applies multimodal offline reinforcement learning to sepsis management. It is not GBM and is not the same simulated, mechanistic setting, but it further weakens any claim that combining multiple clinical modalities with a treatment policy is unique.

Together with M4RL and Brain-WM, these papers mean the project cannot currently claim a new general fusion algorithm, new general robust-POMDP method, or first integrated GBM therapy framework. A potentially distinctive contribution would have to be the carefully controlled GBM-specific evaluation of dependent biology-like and repeated MRI-like evidence as an input to sequential treatment choice, across two independently defined progression endpoints and prespecified signal-model failures. That exact intersection remains an unverified novelty hypothesis; targeted searches are not an exhaustive review.

Before evaluating the longitudinal-fusion question, the implementation must:

1. Define serial MRI/ADC observation timing, signal generation, missingness, and dependence with the baseline biology-like report.
2. Update the belief with repeated observations without double-counting the same latent burden or shared noise.
3. Compare joint-dependence, independence-assuming, fixed-likelihood, and uncertainty-set policies on identical virtual patients, actions, scan schedules, and random streams.
4. Freeze the primary utility and both progression endpoints before development evaluation. Report adverse regimes and negative controls, even when they defeat the candidate.

Until then, the verified result is only an implementation result: the baseline joint-likelihood function matches an independent latent-variable integral and normalizes over the two reports and continuous MRI score in tested parameter settings. The focused numerical tests passed in a scratch copy (`38 passed`). This validates a probability calculation; it does not show policy improvement or novelty.

## What still limits the method's potential

- The current primary contrast is still a likelihood-ablation test: a fixed joint model is compared with the same model forced to assume residual independence. Where the joint model is given the generating dependence, a positive result would mainly show that correct specification can beat a misspecified likelihood. It would not establish a method for learning or handling unknown dependence.
- The strongest next method test is dependence identifiability and uncertainty. First test whether paired observations can identify the needed signal parameters under the actual simulator f0 distributions. Do not fit `d` from paired signals alone when its loadings are not identified. If uncertainty remains, freeze an ambiguity set over admissible signal models using development worlds only, then compare a model-averaging or robust policy against fixed-dependence and independence policies on held-out, out-of-grid worlds. Report regimes where it helps and where it harms.
- The current signal likelihood is applied once to baseline molecular-like and MRI-like scalar observations. Later updates use noisy total-burden scans, not repeated joint MRI/ADC features or images. The current code does not yet implement the full longitudinal multimodal mechanism named in the umbrella title.
- Track A expression, Duke MRI, and Track C virtual patients are not patient-linked. The molecular-to-resistance relationship is assumed in simulation; it is not measured by the separate cohorts.
- M4RL already combines GBM biology and reinforcement-learning drug scheduling, and Brain-WM already jointly predicts GBM treatment and future MRI. Therefore “best,” “first,” and general framework novelty remain unsupported. The narrow dependence-uncertainty contribution is still an unverified hypothesis, not a result.

## Dependence identifiability check (2026-11-06)

The signal generator currently treats `q`, `a`, `b`, and `d` as scenario assumptions. It does not estimate them from paired patient data. Under the standard-normal latent-resistance case, marginalizing the latent resistance score gives a bivariate-normal pair of latent signal scores with correlation

`rho = a*b + sqrt(1-a^2)*sqrt(1-b^2)*d`.

The observed binary molecular report and continuous MRI score then depend on `q` and `rho`, not on `a`, `b`, and `d` separately. Different parameter tuples can therefore produce the same observable signal distribution in this normal-score case. A deterministic helper and regression test record this identity. This is an identifiability diagnostic, not a fitted model or a patient result.

I then ran deterministic quadrature under three log-uniform `f0` priors from the calibration design (`py -3.14 src/210_trackc_dependence_identifiability.py`). I compared `(q,a,b,d) = (0.8,0.5,0.4,0.6)` with `(0.8,0.8,0.5,0.531615)`. Both share the same normal-score signature, `rho = 0.676235`. With 256 Gauss-Legendre nodes and 1,601 MRI-score points from -8 to 8, both marginal densities integrated to approximately 1. The forward KL divergences were 0.00935 nats for the low-shift range, 0.00434 for the default range, and 0.01014 for the high-shift range. Thus these truncated `f0` priors distinguish this one parameter pair in principle; the normal-score identity does not prove exact non-identifiability for the full calibration family. The divergence values describe one pair with `q` fixed at 0.8. They do not establish practical finite-sample identifiability when `q`, `a`, and `b` are unknown. The full machine-readable output, including reverse KL, density differences, and normalization checks, is [`output/trackc_dependence_identifiability/diagnostic.json`](../output/trackc_dependence_identifiability/diagnostic.json).

Track A and Duke data are not patient-linked, and neither supplies paired signal/resistance labels. Fitting `d` by using simulated latent resistance would be oracle calibration, not evidence that the project can learn dependence from real observations. Before an outcome benchmark, test finite-sample identifiability with the nuisance parameters unknown under the actual `f0` ranges. If estimates remain weak or unstable, use a predeclared uncertainty set over signal models rather than claim that `d` was learned. The proposed robust policy still needs implementation, fair baselines, and held-out evaluation.

### Predeclared local-information diagnostic (not run when this protocol was written)

Before a finite-sample recovery simulation, I will calculate the expected Fisher information for `theta = (q, a, b, d)` from the exact marginal paired-signal likelihood. I will evaluate the fixed point `(0.8, 0.5, 0.4, 0.6)` under four distributions: the full reference `f0` range and the three calibration ranges listed above. The full reference range is a standard-normal-score negative control; the three truncated ranges are the calibration conditions of interest.

For each condition, I will use 256 Gauss-Legendre `f0` nodes, 1,601 equally spaced MRI scores from -8 to 8, and central finite differences with step `1e-4`. I will integrate the discrete-report/continuous-score Fisher matrix by the trapezoid rule. I will call an eigenvalue nonzero only when it exceeds `1e-6` times the largest eigenvalue. I will report the full eigenvalue spectrum, effective rank, condition number, and the nuisance-adjusted local Cramér–Rao standard-error bound for `d` when the matrix is full rank. A rank-deficient matrix will have no finite bound reported for `d`.

This is a local, model-based information diagnostic at one assumed parameter point. It is not a parameter-recovery experiment, power analysis, patient estimate, or policy result. A later finite-sample study must use registered development seeds and must include unknown `q`, `a`, and `b`; the local diagnostic cannot replace it.

**Observed result:** the probability densities integrated to 1 in all four conditions. The reference-normal control had effective rank 2, as expected from the exact `(q, rho)` dependence. The three truncated calibration ranges had rank 4 at this one point, but condition numbers were 55.37, 78.39, and 54.71. The nuisance-adjusted local Cramér–Rao bound for `d` was 4.49, 5.14, and 4.47 per `sqrt(n)`, respectively. This indicates weak local information about `d` when `q`, `a`, and `b` are also unknown, despite full local rank. It does not show that finite samples recover `d`. The machine-readable matrices and eigenvalues are in [`output/trackc_signal_fisher_identifiability/diagnostic.json`](../output/trackc_signal_fisher_identifiability/diagnostic.json). A post-protocol numerical refinement doubled the quadrature and MRI-grid resolution and halved the difference step. Effective ranks stayed the same; the `d` bounds changed by less than 0.0003% across truncated ranges. See [`output/trackc_signal_fisher_identifiability/convergence_check.json`](../output/trackc_signal_fisher_identifiability/convergence_check.json).

**Verification:** the focused identifiability, Fisher-information, and signal-conditioning suites passed 38 tests in a scratch copy. The full Track C scratch suite passed 316 tests and skipped one seed-registry test because `output/` was absent from the scratch copy; that test file separately passed 10 and reproduced the documented skip. After removing a trailing blank line from the new test file, its focused suite passed 3 tests, and SHA-256 comparison found no differences across all 429 `src/` and `tests/` files between scratch and worktree. These checks validate code behavior, not method superiority.

### Predeclared Fisher-information grid stress test

To check how much the one-point result depends on the assumed signal strengths, I evaluated every point in this fixed grid: `q` in `{0.5, 0.7, 0.9}` and each of `a`, `b`, and `d` in `{-0.5, 0, 0.5}`. I evaluated all 81 parameter points under the same four `f0` distributions used above. The `q = 0.5` slice is a negative control because a coin-flip molecular report carries no information.

For this grid check, I first proposed 64 Gauss-Legendre `f0` nodes and 401 MRI-score points from -8 to 8, with central differences of `1e-4` and the same `1e-6` relative eigenvalue threshold. A numerical pilot on the 81-point reference-normal control showed effective rank 3 at 32 points, although the exact normal-score model can have rank at most 2. The coarse quadrature therefore failed its negative control. Before running the full grid, I amended the resolution to 256 `f0` nodes and 1,601 MRI-score points, matching the validated single-point diagnostic. I kept the finite-difference step, rank threshold, parameter grid, and all four distributions unchanged. I report all points, with no parameter selection. The coarse pilot is recorded as a numerical failure, not a signal or policy result. This grid is a model stress test, not a calibrated biological range, exhaustive identifiability proof, finite-sample study, or policy result. It requires no random seed.

**Observed result:** all 324 combinations were evaluated (81 parameter points in each of four distributions). Every marginal density normalized within the `1e-5` tolerance. The `q = 0.5` negative control left `d` unidentified throughout. The normal-reference control had rank 1 at 31 points and rank 2 at 50; no point exceeded rank 2. Under the three truncated calibration distributions, full rank occurred at 44/81 points for low- and high-shift ranges and 40/81 for the default range. At full-rank points, the nuisance-adjusted local bound for `d` had these ranges and medians:

| `f0` distribution | Full-rank points | Local bound for `d`, minimum / median / maximum per `sqrt(n)` |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Low shift | 44/81 | 2.73 / 7.97 / 31.69 |
| Default | 40/81 | 2.66 / 11.12 / 34.19 |
| High shift | 44/81 | 2.80 / 7.92 / 30.19 |

This shows that local full rank is not typical across the entire grid, and even where rank is full, information about `d` can be weak. The Fisher calculation is local and model-based; it does not establish finite-sample parameter recovery, biological calibration, treatment benefit, or novelty. The complete rows and protocol are in [`output/trackc_signal_fisher_grid/diagnostic.json`](../output/trackc_signal_fisher_grid/diagnostic.json). ## Targeted prior-art update (2026-11-06)

A targeted search for unknown observation dependence found methods that already handle uncertainty in sensor cross-correlation and POMDP observation models. Robust fusion methods minimize worst-case error over uncertain cross-covariances ([Wu & Hu, 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13634-019-0640-6)); distributionally robust POMDPs optimize against an ambiguity set for transition-observation distributions ([Nakao et al., 2020](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/19M1268410)); and a 2026 preprint studies policy robustness to observation-model perturbations ([Kraske et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21256)). This makes “robust fusion” or “robust POMDP” alone an established-method claim, not a novelty claim.

The targeted search also found broader GBM overlap: M4RL already combines multiscale GBM biology with RL treatment scheduling ([Liu et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13155510/)); Brain-WM jointly predicts treatment and future MRI ([Wang et al., 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07562)); and a 2026 preprint combines reaction-diffusion, learned correction, adaptive updating, and MPC treatment scheduling ([Liu et al., 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13877)). MRI-calibrated, uncertainty-aware treatment optimization for high-grade glioma also predates this project ([Chaudhuri et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10598726/)). These sources directly constrain broad novelty claims; they do not establish that every specific intersection has been studied.

Two additional 2026 studies narrow the gap further. FA-DeepMSM fuses 3D MRI features with clinical and molecular variables for GBM survival prediction and reports external validation ([Hwang et al., 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-34134-9)); it does not perform treatment-policy optimization. A Research Square preprint analyzes pre/post-chemoradiotherapy serum proteomics and metabolomics alongside AI-segmented MRI tumor volumes in 55 patients and relates those changes to survival outcomes ([preprint, PMC13015611](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13015611/)); it is observational biomarker analysis, not a therapy optimizer. A 2026 systematic review counted 12 GBM/HGG studies among 21 neuro-oncology digital-twin studies, with MRI in 17, tumor-growth simulation in 8, treatment-response modeling in 7, and closed-loop calibration in 8 ([systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13036663/)). This makes the umbrella claim of combining tumor biology, MRI, progression modeling, and personalized therapy especially broad; the distinctive contribution, if any, must be a specific, fairly tested decision method.

A newly verified 2026 primary study further overlaps the broad multimodal title: Liu and Liang compared cross-attention fusion of preoperative T1, T1CE, and T2 MRI with single- and early/late-fusion models in 386 IDH-wildtype GBM cases, then combined the imaging risk score with clinical variables for survival prediction and leave-one-cohort-out validation ([Frontiers in Oncology, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fonc.2026.1867582)). It predicts survival; it does not optimize treatment policies. Therefore, multimodal GBM prediction and risk stratification are not distinctive claims for this project. Any defensible distinction must come from a narrow policy-evaluation question and a fair held-out test, not the umbrella integration alone.

The resulting research target remains narrow and unverified: evaluate, on frozen held-out GBM simulation worlds, whether a dependence-aware signal policy adds decision value beyond independence, fixed-dependence, robust/averaged-dependence, single-channel, no-signal, and same-information learning controls. The current code gives the joint policy the generating dependence coefficient, so it does not yet test learning or robustness to unknown dependence. The dependence-to-biology mapping remains synthetic and assumed. No targeted search result justifies claiming that the proposed narrow test is unique, and no policy outcome comparison has been run.

## Finite-sample recovery diagnostic (2026-11-06)

The frozen synthetic study evaluated seven truth anchors, three calibration `f0` ranges, cohort sizes 18, 28, 100, and 500, 25 replicates per condition, and all 81 likelihood-grid candidates (2,100 cohorts total; 84 condition groups). The fit did not receive the generator's latent `f0` values; it integrated them out with 256 quadrature nodes. Seeds `20261880–20261904` were drawn from the reserved Stage 2 development block. The complete protocol and all replicate-level results are in [`trackc_finite_sample_recovery_protocol_2026-11-06.md`](trackc_finite_sample_recovery_protocol_2026-11-06.md) and [`output/trackc_finite_sample_signal_recovery/diagnostic.json`](../output/trackc_finite_sample_signal_recovery/diagnostic.json).

Across the 375 nonzero-`d`, `q>0.5` cohorts at each sample size, the result was:

| Virtual patients | Correct sign of `d` | Exact truth in MLE tie set | Median width of `d` values within ΔlogL ≤ 2 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 18 | 62.4% | 13.1% | 1.0 |
| 28 | 69.3% | 15.5% | 1.0 |
| 100 | 76.0% | 30.7% | 0.5 |
| 500 | 95.5% | 77.9% | 0.0 |

The profile width uses the fixed grid and is descriptive, not a confidence interval. At the 18–28 cohort sizes most relevant to this project, sign recovery was modest and the median near-maximum set covered the full tested `d` range `[-0.5,0.5]`. At `q=0.5`, all 300 negative-control rows now have a null sign score; the median near-maximum `d` width was the full 1.0 at every sample size, as expected when `d` is unidentifiable. A post-run reporting correction excluded this negative-control anchor from sign accuracy; it changed no generated data, likelihood, or fit and the simulation was not repeated. The correction is recorded in the protocol and output JSON.

These results weaken the case for plugging a single estimated `d` into the policy at small cohort sizes. They support testing a policy that accounts for a broad dependence uncertainty set, but they do not show such a policy helps. The design is favorable to the estimator because every generating truth lies on the candidate grid and the correct `f0` prior family is known. It remains an assumption-only simulator; no patient data, treatment outcomes, or policy comparison was used. After the reporting correction, the focused recovery and seed-registry suites passed 16 tests in a scratch copy; one existing evidence test was skipped because its sealed-output fixture was absent there.
