# Track C exploratory switching and reversion sensitivity screen

Completed 2026-10-31 UTC. This is a development-only simulation, not a sealed test or evidence of clinical benefit. Rates are assumed stress values; they are not measured temozolomide or human recovery rates. Earlier H1 failures are unchanged.

The screen used 36 shared simulated scenarios, two observation-noise replicates, 500 filter particles, four fixed policies, and nine combinations of assumed on-drug switching and off-drug reversion rates (0, 0.01 and 0.04 per day for each). The underlying scenario and observation-noise draws were shared across policy and rate comparisons; internal filter random streams can diverge. The rate-zero condition does not remove the model's initial resistant population. Outcomes are capped time to progression in the simulated model, not patient survival.

The forced-holiday policy had a higher mean than front-loaded treatment in eight of nine cells, but a lower worst-10% scenario-mean score in seven of nine. For example, when both rates were 0.04/day, mean time was 743.5 versus 665.7 days, while the worst-10% score was 208.2 versus 361.7 days. When both rates were zero, means were 694.6 versus 662.9 days and worst-10% scores were 125.7 versus 132.2 days. In the 0.04/day switching, zero-reversion cell, mean time was 344.8 versus 356.0 days and worst-10% scores were 84.1 versus 89.9 days. These are descriptive comparisons selected for explanation; they do not establish general effects, mechanism causality, or a clinically meaningful advantage.

Only 36 scenarios support each tail estimate: the lower-decile score is determined by three full scenarios plus a fractional contribution from a fourth. Two noise replicates make uncertainty estimates coarse. The saved output supplies paired mean bootstrap intervals but no interval for the worst-10% contrast. This screen cannot establish worst-case non-inferiority, and the two favorable tail cells must not be selected as independent confirmation. Any new final test requires a separately documented protocol, predeclared estimands, justified sample size, preserved previous failures, and fresh seeds.

The actual execution used a separate seven-worker parallel harness, not a direct invocation of the sequential script named in the results. Its saved code, exact command and hash are recorded in `output/trackc_dev_plasticity_sensitivity/execution_manifest.json`; a small parallel run matched the sequential smoke output except for runtime. The execution harness itself does not enforce the sequential script's no-overwrite guard. Do not rerun it into the existing output directory. The result is `output/trackc_dev_plasticity_sensitivity/dev_sensitivity_results.json`, SHA-256 `e0246ecd49e329fbc319057459f62fa47e2940b9d3f648514c72bbc645824e24`, runtime 1007.8 seconds. Result arrays were checked for shape, finite values and the 12-course cap.

## Post-hoc choice bound

Script `src/178_trackc_choice_upper_bound.py` reuses the same development outcomes and imagines choosing either front-loaded dosing or wide containment separately for each scenario after seeing both policies' realized replicate-averaged results. This is an unimplementable, clairvoyant upper bound for choosing between those two fixed policies per scenario; it is not a bound on time-varying adaptive policies or other schedules. Compared with the better fixed policy in each cell, this selector gained 0.62–11.98 mean days. Its worst-10% gain was zero in seven of nine cells and 16.16 and 3.26 days in the other two. These are descriptive results on the same 36 scenarios, not proof of a biological limit, policy performance, or a clinically meaningful effect. They indicate limited apparent tail headroom for this specific two-policy selection problem and do not establish that the better policy is identifiable from observations. The independently checked output is `output/trackc_policy_choice_upper_bound/development_bound.json`.

## Targeted biological data search

No publicly downloadable GBM temozolomide rechallenge series at multiple prior drug-free washout durations was identified in this targeted search. [Banelli et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4825557/) report rechallenge after at least 30 days off drug; [Rabé et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6944699/) report U251 rechallenge after 12 weeks off drug. These are separate experiments with one washout duration each, not a comparable recovery time series. [Kuo et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9101568/) reports regrowth measurements after rechallenge and drug removal, which do not measure sensitivity at different prior washout durations. None identifies the simulated off-drug transition rate. Rabé and Kuo state CC BY 4.0 article licenses; no explicit open reuse license was located for the Banelli article. This limited search cannot prove that no qualifying dataset exists elsewhere.
