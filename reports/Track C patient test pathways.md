# No Open Dataset Tests Adaptive Benefit

**No published, de-identified, open-download GBM dataset found in this search can test whether Track C’s adaptive TMZ policy benefits patients.** The open cohorts report broad treatment categories or survival, but do not link actual TMZ doses and dates at each decision to tumor state, later outcomes, and toxicity. Controlled trial data are excluded under your open-download-only requirement. The nearest open patient-data alternative is an MRI classification study on UCSD-PTGBM, but it does not test treatment benefit and this project already used that cohort, so it cannot serve as a new confirmatory test. **No direct patient-policy test can be run today under your requirements.** The prior sealed simulation failures and stop rule remain unchanged.

## Open GBM data lack decision-level TMZ histories

The open published datasets located here do not contain the full record needed to replay or evaluate the adaptive policy. Public Munich, Tainan, and Utrecht GBM tables contain coarse therapy indicators and survival measures; their published fields do not record TMZ dose and administration by cycle, MRI or tumor state at each decision, treatment holds, or toxicity. An association between “TMZ yes” and survival would answer a different question from whether changing dose according to tumor state improves outcomes. ([2026 dataset paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13040-026-00549-x); [CRAN package reference manual](https://mirror.dogado.de/cran/web/packages/glioblastomaEHRsData/refman/glioblastomaEHRsData.html))

The published MRI cohorts have the complementary gap. UCSD-PTGBM is de-identified and downloadable under CC BY 4.0; it contains **178 patients and 243 post-treatment MRI timepoints**, with labels distinguishing residual or recurrent tumor from treatment-related change. OS and PFS are available for a subset of **94 patients**. Those data can test MRI classification or prognosis under observed care, but the dataset description does not provide actual TMZ dose histories by cycle or treatment-decision records. It therefore cannot measure the benefit of Track C’s adaptive dosing rule. ([UCSD-PTGBM paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12886825/); [TCIA collection](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/ucsd-ptgbm/))

Other open files reviewed, including CFB-GBM v4, MU-Glioma-Post, LUMIERE, and RHUH-GBM’s public clinical and processed-image components, likewise do not publish the complete combination of delivered TMZ by decision, decision-time tumor state, subsequent outcome, and toxicity. RHUH’s raw DICOM images require controlled access; its processed and clinical components are public. ACRIN-DSC-MR-Brain/ACRIN 6677 is classified by TCIA as NIH Controlled Data Access and is excluded under your rule. This is a finding about the published manifests and documentation reviewed; it does not prove that no unpublished files exist. MU-Glioma-Post and UCSD-PTGBM were already examined in this project, so new results on those sources cannot be presented as confirmatory under the project’s exposure rule. ([CFB-GBM collection](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/cfb-gbm/); [MU-Glioma-Post collection](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/mu-glioma-post/); [LUMIERE paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9755255/); [RHUH-GBM collection](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/rhuh-gbm/); [ACRIN collection and access policy](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/acrin-dsc-mr-brain/); [project exposure rule](../paper/research_plan_complete_project_form.md))

## Fixed-schedule trials do not test Track C’s policy

Human trials have compared fixed TMZ schedules, but those results do not test a patient-state-triggered rule. RTOG 0525 randomized **833 patients** to standard five-days-per-28-day-cycle TMZ or dose-dense 21-days-per-28-day-cycle TMZ. The dose-dense schedule did not improve overall or progression-free survival and increased severe toxicity. These are aggregate fixed-schedule results, not openly downloadable patient-level decision histories for Track C’s policy. The NCI archive routes submitted NCTN/NCORP patient-level datasets through dbGaP, but this review did not verify whether RTOG 0525 itself is in the current inventory. No open-download patient-level RTOG 0525 file was identified. ([RTOG 0525 trial report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3816958/); [NCI archive access description](https://dctd.cancer.gov/research/networks/nctn/data-archive); [dbGaP access FAQ](https://dbgap.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/FAQs/data-access-requests/))

The morning-versus-evening TMZ study is another close but insufficient example: patients kept dosing diaries and the study collected outcomes and adverse events, yet the publication does not release patient-level records. Its randomized question concerns dosing time, not changing TMZ in response to each patient’s evolving tumor state. GBM AGILE’s “response-adaptive” randomization changes assignment across trial arms; it does not assign Track C’s within-patient dose rule. No located human GBM study both tested that adaptive TMZ policy and released qualifying patient data for open download. ([TMZ chronotherapy trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9113320/); [GBM AGILE trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03970447))

## A forecast-only study is a non-satisfying fallback

UCSD-PTGBM could support a real-patient prediction study with the exact endpoint **patient-held-out classification of residual or recurrent tumor versus treatment-related change on post-treatment MRI**. That endpoint is published and measurable from its open labels. It would test image interpretation, not whether adaptive TMZ improves patient outcomes. The cohort is already exposed in this project’s annotation-change work, so the new result would also be exploratory under the project’s existing rule. It does not meet your stated requirement and is not offered as the Track C patient-policy test. ([UCSD-PTGBM paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12886825/); [project source-exposure rule](../paper/research_plan_complete_project_form.md))

## Keep the patient-policy gate closed until a qualifying open dataset exists

A direct retrospective policy-benefit test requires, at minimum, one de-identified patient-by-decision record linking actual TMZ dose and timing, prior treatment and interruptions, tumor measurement available before each decision, toxicity and other decision factors, and later progression or survival. The data must also contain enough patients receiving the relevant alternatives to support a causal comparison; time-varying treatment choices are confounded by the prognosis and toxicity that trigger them. Current public datasets do not meet these conditions. ([Hernán and Robins, target-trial emulation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4832051/); [Murphy et al., dynamic treatment strategies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4216645/))

The next actionable step is a cohort search gate, not scoring: require a published source, de-identified patient-level data, direct open download, an unexposed cohort, and the complete decision-level fields above before writing a new patient-policy protocol. Do not count dbGaP, Vivli/PDS, SAILOR, Duke, or other request/approval-based sources. If no qualifying open dataset appears, the exact patient-benefit test remains infeasible under the stated access constraint. A prospective clinical test would require a qualified neuro-oncology team, institutional review and determination, **appropriate informed consent or an approved waiver, as applicable**, and safety oversight; it is not a student-run treatment experiment. ([Society for Science human-participant rules](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/); [HHS Common Rule](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/regulations/45-cfr-46/index.html))

The 31 October amendment still applies to the old simulator: retain both sealed H1 failures and do not open another sealed simulation test. A future patient-policy study would be separate and must not replace those results. Citation sources were checked against live or official pages, but archived snapshots were not created or checked. ([Track C status and amendment](../paper/trackc_final_status_2026-10-31.md))

## Conclusion

Under your requirements, **there is no currently identified direct real-patient test of adaptive TMZ benefit**. Keep searching only for an open, previously unexposed patient dataset with treatment decisions, actual doses, decision-time tumor states, outcomes, and toxicity. The available open MRI option answers a different question and cannot satisfy the request; the available trial IPD routes are excluded because they are not open downloads.
