# Strengthen GBM research with independent evidence

**Research date: November 16, 2026.** The strongest additions are an independent-mechanism test of treatment control, a patient-paired recurrence study, and an empirical measurement study connecting MRI error to forecasting decisions. Each addresses a different weakness in Tracks A–C. Adding all three immediately would spread effort too thin: choose one primary paper question and one supporting extension. A new neural architecture or another expression signature offers less value than an independent measurement, a strong simple comparator, or a test designed to disprove the proposed link. The current negative results remain part of the evidence. None of the options below has passed all data-access, cohort, novelty, and power gates, and none guarantees publication.

## The best addition depends on the paper’s central claim

The project currently supports a research program about **how reliable GBM measurements support prediction and simulated decisions**. It does not establish one patient-level molecular–MRI–treatment model. Track A’s regional predictor and TMZ-response bridge failed their frozen rules; Track B’s locked forecasting comparisons failed; the MRI-to-biology integration checks failed; EARF is implemented and locked but unevaluated. Track C remains simulation-only, with its final run requirements and precision rule unchanged. These statements follow the latest handoff and supersede older, more optimistic drafts. ([Project state](../START_HERE.md); [EARF protocol](../paper/integration_earf_protocol_2026-11-14.md))

For **near-term feasibility**, rank independent-mechanism falsification first. It uses the existing simulator without needing a new clinical dataset, although it still inherits the blocked evaluation gates. For **new biological evidence**, rank paired recurrence robustness first, followed closely by orthogonal RNA–protein spatial validation. For **strengthening the existing MRI forecast paper**, empirical measurement error is the most direct addition, but access to repeat-scan data is a material obstacle. These rankings are planning judgments, not measured performance.

| Candidate addition | Distinct scientific contribution | Main limitation | Estimated effort after access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent-mechanism falsification | Tests whether EARF survives a generator different from its own assumptions | Simulation evidence; evaluation gates still apply | Several weeks |
| Paired recurrence robustness | Tests whether measured state shifts survive state definition and composition changes | Independent replication and exact paired count unresolved | 3–6 weeks |
| RNA–protein spatial validation | Uses a different assay to challenge an RNA-defined niche | Section matching, donor count, and novelty need checking | 4–8 weeks |
| Empirical MRI measurement reliability | Tests whether forecast differences survive real acquisition/label variation | Repeat-scan access; small cohort | Several weeks or longer |
| Drug perturbation transportability | Challenges molecular claims with measured experimental perturbation | Few independent donors; transcription is not viability | 2–5 weeks |
| Digital pathology–molecular concordance | Adds an independent image modality and cross-cohort test | Large files, sample joins, already-exposed cohorts | 6–12 weeks |

These estimates exclude access delays, annotation work, failed feasibility gates, and mentor review. Treat “Track D/E/F” as organizational names only. A substantive new endpoint matters more than the track count.

## Challenge treatment control with mechanisms it never saw

**Proposed question: Does evidence-anchored control remain useful when its model of the tumor or observation process is wrong?** EARF currently constructs its ambiguity box and its primary simulated world distribution from the same reliability anchors. The protocol explicitly identifies this partial circularity. A more demanding extension would develop the controller on declared mechanisms and evaluate it on an entirely held-out mechanism, such as reversible state switching, delayed drug effects, transient treatment-associated MRI change, or signal reliability that changes over time. First inventory mechanisms already exposed in Track C; relabeling an existing sensitivity grid would add little. ([EARF protocol](../paper/integration_earf_protocol_2026-11-14.md))

The primary comparison should remain paired and fair: EARF versus no-signal control and a prespecified simple policy, with matched observation access, treatment budgets, patient draws, and objectives. Prespecify one primary utility contrast on the held-out mechanism family. Report harmful decisions, lower-tail outcomes, and regret as secondary endpoints. A positive result requires the applicable frozen uncertainty and precision criteria; a benefit confined to the familiar generator is a failure of the broader robustness claim. This proposal does not authorize a run or relax the 14-day precision target.

This direction has a credible scientific contribution because it tests **decision robustness across mechanisms**, rather than searching for another favorable parameter setting. However, neither robust optimization nor model selection is new. Hiremath and colleagues already study identifiability and model selection in high-grade glioma; risk-aware digital-twin optimization also has close prior art. The control researcher verified these references at abstract/search level during this session, not through a completed new full-text comparison. The exact contribution therefore needs a focused methods-and-code novelty audit. ([Hiremath et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12105797/); [Chaudhuri et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10598726/))

A related, narrower extension asks whether **observationally similar tumors imply conflicting treatment choices**. Construct paired synthetic models whose observed burden histories agree within a fixed tolerance, then test whether their preferred schedules differ. Compare point-estimate MPC, robust MPC, EARF, no-signal control, and a simple rule. The outcome is action disagreement and regret under both plausible worlds, not parameter recovery alone. Failure means the proposed protection does not reduce prespecified regret, or the models do not actually meet the locked observational-equivalence criterion. This extends existing identifiability and action-sensitivity work; it is not a wholly separate discovery.

Explicit toxicity/recovery states and additional RL agents rank lower. TMZ scheduling, resistance, and toxicity already have prior art, and this search did not verify an open patient-linked dose–blood-count–response dataset suitable for calibration. A course penalty is not measured toxicity. Add complexity only when it yields a distinct, observable, falsifiable prediction. ([Segura-Collar et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9616068/), abstract-level verification in this session)

## Add biological evidence through pairing or an independent assay

**Best biological candidate: recurrence-state robustness.** GSE174554 and its associated longitudinal atlas provide a concrete route to paired primary–recurrence analysis. The original RNA analysis describes **36 matched patients**, while the official GEO series summary lists **40 primary and 40 matched recurrent specimens**. These are not interchangeable eligible counts. Reconcile released samples, assay types, patient identifiers, and first-versus-later recurrence before selecting a cohort. Mesenchymal shifts and AP1 regulation are already reported; rediscovering them would be replication. ([Wang et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43018-022-00475-x); [GSE174554](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE174554))

The stronger question is whether a patient’s measured malignant-state change remains stable under **independent published state definitions and changes in sampled cell composition**. Freeze the state definitions, primary contrast, and robustness criterion before opening expression values. Use patient-paired pseudobulk; cells and libraries remain nested observations. Compare against composition-only and simple gene-set baselines. Require an independent source for a transportability claim, checking biological origins and study lineage rather than assuming different accession numbers mean different patients. Failure includes missing pair mappings, material sign changes under reasonable definitions, or unsuccessful external replication. Two destructive tissue snapshots do not identify state-transition rates or prove that a particular treatment caused the change.

**Potentially stronger measurement anchor: spatial RNA-to-protein validation.** Greenwald and colleagues deposited spatial RNA at GSE237183 and processed spatial RNA/CODEX resources at Zenodo 8105466. Their study already establishes layered organization and hypoxia-related niches. A useful new question would test whether one frozen RNA-defined relation survives protein measurement and a spatial null preserving tissue density. Establish whether assays represent the same section, adjacent sections, or different specimens; do not assume same-cell correspondence. ([Greenwald et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11088502/); [Processed resource](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8105466))

The primary endpoint could be donor-level excess protein-confirmed adjacency or a distance effect. Compare the spatial model with cell abundance alone, distance to necrosis alone, and density-preserving permutations. Hold out complete donors. Failure includes unverifiable section mapping, disappearance under the spatial null, or failure across donors. This can supply independent measurement evidence that another RNA-only signature cannot. It still does not prove cell signaling or a causal immune mechanism. Related recurrent perivascular collagen/macrophage niches are already published, further narrowing novelty. ([Onubogu et al.](https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/179853))

**Experimental perturbation is a smaller, distinct option.** GSE148842 contains acute-slice drug experiments from six GBM resections, plus a separate patient-specific screen. Audit which donors actually have matched treated/control samples for the chosen drug. Test whether a frozen baseline state score predicts donor-held-out transcriptional perturbation beyond proliferation-only and matched-gene-set baselines. Use one drug and one primary pathway endpoint. Failure means insufficient paired donors or no improvement over the simple baseline. This is measured perturbation, but not clinical efficacy, viability AUC, or a TMZ kill-rate estimate. The small independent donor count makes an exploratory study more realistic than a strong standalone predictive claim. ([Zhao et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8114529/); [GSE148842](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE148842))

Digital pathology is a genuine new modality but a heavier commitment. CPTAC’s primary GBM study covers 99 treatment-naive tumors; that is not a verified count of usable slide–RNA pairs. A sensible question would test interpretable H&E features against an independently measured phenotype, beyond purity and cell composition, using patient-grouped tiles and a simple linear model before a larger network. CPTAC and TCGA already contributed to project integration analyses, so neither can be called globally untouched. Sample joins, endpoint-specific reuse, stain/site effects, and external independence must be resolved first. ([CPTAC primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33577785/); [Official imaging collection](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/cptac-gbm/))

## Measure when MRI errors change the scientific conclusion

**Proposed question: Are forecast gains larger than real measurement variation?** The project already propagates assumed spatial boundary uncertainty. The missing evidence is empirical calibration using repeat acquisitions or independent expert labels, followed by a frozen downstream forecast comparison. RIDER-NEURO-MRI lists **19 recurrent GBM patients** with repeat acquisitions roughly two days apart, including repeat DTI and contrast-enhanced FLAIR in **17**. The current official image record points to NIH controlled access. It is a candidate, not a ready unrestricted dataset; sponsor eligibility and exact usable pairs remain open. ([Official RIDER record](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/rider-neuro-mri/))

Estimate paired log-volume differences, boundary displacement, and growth-direction flips. Propagate the empirical errors through the fixed forecaster and compare no uncertainty, simple isotropic perturbations, and the existing correlated-boundary model. The patient is the inference unit. Separate acquisition, registration, and annotation effects where possible. Failure means the richer error model does not improve held-out proper scores/calibration or does not change model rankings. A short repeat interval does not prove zero biological change, and a small cohort cannot establish a universal scanner-noise floor.

Repeatability itself is established prior art: Shiri and colleagues studied test–retest and registration effects in GBM radiomics; Suter and colleagues evaluated longitudinal automated measurement consistency on LUMIERE. The defensible addition is the **consequence for forecast comparisons and simulated decisions**, with independent empirical error evidence. Re-segmenting already-used cohorts does not restore untouched status. ([Shiri et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32615647/); [Suter et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10513769/))

A lower-cost extension is a temporal forecast-abstention benchmark: can a score available before the next scan identify forecasts that should be withheld? Measure patient-level risk–coverage curves and error versus persistence at fixed retained-patient coverage. Compare recent change, size, and scan interval against the proposed uncertainty score and random rejection. Set thresholds on development/calibration patients only. Failure means no incremental value over these simple scores or poor external calibration. Generic conformal glioma segmentation already exists; voxel coverage does not imply patient-trajectory coverage, and site shift does not automatically preserve conformal guarantees. ([CONSeg](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40610235/))

Postoperative segmentation transport and treatment-phase audits are useful supporting studies. RHUH-GBM describes preoperative, early postoperative, and recurrence images, but exact labels, rights, dates, overlap, and complete cases still need verification. Surgery changes anatomy; recurrence-selected visits do not make a general next-scan cohort. Likewise, MRI change is not automatically biological growth: RANO 2.0 explicitly addresses treatment timing and confirmation of apparent early progression. Without trustworthy treatment and confirmation labels, target observed image-label change rather than true resistance or pseudoprogression. ([RHUH-GBM](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/rhuh-gbm/); [RANO 2.0](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.23.01059))

## Build one decisive paper before expanding the program

The most coherent near-term paper question is: **When do uncertain GBM signals justify changing a simulated treatment decision?** Existing A/integration failures motivate limits on signal trust; C supplies the decision experiment; independent-mechanism falsification challenges the result. Include B only where its actual evidence informs observation assumptions. The molecular, imaging, and simulation cohorts must remain separate. A second, biologically focused paper could instead center recurrence-state robustness or cross-assay spatial validation. Forcing every track into one manuscript would weaken both stories.

**First two weeks:** choose one primary question, complete a closest-prior-art comparison, and perform metadata-only eligibility screening for one biological or measurement candidate. Record sample joins, rights, biological origins, prior exposure, available endpoints, and exclusions. Write a power/precision plan before outcomes. Close the existing Track C run gates if selecting control falsification. Keep CFB/WAW protected under the locked Track B chain; do not divert their outcomes into new model selection.

**By six weeks:** finish a synthetic-validated implementation and one frozen evaluation if gates permit. Produce a patient/donor flow diagram, baseline comparison, uncertainty display, and explicit failure analysis. For a new cohort, separate discovery, calibration, and confirmation by independent patients and sources. If the cohort fails eligibility, report feasibility and switch only through a dated new plan; do not replace a failed endpoint after inspecting results.

**By twelve weeks:** prioritize independent replication, reproducible environments, source/output hashes, a complete analysis history, and a manuscript organized around one claim. Independent perturbation/organoid validation is a valuable longer-term route, but current candidate listings have not established sufficient independent origins or usable assay joins. Organoid viability alone cannot identify patient-specific treatment dynamics. ([Verduin et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10733660/), abstract-level verification in this session)

This was a focused literature and dataset screen, not a systematic review. Biological core articles were retrieved in full; some official records and control references remained search/abstract-level because retrieval stalled. No new patient values, reserved outcomes, or policy results were analyzed. The immediate recommendation is **one independent challenge to the central claim**, supported by a small eligibility audit, rather than launching every candidate track.


## Metadata follow-up on November 16, 2026

The follow-up narrows the biological recommendations. The [recurrence audit](../paper/recurrence_metadata_gate_2026-11-16.md) distinguishes the original paper's 86 specimens from 49 patients and its 36-patient paired subgroup from the current GEO listing. An alternative official text endpoint succeeded after the browser page was blocked: GEO itself does claim 40 primary and 40 matched recurrent specimens. The interim attribution of that count only to secondary sources is superseded. The actual listing has 113 GSM records, including 111 human and two mouse records; 81 titles explicitly identify snRNA samples. A conservative metadata rule finds 27 pair IDs containing exactly one primary and one recurrent snRNA record. Three additional groups have both stages but repeat libraries or multiple recurrence specimens; six pair IDs have only a recurrent snRNA record. These are mapping candidates, not certified eligible pairs: diagnosis, first-recurrence status, repeat-library handling, processed-data linkage, and overlap still need reconciliation. Raw reads are in EGA EGAS00001004909; the paper places processed expression and spatial/peak data at GEO. Do not treat the series-level 40-pair claim as a verified usable count.

The [spatial audit](../paper/spatial_protein_metadata_gate_2026-11-16.md) verifies an open CC BY 4.0 Zenodo release, version 12624860, with 38 files totaling 25,333,309,514 bytes. RNA and CODEX use near-adjacent sections, not the same cells. Five donor codes overlap, but no complete section-to-image crosswalk was verified, so zero donors are currently certified eligible for the proposed matched cross-assay endpoint. This count does not mean the data lack valid pairings; it means this audit did not establish them. Keep this as a conditional exploratory option. The older six-donor regional-benchmark rule is not a universal threshold for a distinct new endpoint; the new question needs its own precision plan. Do not download the full deposit before resolving mapping.


### Supplementary sample table reconciliation

The next metadata-only step retrieved Supplementary Table 1 and extracted only sample identity, stage, pairing, diagnosis, IDH, and assay fields. Among 86 rows marked snRNA-seq, it lists 40 primary and 46 recurrent specimens; 83 rows are IDH-wildtype and three IDH-mutant. There are 33 simple Table 1 pairs with one primary and one recurrent specimen, both IDH-wildtype. Of these, 27 have unique exact specimen-ID matches to GEO snRNA records with agreeing stage labels. This provides a concrete candidate subset for protocol design, not a final eligible cohort: first-recurrence identity, processed-matrix coverage, quality, and origin independence remain to be established. Other groups require resolving missing GSM matches, different specimen naming, repeated libraries, or stage disagreement. Pair numbers may be renumbered between sources; compare specimen membership rather than the numeric labels. See the [specimen crosswalk](../paper/recurrence_specimen_crosswalk_2026-11-16.md) and its curated metadata manifest. No expression or survival values were extracted. The earlier series-summary counts remain provenance records rather than selection rules.


### File coverage and closest-prior-art update

All 54 specimens in the 27-pair candidate subset have complete matrix/features/barcodes triples in the official archive listing: 61 libraries totaling 713,265,211 compressed bytes. Seven specimens have multiple libraries; their capture/resequencing relationship remains to be established. File contents and biological quality were not inspected.

A focused search identified [Alexopoulos and Karampelas (October 25, 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1177/15578100261499487), which already analyzes patient-level recurrence trajectories in Wang and CARE with uncertainty and malignant-annotation checks. Its accessible abstract is close prior art; full methods were restricted. Consequently the broad paired-recurrence proposal above is downgraded as a novelty claim. The narrower candidate is technical measurement reliability with a fixed biological construct, not generic trajectory discovery or a new gene module. See the [feasibility and draft plan](../paper/recurrence_reliability_plan_2026-11-16.md) for endpoints, comparators, gates, and precision limitations. No new protocol is adopted.
