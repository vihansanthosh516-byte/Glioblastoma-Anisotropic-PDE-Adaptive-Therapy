# Make Spatial Boundary Forecasts More Reliable

## Correction: synthetic nuisance factors were coupled

The historical five- and twenty-cohort conformal outputs, and the 100-series full-hybrid outputs, did **not** vary scan-gap order independently of anisotropy axis. The generator derived both from `replicate % 3`. Patient-level train/calibration/test exchangeability within that induced joint distribution is not thereby disproved, but the runs do not test robustness to independently varying gaps and axes. Earlier text claiming independent randomization was wrong. The old outputs remain preserved as results for the coupled design. Corrected independent-factor conformal pilot and recheck now have separate files: [5 cohorts](../output/track_b_conformal_factor_independent_5rep_20261080.json) and [20 cohorts](../output/track_b_conformal_factor_independent_20rep_seed20261070.json). No patient images were used.

Track B does not yet have a reliable estimate of its boundary-error covariance: the fitted correlation length repeatedly lands on the 80 mm search edge and the fitted scale remains far above the simulated truth even with 256 anchors. That is a warning that the parameter is unidentified or the residual model is misspecified, not evidence that the biological correlation length is 80 mm. The practical fix is to treat covariance fitting as a diagnostic and calibrate the **whole predicted boundary at the patient level**, with width measured alongside coverage. The corrected independent-factor 20-cohort synthetic recheck is below: it reached 97.5% coverage at nominal 90% under misspecified covariance, but median bands occupied 48.5% of the toy grid and the worst cohort reached 80%. This is a development result, not MRI validation. The proposed two-part output—geodesic boundary expansion plus a conformal bound on disconnected missed tumor volume—has already been published for static tumor masks, while trajectory-wide conformal bands for randomly timed scalar biomarkers and class-aware weighted conformal volume intervals also exist. A longitudinal PDE boundary forecast remains a possible application-level gap, not a demonstrated new mathematical method.

## More anchors do not replace more patients

**Project evidence.** In the 12-patient cross-fit forecast, all folds selected ell = 80 mm with 256 anchors and estimated sigma = 3.18–3.52 mm against a generating sigma of 0.6 mm. In a separate exact-Gaussian covariance diagnostic using the same estimator and 256 anchors, the true 4 mm range was selected in 6/10 replicates with 8 independent fields and 9/10 with 32. These small simulations point toward the number of independent patient fields as a constraint, but do not isolate whether the full forecast failure comes from few patients, model discrepancy, or both. ([12-patient fit](../output/track_b_crossfit_wide_12_256anchors_seed20261028.json), [exact-model diagnostic](../output/track_b_calibration_identifiability_pilot_20261029.json))

**Literature evidence.** With fixed-domain sampling, Zhang shows that Matérn variance and range are not generally separately consistently estimable, even when sampling becomes arbitrarily dense inside a bounded domain; the exponential covariance is the Matérn special case with smoothness one-half. That is a caution about dense measurements on one field, not a theorem that directly proves failure for this multi-patient, boundary-surface estimator. More independent patients add independent fields; more anchors mostly sample each patient’s bounded surface more densely. ([Zhang, 2004, full text](https://www.stat.purdue.edu/~zhanghao/Paper/JASA2004.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214504000000241))

The estimator comparison should be empirical. The current variogram WLS is inexpensive and useful as a transparent baseline, but a fitted minimizer hides a flat or boundary-truncated parameter profile. Profile ML/REML can show how much the likelihood supports different ranges and scales; it does not remove fixed-domain identifiability or model-family error. Xu and Brown describe profile-likelihood intervals for weakly identified geostatistical covariance parameters and report better interval coverage than Wald intervals in their simulations. Zimmerman and Zimmerman found simple OLS and some WLS estimators nearly matched ML/REML in the Gaussian intrinsic-field models they tested, so the likelihood fit should be compared rather than presumed superior. A profile-weighted covariance mixture or hierarchical Bayesian model can propagate plausible parameter values into forecasts, but its result remains sensitive to the assumed family and prior. Resample whole patients—not individual anchors—to quantify sampling uncertainty. ([Xu & Brown, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spasta.2024.100821), [Zimmerman & Zimmerman, 1991](https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.1991.10484771))

| Option | Best use | Main limit |
|---|---|---|
| Variogram WLS | Fast baseline and empirical spatial-dependence summary | Can return a precise-looking edge minimum without showing parameter uncertainty |
| Profile ML/REML | Compare likelihood support across range, scale, nugget, and mean terms | More computation; still conditional on the covariance family and field assumptions |
| Profile mixture or Bayesian hierarchy | Propagate covariance-parameter uncertainty instead of plugging in one value | Prior/profile and model-family sensitivity; no distribution-free coverage guarantee |
| Patient bootstrap | Show uncertainty in between-patient estimates and score differences | Unstable with few independent patients; never bootstrap anchors as if independent |
| Patient-level conformal calibration | Calibrate future prediction error without requiring the Gaussian covariance model to be correct | Marginal guarantee requires exchangeable patient units; bands can be too wide |

The first rule for a fitted range should be explicit: if its profile remains flat or its minimizer hits the 80 mm limit, report the range as **not identified on this grid**, retain the profile or predictive mixture, and do not present 80 mm as a biological estimate.

## Calibrate the forecast after freezing it

**Literature evidence.** Split conformal prediction can calibrate a fixed predictor using one nonconformity score per independent patient and give finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability. It does not require the covariance model to be correct. A symmetric Hausdorff score between predicted and target surfaces turns the score into a boundary radius; taking the maximum across future horizons for each patient would make the calibration target simultaneous over those horizons. The patient trajectory—not each voxel, anchor, scan, or forecast horizon—must be the exchangeable unit. If several forecast origins come from one patient, group them inside that patient’s score or split them by patient. ([Lei et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2017.1307116))

Small calibration sets make the guarantee coarse. The split-conformal rank is ceil((n + 1)(1 − alpha)); with 20 calibration patients, the 90% radius is the 19th order statistic and the 95% radius is the maximum calibration score. A finite 90% quantile requires at least 9 calibration patients; 95% requires at least 19. A 20-patient calibration set can produce a finite 95% band, but it gives little information about how stable that maximum will be. The guarantee is marginal over new exchangeable patients, not a guarantee for each scanner, site, subtype, or distribution shift.

For the candidate, define one joint score per patient. Let `B_i` be the largest boundary-expansion score across prespecified future horizons, and let `V_i` be the largest missed-disconnected-component volume across those horizons. Choose positive scales `a` and `b` using proper-training patients only, then set `S_i = max(B_i/a, V_i/b)`. Calibrate the finite-sample conformal quantile `q` from one score per calibration patient. If the forecast and score rule are frozen before calibration and patients are exchangeable, then `S_new <= q` implies both boundary and missed-volume limits hold at every prespecified horizon with marginal probability at least `1 - alpha`. This uses one joint rank and avoids spending alpha twice. It is a standard conformal construction, not a new theorem; its possible contribution is whether the resulting sets stay informative on longitudinal GBM data.

Use `k = ceil((n_cal + 1)(1 - alpha))` and the `k`th calibration order statistic; set `q = infinity` if `k > n_cal`. At 95% joint coverage, at least 19 calibration patients are needed for a finite threshold, and with 20 patients the threshold is the maximum score. Separate 97.5% calibration for each of two outputs would need at least 39 calibration patients per output for a finite threshold. The scales, component matching, boundary score, missing-volume score, horizon set, and any bias correction must be fixed without calibration labels. Keep every forecast origin from one patient inside that patient's single maximum score. Report held-out joint coverage and output size together; coverage without a width ceiling is not evidence of useful uncertainty.

**Novelty verdict.** This search found no paper with the exact combination “patient-level 3D spatial tumor-boundary trajectories + topology-aware conformal sets + a reaction-diffusion PDE forecast,” but that absence does not establish novelty. Davenport already proves mask-level conformal containment; Vargas combines geodesic expansion with instance-level conformal handling of missed tumor foci; Tassopoulou calibrates whole randomly timed trajectories; TUNE++ combines topology and uncertainty; CA-WCP handles asymmetric weighted tumor-volume intervals; and Laslo et al. already hybridize mechanistic tumor dynamics with generative imaging. PGSMT adds progression-aware longitudinal segmentation. The current proposal therefore has **weak novelty evidence and no demonstrated new theorem**. A simple composition of existing trajectory calibration and spatial sets is unlikely to be enough. Before any patient analysis, define a mathematical operation that is not present in these papers, then compare it against each direct composition on identical synthetic forecasts. The corrected 20-cohort recheck below does not test that candidate: it has one future horizon and uses a symmetric Hausdorff radius, not geodesic expansion or a missed-component volume bound.

## Run a staged open synthetic comparison first

**Ranked recommendation.** First, make the primary reliability test patient-level and trajectory-wide, with all forecast horizons held together per patient. Compare the existing symmetric-Hausdorff band, the Vargas-style geodesic/structural output, and a simple max-over-horizon split-conformal baseline on exactly the same frozen PDE forecasts. Second, isolate covariance recovery with independent synthetic fields: vary patient count (8, 16, 32, 64) separately from anchor count (24, 64, 256), and compare current WLS, profiled ML/REML, and profile-mixture forecasts. Use exact exponential truth first, then Matérn/aniso misspecification, varying patient scales, and separate global, smooth spatial, and patient-specific mean-bias conditions. Randomize scan gaps, anisotropic axes, condition assignment, and seeds independently of patient index. Third, train any mean-bias correction only on training patients, calibrate on a disjoint patient set, and evaluate once on untouched patients. Publish the generator, configurations, and seeds so another researcher can reproduce the comparison. No GPU or patient images are needed for this phase; keep the PDE grid and forecast draws at the reduced CPU setting until a clean runtime benchmark supports scaling. The current record lists 8 logical processors and 31.6 GiB RAM; the corrected run has no recorded wall-clock time, so a full-factorial runtime estimate would be invented. ([hardware/runtime record](../output/track_b_runtime_profile_32cubed_loaded_host.json))

The result must fail visibly. If the profile is flat or the range repeatedly hits a search edge under correctly specified data, the covariance parameter is unidentified for that design. If untouched patient-level coverage falls below its target beyond Monte Carlo uncertainty, calibration fails for that scenario. If coverage is achieved only with bands above a width ceiling agreed before analysis, the method is calibrated but not practically informative. If bias correction narrows bands only for the known global translation and fails for smooth or patient-varying bias, retain it only as that narrow special case. If the trajectory-wide method does not improve width or proper forecast scores over the Vargas-style and max-score baselines while maintaining coverage, the proposed method has no demonstrated advantage.

**Public-data follow-up.** CFB-GBM v4 is an openly listed TCIA collection of 264 glioblastoma patients, 3,981 images, and 208.01 GB, with CC BY 4.0 listed for images/segmentations and clinical TSVs. It contains pretreatment scans and post-treatment scans around four and six months when available; sequences and annotations vary across patients. The current TCIA page lists GTV annotations for 261, 160, and 122 patients at t0, t1, and t2, but the v2.0 data paper lists 112 at t2. Resolve this version difference from the downloaded v4 manifest before counting eligible patients. ([TCIA CFB-GBM v4](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/cfb-gbm/), [CFB-GBM v2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17884))

The 2026 PREDICT-GBM study independently shows that ground-truth choice can change the apparent result: its ordinary follow-up masks come from BraTS segmentation, while a sensitivity analysis on 40 RHUH patients uses expert-corrected recurrence masks. The relative ranking stayed similar, but absolute coverage fell and the model-versus-standard-care gap narrowed. The authors state that a larger expert-annotated cohort is needed. Therefore, calibrating against an automated mask guarantees agreement with that annotation process under exchangeability; it does not guarantee agreement with expert contours or biological tumor extent. Compare manual/expert-corrected and model-generated labels separately wherever provenance permits. ([PREDICT-GBM final article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-03194-0))

The v2.0 paper says nnU-Net generated most missing longitudinal GTV masks after fine-tuning on original CFB ground-truth contours. Five radiation oncologists reviewed and corrected a 44-mask sample; the authors note that correction may introduce anchoring because reviewers could not delineate every case from scratch. Thus, 97% annotation completion does not mean 97% independent manual ground truth. The study must recover per-mask provenance and separate original manual, expert-corrected, and model-generated targets. Primary calibration must use an adequately sized complete series of original manual masks; if that set cannot support the predeclared calibration and test sizes, stop or redefine the target as agreement with provided annotations. The dataset defines GTV as clinically visible tumor, so success predicts annotation boundaries; it does not validate the microscopic invasive front or true biological tumor extent.

CFB-GBM also selects adults who were deceased, treated outside a clinical trial, and imaged at one center. Coverage would apply only to an exchangeable population resembling this selected cohort, not to all patients with GBM. Its size exceeds desktop RAM as a single working set, so any later analysis should stage and process patient-by-patient. It cannot identify pure scan noise because longitudinal masks also contain true growth, treatment response, segmentation, and registration discrepancy.

## Conclusion

The reliable target is a future patient's annotated GTV trajectory, not a single covariance parameter or microscopic tumor boundary. The next study should test whether patient-level, full-horizon calibration can produce simultaneous boundary and missed-component guarantees at an acceptable width, while treating covariance fitting, bias correction, annotation provenance, and PDE prediction as separate sources of uncertainty. The method is not yet shown useful on MRI, and its novelty is not established; the strongest defensible contribution would be a carefully controlled, reproducible comparison showing when trajectory-wide PDE boundary sets remain calibrated against independently manual annotations and when width, label uncertainty, or model discrepancy makes them fail.
