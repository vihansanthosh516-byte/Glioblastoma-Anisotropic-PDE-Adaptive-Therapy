# Close Track C Without Reopening Tests

Track C has a defensible resolution, but not a way to turn its assumptions into biological facts or erase its failed tail tests. Close it as an **exploratory, assumption-conditional simulation of mean-versus-worst-decile trade-offs**. Preserve both sealed H1 failures, correct the CVaR prior-art claim, and correct the “three days of headroom” description: script 153 did not calculate a maximum. Tail-risk sequential drug control is established in other pharmacologic settings, and a 2026 cancer-dosing article reports CVaR10, though that article is a low-confidence precedent. The available washout studies measure isolated phenotypes, not the repeated TMZ sensitivity trajectory needed to estimate the simulator’s off-drug reversion rate. The 31 October stop rule remains the correct gate.

## Washout studies measure phenotypes, not Track C’s rate

The biological blocker needs narrower wording. Banelli et al. tested patient-derived GBM cultures after at least 30 days without TMZ: rechallenge sensitivity partly returned in two cultures, but each had only one reported washout duration. In contrast, Rabé et al. found that U251 cells remained partly resistant after a three-month washout, with resistance restored after brief TMZ exposure. These are meaningful functional observations, yet they do not estimate a common per-day resistant-to-sensitive transition rate, and their different outcomes argue against assuming one universal rate across GBM models. ([Banelli et al., 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4825557/); [Rabé et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6944699/))

Rabé’s public RNA-sequencing time course samples cells during TMZ exposure, not at multiple drug-free intervals followed by functional dose-response. The dormancy study measured cells after 7 and 15 days without TMZ and documented proliferation recovery after 15 days; dormancy exit is not the same endpoint as restored TMZ sensitivity. Neither expression snapshots nor regrowth alone identifies Track C’s transition rate. The accurate statement is: **this targeted search found no public, same-model series with functional TMZ sensitivity measured across several washout durations**; it does not prove that no such data exist anywhere. ([Rabé et al. and BioProject PRJNA479416](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/PRJNA479416); [Oncogene dormancy study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41388-020-1302-8))

That resolves blocker 1 for the present project: label the rates as assumed, retain the sensitivity screen as descriptive development evidence, and do not fit or choose rates from single washout points. A future protocol can be considered only after one study measures baseline and multiple prespecified drug-free durations in the same preferably patient-derived GBM model, with replicate-level post-washout TMZ dose-response and documented schedules. An outside reviewer must agree that those data bound the model rates before outcomes are examined, as the amendment already requires. ([Track C status and amendment](../paper/trackc_final_status_2026-10-31.md))

## The two sealed tail failures remain the result

Blocker 2 cannot be repaired by another look at the same outcome. Test 171 failed the prespecified lower-tail non-inferiority rule in the state-linked moderate suite: arm-minus-front CVaR10 was −4.18 days, with 95% CI [−7.97, −1.44], below the required lower-bound threshold of −7 days. Test 173 failed in the nominal suite: −3.78 days, 95% CI [−8.04, −1.33]. The tests failed in different suites; 173 was a sequential retest of the same selected rule family, not an independent new method. Mean time was higher for the tuned arm, but that secondary mean contrast does not reverse the tail failures. ([Verified sealed results](../paper/trackc_final_status_2026-10-31.md); result files: [test 171](../output/trackc_sealed_tail_hybrid/sealed_results.json), [test 173](../output/trackc_sealed_tail_trigger/sealed_results.json))

The development screen supports a qualified trade-off description, not a replacement pass. Across nine assumed rate cells, the forced-holiday policy had higher mean time in eight cells and lower CVaR10 in seven; each cell’s tail rested on only about 3.6 scenarios, and no interval for the tail contrast was produced. Keep this screen labeled development-only. Do not select favorable cells, change the margin or endpoint, or run a new sealed test. Those steps would condition the claim on observed outcomes. ([Track C sensitivity report](../paper/trackc_sensitivity_screen_2026-10-30.md))

## Script 153 does not show a three-day ceiling

Blocker 3 rests on a mistaken description of the headroom run. Script 153 compared an implemented known-parameter oracle with the best fixed schedule on one training draw of 200 scenarios, one observation-noise seed, and a grid of 80. The reported CVaR10 difference was 3.4 days, with bootstrap interval [1.26, 6.93]. The oracle searched a finite set of schedules and local refinements; its feasible value is a lower bound on the true full-information optimum. The script omitted a bound for policies limited to noisy observations. So 3.4 days is **one training-set comparison, not a hard ceiling on adaptive benefit**. ([Headroom output](../output/trackc_headroom/headroom_results.json); [script 153](../src/153_trackc_headroom.py); [oracle implementation](../src/trackc_oracle.py))

Use that run only to say that the implemented oracle beat the selected fixed rule by 3.4 CVaR10 days on that training draw. It neither explains away the sealed failures nor proves the model has little room for all policies. The current decision does not require another headroom calculation: the sealed endpoint already failed, and the stop rule closes further testing until measured kinetics and independent review become available.

## Prior art rules out a broad novelty claim

Blocker 4 is resolved by correcting the literature claim. Wang, Scott, and Vladimirsky’s 2024 paper selects adaptive cancer therapy by the probability of reaching a tumor goal within a cumulative treatment-cost threshold. Browning et al.’s 2026 preprint examines parametric, measurement, and structural uncertainty in an adaptive-treatment model and shows why model misspecification can break cross-schedule predictions. Liu et al. already combine mathematical models and reinforcement learning for GBM treatment scheduling. Keramati et al. provide stronger peer-reviewed evidence that CVaR-based sequential pharmacologic control is established: their AAAI 2020 paper learns CVaR-oriented policies and evaluates CVaR0.25 in HIV drug-treatment and insulin simulators, with performance intervals across repeated runs. It is not cancer, GBM, or TMZ, and its endpoints differ from Track C. Together with toxicity-constrained adaptive dosing and the adaptive-therapy translation review, this work rules out broad claims that Track C is the first adaptive, risk-aware, uncertainty-aware, or RL-based treatment-control study. ([Wang et al., PLoS Computational Biology, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012165); [Browning et al., 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18387); [Liu et al., Science Advances, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adv3316); [Gevertz et al., npj Systems Biology and Applications](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41540-025-00635-6); [West et al., eLife, 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.84263); [Keramati et al., AAAI 2020](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v34i04.5870))

The earlier statement that no cancer-dosing paper reports worst-10% CVaR is incorrect, but this cancer-specific precedent deserves low confidence. Alhaji, Ahmad, and Abba report CVaR0.1 of final tumor burden for a DQN adaptive cancer-therapy policy against no-treatment and maximum-dose policies. The article identity and DOI resolve, but indexing in Scopus or Web of Science was not verified in this search. One reference misattributes a separate chemotherapy-RL paper: its Eastman citation does not match the verifiable 2021 Scientific Reports article. This citation mismatch lowers confidence in the reference list, but does not establish misconduct or invalidate Alhaji et al.’s simulation results. The reported evidence is limited: 50 virtual patients, CVaR used for evaluation rather than policy training, a final-burden endpoint rather than capped time-to-progression, and no evident paired interval for CVaR contrasts or external validation. Treat it as a direct but low-confidence cancer-specific CVaR10 precedent, not as validation or a close replication. ([Alhaji et al., 2026 article and PDF](https://www.ijisrt.com/assets/upload/files/IJISRT26JAN1026.pdf); [verified Eastman et al. chemotherapy-RL paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-97028-6); [Besançon et al., JASIST 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/asi.24896))

The bounded remaining distinction is only that this search did not find a close GBM/TMZ study using lower-decile capped time-to-progression as the primary policy-selection objective in this exact two-population model. That narrow search gap is not a novelty result. Describe the completed work by its actual scope and disclose that its selected policy failed the tail criterion.

## Patient cohorts support a separate question

Blocker 5 is a scope issue. UCSF-ALPTDG includes 298 diffuse-glioma patients, including 80 with GBM, with consecutive post-treatment MRI scans, expert tumor annotations, and clinical history. Its data-use terms support a potential patient-held-out imaging-change forecast. But the cohort’s standard-care imaging and regimen history do not provide course-aligned TMZ exposure plus repeated sensitivity assays. It therefore cannot calibrate the resistance/reversion rates or validate Track C’s adaptive dosing rule. Any such imaging forecast would be a separate analysis with its own eligibility, permission, endpoint, and protocol. ([Fields et al., Radiology: Artificial Intelligence, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11294954/))

Clinical trials show why treatment schedules and patient outcomes are not enough to infer Track C’s biology. RTOG 0525 randomized patients to two fixed TMZ schedules and found no significant survival advantage for dose-dense treatment, with more severe toxicity; it did not measure tumor sensitivity through washout. Clinical outcomes can inform a future patient-centered model only with an appropriate causal design and benefit-harm endpoint, not by retrofitting this simulator’s assumed rates. ([Gilbert et al., Journal of Clinical Oncology, 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/3816958/))

Blocker 6 is a guardrail already doing its job. Keep the 31 October amendment: no new sealed test, no changed margin, and no promotion of development results. The planned 2,000-scenario primary outcome remains not executed. A future proposal requires measured multi-duration off-drug recovery data, outside review of the rate bounds, and prospective precision planning before any new protocol is considered. The current Track C disposition is complete without violating that rule.

## Conclusion

Report Track C as a simulation-only comparison of mean and lower-decile capped progression time under assumed parameters. State both H1 failures and describe the 3.4-day oracle result narrowly. CVaR and tail-risk sequential dosing are established methods; a cancer-specific CVaR10 evaluation exists but is low-confidence, while the exact GBM/TMZ capped-TTP distinction remains descriptive and does not establish novelty or success. The located washout studies and patient cohorts do not identify the simulator’s TMZ recovery rate or support a patient-benefit claim. Keep the stop rule in force unless the specified kinetic evidence and review gate pass.
