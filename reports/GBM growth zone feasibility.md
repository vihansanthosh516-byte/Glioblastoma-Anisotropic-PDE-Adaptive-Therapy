# Test Tumor Growth Zones Without Overclaiming

The proposal can become a methodologically credible **exploratory prediction study**, but the current plan is not ready for patient scoring and does not establish a new biological growth model. Its observable target is the appearance of new label-1/3 voxels on the next scan, not verified growth of viable tumor. The read-only gate found 82 visit-2-to-visit-3 pairs, comprising 67 GBM and 15 other gliomas; all 82 exact pairs already occur in earlier Track B analysis, so a 50/32 split cannot provide fresh confirmation. A well-posed screened-diffusion score, compared with identical-input logistic and weighted-geodesic baselines, could isolate whether the PDE adds spatial ranking information. The current top-N metric uses the future positive volume and therefore is an oracle diagnostic, not an independent prediction result. With roughly 50 development patients, a small logistic model is feasible with patient-balanced fitting, while the proposed high-capacity B2 model is likely underpowered and overfit. The defensible contribution is a careful, failure-aware test of incremental value on a reused cohort; novelty and success remain unproven.

## The target is annotation change, not tumor growth

For patient \(i\), define \(T_{ik}(x)=1\) when the visit-\(k\) mask labels voxel \(x\) as 1 or 3. After a prespecified alignment of the two scans, define \(Y_{ik}(x)=1\{T_{i,k+1}(x)=1,\ T_{ik}(x)=0\}\). This is a **newly observed label-1/3 voxel**. The MU-Glioma-Post label definitions distinguish non-enhancing core, FLAIR hyperintensity, enhancing tissue, and cavity/post-surgical change; label 1 itself can include cystic, necrotic, or hemorrhagic tissue, and label 2 combines infiltrative tumor with edema. Subtracting the masks therefore does not identify new viable cells, invasion, clinical progression, or treatment failure ([dataset paper, full text opened](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12635165/)).

The current 20-mm candidate region should be frozen using only visit-\(k\) information: brain tissue within 20 mm of the union of the current label-1/3 region and cavity, excluding current tumor, cavity, and non-brain/CSF voxels. Current label-2 tissue can remain eligible and serve as a predictor. Report what fraction of all follow-up new label-1/3 voxels falls inside this ROI; positives outside it are missed by the study’s scope, not evidence of a no-growth case. Also report the count of candidate voxels, class prevalence, and patients with no positive or no negative candidate voxels. AUC is undefined for those patients; analyze no-positive-in-ROI patients separately as false-alert cases, using an alert threshold selected only in development.

The label difference mixes tumor evolution with treatment response, pseudoprogression, contrast timing, segmentation variability, and alignment error. RANO guidance recognizes that post-radiotherapy changes can mimic progression ([RANO 2.0, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.01059)); longitudinal glioma segmentations show poor spatial agreement for postoperative enhancing GBM, and registration work documents that surgery and evolving pathology can break ordinary image correspondences ([Visser et al., abstract-only](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nicl.2019.101727); [Canalini et al., full text opened](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnimg.2022.977491)). The shared 1-mm atlas grid and matching NIfTI headers in the local audit do not demonstrate accurate pairwise alignment at the lesion boundary. A single cohort-wide median-shift cutoff is inadequate: define patient-level registration quality checks, inspect prespecified landmarks or anatomical correspondence, and report sensitivity to the unregistered and approved registered target. The transform may align the outcome masks, but follow-up images must not supply prediction features or alter the visit-\(k\) candidate ROI.

Do not describe residual disagreement as scanner noise. The earlier Track B reliability work correctly treats forecast-to-mask residuals as effective predictive discrepancy because biology, treatment, registration, and segmentation are not separately identified from these scans ([medical boundary error review](../research_notes/GBM%20covariance%20calibration%20reliability/medical_boundary_errors.md)). The same limitation applies here even though the new proposal avoids fitting a patient-specific spatial covariance. Record treatment and scan-gap information when available; if interval treatment can be reconstructed reliably, use one prespecified low-dimensional treatment indicator in every learned arm or stratify by treatment status. If timing cannot be reconstructed, state that as a central limitation rather than implying the field has isolated growth biology.

## The 82-pair split cannot confirm a fresh result

The 82 count is supported for a narrow gate: patients with three nonempty initial masks and a visit-2-to-visit-3 interval of 14–365 days. It does not certify all eligible visit pairs per patient, nor does it establish registration quality, clinical comparability, or evaluability for the proposed ROI. The cohort is mixed histology: **67 GBM and 15 non-GBM gliomas**. Since the stated study is GBM, either restrict the primary analysis to the eligible GBM patients and recalculate the sample and split, or call it a mixed-glioma study and report histology-stratified results as exploratory. The 15 non-GBM cases are too few to support a reliable separate comparison. The audit supports one prespecified visit-2-to-visit-3 pair per patient; using additional pairs would require a separate eligibility audit and patient-clustered analysis ([read-only data gate](../research_notes/GBM%20growth%20zone%20feasibility/data_gate.md)).

Most consequentially, the audit’s metadata join found that **all 82 exact candidate pairs already appear in the earlier Track B forecastability output**. That analysis did not report this exact growth-zone AUC, so the endpoint can still be new to the project; it does mean the patients, visits, and masks are not untouched. A new random or hash-based 50/32 split cannot undo that prior exposure. If retained, the 32-person set is a post-hoc internal evaluation of a newly specified endpoint, not a fresh external or confirmatory test. A confirmatory claim requires compatible patients whose images and labels have not been used during prior exploration, or prospective data. If no such openly available cohort can be verified, the honest path is an explicitly exploratory MU analysis with prior use disclosed.

The dataset is listed by TCIA as openly reusable under CC BY 4.0 with attribution, and the dataset paper describes public release. However, the audit could not open the TCIA page directly, and the local-release check matched names and file counts rather than content checksums. The local tree contains 596 time-point directories, while the paper reports 594 time points with masks; the audit found 594 directories with a mask and two image-only directories. Before redistributing files or freezing provenance, verify the exact TCIA release/version and license in the official collection page and record source identifiers plus file checksums ([TCIA collection](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/mu-glioma-post/); [dataset DOI](https://doi.org/10.7937/7K9K-3C83); [CC BY 4.0 terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)).

## A screened PDE and matched path baseline make the test fair

The proposed “reach” field is not yet a specified mathematical model. A tractable, well-posed candidate is
\[
-\nabla\!\cdot(A_\theta(x)\nabla u_i(x))+\kappa^2u_i(x)
=\mathbf{1}_{T_{ik}}(x),\qquad x\in\Omega_i,
\]
where \(T_{ik}\) is the baseline label-1/3 region, \(\Omega_i\) is the brain-tissue domain with cavity and CSF removed, \(A_\theta(x)\) is a uniformly positive tissue-weight map, and \(\kappa>0\) is a screening parameter. State the outer-brain and cavity boundary conditions explicitly; a no-flux condition at the cavity makes it a reflecting obstacle that routes the field around it, not evidence that recurrence cannot occur near the cavity. The positive screening term gives a unique solution under standard no-flux conditions. By contrast, pure Neumann Poisson with a positive tumor source is incompatible: integrating over the domain gives zero total boundary flux on the left but a positive source integral on the right. Use a conservative finite-volume or graph-Laplacian discretization so tissue-interface fluxes and cavity handling are explicit.

The PDE should yield a **spatial ranking feature**, not be called a biological growth forecast. The proposed FLAIR/white-matter speed map is scalar and locally isotropic; without patient-specific DTI tensors, it is not directional tractography. Fix the global scale of \(A_\theta\) and fit relative tissue weights or a dimensionless screening ratio inside development folds. Otherwise absolute diffusion and screening values are confounded with the free logistic coefficient on \(u\). A stationary field has no elapsed-time horizon. Either call it a static spatial prior for the next observed scan and include the scan interval identically in every learned comparator, with interval-stratified sensitivity, or change the model to a time-dependent diffusion equation and treat that as a different, more demanding study.

The primary comparison must isolate the PDE contribution. Keep distance-only as a reference, but make the main non-PDE logistic baseline use the **same visit-\(k\) information** as the hybrid: distance, current label-2 indicator, FLAIR intensity, white-matter prior, cavity distance/indicator, and elapsed days. If treatment timing is usable, add the same low-dimensional treatment indicator to both. The hybrid is that same model plus \(u_i(x)\). The plan’s original four-parameter B1 is computationally feasible; in five-fold development CV, each outer training fold has only about 40 independent patients before any inner tuning, so parameter selection must remain small and patient-balanced. Add a weighted fast-marching/geodesic arm with the same tissue costs and cavity treatment; diffusion aggregates paths while a geodesic uses the least-cost path, so this comparison asks whether diffusion’s path averaging/smoothing adds anything beyond matched features and shortest-path structure. Keep coefficients, standardization, tissue weights, and tuning inside patient-level training folds. Gradient boosting on many voxel features should not be a primary “best method” comparator with only about 50 development patients; voxel rows are correlated and do not create a large independent sample. Omit B2 initially, or report it as exploratory only with shallow complexity, patient-balanced fitting, and nested patient-level tuning. AUC tests ranking, not probability calibration, so call outputs scores unless calibration is separately evaluated.

Prior art rules out broad novelty claims. Akbari et al. predicted later recurrence location from preoperative multiparametric MRI with a replication cohort (abstract-only in this review) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26813856/)); ReSPOND mapped infiltration and later recurrence from multicenter preoperative MRI (full text opened) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11363410/)); Lao et al. combined postoperative cavity/niche proximity with voxelwise MRI classification (abstract-only) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34963559/)); and postoperative voxelwise radiomics already predicts later local recurrence ([article; full-text excerpts, not fully opened](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers15061894)). TaDiff-Net forecasts future glioma masks (arXiv page/abstract checked; full methods not checked) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05406)), while PREDICT-GBM and GliODIL benchmark learned and PDE-based recurrence maps ([PREDICT-GBM, indexed full-text passages checked](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-03194-0); [GliODIL, article text checked](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-60366-4)). Diffusion and weighted shortest-path tumor-front models have also been compared directly ([Bortfeld and Buti, full text opened](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6560/ac8043)). The targeted search covered postoperative recurrence localization, voxelwise MRI radiomics, future-mask prediction, and PDE/geodesic tumor-front models; it did not include complete citation chasing or full-text review of every close paper. The candidate is distinct mainly by its narrow evaluation question, not by inventing PDEs, cavity maps, FLAIR predictors, or recurrence localization.

## Patient-level evaluation limits the claim

Use one prespecified visit pair per patient for the primary analysis. For each evaluable patient, calculate voxel-ranking AUC within the frozen ROI, then estimate the mean paired difference \(\Delta=\operatorname{mean}_i[\mathrm{AUC}_i(H)-\mathrm{AUC}_i(M)]\), where \(M\) is the feature-matched logistic baseline. This estimand gives each patient equal weight and is conditional on having both positive and negative candidates. Report the evaluable patient count and all exclusions. Resample whole patients—not voxels or scan pairs—for uncertainty; if multiple pairs are later included, aggregate them by a prespecified patient-level rule and resample the entire patient cluster. With only 32 nominal held-out patients, and fewer if restricting to GBM or excluding registration failures, the plan has not shown that a 0.02 AUC difference is detectable. The effective sample size is patients, not millions of voxels.

The proposed top-N score sets \(N\) equal to the true number of new voxels at the next scan. If that true positive count is \(P\), then precision@P equals recall@P exactly because both are true positives divided by \(P\). It also uses future information to choose the prediction budget. Keep it only as an **oracle-size localization diagnostic**. Add a non-oracle fixed alert budget or score threshold chosen in development, and report precision, recall, and false-alert burden at that frozen operating point; include PR-AUC because the positive voxels are sparse. The current rule “point estimate at least 0.02 and confidence interval above zero” does not establish a gain of at least 0.02. If 0.02 is the prespecified minimum useful effect, require the paired patient-level 95% interval’s lower bound to exceed 0.02; otherwise report whether the gain is positive and how uncertain its practical size remains. A bootstrap interval at this sample size is an uncertainty summary, not a guarantee of adequate power.

Before any patient scoring, the next gate should be a read-only protocol freeze: confirm the exact GBM-only or mixed-histology cohort, the single visit pair and interval distribution, the label and ROI rules, treatment metadata, local release provenance, registration quality, and whether a genuinely unused external cohort exists. Then freeze the screened PDE, matched logistic and geodesic comparators, patient split, fold-contained preprocessing, metrics, exclusion rules, and success language. Test the numerical solver on small manufactured cases and test the scientific discrimination on an independently generated random-walk or agent-based process, not data generated by the same PDE; otherwise the hybrid wins by construction. Stop before held-out scoring if the outcome cannot be registered and audited reproducibly. Stop tuning and report no added value if the hybrid fails the prespecified development-fold comparison. If there is no unused cohort, do not label any MU split confirmatory.

## Conclusion

The strongest version is a modest, transparent experiment: does a shared screened-diffusion feature improve equal-patient ranking of next-scan annotated core/enhancing additions over the same covariates and a matched weighted-geodesic model? A positive result would support only that incremental claim on this already-used dataset; a negative result would still show that the extra PDE structure did not earn its complexity. The next action is to close the read-only provenance, target, and registration gate and decide whether the work will be exploratory or can obtain genuinely untouched data. Do not start patient scoring until those choices are frozen.
